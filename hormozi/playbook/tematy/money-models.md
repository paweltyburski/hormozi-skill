# money-models

The canon already covers the Money Model, Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA), the Four Types of Offers, the Offer Stack, Point of Greatest Deprivation, the Five Upsell Moments, all the named attraction, upsell, downsell and continuity plays (Win Your Money Back through Waived Fee, Big Head Long Tail), the Crazy Eight and the Three Levels of Advertising. The recordings add: take-rate and conversion benchmarks, the full arithmetic of the gym challenge funnel, ratios for choosing between one-time and recurring, extra Lost Chapters plays (value grid, Pick Your Price, Discount plus One-Time Fee, lifetime upgrades and discounts), a long run of live diagnoses where he turns an "ads" or "conversion" question into a back-end or cash-timing question, and several admitted revisions of his own view.

## Rules and thresholds

1. The core test, restated many ways: net cash from one new customer in the first 30 days should exceed twice (acquisition plus fulfilment), so that one customer pays for the next two. [YT-XwZH-lOKG9c@00:10:04] [POD-0436@00:02:24] [LS-6_CCutkM11g@00:12:07]
2. Required first-sale cash is about two times CAC plus COGS (his numbers: CAC $100, COGS $50, target $250). [YT-k-3PoOT4vOM@00:13:30]
3. Under $10M a year, one product in one channel; between $3M and $10M, put the focus on back-end lifetime gross profit. [POD-0439@00:20:25] From $1M to $10M a back end taking LTV from $1,000 to $5,000 is what pays for broader channels. [YT-R7Fin9HFhoM@00:03:10]
4. With several offers, advertise the one with the best LTV:CAC and stack the rest behind it, unless its market is tiny; compare a new offer to the old on conversion rate and lifetime gross profit. [YT-jzKpAtzKQ54@00:14:55] [POD-0782@00:23:53] [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@02:19:10]
5. Impulse price: typically about $600 is the break point for a first buy from cold traffic, and he says 20-30% of buyers could pay two to three times that; the consumer impulse range is roughly $300-600. [POD-0070@00:07:12] [POD-0147@00:07:45] [LS-lidFtb1Ga0o@00:13:28]
6. Ascension target: advice to one caller on a 7% upsell, at least 25% to the next offer, aim for 50%; one $30M course business sat at 7% and that was its real problem. [POD-0804@00:03:15] A paid wall at week 16 rolled out gradually got 10% at once and 27% within 60 days. [YT-yPDQCfrwh8E@00:19:34]
7. Ask "what share of core buyers take the upsell?" Barely 20% points to an offer problem on the back end, the front end, or in how they fit. [YT-n6SHLmKcY0E@00:03:04]
8. Upsell take rates: 20-30% is normal for bolt-ons; 20-40% when pitched as a new sale; 90%+ can be reached for an assumed close or when the item feels essential and frictionless; 80-90% is his hope (not a result) if framed as the default. [YT-j1tA4l7R2c0@00:01:37] [YT--Koq14DPXC4@00:40:53] [YT-xy1h4qqF778@00:03:55] [POD-251114-20@00:05:18]
9. An upsell pitched only at the end of a programme gets about a third; pitched at halfway, two-thirds and a last chance it gets half to two thirds or more. [YT-8VqSFBMMS4M@00:10:38]
10. Price each new tier 5-10x the one below, expect about 20% (20-30%) to take it, and want each tier to roughly double revenue; the next rung after a $1,000 product is $5,000-$10,000, not 1.5x. [YT-ZuJryiwxjDw@00:08:59] [YT-ZuJryiwxjDw@00:17:23] [LS-32fVmwvLmTM@00:56:26]
11. When raising prices, he says go up by a significant margin: $100 vs $120 is basically the same decision, and at $120 about $167 is also described as the same decision. [POD-0032@00:07:52] With a $100 product (90 buyers) and a $1,000 product (10 buyers) at 40% margin, the expensive tier yields about three quarters of profit. [YT-uWdIgftpvBI@00:09:16]
12. Prepay: annual billing typically at about 16% off (buy 10, get two free), with a quarterly option if the full prepay is too heavy; offered as an option it is taken by 10-15% and 10% uptake roughly doubles upfront cash, with break-even within 7 days as the goal. [YT-41EvCgwPrDc@00:24:45] [YT-oRMG_HpOAN4@00:47:02]
13. Pay-in-full discount of at least 10%; uptake about 15-20% for buy-10-get-2 but 30-40% when a moderate discount is paired with faster, safer or easier perks. [YT-A_tx40lNpf8@00:48:36] Prepay discount of 10-20% on a committed year gets about 20-30%. [YT-nSQdjim8CsE@00:14:59]
14. One-time versus continuity split is set by the price ratio: standalone at 1.33x the continuity payment gives 50% continuity, 1.66x gives 60%, 2x gives 70%, 2.33x gives 80%, 2.66x gives 90%. [POD-250819-07@00:10:26] To push more into membership, raise the one-time price in about 10% steps above the roughly 30% parity premium. [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@01:38:49]
15. A fee of 1.5-3x the monthly rate brings more month-to-month buyers and upfront cash; a standard 3-5x fee drives commitment. [POD-250819-07@00:29:33] Front-loaded discounts convert more but may have higher churn; back-loaded convert fewer and have lower churn. [POD-250819-07@00:24:23]
16. Rollover: price the next offer at least 4x the credit so the discount never exceeds 25%. [POD-250819-05@00:37:39] Personalised videos to 200 past customers with a $4,000 credit brought back about 20% and $1.9M annual revenue. [POD-250819-05@00:36:51]
17. In his opinion, downsell only callers who fail qualification, never those who qualify for the core offer; make downsells low-effort and often high margin (almost 100% for them). [YT-MwhsM7qijy8@00:08:32] [YT-XwZH-lOKG9c@01:19:47]
18. Order upsells with the higher-profit one first; if a lot of customers buy small, bump them to medium, and so on up the tiers; avoid nickel-and-diming, since most times many things people would charge for should be goodwill, so pick the select offers with the most upside. [POD-250819-05@00:05:29] [POD-250819-05@00:10:00] [POD-0106@00:01:32]
19. Time the upsell to the new need the first win creates, not to the moment of peak satisfaction. [POD-0861@00:11:17] [YT-sBJppqCeFGI@00:21:21]
20. Education: price the one-time value as high as the market allows, price the consumable at what people would pay for it alone, and do not mistake a payment plan for continuity. [YT-FiL0r5_dUvM@00:07:19] [YT-FiL0r5_dUvM@00:02:21]
21. Fastest cash source order: current customers, past customers, then the leads list; match the play to the deadline (text blast for tomorrow, internal promotion for two weeks, probably the free-then-upsell hybrid if you can wait a month). [LS-0RjIw9M1j28@01:23:21] [YT-SvIcS-Q1Hl4@00:11:04]
22. Gym benchmarks: strong retention should stack $5,000-$10,000 a month recurring; lower the entry barrier to get people onto recurring faster at the cost of upfront cash; the recurring base alone then covers operations at 60-70%+ margin. [POD-0103@00:05:39] [POD-0103@00:01:33] [POD-0103@00:03:58]
23. Challenge-funnel benchmarks (gym): about $5 lead, 1 in 4 buys the ~$600 challenge, CAC about $20-25, $200-$300 supplements two days later; about $680 gross profit per paying customer within 48 hours; about 80% of orientation attendees buy two or more supplements. [POD-0557@00:06:00] [POD-250819-01@00:08:14] [POD-0172@00:09:22]
24. Challenge back-end conversion: over 75% at halfway appointments, 3 of 4, two thirds, and a ~70% target if done right; about $500-$600 per six-week programme. [POD-0198@00:15:44] [POD-0462@00:07:50] [POD-250819-01@00:09:00] [POD-0491@00:34:09]
25. Industry norms he cites against it: about 35% of trial buyers convert and a $99 member stays about 5 months; about 30% of trial takers call to dispute billing, so sell the continuity, not the trial. [YT-nSQdjim8CsE@00:09:15] [YT-pxVeOkOVr2w@00:11:10]

## Tactics and scripts

1. Assume-close the upsell with a binary choice ("chocolate or vanilla?"), then ask "you don't want anything else, do you?"; take rate rises sharply. [POD-0170@00:03:26] [YT-xy1h4qqF778@00:00:00] [YT-j1tA4l7R2c0@00:01:37]
2. Menu upsell script: seed, teach food first, unsell, recommend cheaper outside brands for generics, prescribe with daily habit anchors, offer flavour choices, close on the card on file. [LS--HbwUdqUcEs@01:29:17]
3. Prescription upselling: write dose and timing beside each item on the order form, then ask for payment; unselling first builds goodwill. [POD-250819-05@00:14:38] [POD-250819-05@00:15:28]
4. Get the card from the signed contract and make the add-on a one-click yes; "use the card on file?" removes the small hidden costs of buying. [POD-0437@00:07:06] [POD-250819-05@00:18:46]
5. Sell supplements at a nutrition consult about 48 hours after sign-up, not at sign-up. [YT-6m6DCQMASEM@00:02:59] Refusers of the core service get a free programme plus a free nutrition orientation; they spent 50% more on supplements. [POD-251114-20@00:00:47]
6. Win Your Money Back at the halfway point: tell members honestly the goal will not be reached in time, declare them winners, credit $600 over 12 months ($50 a month). [POD-0557@00:06:00] Make earn-back criteria both activation and advertising actions. [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@03:10:25]
7. Spread any credit or free-time discount across the term, not off the first payment; it keeps cash flowing. [POD-250819-07@00:17:40] [YT-n6SHLmKcY0E@00:34:25] [LS-B2QbLzHvKbg@04:01:40]
8. Two options at the close (about $199 up front or a monthly membership): a very high option A ($500-$700) pushes ~90% into continuity; equal prices push cash up front. [POD-0196@00:03:04] [POD-0196@00:08:51]
9. Anchor tier: build a version at 10x (up to 50x or 100x), put it first on the menu; around 10% buying it roughly doubles revenue; change only secondary features. [YT-SmiOK8Yun4s@00:23:50] [YT-yEKu6q0W3gs@00:00:00] [POD-0802@00:30:51]
10. Magnetic middle: place the middle tier to decide what you push; if small buyers dominate make medium look like a bargain, if mediums dominate raise the medium to nudge them toward large. [POD-250819-05@00:08:40] [YT-HwPXctiw_rY@00:45:32]
11. Layaway: delivery starts only when paid; the buyer sets the pace; he says it works "nine times out of ten". [YT-rMJIOK_FgJk@00:03:43] [YT-A_tx40lNpf8@00:52:47] [YT-N5MExtki_VI@00:25:14]
12. Payment-plan ladder: pay in full by card, smaller down payment with weekly payments, short fixed term, then free trial with commitments; the full ladder can reach nine offers. [POD-0182@00:05:14] [POD-250819-06@00:10:23]
13. Free variants: limited free (inferior service, then upsell), splinter free (one part that cannot work alone), invisible free (card taken, billed unless they say after day one it was not worth it). [YT-XwZH-lOKG9c@00:39:22] [YT-XwZH-lOKG9c@00:43:42] [YT-XwZH-lOKG9c@00:45:07]
14. Pick Your Price: advertise free, buyers choose what to pay at checkout, bonuses at three payment levels; close by testing commitment, laying out what each level adds, asking which card, then silence. [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@02:00:08] [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@02:04:07]
15. Discount plus One-Time Fee: separate recurring and upfront charges so ads can show a huge first-period discount (first month 88% off plus a $1,000 setup). [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@02:30:55] [POD-251114-23@00:01:38]
16. Waived fee: large setup fee plus monthly, or waive setup for a 12-month term with an exit if no result in 90 days; have buyers initial that early exit means owing the waived fee. [LS-eW04CvHKvMo@01:32:52] [POD-250819-07@00:26:10]
17. Value grid: list every prospect type against every offer instead of a stair-step; any new offer gets four checks (stage, problem, way, time); add conversations one at a time, best earner first. [POD-251114-15@00:02:24] [POD-251114-16@00:11:53] [POD-251114-16@00:11:27]
18. Retention bonus design: Hormozi pairs a when (delay or milestone) with a what (one-time, variable or lifetime upgrade); a gift timed after the typical churn point (fifth purchase). [POD-251114-21@00:03:52] [POD-0806@00:16:50] [YT-eX3Ch_HuR70@00:35:20]
19. Annual maintenance plan as an insurance-positioned add-on whose real job is a yearly visit that surfaces new work (roofing $180-$240 a year, target 80% take; signs $15 a month). [LS-IG9IWQVmxqk@00:50:46] [YT-o64cI6tebnU@00:12:27] [LS-noE1MQPMvAc@01:24:25]
20. Sell the system once, then the recurring inputs it consumes; split deliverables into one-time (expires once learned) and consumables (accountability, community, tested ads). [POD-0418@00:09:35] [YT-FiL0r5_dUvM@00:03:42] [POD-0503@00:16:53]
21. Call unhappy customers: one in four buying a 4x offer recovers all refund losses; exit interviews resell over half of leavers. [YT-afbP6sB_Atc@00:33:42] [YT-y3T4GqoH0Us@00:07:16]
22. Self-liquidating offer on the opt-in thank-you page ($37 pack, $197-$397 in another case) so a small share of buyers pays for acquisition. [LS-I0nCuqu7Cac@02:13:15] [LS-TLpLmhWqKEU@01:25:21]

## Changes of mind and contradictions

1. Low barrier offers: he long bashed them, partly for polarising marketing, and now says they work for businesses with stacked lifetime value. [POD-0103@00:02:54] The same recordings still favour charging $500-$600 over free trials. [POD-0607@00:10:12]
2. Downsells: he now favours them less, since giving the cheap product away free beats selling it to a few (10% take at one tenth the price is about 1% revenue). [YT-reisEL_D7xc@01:11:47] Against that, in diligence he told a firm to give away a downsell nobody took and lead flow tripled. [YT-cnbHgYYHzyE@00:35:35] And the earlier "nobody walks out without signing" rule. [POD-0182@00:08:11]
3. Upsell take rate: 20-30% typical, 20-40% as a new sale, 90-95% as a default. [YT-j1tA4l7R2c0@00:01:37] [YT--Koq14DPXC4@00:40:53] [YT-xy1h4qqF778@00:03:55]
4. Annual: ~10-15% take as an option, 30% when pitched, annual-first with monthly as downsell for one caller; one brief 12-month upfront offer worked worse than a defined-end programme. [YT-oRMG_HpOAN4@00:47:02] [YT-j2TZMFkj71Q@00:00:00] [LS-TLgSkSQUql4@00:00:00] [YT-hnfh2jqNVEQ@00:07:19]
5. Upsell timing: pitch at peak satisfaction fails; at the end of a programme it gets a third; he now says sell at the new need or halfway. [POD-0861@00:09:11] [YT-8VqSFBMMS4M@00:10:38] [POD-0861@00:11:17]
6. Membership as front or back end: a membership works as a back end after a job, not a front end, yet Savage X Fenty makes membership the primary offer and a $9 community serves as entry. [POD-0879@00:15:27] [YT-eX3Ch_HuR70@00:41:22] [YT-hHkdbr6_JJs@00:02:23]
7. Defined programme before recurring versus long free-month commitments: continuity priced near $1,500 after $6,000 feels like a downsell, yet a free first month for a long contract lifts conversion. [YT-Gh9zWsP8JpI@00:01:02] [YT-pxVeOkOVr2w@00:10:25]
8. Same funnel, different numbers: $10 lead and 1 in 5 buying for about 12:1, versus $5 lead and 25% for about 34:1 in 48 hours; $300 six-week challenge versus $500-$600. [POD-0481@00:01:36] [POD-250819-01@00:08:14] [POD-0536@00:03:13]
9. Gym Launch origin: 191 sales at $600 in 19 days (~$120,000), about $60,000 in 14 days, about $100,000 per 21-day launch. [POD-200710-01@00:04:49] [POD-0557@00:20:30] [POD-0427@00:20:16]
