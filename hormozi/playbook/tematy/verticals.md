# verticals

The canon covers this topic only through gyms and a few track-record claims: Low Barrier Offer trials, Sell to Goal with meals, supplements, continuity and prepay, the gym-turnaround service that became the Gym Launch licence business, landmark-based local ads, and "charge once for one-time value". The recordings add far more: gym unit economics and thresholds, a run of e-commerce and physical-product cases (a custom railing store, a gaming-table maker, a hair-extension wholesaler, a luxury-watch dealer, subscription boxes, apparel drops), local and home services, clinics, agencies, and his repeated claim that most owners misjudge what business they are in. There is no material on tenders, quoting, or multi-stakeholder sales cycles (one line on the last: section 2). Made-to-order and manufacturing appear only as short coaching cases, so treat them as thin evidence.

## 2. Rules and thresholds

Gyms and other location businesses
1. Small owner-run gyms lose about 10% of members a month and add about 10 a month, so they stall near 100 members. [POD-0082@00:02:18] [POD-0113@00:00:00]
2. Proactive retention outreach typically pushed gyms' churn from 8-12% in month one to about 3% by month three, staying under 3%, taking about 12 weeks. [POD-0167@00:02:20]
3. A blended price near $100 a month does not work for most gyms; large-group training must sit at $150-$225 and the semi-private upsell at $500-$700. [POD-0029@00:04:13] [POD-0070@00:02:48]
4. Bare-minimum price for a capped 2-3 session plan: $167 ($39/week). Industry data: profitable gyms charge about $172 against an average of about $132. [YT-mOKl6dodMTg@00:10:28] [POD-0139@00:06:41] [POD-0085@00:00:50]
5. The average gym nets only about 12.5%, so a small price rise moves profit sharply: about +$38 a month on $129 gave about 3.1x profit (as stated). [YT-mOKl6dodMTg@00:03:51] [YT-XwZH-lOKG9c@01:10:35]
6. Do not open a second location until the first shows high net profit for six months ($20,000 a month), the owner is absent and it still grows. [POD-0087@00:03:26] [YT-h6y0nYVZgwE@00:59:07]
7. Around $50,000 net per facility interests nobody; corporate units should run 30%+ margins before licensing or franchising. [YT-_8olk4-VFP8@00:02:12]
8. A unit that pays back $300,000 on average in year one on a $100,000 build is more enticing to investors; a three-year payback is mediocre. Judge sites by cash-on-cash return. [YT-3fsJFUvA6Ts@00:13:28] [YT-I64gYLBrics@00:46:18]
9. Do not franchise until one site is profitable and you know LTV, CAC, churn and upsell rates; expect franchisees to execute at about 35% of your level. [POD-200710-01@00:26:48] [POD-200710-01@00:27:33]

Products and e-commerce
10. Hormozi would probably pick a physical product that is cheap to make, consumed regularly and needs no willpower, like a nose strip (example: cost 2 cents, price $1). [YT-LvHDT0ZxSmw@00:00:00] [POD-0447@00:00:00]
11. A media-arbitrage e-commerce model tends to cap around $10-12M, with margin and cash flow shrinking as CAC rises. [YT-8C_6qojTA78@00:12:43] [YT-8C_6qojTA78@00:14:21]
12. E-commerce growth moves in steps capped by inventory cash, traffic, distribution or supply, and flattens until each is unlocked. [YT-qsXxckCbci0@00:00:45]
13. Margin reference points: a physical subscription box about 30% gross and about 6.5% net; meal delivery at 10% gross leaves $70 of gross profit on $700 of revenue. [POD-0819@00:43:36] [YT-orMbq2LtzKE@00:44:04] [YT-Cr0KdqZ954c@00:01:02]

Services, agencies, professionals
14. SMB customers stick about 4-6 months at $1,500-3,000 a month and 30-40 months at $300-400. [YT-jqo0lVveh98@00:08:57]
15. SMB lead-gen at $1,500-$3,500 a month is a no-man's land: go very cheap with near-zero delivery cost, or mid-market at about $10K a month. [LS-I0nCuqu7Cac@03:45:40] [LS-B2QbLzHvKbg@03:32:17]

## 3. Tactics and scripts

1. Open a site full: start ads when the lease is signed, presell memberships for about 30 days (about $100,000) and fund the build-out from that cash. [POD-0557@00:04:31] [YT-i7bLRKwKSms@00:05:39] [YT-aBPWCdaJJqA@00:18:51]
2. Charge $500-$600 upfront for a six-week challenge instead of a free trial, so each sale funds the next customers. [POD-0607@00:10:12] [LS-32fVmwvLmTM@00:50:57]
3. Step-down ladder in the sales room: nine walk-ins gave about $7,200 versus $300 for the plain membership pitch. [POD-0182@00:09:46]
4. Sell the pairing product at the same visit: a one-on-one nutrition consult after the challenge sale earned about 5x rivals' supplement revenue per member. [POD-0766@00:01:09] [YT-xy1h4qqF778@00:04:12]
5. Reprice for the smaller group rather than the old price: bootcamp $99 to $299 lost a third of clients and doubled revenue. [YT-BZQtuK-ucDM@00:03:49] [YT-mOKl6dodMTg@00:00:51]
6. Phone script for local appointments: first name as a question, name the source, give a landmark, ask what time works, offer to text the address. [YT-aFoMYz_jWcs@00:11:00]
7. E-commerce offer mechanics: show how to use the product, lower risk with unboxing and a seven-day delayed charge, one-click checkout, bonuses as usage training, outcome in the product name, stock counts and a short price window. [YT-NA61omfYgvI@00:03:20] [YT-NA61omfYgvI@00:05:01] [YT-NA61omfYgvI@00:06:43] [YT-NA61omfYgvI@00:07:32]
8. Physical-product scarcity: limited flavours or sizes, sell out, announce it, no more than about monthly; or treat the marketplace sale as a break-even lead and limit the back end. [POD-0583@00:16:44] [POD-0459@00:24:10]
9. Partner through the partner's biggest problem or existing customers: employers handing new hires free massages, trainers' software needing first clients. [POD-230819-17@00:29:33] [YT-XwZH-lOKG9c@00:51:24]
10. Rainmaker version: run ads and sell for a local facility that fulfils near cost, keep the spread, and pre-negotiate that you keep retail sales. [POD-0481@00:01:36] [POD-0481@00:09:18]

## 5. Changes of mind and contradictions

1. Locations: he urges single sites and thinks three or four stores probably leave owners stressed and not making much, but his own gyms opened one every six months from presales, and later he says reinvesting everything left him asset-rich and cash-poor. Acquisition.com now favours brick-and-mortar chains with three or more sites. [POD-0087@00:05:02] [YT-d0dBZbd13jM@00:05:55] [YT-EPEjYEihZ1s@00:01:36] [POD-0627@00:38:54] [POD-0642@00:29:14]
2. Licence versus owning: licensing scaled Gym Launch, yet he later says it missed about $900M by selling one-time licences, and a portfolio company grew $0.5M to $12M by owning locations instead of licensing. [YT-K8MFC9t7snY@00:00:00] [YT-O1As2zxy0es@00:07:49] [POD-0558@00:11:36]
