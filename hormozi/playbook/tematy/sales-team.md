# sales-team

The canon covers the basics of running reps: copy the top rep at double volume, about 70% of leads per rep, commission on cash collected, Greatest Hits, best-call recordings and role-play in week one, per-rep NPS/CSAT/refunds, best leads to best closers, and specialisation at 250+ staff. The recordings add what the canon admits it lacks: hiring profiles and interview formats, the BDR / SDS / closer ladder with pay bands, how to judge and cut reps, conviction as a management variable, coaching mechanics (call review loops, interruption-style role-play), sales-manager selection, and when to hire more reps instead of improving the ones you have. The sections below are an index; the cited recordings hold the detail.

## Rules and thresholds

Hiring, judging and cutting
1. Cut the bottom 10% of reps on a regular rhythm (quarterly in one recording); he claims about a 30% productivity jump each time. [YT-bgmh7p1s7zs@00:07:50], [YT-2lA_A8BGRRs@00:05:27]
2. A rep below KPI for two weeks goes on a performance plan and is treated as a trainee again; two more weeks without improvement ends employment. [YT-2lA_A8BGRRs@00:08:04]
3. For simple offers, in his experience a new rep who cannot close in the first week or two likely will not, so he cuts them; a tryout is judged in about the first week, and he says you can tell within about 14 days (often seven) whether a trained rep will be exceptional. [YT-oi7bnS8uyJM@00:27:10], [YT-AGCtZmgJ1JA@00:05:29], [POD-0995@00:07:12]
4. Promotion to closer is try-or-die: ten deals, at least one closed in the first two weeks; anyone whose results drop below the qualifiers goes back down a level. [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:27:01]
5. Door-to-door style reps drop off at day 14, day 30, month 3 and month 6; past six months they tend to stay. Surviving the first three months is the threshold in high-volume transactional selling. [LS-tnDC4ZJ0oy8@02:16:46], [POD-0545@00:38:08]
6. If reps could take about 10 sales calls a day and take only 4, cut the lowest closers instead of adding leads; the remainder get a rhythm and close more. [YT-c_6BrF7jOGk@00:17:35]
7. As a rule of thumb he finds about 70% calendar utilisation the sweet spot per rep (he doesn't let it go above 85% and tries not to let it drop below 60%); weeks with many booked calls and weeks with few produced similar sales because reps work the pipeline harder when it is quiet. [YT-A_tx40lNpf8@00:25:00], [YT-A_tx40lNpf8@00:27:50]
8. Selection beats training at the margin: lifting a rep from 2 to 5 takes about three times as long as 7 to 9 (his estimate). He also says about 95% of sales organisations scale by hiring many and letting the weak die, and that training lets you pay less and ramp faster. [YT-WrCt0R3FBFs@00:39:51], [POD-0884@00:11:28]

Economics and capacity
9. Production benchmarks he gives: an outbound cold-calling rep about 50 deals a year at about $40,000, so about $2M each; a top rep returns about 5:1 on pay (about $1M gross profit for up to roughly $200K). [POD-0362@00:00:45], [YT-jqo0lVveh98@00:39:30]
10. In-house total cost of sales should be about 12% of revenue or less (management, software, pay); outsourced closers typically charge about 20%. [LS-QgmRlzh0Y7M@00:54:46]
11. Outbound ladder ratios: roughly 2 BDRs per SDS and 2 SDS per closer. BDR target 100 dials a day, about 20% pickup, 1 to 2 shown appointments. On-target earnings: BDR about $40K, SDS about $55K-$70K, closers about $120K-$225K. [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:08:24], [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:09:56], [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:25:26]
12. Pay design: self-generating sellers get more upside; for reps who are fed leads he prefers roughly a 50-50 base/variable split derived from market value (example: $200K role = roughly $80K-$100K base + $80K-$100K variable if hitting goals). A high share of revenue going to sales pay signals an underdeveloped sales motion. [LS-noE1MQPMvAc@00:57:34], [LS-noE1MQPMvAc@00:59:53]
13. Pay closers on cash collected so a payment plan pays less than paid-in-full (example: 10% commission). He also says pay rarely changes behaviour apart from the cash-collected rule; fast feedback does the rest. [YT-4GQLJjH9-oA@00:23:30], [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@02:15:11]
14. Whether to pay customer-success staff commission depends on the business: typically yes if the role includes selling, and he generally dislikes a percentage of the recurring book; where they are paid on retention, weight it by progress to target metrics. [YT-QTZsh3BgOwY@00:48:57], [YT-yPDQCfrwh8E@00:18:10]

More reps or better reps
15. Below about $1M revenue (one recording says $3M) the answer is almost always "more": a second rep probably beats polishing one rep's close rate; at larger size compare the effort for the same return, for example 20 reps with a 20% lift equals four hires. [POD-0852@01:13:38], [POD-0808@00:05:54], [POD-0775@00:04:07]
16. Risk-adjusted view: in his example a team closing 10% could probably triple; one closing 50% probably has a low chance of a lift. With one rep at 30%, three added reps beat getting that rep to 40%. [POD-0775@00:06:10], [YT-HxEQCHpZzHk@00:24:40]
17. When leads exist and calling capacity is the cap (a minuscule sales team), hire more sellers; if a 2-5x lift from faster lead contact exceeds one person's cost, hire that person. [YT-xRxVpjeHmeA@00:25:52], [YT-unshZobTt6Q@01:09:18]
18. Past the earliest stage, founder selling should hand off: you can sell yourself up to about $1M a year, sales-team management comes in at maybe $3-10M, and $10M-$30M means giving up leading everyone directly. Under $100K a year, do not outsource marketing and sales. [YT-awOQDy_o9ww@00:04:04], [YT-d0dBZbd13jM@00:21:39]

Managers
19. A player-coach works for small teams; past about six reports leading is a full-time job. A sales manager's job is full time (review recordings, list the errors, fix the one with the biggest effect on close rate). [POD-0853@00:33:34], [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@00:24:05]
20. Great closers are usually poor sales managers; the manager is an operator and culture owner, ideally attentive, caring and fond of teaching, with no ego about reps being better sellers. Promote well-liked middle-of-pack reps and let top closers close. [POD-0399@00:37:00], [YT-6m6DCQMASEM@00:11:18], [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@00:25:18], [POD-0853@00:31:45]
21. Hire a sales manager who has already built the same kind of team (similar sector and sales cycle); if you cannot, learn to sell yourself first, then train the next manager. Pick the best human, not the best salesperson. [POD-0399@00:35:42], [YT-WrCt0R3FBFs@00:39:51], [LS-MPHn-nW30QU@00:15:31]

Conviction, scripts and call metrics
22. Close rate is roughly skill times belief, and belief is a continuum that can run far higher than skill; score each rep's conviction (0-10) and ask for the bet-$1,000 or sell-it-to-your-mother test. [YT-42SOlVhDwWE@00:01:37], [POD-0575@00:19:46], [YT-q32-l3Yoqg4@00:34:02]
23. When a rep who sold well goes cold, suspect lost conviction before lost skill, and do not reach for training first. [YT-WrCt0R3FBFs@00:42:42], [YT-bHnVItVGJPA@00:01:35], [POD-0171@00:00:45]
24. In his experience, a question-based framework is easier to teach than a rigid script for ramping up reps, except for short, high-transaction sales (about $200 or less, about 20 minutes). A script is the standard nobody is above, otherwise the team cannot be coached; about 95% of teams lack a common script (his claim). [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:33:46], [YT-sGakuNs9mT4@00:05:28]
25. Drill script adherence first: if everyone follows it and it fails, the script is at fault; if not, you cannot diagnose anything. Reward adherence rather than the close, and change the script when it fails. [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@00:53:04], [YT-U_s0ekwPK5g@01:11:27], [YT-6m6DCQMASEM@00:14:09]
26. Best reps listen about twice as much as they talk; for closers who tend to talk a lot, the prospect should speak roughly two thirds of the call; speech should run about 135-185 words per minute. [YT-cy2k1GdA-9o@00:17:25], [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@02:21:53], [YT-sGakuNs9mT4@00:02:35]
27. Track close rate both per show and per offer made, per rep: a rep can inflate close rate by offering only sure buyers. Self-reported close rates are unreliable (gym owners said 51%, software showed 34%). [YT-c_6BrF7jOGk@00:01:31], [POD-0170@00:00:48]
28. Pair a speed or volume metric with a quality metric in every role. Judge anyone's skill by the number and quality of metrics they track, not just outcomes. [YT-3oD41B66NsM@00:00:00], [YT-cy2k1GdA-9o@00:45:08]
29. Keep inbound and outbound as separate teams with their own setters and closers; inbound is a promotion that top closers earn. Do not let one setter both cold-call for new appointments and nurture existing ones. [YT-oi7bnS8uyJM@00:29:35], [YT-0EqJD2o-Mnk@00:22:42], [YT-c_6BrF7jOGk@00:05:40]

## Tactics and scripts

Hiring and screening
1. Screen entry sales roles in group calls (about 10 candidates, 1-2 minutes each) with the script sent in advance; one-to-ones only at the offer stage. Hormozi thinks heavy resume screening is typically a mistake for transactional short-cycle roles. [POD-0940@00:12:13], [LS-noE1MQPMvAc@00:48:16], [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@02:11:49]
2. Test coachability in the interview with a role play, feedback and a retry; also check response speed, intelligence and listening. [YT-2lA_A8BGRRs@00:00:00]
3. For high-volume sales hires: send the script on application, ask for a 60-second video of it, then run a group drill with feedback. [POD-0933@00:27:32]
4. Senior sales hires must be recruited outbound, like customers, since the best already have jobs. About 100 LinkedIn reach-outs yield one hire, so doubling a five-rep team in a quarter needs about 500 reach-outs and about 50 interviews. [POD-0399@00:35:02], [YT-KhFlD54nQrY@01:07:07]
5. Hire BDRs as 30-day contractors who must hit KPIs, or pay full base with a 30-day proof window. Recruiting copy should name the hardship and the reward honestly. [POD-0870@00:11:38], [LS--HbwUdqUcEs@02:02:05]
6. Screening is often the bottleneck: for BDRs they let everyone in and let results decide; about 20 attempts per hire in a one-on-one funnel were replaced by group interviews of about 10. [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:41:46], [POD-0362@00:04:54]

Onboarding and training
7. 14-day onboarding: listen to about 40 good recorded calls, work the script, role-play every morning until a pass test, then a half schedule so they burn through leads while reviewing their own calls. [YT-2lA_A8BGRRs@00:01:32], [YT-2lA_A8BGRRs@00:03:05]
8. Weekly coaching loop of 60 minutes per rep: 30 reviewing calls at double speed, 5 naming one or two fixes, 25 role-playing them. [YT-2lA_A8BGRRs@00:03:54]
9. Daily 60-minute practice, five days a week: 25 minutes reading the script aloud, 5 on obstacles, the rest reviewing recordings. [YT-q32-l3Yoqg4@00:34:02], [YT-oi7bnS8uyJM@00:23:11]
10. Role-play with constant interruption and one correction at a time (expect 30-40 interruptions per script, 2-6 drills per fix); stop at about 30 seconds and have them redo it. [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@00:21:33], [POD-0837@01:06:45], [POD-0778@00:42:25]
11. Write call frameworks as questions; run the script through a readability checker (under third-grade level took one team from 14% to 35% close in four months). [YT-bgmh7p1s7zs@00:01:34], [POD-0770@00:00:00]

Management cadence and culture
12. Meeting rhythm: weekly and monthly team meetings, weekly one-on-ones, a morning training huddle (mandatory for trainees and anyone on a plan), and a short end-of-day huddle. [YT-2lA_A8BGRRs@00:09:24]
13. Daily public leaderboard with a sale notification; six-week contests in mini teams pairing strong with weak reps; prize budget about 25% of a monthly paycheque. [YT-bgmh7p1s7zs@00:08:38], [YT-oi7bnS8uyJM@00:28:02]
14. Career ladder of four levels per role (junior to senior), advancing about every 8 weeks on objective qualifiers (first: 50 shown appointments), with small per-deal raises; for repeated roles a zigzag path of titles, perks and pay. [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:23:04], [YT-oi7bnS8uyJM@00:29:00], [LS-tnDC4ZJ0oy8@02:14:23]
15. Bring customer success wins to sales: weekly meeting, testimonials read each morning, a client joining a call; stack appointments on days graduates share results (close rate near 100% on those days). [YT-bHnVItVGJPA@00:03:09], [YT-bHnVItVGJPA@00:05:12], [YT-AGCtZmgJ1JA@00:06:24]
16. Lead handoff: setter builds up the closer, three-way intro by phone, closer sends a voice or video message the morning of the appointment. [POD-0017@00:00:50], [YT-c_6BrF7jOGk@00:18:22], [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@00:39:23]
17. Make the "no" the goal for fearful reps (target 100 nos), and reward getting prospects to a decision either way. [POD-0479@00:33:36], [POD-0564@00:14:35]
18. Referral ask: asking every buyer who else they would bring lifted new reps well above the team (about 1 in 3 buyers gives names). [YT-qpQvdBFW_yI@00:00:46], [YT-cy2k1GdA-9o@00:10:21]

## Cases by kind of business

Physical products and e-commerce
- $48M Murphy-door maker with a builder channel: sales team was a one-time monetisation lever, so scale ad spend on the existing channel. [LS-6_CCutkM11g@05:42:44]

Made-to-order, manufacturing and high-value dealing
- Luxury-watch dealer capped by his own deal flow: duplicate outbound sourcing with offshore assistants and document pricing judgment. [POD-0858@00:05:35]
- Robotic cotton-candy machine maker at about one deal per 100 cold emails: asked what stops 10,000 a day with setters feeding one closer. [POD-0908@00:44:30]

Wholesale, resellers and brokers
- Real-estate wholesaler whose sales swing after record months (17 reps, $42K base + 10% of fees): enforce word-for-word scripts and daily training, replace the leader if adherence fails. [POD-0933@00:20:31]
- UAE brokerage where the founder bills two thirds: fill desks now and move from top closer to trainer and recruiter. [POD-0931@00:00:00]
- Mortgage-leads company: a struggling rep's hesitant answer on lead quality showed lost belief; he tore up the script. [POD-0564@00:10:54], [YT-bHnVItVGJPA@00:00:00], [YT-q32-l3Yoqg4@00:30:10]
- 304M cybersecurity firm with lumpy 536K deals: do not move reps to small deals; buy a minnow-selling company. [POD-0858@00:22:48]

Local and home services
- Residential trash hauler: go door-to-door for homes, structure the offer so first-transaction cash covers bin, first quarter and commission; rep paid $50 per sale at about 5 a day. [YT-Wr6n_zNKvMk@00:00:00]
- Pressure-washing/gutter firm: keep stacking subscriptions, hire commission door knockers, no online ads yet. [POD-0809@00:05:45]
- Door-to-door window company losing reps in week one: rep selection, realistic door drills, early rewards, truthful recruiting copy, recruit-a-team path. [LS--HbwUdqUcEs@01:50:48]
- $3M roofer put knockers on purchased leads, got lazy and costly (CAC about $7,500 vs about $6,500 per customer). [YT-0EqJD2o-Mnk@00:18:17]
- Solar rep sold four times the next best by calling new bookings immediately; solar firm with $500 per two weeks base for remote BDRs told to pay a real base; solar agency in a commodity market. [YT-cy2k1GdA-9o@00:03:34], [POD-0870@00:10:53], [POD-0861@00:39:37]
- Martial-arts owner (5 schools) needing instructors who sell: selling is teachable in a day or two; find martial artists through daily recruiting. [POD-0892@00:22:29]
- Chiropractor chain: patient coordinator closes the plan as a commissioned manager; trial at one clinic. Chiropractors who dislike selling: select for the trait, role-play three times weekly. [POD-0818@00:31:38], [POD-0853@00:24:04]

## Changes of mind and contradictions

- Earlier he believed top sellers know secret words; now he says they do the same things as average ones but reliably. [POD-0073@00:00:00]
- Speed of scaling: 0 to 40 reps in about a quarter (+$21M profit), 0 to 80 in 90 days with 15 contingency recruiters (kept 50), versus an outbound team that needed 12 months and a company squeezed after 4 to 40 reps. [YT-cy2k1GdA-9o@00:50:52], [LS-_ooj0V4hpgw@00:51:21], [POD-0758@00:06:40], [POD-0884@00:08:34]
- Hire more or cut: "hire more salespeople than you think you need" versus cutting the bottom 10% each quarter and halving a bloated team. [POD-0228@00:04:47], [YT-2lA_A8BGRRs@00:05:27], [YT-2lA_A8BGRRs@00:06:13]
- Training versus selection: skills teachable in a couple of weeks and scale-by-training, versus selection mattering more and cut-regularly norms. [POD-0893@00:33:34], [YT-WrCt0R3FBFs@00:39:51], [POD-0884@00:11:28]
- Pay: commission as the lever versus "pay rarely changes behaviour" and "natural winners treat commission as the ticket". [POD-0085@00:09:03], [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@02:15:11], [POD-0488@00:31:09]
- Pay metric: closers on cash collected versus reward adherence, activity or reaching a decision rather than the close. [YT-4GQLJjH9-oA@00:23:30], [YT-U_s0ekwPK5g@01:11:27], [POD-0564@00:14:35]
- Contests: a contest lifts sales sharply but the lift fades within 3-4 days, yet six-week contests are listed as core culture. [POD-0084@00:00:58], [YT-2lA_A8BGRRs@00:05:27]
- Star who damages culture: fire quickly, versus first isolate the actions that make a toxic top seller succeed, run a 30-60 day push, and learn what a star does before firing. [LS-noE1MQPMvAc@00:19:26], [LS-TLgSkSQUql4@00:27:45], [LS-R7McLmx73Xw@00:46:47]
- Who can sell: high-ticket ($3,000) sales cannot be outsourced well, versus making the process simple enough for average or front-desk staff, with clicks and checkboxes. [YT-_tF3ODk56hg@00:11:55], [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@01:10:56], [POD-0875@00:42:08], [YT-1UhvBSQFy6A@00:21:38]
- Discovery style: stay high level in discovery, versus a founder's authority-based consultative style that cannot be taught. [YT-4GQLJjH9-oA@00:17:50], [LS-I82d3jxg_Aw@00:30:51]
