# strategy-positioning

The canon already covers the Starving Crowd, the Three Levers, the Four Market Variables, Riches in the Niches, the 80/20 Customer Analysis, the BCG matrix and "fix before chasing". The recordings add: a kit for resizing a market; concentration risk in customers, channels, suppliers and platforms; how a small firm competes with bigger players, plus a moat list; revenue-stage focus rules with many "should I add this business" verdicts; and partner, affiliate, reseller, equity and franchise rules. Mostly live Q&A, so cases outnumber rules.

## Rules and thresholds

Concentration of customers, channels and suppliers
1. Buyers discount heavily or walk when a few big customers are most of revenue; bankers and PE named churn, the owner's name as the brand, and dependence on one ad channel. [YT-pWbSl7d0tEc@00:04:35] [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:03:20]
2. Four fixes for key-customer risk: add many small customers, lock the big one into a long contract, win more big ones, or let it go. Before taking a very large client, decide whether to take it on at all and whether to go after more large or small ones. [YT-sjt5G3YPjmY@00:30:11] [YT-HKbFUWJwEG0@00:39:43]
3. One saturated channel is not a capped market: add a channel or raise lifetime gross profit per customer first. A plateau is the market's fault only if you hold most of it or it is shrinking. [YT-A4L3byKcYQg@00:03:10] [YT-s59jbVBprn0@00:02:18]
4. Told to one caller: the only proper reason for a new avatar is running out of the current one; he preferred diversity through channels. [YT-QwEb78e5a8Y@00:10:24]
5. Own the channel rather than hold a contract (contracts get cancelled); own only the one link whose loss would stop the business and partner for the rest; if your monetisation per lead beats your supplier's, buy the supplier. [YT-Lc8DNduiwKA@00:39:07] [YT-orMbq2LtzKE@00:58:27] [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@00:19:25]
6. A business built on a platform trick vanishes: ask whether what you sell exists in 20 years. [POD-0430@00:26:07]
7. Customer value is a power law: top 20% give 80% of profit, top 4% give 64%, top 1% give 51%. [YT-ZuJryiwxjDw@00:04:29]
8. Volatile customers (very small businesses) create structural churn no product fix removes; serve them only at near-zero cost to serve, or sell to small business at the cheap automated or the expensive proven end. A very large client is costly to replace; a small owner on a 12-month contract stays about three months. [POD-0850@01:06:40] [YT-thDTmy7VGIw@00:04:05] [YT-jqo0lVveh98@00:11:14] [YT-U_s0ekwPK5g@00:49:39]

Competing with bigger or established players
9. Rivals do not steal customers, you lose them; a new advertiser takes a crumb because you use one method on one slice of one platform. Answer by over-delivering to current customers and adding channels. [POD-0190@00:01:25] [LS-noE1MQPMvAc@00:11:14] [POD-0238@00:05:01]
10. Copycats: for most owners suing is usually not worth it, so sue only through a law firm; get louder on your own message and never name them; the leader's best defence is to copy first (new plays every 14 days versus quarterly). [POD-0124@00:06:20] [YT-cGup9yYrOoc@00:04:08] [POD-200710-01@00:19:49]
11. Do not copy small rivals' prices or offers (most barely survive), but when a much larger competitor has a working offer, replicate it before iterating. [POD-0464@00:12:28] [LS-I82d3jxg_Aw@00:42:45]
12. Two ways to dominate: acquire customers cheaper than anyone, or earn more per customer and outbid everyone; high LTV:CAC is an "ethical moat". [LS-6_CCutkM11g@03:18:36] [YT-jzKpAtzKQ54@00:08:01]
13. Large incumbents are beatable at the edges unless they have network effects. Against a VC-funded leader pick an underserved narrow avatar and out-select it; a small specialist beats an incumbent once delivery costs nothing. [LS-6_CCutkM11g@02:44:36] [YT-U_s0ekwPK5g@00:14:27] [YT-IFpHasZ7jN4@00:38:51]
14. Cheapest is a strategy only if built in from day one; cut price only if you can cut your cost to about a tenth of rivals'; be the volume player with technology built in, or premium, with little room between. [YT-4GjwtnA76ig@00:29:41] [POD-230819-03@00:41:12] [POD-0809@00:04:12]
15. With a product equal to everyone's, compete on speed, risk reversal, service and brand; sell in a vacuum. [LS-QgmRlzh0Y7M@00:23:28] [YT-FMzKk73iUhw@00:11:32]
16. Thirteen moats; pick one and go all the way (scarce resource, scale cost, bundling a rival's feature free, capital intensity, IP, owned distribution, network effects, vertical integration, exclusive contracts). A patent needs a legal budget; what is hard in your industry also protects you. [YT-Lc8DNduiwKA@00:00:00] [YT-Lc8DNduiwKA@00:10:21] [LS-TLpLmhWqKEU@01:16:44] [YT-oRMG_HpOAN4@01:17:00]
17. Number one takes about 80-90% of the economics (his estimate); entering a market, he (citing Elon) says to be an order of magnitude better. Collaborate with local rivals. [YT-U_s0ekwPK5g@00:11:32] [YT-KhFlD54nQrY@00:39:50] [YT-VxKwz6hBVZU@00:15:41]

Niching and customer selection
18. Narrowing the avatar doubled, tripled or sometimes 5x'd revenue while cutting the audience about 85%; revenue dips first, then retention, pricing power and lower CAC (portfolio profit 3x in 12 months). [YT-9gVdCR7W8o8@00:06:39] [POD-0477@00:25:44]
19. Niching works because it is easier to be great at one thing: standardise and productise down to ad, page and price. [POD-0415@00:05:15] [YT-TooAB8Ow6cQ@03:10:15]
20. For most businesses below about $10M a year narrowing earns more; above it the market's size matters. [POD-230819-03@00:28:46] [POD-0585@00:04:19]
21. Choose avatars by LTV:CAC times market size; never average prices across avatars (an A client can spend 10x a C client). Changing avatar is among the biggest changes: not while the market is unsaturated. [YT-TooAB8Ow6cQ@03:14:33] [YT-3yAiVjcImQ4@00:03:03] [LS-lidFtb1Ga0o@00:24:51]
22. Segment by psychographics too: Gym Launch refused yoga, cycling and personal trainers though the model fit. Wrong-customer signs: 3-4 month life, heavy support, price resistance. [POD-0477@00:21:03] [YT-CoPs-Bk8M9Y@00:05:23]
23. Serve customers who capture most value: spend $1,000 to deliver and charge $7,000-10,000, not $500 for $1,000; 100 buyers at $10,000 beat 10,000 at $100. [YT-CoPs-Bk8M9Y@00:15:00] [POD-0785@00:12:51]
24. Top 10% of Americans hold about 69-70% of wealth; rich buyers pay better. By his definition market income matters at very high tickets, about $1,000 a month minimum or more. [POD-0938@00:02:07] [YT-m-k0_pQJ1fY@00:08:24] [POD-0071@00:01:35]

Resizing the market
25. Five directions: up, down, adjacent, broader, narrower. Each step down multiplies prospects about 10x (sometimes more), each step up divides by about 10; up-market price is typically 5-10x yours, down-market one fifth to one tenth. [POD-0531@00:05:33] [POD-0531@00:08:37] [YT-A4L3byKcYQg@00:04:09]
26. Start the next vertical after speaking to about 40% of the industry; before that you have share, not saturation. Hormozi says Gym Launch picked the avatar it gave the most dollars of value; an adjacent market shares business traits, owner type and customer type. [POD-0531@00:11:39] [POD-0531@00:14:25] [POD-0531@00:09:27]
27. Up-market means organisations above you (associations, franchisors); adjacent means a neighbouring segment such as hair salons to nail or lash. [YT-A4L3byKcYQg@00:00:45] [YT-A4L3byKcYQg@00:02:08]
28. Advice to one caller, not a general rule: move to a new segment at about 100x order value for similar effort, keeping the old business as fallback; stay small-ticket only with annual reorders and 80-90% retention. [LS-eW04CvHKvMo@02:12:47] [LS-eW04CvHKvMo@02:09:42]

Focus versus expansion
29. To about $1M a year: one product, avatar, channel. At $1-3M add a second product line, one customer type, 1-3 standard deliverables; $1-10M raise lifetime gross profit; $10-50M professionalise. [YT-jfW6gL6hKhk@00:07:34] [YT-EZLQzR-_83A@00:01:42] [YT-qsXxckCbci0@00:29:26] [YT-qxQIcDrre1E@00:46:33]
30. Variants: one of each to about $3M; several businesses work usually above $100M revenue; one genuinely new initiative a year, fewer as you grow. [YT-neTSqOAMgao@00:45:06] [POD-0063@00:02:47] [YT-YONIUpk-kAs@00:48:34]
31. Advice to one caller: no second business or niche until you hit the first one's market cap. You can own many companies but be CEO of one; a venture grows about 5x faster all-in; compare the new year zero with the old year four. [YT-neTSqOAMgao@00:27:30] [LS-0RjIw9M1j28@00:15:57] [POD-0840@00:13:19]
32. A new product line uses existing resources, a new business needs new people and capital; cross-selling a separate business is usually a mistake: a $3M company turned into two $1.5M ones with less profit. [POD-0284@00:00:00] [POD-0418@00:22:32]
33. Advice to one caller: don't add an offer while the current one can still double; side streams are fine only if they add no new workflow. [POD-0820@00:46:08] [LS-ehmBlLNGgsA@00:32:28]
34. Spend about 90% of time on more and better of what works; implement a change only if you expect 20%+ lift. [YT-reisEL_D7xc@00:13:49] [POD-0862@00:01:53]
35. Pivot only when a core assumption is proven false; otherwise push (about nine times in ten). If a bigger version of your business exists, you lack skill, not a model. [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@06:05:56] [YT-F84olnKkseM@00:31:23] [LS-WeH2E1fGgaw@00:22:22]
36. In his view, consider a private chain when a site costs under about six months of first-year profit (a loose rule of thumb); nail one site first; open extras only to prove a concept for licensing. [YT-O1As2zxy0es@00:05:31] [YT-uaLNfijnp-8@00:23:30] [POD-0184@00:01:32]
37. Benchmarks: about 90% of businesses never pass $1M and 0.4% reach $10M; value can jump past about $5M profit. [YT-Nh8Oc7ERdIU@00:00:00] [POD-0869@00:08:17]

38. Take a partner only for skill, money or time you lack; two with the same strengths fail. [YT-yJfn_pWzFqg@00:00:48] [YT-JShQ8BX08rs@00:00:46]
39. People want equity for control or an exit payout; otherwise give revenue or profit share (e-commerce marketing providers commonly take about 10% of attributed sales). For an adjacent service consider being an affiliate instead of building (you can switch providers); buying the partner's business makes sense as the exception, when the enterprise value you send surpasses your own vehicle's; software affiliates get 20-30%, sometimes 40-50%. ## Tactics and scripts

1. Customer analysis: survey current and past customers, rank by spend, keep the top 20%, find the 3-5 shared traits, rewrite messaging for them and refuse the rest. [POD-0477@00:04:57] [POD-0477@00:06:39] [POD-0477@00:08:03] [POD-251114-02@00:03:07]
2. Compare the last 90 days' worst and best customers; find the cohort that never churns and aim marketing at it, even at higher CAC ($100 at 1 in 10 beats $200 at 1 in 3). [YT-hnfh2jqNVEQ@00:03:17] [YT-bgBIO6nZawg@00:27:00] [YT-k-3PoOT4vOM@00:37:05]
3. Leave the low end gradually: cap it at a share of sales (example 30%) or give each seller a small easy-sale quota and shrink it. [YT-CoPs-Bk8M9Y@00:22:04]
4. Name who the offer is not for in the first copy lines; state it as "I help [narrow who] get [outcome] without [pain] through [mechanism]". [YT-kGfzLjPNsBU@00:08:05] [YT-nIk3DedjxJM@00:18:03]
5. Test a market before committing: $250-$1,000 of ads per market; open or partner where leads are cheapest. [POD-0481@00:16:59] [YT-unshZobTt6Q@00:08:17]
6. Underdog lines: concede the leader's size, then contrast the motivated owner with their anonymous employee; offer your own phone number; same price with the owner's personal attention. [LS-I0nCuqu7Cac@03:03:13] [LS-eW04CvHKvMo@00:51:49] [LS-tnDC4ZJ0oy8@02:05:02]
7. Screen a small acquisition for key-man risk, customer concentration and a clear reason to buy; a retiring owner plus 70% of revenue from one client is a pass. [YT-BMF2fWHyDrg@00:14:09]
8. Park shiny ideas on a list, revisit after current problems are solved; decline with "amazing opportunity, just not my amazing opportunity". [YT-zNiXk_3C_Io@00:15:20] [POD-0480@00:12:39]
9. Open a partnership with "the deal must make sense even if we hated each other"; stalled partners can choose a larger share for the worker, a shotgun offer, or an equity swap. [POD-0753@00:06:27] [YT--Koq14DPXC4@00:19:46]

## Changes of mind and contradictions

1. Order of levers: market first (a great rating overpowers lower items, unless a higher-order grade nullifies a bad one) against his recommendation for most people to "fix the offer first, then persuasion, then look for a market". [POD-230819-03@00:26:18] [YT-4XWlHPyAico@00:06:26]
2. Niche versus total market: niche is right at the start, but he found himself in a pond among peers with oceans; against TAM as a weak yardstick and best-in-niche probably beating widening. [POD-0585@00:04:19] [YT-aOq1Hdz0w3k@00:09:35] [YT-reisEL_D7xc@00:53:33]
3. Changing segment: advice to one caller, not a general rule: a new segment at about 100x order value, or a better avatar accepting a dip, versus "not while unsaturated". [LS-eW04CvHKvMo@02:12:47] [POD-0894b@00:07:21] [LS-lidFtb1Ga0o@00:24:51]
4. Integration: vertical integration creates large enterprise value, buy the supplier; yet roofers were told to pick one and not to fund one business with another. [YT-A4L3byKcYQg@00:08:01] [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@00:19:25] [YT-RdAKXJlMIZM@00:16:27] [POD-0820@00:20:29]
5. Partners: every solo business made money and every partnership lost; yet 3-4 founders is a sweet spot and several vested partners grow faster. [POD-0491@00:23:20] [POD-0491@00:43:55] [POD-0590@00:50:22]
6. Copying: do not copy competitors, and reacting to one cost about $25M, versus replicate a much larger rival's offer first. [POD-0464@00:12:28] [YT-ln24y0FPJHo@00:12:33] [LS-I82d3jxg_Aw@00:42:45]
7. Premium or entry: premium first, Tesla-style, and top-down launches, against rivals that moved upmarket too early and a straight premium offer being the least efficient way to capture a market. [YT-KhFlD54nQrY@00:00:00] [POD-0938@00:08:38] [YT-YZdE8U5eD_s@00:29:41] [POD-251114-04@00:07:57]
8. Scope: Ramsey's firm reached about 14 profit centres under one mission, against one new initiative a year. [YT--HJg4TYBgtI@00:00:46] [YT-YONIUpk-kAs@00:48:34]
9. Franchising as impatient and costly versus the tool when capital is heavy. [POD-0804@00:11:56] [YT-O1As2zxy0es@00:08:43]
