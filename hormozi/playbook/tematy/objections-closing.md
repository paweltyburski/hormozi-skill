# objections-closing

The canon covers the Three Core Beliefs, the five concern types (time, stall, money, fit, decision maker), CLOSER, the downsell flow with the 1-to-10 desire check, "never negotiate price", "fair enough?", the "you don't want anything else, do you?" upsell and ID-then-card. The recordings add a theory of obstacles versus objections, a bench of named overcomes per concern (burn-you-twice, reason close, hypothetical, best case/worst case), the Triple A for detail questions, no-based phrasing, a wallet-stall playbook, guarantee design, honesty and compliance limits, and a negotiation toolkit.

## 1. Rules and thresholds

1. About 30% of prospects arrive already decided and about 50% must be won (his estimate); another framing: of 100, 10 never buy, 10 always buy, and skill matters for the middle 80. [POD-0045@00:04:40], [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@00:16:31]
2. A normal sale might close about 1 in 4 to 1 in 2 (1 in 2 if really good); assumed-close upsells can sell about 9 to 9.5 out of 10, against 20-30% for ordinary ones. [YT-xy1h4qqF778@00:03:55], [YT-j1tA4l7R2c0@00:01:37]
3. Close-rate examples by context: 80%+ in-person low ticket, about 5% investment sold on one or two calls, and in his opinion about 40% for this company's mid-price consumer service on a two-call close (that business was at 27%). [YT-c_6BrF7jOGk@00:07:16]
4. Waiting up to eight seconds after the ask was linked to about 30% more sales (meta-analyses he recalls); roughly 20-30% of sales come after the first no. [YT-sGakuNs9mT4@00:12:06], [YT-sGakuNs9mT4@00:12:53]
5. The ID-for-card swap settles about 80% of wallet stalls (his estimate). [YT-Ul87yrDKZ78@00:04:08]
6. In his opinion, after about six step-downs and still a refusal, it is not about money: you lost them earlier. [YT-gza5RtQCVsA@00:08:38]
7. Over 10% cancellations on pay-later customers means over-promising, weak conditions or too high a price. [POD-250819-04@00:23:53]
8. A sale rests on two questions: is it worth the price if it works, and do I believe it will. A deal also needs budget, authority, need and timing from the buyer plus three trust scores (product, salesman, company). [POD-0020@00:03:46], [POD-0112@00:00:49], [POD-0227@00:01:41]
9. A price objection means value is unclear: typically work on conviction, sometimes on perceived time and effort, rather than outcome size. "I can't afford it" means pain was not established before the price. [POD-0090@00:01:24], [POD-0033@00:00:00]
10. Obstacles arise before the price (you disagree with their framing); the same thing after the price is an objection. Clear them early, because otherwise they blow up at the close. [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@00:21:44], [POD-0524@00:38:21], [YT-c_6BrF7jOGk@00:08:58]
11. Defuse the recurring objections before asking for money, since afterwards your questions look like extraction. An objection is not always a no; "that's expensive" is often an observation. [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@01:37:29], [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@02:28:57]
12. Find which kind of no it is: value, details, authority, budget or timing. Feature and detail objections are preference objections: the buyer wants the outcome their own way. [POD-0869@00:25:02], [POD-0791@00:08:26]
13. "I'm busy" is a priorities objection; "give me some time" is decision avoidance. They need different answers. [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@02:02:29]
14. Weak motion: right people but cold means educate more; wrong people but hot means qualify harder. "Let me think" from an unqualified prospect is wasted time; from a BANT-qualified one it is an education problem. [LS-noE1MQPMvAc@00:46:16], [LS-QgmRlzh0Y7M@00:16:00]
15. When a once-strong rep declines, suspect lost conviction before training. [YT-bHnVItVGJPA@00:01:35]
16. The best closers ask most often, so each ask must preserve rapport; yet do not ask until the buyer states why they are here and owns the problem. [YT-RVbvhPGFi6E@00:02:41], [YT-q32-l3Yoqg4@00:15:24]
17. Objection step: isolate, resolve, ask again; sales scale with the number of asks. [POD-0388@00:05:01], [YT-c_6BrF7jOGk@00:20:44]
18. Never answer a question unless you know the answer is acceptable; reply with a question about the question. [POD-0791@00:06:06], [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@01:22:38]
19. If the obstacle surfaces at the end anyway, never cite what they said earlier; stay neutral and curious. [POD-0202@00:04:40]
20. The goal is a decision, not a purchase; a clear no beats a miserable maybe. When the buyer says yes, stop selling and take payment details. [POD-0524@00:43:45], [POD-0575@00:05:33], [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@01:14:04]
21. He suggests no-based wording on a couple of milestone questions or near the edge, not on mining questions. [YT-ETEFhDICm5o@00:05:38]
22. Never change the price to close; test price in advance and flex terms or components. A real promotional deadline is fine. [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@02:29:59], [POD-250819-06@00:02:20], [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@02:38:31]
23. If the quote draws no gasp it is too low; a high first number lets you walk down. Raise price until conversion times price falls. [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@00:08:29], [YT-uaLNfijnp-8@00:28:43]
24. Answer price pushback with margin: at 15% margin on $100, a 15% discount is working for free. [POD-0218@00:03:30]
25. One early discount tells the buyer everything is negotiable; if take rates are poor, change the price for everyone. [POD-0085@00:06:25], [YT-HsQeQM1jUeg@00:24:46]
26. Wealthy buyers judge return to cost; less wealthy ones react to the absolute price. People accept monthly prices more than lump sums. [POD-0938@00:32:20], [YT-Gh9zWsP8JpI@00:01:58]
27. Guarantee strength is proportional to the risk reversed; aim for net conversions (if 10% back sells like 100%, give 10%); test on calls first. [POD-0451@00:20:15], [YT---9kOXNCUdE@00:31:39], [POD-0418@00:15:04]
28. His rule: refund anyone who asks and fix real mistakes. Separate cancellation from refund requests. [POD-250819-03@00:07:25], [YT-TooAB8Ow6cQ@03:27:28]
29. Promising less and disclosing more will probably sell to about the same number but a different mix of buyers. Avoid false urgency and scarcity. [POD-0825@00:09:43], [POD-0825@00:25:02]
30. Name the first number yourself, high, because it anchors. Build a BATNA first. He trades small concessions that stay advantageous to him. [YT-Jc2UW3nlNBA@00:06:22], [YT-Jc2UW3nlNBA@00:00:47], [YT-Jc2UW3nlNBA@00:14:40]
31. Ignore a buyer's promise of a fast close (30 days promised, 112 taken); he says buyers take as long as they want. No contract, no deal. [YT-pWbSl7d0tEc@00:28:25], [POD-0590@00:05:08], [POD-0486@00:17:15]

## 2. Tactics and scripts

Stall ("I need to think about it")

1. Reply "awesome, what's your main concern?" and repeat until concerns are exhausted. [LS-QgmRlzh0Y7M@00:18:11], [POD-0040@00:00:00]
2. Longer set: considerations, decision variables, biggest fear, what makes it a no, what makes it a yes. [YT-RVbvhPGFi6E@00:10:39], [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@01:17:05]
3. Time brings no new information; deciding takes information, and you are the only source. [POD-0040@00:01:06], [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@01:05:33]
4. Teach the decision as three yes/no checks (solves it my way, want to work with you, access to money), then ask for the card. [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@01:07:04], [POD-0524@00:50:50]
5. Unicorn close: turn the stall into a hypothetical; after a 1-to-10 ask what makes it a 10 and why not a 2. [POD-0388@00:03:06], [YT-WrCt0R3FBFs@00:58:06]
6. Stalling is also a decision: ask which future they are killing today. [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@01:09:37]
7. Past-present-future frame for the last "I need to think". [YT-WrCt0R3FBFs@00:52:45]

Money

8. Four steps: is it a lot of money for you (that is why you'll commit); if it delivers, the issue is belief; you pay either way, money or time; resourcefulness, not resources. [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@00:37:22], [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@00:38:26], [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@00:40:12], [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@00:42:29]
9. Agree it is expensive, restate the value, then offer the cheap cycle or fixing it for good; normalise the fear. [POD-0047@00:02:26], [POD-0047@00:08:20]
10. Divide the price of the most expensive thing they own by uses or hours. [POD-0069@00:02:32]
11. Check "can't afford it": staff spending, old-identity habits (drinking, smoking), a hypothetical $50,000 surgery. [POD-0053@00:05:43], [POD-0437@00:09:55], [YT-WrCt0R3FBFs@00:47:52]
12. Budget objection: rank items by necessity, drop the least important and offer a free substitute. [YT-bx48qPlaGvE@00:05:10], [POD-0437@00:09:12]
13. Call the price "extremely expensive" before stating it, not "only"; write it down. [POD-0524@00:34:21], [YT-ZuJryiwxjDw@00:30:37]
14. Show full price first, then the prepay discount subtracting. [YT-1UhvBSQFy6A@00:25:27]
15. Lower budget: change price and terms together, never price alone. [POD-0869@00:26:38]
16. "Do it for less?" Reply you could do it for more; this counter-anchors and often closes. [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@02:30:30], [YT-m5ordaa7NN4@00:03:36]
17. Answer a discount request with a bonus, then terms that buy you something (introductions, testimonial). [YT-unshZobTt6Q@00:25:36], [YT-qpQvdBFW_yI@00:08:00]
18. Step-down by exchange: $300 for three weeks to $49 a week with 12-week commitment, then $39 for a testimonial, then $29 with a friend within 30 days. [YT-gza5RtQCVsA@00:03:11], [POD-0196@00:04:50]
19. Service ladder: card in full, down payment plus weekly, short fixed term, trial with commitments. [POD-0182@00:05:14]
20. Two options at the close (about $199 up front or membership), so the question is which. [POD-0196@00:03:04]
21. Add asks rather than removing bonuses, so different prices are explained by different terms. [YT-gza5RtQCVsA@00:03:57]

Cheaper competitor

22. Comparison close: which would you pick at equal prices, why, then those reasons are why you cost more. [YT-9lLLBlo0YXA@00:01:22], [LS-QgmRlzh0Y7M@00:27:06]
23. Prevent "you're more expensive": frame local cost to global cost before price, ideally in a pre-call video. [LS-TLgSkSQUql4@00:25:59]

Time, fit, belief

24. Time: macro (24 hours, priorities), when-then (busy now, busy later, so start now), micro (the current routine is not working). [POD-0524@00:43:45], [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@00:35:44]
25. Fit: new identity brings new priorities; the plan must change for the result to change. [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@00:46:14], [YT-WrCt0R3FBFs@00:49:31]
26. "Won't work for me": how much proof would it take, and when does your requirement become more unreasonable than the proof? [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@00:24:58], [POD-0110@00:04:07]
27. "Too good to be true": have them design the ideal programme, ask "if true, what else would be true", walk through the cost of faking the proof. [POD-0110@00:00:46], [POD-0110@00:05:41], [POD-0110@00:07:51]
28. Burned before: a bad past purchase should not stop a good one. [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@01:03:05], [POD-0788@00:06:07]
29. Reason close: the reason they give is the reason they need it. Hypothetical close: if that did not exist, would you? Then match conditions to the yes. [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@01:13:38], [YT-WrCt0R3FBFs@00:58:51]
30. Best case versus worst case; the guarantee makes both risk-free. [POD-0052@00:00:00], [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@01:47:26]
31. "We already work with someone": why talk to me if they deliver? [LS-I82d3jxg_Aw@00:56:34]
32. "Just want information": ask what problem they want solved. [POD-0479@00:40:53]

Decision maker

33. Ask early who else must be involved; reschedule so all attend. [YT-c_6BrF7jOGk@00:19:58], [POD-0202@00:02:37]
34. Four moves: what if they say no, their likely objection, past agreements, then forgiveness not permission; reframe to support, project who they will blame. [YT-oi7bnS8uyJM@00:11:46], [POD-0479@00:42:21]
35. About 1 in 3 go ahead when asked what happens if the other says no. [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@01:54:46]
36. Short no-questions guarantee (3 days) or delay payment to a set day after the yes. [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@00:57:09], [POD-0785@00:34:25]

Triple A and questions

37. Acknowledge, associate (generic positive, foil, authority), ask. A foil lets you say the hard truth to a made-up person. [POD-0791@00:02:04], [POD-0791@00:03:34]
38. Detail questions: ask the intention behind them, test your answer, then show your features reach it better. [POD-0791@00:09:11], [POD-0791@00:10:09]
39. When pressed to answer, use the secretary-versus-surgeon or mechanic analogy. [YT-RVbvhPGFi6E@00:07:55], [POD-0030@00:00:45]
40. Meet objections with childlike curiosity; humour de-escalates. [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@00:10:14], [POD-0388@00:00:45]
41. Dig by alternating "can you give me an example" and "tell me more". [YT-sGakuNs9mT4@00:16:58], [LS-noE1MQPMvAc@00:49:33]
42. The aha: why they failed before, either the wrong plan or no accountability. In B2B he would never question work ethic. [POD-0150@00:00:53], [POD-0150@00:04:45]
43. Get a positive label early and call it back at the close. [POD-0507@00:08:41]

Card and wallet

44. Ask for ID before the card; if they question the card, "that's how it has always been done." [POD-0179@00:01:07], [POD-0170@00:01:51]
45. A forgotten wallet probably points to an earlier mistake in the sale. [YT-Ul87yrDKZ78@00:02:27], [POD-0204@00:00:00]
46. Fallbacks: take any card and swap later, wait on phone sales, start with $49 of $300, delay the charge about two days, ACH from the banking app, set the charge for payday. [POD-0204@00:01:33], [POD-0204@00:02:23], [YT-Ul87yrDKZ78@00:02:44], [YT-Ul87yrDKZ78@00:05:04]
47. Take the card before the price, framed as completing the profile; penalty fees explained only after you hold the card. [YT-1UhvBSQFy6A@00:08:25], [POD-250819-06@00:20:22]

Bonuses and framing

48. Hold bonuses in reserve: ask on the base offer, then reveal the one that answers the stated obstacle. [POD-0451@00:02:30], [POD-0742@00:23:08]
49. Concern loop: normalise the fear, show how it is prevented, "fair enough?", close; repeat. [POD-0448@00:01:00]
50. Sell against the don't, and use a four-angle grid (more good with you, less bad with you, more bad without, less good without). [POD-0852@00:49:16], [YT-3yAiVjcImQ4@00:13:47]
51. Damaging admission first, then "but" and the strength. [YT-JfbtySq6_B4@00:00:45]
52. Ghost product: tell the buyer to skip one item; he reports near-100% closes afterwards. [YT-bx48qPlaGvE@00:00:46], [POD-0437@00:01:46]

## 4. Changes of mind and contradictions

1. "Fair enough" in the canon versus plans to shift some scripts toward some no-based phrasing, yet "fair enough?" is still taught after each step. [YT-ETEFhDICm5o@00:06:24], [YT-gza5RtQCVsA@00:06:17]
