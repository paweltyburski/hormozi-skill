# sales-process

The canon covers CLOSER, the Three Pillar Pitch, AAA, Proof Over Promise, What Would It Take, BANT, the Basic Sales KPIs, show-rate levers, Lead Scoring, Sales Aids (the pre-call VSL), the Three Core Beliefs, the five concern types and the 1-to-10 desire check. The recordings add: benchmarks for every funnel step, close rate as a pricing signal, a "friction" doctrine (qualification and education are the two levers), speed-to-lead and booking-window rules, obstacle versus objection ("kill zombies"), no-based closing, conviction and tonality, script and training mechanics, long enterprise cycles, and many live cases.

## 2. Rules and thresholds

### Close rate and pricing
1. Healthy close rate is about 30-40%; above the mid-40s may be too high; 70-80% usually means price could double or triple. [POD-0822@00:22:06] [YT-sBJppqCeFGI@00:03:07]
2. Headroom ladder: 80%+ probably means 3-4x room in price; 60-80% probably 2-3x; 50-60% 1.5-2x; 40-50% 1.25-1.5x; 30-40% fair; under 30% get better at selling before cutting price. [YT-A_tx40lNpf8@00:00:46] [YT-ZuJryiwxjDw@00:31:52]
3. Pricing-guide version: each 10 points of close above 35% leaves room for a 25-50% rise; owners back off at the first no, and two yeses in three suggest about 2.5x room. [LS-tnDC4ZJ0oy8@00:44:47] [POD-0894@00:06:08]
4. Judge price changes as arithmetic: price x2 with close 30% to 20% is about +33% revenue per call; keep raising until rate times price stops growing revenue per conversation. [YT-QTZsh3BgOwY@00:05:32] [YT-41EvCgwPrDc@00:21:09]
5. His recollection: $15K to $25K for a three-month package cost only about 2-3% conversion when the front end qualified a bit more. [LS-B2QbLzHvKbg@03:26:24]
6. Targets depend on context: low-ticket in-person might close 80%+, investment sold on one or two calls about 5%, his target for a mid-price two-call consumer service about 40%; typical B2B phone service 30-35%, his aim above 85% when the buyer does not feel sold. [YT-c_6BrF7jOGk@00:07:16] [POD-0781@00:21:08]
7. Read close rate with offer rate and cash collected up front, per rep: low close with high cash share is a hard-line seller; high close with token deposits is "yes to anyone". [YT-c_6BrF7jOGk@00:01:31] [YT-c_6BrF7jOGk@00:04:33]
8. His estimates: about 10% never buy, 10% always buy, 80% need help deciding; about 30% arrive pre-decided. [POD-0564@00:17:30] [POD-0045@00:04:40]

### Show rate, speed and availability
1. Show-rate benchmark for appointment businesses is 70%; a 17-item nurture checklist took portfolio companies from 30-40% to about 85%. [YT-c_6BrF7jOGk@00:03:32] [YT-U_s0ekwPK5g@01:07:40] [POD-0633@00:22:03]
2. Contact a new lead within 30 minutes, ideally 5, for about 3x sales; the 60-second version is quoted as +391%, and over 5 minutes cuts close odds about 80%. [POD-0017@00:01:56] [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@00:03:03]
3. Show rate depends first on open slots: do not display a half-filled calendar; booking 4 days out gave 25% show or less; book same-day or next-day only. [POD-0675@00:07:25] [POD-0064@00:07:26] [POD-0064@00:04:42]
5. A strong brand removes the problem: Acquisition.com's show rate is near 100% with a nine-day wait. [YT-U_s0ekwPK5g@01:09:11]
6. People typically show for what they paid for 85-90% of the time or more; choose a discount over a free decoy when no-shows are costly. [POD-251114-06@00:05:36] [POD-250819-04@00:05:58]
8. Channel rates: Meta lead to in-person sale about 10%, cold webinar opt-in 2-3%, web page 1-2%; cold webinar show about 25%; a local in-person workshop shows 20-25% of leads and closes about a third. [YT-A_tx40lNpf8@00:55:53] [LS-lidFtb1Ga0o@00:33:25] [POD-0809@00:31:28]

### Friction, qualification, information
1. A sales motion has two levers, qualification and education, both forms of friction. [LS-noE1MQPMvAc@00:43:52] [YT-uaLNfijnp-8@00:20:59]
2. Price shown early plus BANT has almost always made him more money; he can hardly recall qualification making him less. [LS-B2QbLzHvKbg@05:54:06] [POD-0908@00:13:17]
3. Good friction removes only bad prospects; bad friction (a slow page) removes everyone; add layers one at a time. [YT-nrounb8NlFQ@00:32:21] [POD-0459@00:14:39]
4. With speed-to-lead in place and show rate still low, the cause is usually too little friction. [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@01:15:53]
5. Right people but cold means more education; wrong people but hot means more qualification; consumer premium sale is about 80/20 qualification to education. [LS-noE1MQPMvAc@00:46:16] [LS-noE1MQPMvAc@00:53:58]
6. He tends to put price in the VSL when demand exceeds sales capacity; with idle sellers he would probably be more willing to leave it out. [YT-Ds_Qp2U5I8U@00:22:17]
7. Information grows with price and coldness; a deal needing 2-3 calls needs 2-3 hours of information, which a specific VSL and FAQ can probably cover in maybe 45-90 minutes; usually above about $30,000 one-to-one calls fit, but he likes VSLs that only qualify and set. [YT-Ds_Qp2U5I8U@00:21:43] [YT-U_s0ekwPK5g@01:03:31]
8. He prefers offering a front-end downsell only to callers who fail qualification. [YT-MwhsM7qijy8@00:08:32]
9. Track qualified and total ascension rates; a big gap might mean too many unqualified buyers (example 80% against 10%). [YT-QTZsh3BgOwY@01:02:15]

### On the call
1. The aim is a decision, not a purchase. [POD-0524@00:43:45] [POD-0575@00:05:33]
2. Obstacles come before the ask or price, objections after; clear obstacles early or the close can look like extraction. [YT-NcD2t9qt-fM@00:21:44] [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@01:37:29] [POD-0633@00:30:21]
3. Never answer a question unless you know the answer is acceptable; answer with a question. [POD-0791@00:06:06]
4. The best closers ask most often; about 20-30% of sales come after the first no; wait up to 8 seconds after the ask. [YT-sGakuNs9mT4@00:12:53] [YT-sGakuNs9mT4@00:12:06]
6. He suggests no-based wording on a couple of milestone questions, not on mining questions. [YT-ETEFhDICm5o@00:05:38]
7. Never email an invoice; give it over the phone and ask for the sale. [LS-TLgSkSQUql4@00:16:03] [YT-mRlSb0O5QNU@00:07:19]
8. After the yes, stop selling and take payment. [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@01:14:04]
9. In his experience a question framework is easier to teach than rigid scripts, except for sales of about $200 or less. [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:33:46]
12. Skill is volume: about 1,000 cold calls per deal as a beginner; remote phone close rates climb after about 20 pickups. [YT-w9Zvk70uj5Q@00:02:53] [POD-0196@00:01:52]
13. Avoid false urgency, false scarcity and fake qualification frames. [POD-0825@00:25:02]

## 3. Tactics and scripts

### Lead to appointment
1. Have the opt-in page text the lead's number so you call at once; open like a friend with name, who you are, that they just signed up. [POD-0017@00:02:58] [POD-0019@00:00:50]
2. Reminders at 24 hours, the morning of and an hour before, manual layered on automated, SMS first; a nine-email series lifted a webinar show from 18% to 24%. [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@00:16:17] [YT-4GQLJjH9-oA@00:01:06]
3. Incurred cost: ask a small preference (shirt size) and send a personalised video the morning of. [POD-0481@00:02:25] [POD-0804@00:08:15]
4. Ask "anything that could get in the way of showing up?"; four rising frictions: that tie-down, card on file with a fee, paid consult credited, paid consult regardless. [POD-0804@00:06:32] [LS-QgmRlzh0Y7M@00:45:43]
5. Replace a free consult with a $19-29 assessment taken by card; reported 70-80% show. [POD-0818@00:22:41] [YT---9kOXNCUdE@00:06:17]
6. For a same-day slot ask when they planned to work out and offer a time after; a firm "no reschedules" policy doubled show rate. [POD-0064@00:05:43] [POD-0064@00:09:33]
7. Split setters (cold-calling versus nurturing); a nurture specialist runs a three-way intro and the closer's morning-of video. [YT-c_6BrF7jOGk@00:05:40] [YT-c_6BrF7jOGk@00:18:22]
8. Edify the closer in the handoff; the setter tells the prospect to watch the video and the closer opens by asking if they did. [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@00:39:23] [YT-n6SHLmKcY0E@00:49:30]
9. Make the pre-call VSL stick: commitment, a hidden secret word, pause the call if skipped; confirm in reminders that the spouse attends. [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@00:58:24] [LS-A8p2HCI_23E@00:37:37]
10. Pre-call VSL: 3-7 minutes for high-intent traffic, extended with answers to objections heard on calls; interview recent no-shows once and answer their reasons in their words. [YT-SmiOK8Yun4s@00:06:11] [LS-B2QbLzHvKbg@05:22:51]

### Running the call
1. Open with proof, promise, plan in 15-20 seconds and a no-pressure exit; swap rapport chat for a short credential intro. [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@00:49:23] [YT-4GQLJjH9-oA@00:14:50]
2. First ask what action brought them ("why did you reply or book?"), then "why today and not last month?". [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@00:58:01] [POD-0150@00:05:38]
3. Discovery loop: alternate "give me an example" and "tell me more"; stay high level, since detail hands control to the prospect. [LS-I82d3jxg_Aw@00:27:18] [YT-4GQLJjH9-oA@00:17:50]
4. Future-pace the pain (life in a year if nothing changes); record likes and dislikes of past solutions. [LS-QgmRlzh0Y7M@00:16:43] [POD-0785@00:21:33]
5. Pitch: pillars as a sequence or three requirements, one to three analogies each, under about two minutes; done-for-you gets an 11-27 step walk-through, others the light version. [POD-0770@00:03:57] [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@01:05:32] [YT-4GQLJjH9-oA@00:19:23]
6. Describe what will happen for the buyer, not how; sell the goal in their units (pounds and weeks). [LS-IG9IWQVmxqk@00:34:20] [POD-0524@00:36:50]
7. Goal-based price: gap over safe weekly progress gives a time frame, priced per week with an effort-conditional guarantee. [YT-1UhvBSQFy6A@00:10:50]
8. Take the card first as "completing your profile"; ask for ID, then swap it for the card. [YT-1UhvBSQFy6A@00:08:25] [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@01:52:22]
10. Menu close: customer crosses items off and sees each consequence; anchor upsells at 5-10x the core price. [YT-rc7cxL7ql7Y@00:22:12] [POD-250819-05@00:26:40]
12. In Hormozi's opinion, after about six step-downs a refusal is not about money; after the first three steps fail ask "1 to 10", then "why not a 10?" if 7 or less (8+ keep working terms, 7 or less change the product). [YT-gza5RtQCVsA@00:08:38] [POD-250819-06@00:09:50]

### Objections
1. Stall ladder: main concern, what they fear, decision variables, what makes it a yes, what a no. [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@01:17:05] [YT-RVbvhPGFi6E@00:10:39]
2. Doubt: ask how much proof would be enough and when the demand becomes unreasonable; for "too good to be true" have them design the ideal programme. [POD-0110@00:04:07] [POD-0110@00:00:46]
3. Decision maker: ask early who else must agree, ask what happens if they say no (about one in three go ahead), cite past agreements, ask for support not permission. [POD-0202@00:02:37] [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@01:54:46] [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@01:56:19]
4. Detail objection is a preference objection: ask why they care and meet the reason. [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@01:26:31] [POD-0791@00:09:11]
5. "Busy" is a priorities objection, "give me time" is avoidance; busy is when they most need support. [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@02:02:29] [POD-0564@00:29:43]
6. Money: agree, tell a story of someone like them, ask permission; "no money" usually means pain was not built before price. [POD-0033@00:00:46] [POD-0033@00:00:00]
8. Price on the phone: the mechanic close (no honest quote before inspection) books the visit. [POD-0030@00:00:45] [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@01:23:29]

### After the yes, follow-up, training
