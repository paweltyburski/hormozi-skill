# product-delivery

The canon already covers the Sales to Fulfillment Continuum, Create Flow Monetize Add Friction, the Delivery Cube, the Value vs Willingness-to-Pay Matrix, Trim and Stack, service levers for tiers, "valuable before scalable", and the Productize and Specialize stages of the Scaling Roadmap. The recordings add working arithmetic (capacity, group ratios, cost per customer), activation and first-win windows, what quality really costs in extra work, and the ways delivery goes wrong (outsourcing the core, fulfilment someone else controls, pre-selling what you cannot make). They are also the only place with physical-product, made-to-order and supply-constrained cases. Retention routines, pricing and scripts have their own pages.

## Rules and thresholds

1. Services need at least 80% gross margin, 90-95% preferred; 80 to 90 doubles operating leverage, 80 to 95 quadruples it. Templated products run about one rep per 300-1,000 accounts, custom about 5. [POD-0839@00:09:14]
2. Service cost of goods is delivery payroll only (not front desk, HR, rent); cost per customer is delivery payroll divided by customers, in steps. Price each delivery level at 5-10x total cost, 5x being the floor because costs overrun. [YT-9xAMe0QBFhU@00:00:45] [YT-jzKpAtzKQ54@00:10:58] [YT-mRlSb0O5QNU@00:03:55]
3. Supply-constraint levers: raise price, change the delivery ratio, productize, or hire; for a supply-constrained owner the order is price, then group formats, then delegation. [YT-N5MExtki_VI@00:16:59] [POD-0853@00:41:12]
4. Supply test (yes/no): could you serve more at your standard, are you turning people away or waitlisting, are top slots or products always sold out, do you run out of inventory or face long shipping delays (physical products). [YT-sGv2BTUCcCM@00:31:50]
5. Technology or training can lift output per person about 5x; better staffing of open hours often adds 20-40% throughput. [YT-sGv2BTUCcCM@00:32:36] [YT-s7QA1TJKlbQ@00:04:58]
6. Group ladder: move one-on-one clients into groups of about four; above about seven it is one-to-many; change cadence before ratio. Semi-private is about two-thirds of the one-to-one price with eight times the people; one-to-ten is about 10x leverage. [YT-N5MExtki_VI@00:43:25] [POD-0474@00:02:29] [YT-WttZ6b-KUP4@00:04:07]
7. Three sessions a week instead of unlimited roughly doubles capacity; a full gym on daily sessions may profit only $2,000-3,000 a month. [POD-0080@00:00:45] [POD-0095@00:00:00]
8. Gym price shape: lowest tier $170-210 a month, semi-private $500-700, nothing worth offering between $210 and $500. [POD-0080@00:12:42]
9. Support utilization: minutes per task type times daily volume against paid hours; target high 80s, above 90% means hire. [POD-0278@00:04:41]
10. Selling speed by reordering the delivery queue is close to 100% margin and the customer may pay about 2x; for a surge, ask suppliers what a rush takes and charge 2-3x, since even +20-50% cost raises margin in dollars and percent. [POD-0821@00:01:21] [POD-0821@00:04:31]
11. Find activation points by taking customers who stayed (say 12 months), looking back at early actions, ranking by correlation. Examples: custom domain (about 5x longer stay), 5 CRM features, first 5 clients. [POD-0870@00:21:34] [POD-0518@00:08:38] [POD-0418@00:02:31]
12. Measure time to onboarding (purchase to live) separately from time to value, and shorten both. [YT-QTZsh3BgOwY@00:51:39]
13. Engineer a first win within 7 days, up to 14 for long cycles; if the first real result takes 90 days, design a material win in seven. In one tool, users who lasted 14 days stayed. [POD-0415@00:24:12] [POD-0518@00:06:58] [POD-0181@00:14:35]
14. Customers decide to stay at two points, between sale and start and during orientation; days 7-10 carry the highest churn risk. He estimates 2-5 extra months of life from investing most in the first 24-72 hours. [YT-hnfh2jqNVEQ@00:10:26] [POD-0118@00:00:45] [YT-h6y0nYVZgwE@00:25:00]
15. Good to great costs roughly 5-10x the work (his estimate); 4 to 5 stars looks like 20% more and costs about 10x. [YT-cq8GyLrEuAk@00:10:13] [POD-0521@00:04:10]
16. For him, ready to ship when anything he would add or subtract would make it worse (not always); define the problem narrowly and ship once the sliver is done; his litmus test for release is good enough to be better than what came before. [POD-0606@00:10:42] [POD-0642@00:26:48] [YT-bxs5pN0ZnRw@00:06:03]

Unused features raise cancellations because buyers see how little of the price they consume; fix retention by subtraction and keep the original promise. [YT-FiL0r5_dUvM@00:17:14] [YT-N5MExtki_VI@00:46:58] [YT--j8_YCWZ05Q@00:06:49]

## Tactics and scripts

1. Reopen or size delivery from numbers: recurring revenue, a payroll budget (delivery at most 20% of revenue, 4.3 weeks a month) and cost per session; start with the fewest sessions (2 a day, about 90% margin). Check capacity as sessions per day times class size times days. [YT-mOKl6dodMTg@00:04:44] [YT-mOKl6dodMTg@00:08:06] [POD-0122@00:03:02]
2. Size roles from expected customer inflow, activities each customer needs and time per activity, then role calendars; an online coach adding 10 clients a week needs about 1.5 hours per client up front. [YT-JOY6ZzBMb_4@00:01:02] [YT-JOY6ZzBMb_4@00:03:36]
3. Find owner-only steps: in one call he asked whether selling or delivering was slower, listed the delivery steps, and asked which only the owner can do. [YT-uaLNfijnp-8@00:03:11] [YT-uaLNfijnp-8@00:05:48]
4. Before scaling, list every daily action, group into workflows that produce the result, document each and automate one at a time; write decision trees for common cases. [LS-A8p2HCI_23E@00:25:12] [YT-mr4Pw66_490@00:07:06]
5. Beta any new play on at least 20 representative units including the bottom 30%, about two tests a month, expecting roughly two in three to fail; or two customers per 20% performance band. [POD-200710-01@00:21:28] [YT-Rm4zRdLAyjw@00:19:39]
6. To copy what works, interview about 20 top performers on the weak metric, keep only practices they all share, test on weaker clients. [YT-0mqqbuM9sAk@00:01:35] [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:54:28] [LS-9OsUfwTTslc@00:24:43]
7. Shorten the portal (most buyers use a quarter of it, about 90% do not finish) and show one next action at a time. [POD-0181@00:17:22] [YT-rc7cxL7ql7Y@00:09:02]
8. Do not drop new members straight into a group: run a few one-to-one sessions first (4-6, start with 2), book the next session at the end of each, add reminders. [YT-QwEb78e5a8Y@00:04:53] [YT-QwEb78e5a8Y@00:02:27]
9. Onboard in layers: set and reconfirm expectations in 24 hours, connect to the community in the first days, finish with call notes, tailored tutorials and a named contact introduced live. [YT-sPkMHh8zTMI@00:14:31] [POD-0118@00:00:45] [YT-afbP6sB_Atc@00:11:50]
10. Report in small increments: a daily note of what you did (14 touches in two weeks vs one), cumulative results across the relationship, four to six small promises opened and closed in the first 24 hours. [YT-yPDQCfrwh8E@00:02:40] [YT-41EvCgwPrDc@00:29:28] [YT-yPDQCfrwh8E@00:12:19]
11. Productise custom work: find the few packages covering about 80% of jobs and lead each buyer to a template; offer a custom-order option on every product page. [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@04:43:51] [YT-HGZOxBfnF-E@00:20:57]
12. One-to-many assets show the widest cost-to-value gap: a meal-plan spreadsheet took about 100 hours once and 15 minutes per plan. [POD-230819-07@00:26:27] [YT-HsQeQM1jUeg@00:25:21]
13. Quality assurance by random sampling against a checklist scales further than reviewing everything; put scripts on a tablet with check marks. [LS-lidFtb1Ga0o@01:11:48] [POD-0853@00:28:06]
14. Cut delivery cost with AI, automation and offshoring without announcing it, selling at normal rates and keeping the margin. [YT-s7QA1TJKlbQ@00:14:58]
15. Pitch matches delivery model: done-for-you gets a detailed 11-27 step walk-through, DIY and done-with-you the light three-pillar version. [POD-0770@00:01:00] [YT-4GQLJjH9-oA@00:19:23]

## Changes of mind and contradictions

1. Early on he was marketing-first and later saw excellent service multiplies marketing; he now spends a lot more time on product, and says that when supply and demand are both stuck, raise supply first, reversing his old demand-first advice. [POD-0058@00:02:07] [POD-0777@00:10:15] [YT-YONIUpk-kAs@01:11:02]
2. Fix the product first or advertise to expose problems: several recordings say do not advertise a leaky product, another says about 20 real members were enough to expose broken processes, so spend on ads before delivery is perfect. [POD-0759@00:10:31] [YT-VBoRLJimVzc@00:11:17] [YT-h6y0nYVZgwE@00:11:44]

4. Done-for-you versus teaching: the move to teaching grew beyond expectation, yet sell-and-leave then cost $150,000 in refunds; elsewhere he says start hands-on and premium, treat the first offer as paid R&D, and step down later.
