# hiring-talent

The canon already covers the Internal Core Four (Recruiting Mirror), A Players (~5x output for ~25% more pay), Job Description as Ad, the Two-Step Four-Way Fit Interview, the Interview Scorecard, Peter Principle and Try Before You Buy, Cost to Acquire Talent, the Five S's of Advanced Compensation and Strategic Workforce Planning. The recordings add: an order of hires by revenue stage with break-even arithmetic, a "who, not what" diagnostic, hiring-speed numbers, group-interview and work-sample screens, a later shift toward rate of learning, firing rules, pay design (profit share versus equity versus revenue share), and many cases where recruiting, not demand, was the constraint.

## Rules and thresholds

Value of talent and pay
1. An A player might cost 25-50% more (about 1.5x) and be worth 3-5 B players; other recordings claim 5-10x output, ~10x for +30% pay, probably ~25x worth for ~3x cost, with the gap growing by seniority ($50K manager ~5x, $300K salesperson ~14x, $1M employee ~50x by his count). ## Tactics and scripts

1. Time study: log 15-minute blocks for a week, rank by value, delegate the bottom, hire for the biggest slice. [POD-0840@00:40:37] [LS-tnDC4ZJ0oy8@01:46:13] [YT-0EqJD2o-Mnk@00:16:00]

4. Frontline/BDR: let everyone in and let results decide; four-hour paid shifts; promotion to closer is ten deals with one closed in two weeks; cut a rep who likely cannot close in one to two weeks (simple offers). ## Changes of mind and contradictions

1. Equity: loose giving, then refusing, then phantom equity for key players; Ramsey gives leaders none; the clinic owner and agency owners get profit share instead. [YT-PxpPynt1mis@00:03:29] [YT--HJg4TYBgtI@00:17:15] [POD-0933@00:40:41] [POD-0818@00:18:03]
2. What to hire for: now far more for rate of learning ("yes and no" on whether above attitude and prior skills; a zero-skill fast learner may pass a five-skill slow one in ~6-7 months), versus "only work they have done". [POD-0940@00:08:27] [POD-0755@00:06:57] [POD-0174@00:07:48]
3. "Nobody does it like me" was his own failure to train and pay; now everyone is replaceable. [POD-230819-16@00:05:19] [POD-251114-24@00:07:16]
4. Speed: "hire more slowly" versus letting everyone in for BDRs and 80 hires in 90 days; the split is high-volume frontline versus senior. [LS-noE1MQPMvAc@00:17:57] [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:41:46] [POD-0362@00:05:41]
5. Headcount: Gym Launch cut 130 to 85; now the question is how to get the smartest people, and AI favours fewer, better hires. [POD-0146@00:02:26] [POD-0784@00:20:45] [POD-0894b@00:32:51]
6. Turnaround odds: one in five improve after a 14-day plan, versus probably about half the time with coaching. [YT-I64gYLBrics@00:17:07] [POD-0940@00:25:38]
7. Judging new hires: Leila Hormozi says every A player she has had showed within days, yet one or two conversations are unreliable and 90 days is the filter. [YT-Jv3GmZ9Odko@00:06:31] [POD-0995@00:07:12] [POD-0995@00:06:17]
8. Bad-behaviour stars: fire quickly (probably most prevalent on sales teams), versus a "jerk" star fixed in a week by naming behaviours. [LS-noE1MQPMvAc@00:19:26] [POD-0871@00:14:09]
9. Operator: Leila says pick someone like your own strengths; Hormozi elsewhere says hire complements. [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@03:44:00] [POD-0500@00:04:22]
10. Promotions: star technicians failed as managers and the best closer is often a poor manager, yet middle-of-pack well-liked reps are promoted into sales manager roles. [POD-0100@00:01:44] [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@00:25:18] [POD-0853@00:31:45]
