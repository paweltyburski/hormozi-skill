# offers

The canon covers the Grand Slam Offer and its parts (Value Equation, Problem-Solution list, Trim and Stack, Bonus Checklist, four guarantee types, Scarcity and urgency, MAGIC naming), the gym and lead-gen-agency rewrite cases, and the money-model offer types. The recordings add the agency rewrite as a full funnel, many rules for sizing guarantees and bonuses, more on pricing and perceived value (buyer wealth, status, speed), tests for a weak offer, a long run of live clinics applying the framework to small businesses, and some changes of mind (top versus bottom of the Value Equation, over-delivering versus expectations).

## 2. Rules and thresholds

### Diagnosis and order of work
1. Three symptoms of a weak offer: leads hard to get, most people do not want to buy, and those who do compare your price. [YT-4GjwtnA76ig@00:02:22]
2. Fix a business back to front: offer, then conversion process, then traffic; or what you sell, how, to whom, how they find you. [YT-8VqSFBMMS4M@00:06:10] [YT-zzleYxkf39k@00:04:51]
3. Fix the offer before copy and ads; after first traction fix the product. Stages he gives: $0-$1M promotion, $1M-$10M product, $10M-$100M people. [POD-0433@00:17:09]
4. Rate market, offer and persuasion as plus, neutral or negative; move from market to offer when the market is not negative, and from offer to persuasion when the offer is not bad or normal. [YT-4XWlHPyAico@00:03:06]
5. If the business needs a brilliant salesperson, the offer is broken. [POD-0464@00:07:47]
6. In $3M-plus internet businesses the offer, packaging and pricing are usually unoptimised: they undercharge or charge for the wrong things. [POD-0527@00:02:47]
8. If only about 20% of core buyers take the upsell, suspect the offer (said to one caller). [YT-n6SHLmKcY0E@00:03:04]
9. If people refuse even a free offer, ask why: the answer reveals hidden costs you can remove and later charge for. [YT-w7g08dVTwaE@00:06:14]

### Clarity and the single idea
1. An offer is clear if you can explain it in one or two sentences; Hormozi's personal razor is that a good one is "textable". [YT-pxVeOkOVr2w@00:12:57] [YT-YONIUpk-kAs@01:04:37]
2. Rule of One: one big idea that is easy to understand, easy to believe, and interesting; 91 of the top 100 offers in Masterson's review used one. [YT-OyGKFhTf0go@00:00:49] [YT-OyGKFhTf0go@00:01:35]
3. A sale can break only one belief; aim for the fewest "need to believes". [POD-0174@00:02:08] [YT-FMzKk73iUhw@00:16:06]

### Value Equation extensions
1. The dream outcome sets the category and price level; likelihood, delay and effort set price within it. [POD-0521@00:10:30] [YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc@00:03:16]
2. It is a division: a bottom half near zero makes value unbounded. Scoring each driver 0 to 1 compares offers out of 4. [POD-0581@00:06:02] [POD-0581@00:20:22]
3. Time delay has two layers: the long-term result people buy, and the short-term experience that keeps them in; micro time is daily effort, macro time total duration. [POD-0581@00:15:08] [YT-VBoRLJimVzc@00:21:58]
4. Effort is what the buyer must start doing; sacrifice is what they must stop doing. [YT-F3NyhOtRpOE@00:44:22] [POD-0415@00:22:23]
7. Four vectors (speed, risk, cost, ease): winning one is enough; labour services can reach three but should message only the one the buyer cares about most. [YT-s7QA1TJKlbQ@00:00:00] [YT-s7QA1TJKlbQ@00:23:16]
9. Quality means the absence of hard: list what is painful in existing providers and remove it, but keep what the buyer enjoys. [POD-0868@00:20:21] [POD-0869@00:19:16]

### Pricing and value ratios
1. Price can sit up to about 100x delivery cost (not 2-3x) if the buyer still gets a bargain. [POD-0581@00:01:56]
2. Deliver far more than you charge for: a $3,000 package built to deliver $30,000; fee about 10% of value created. [POD-0046@00:03:53] [POD-0449@00:10:01]
3. His own rule of thumb: keep a feature only if it alone more than justifies 3-4x the price. [YT-FiL0r5_dUvM@00:17:14]
5. Price is rarely the buyer's largest cost; remove a bigger hidden cost and you can charge more. [POD-0521@00:12:17]
6. A price objection means value is unclear: typically work on conviction, sometimes on perceived time and effort, rather than outcome size. [POD-0090@00:01:24]
7. Price is the strongest growth lever and the one people forget to test. [POD-0443@00:08:43]
8. For an ordinary service a higher price can raise close rate; under roughly $3-5M, charging more is often among the biggest levers. [YT-OQf2Ba-Lp_4@00:06:38]
9. Restructuring with bonuses, creative guarantees and premium support can raise price 3-5x with the same service. [POD-251114-04@00:08:46]
10. Poor buyers feel a low price as a large share of their means; wealthy buyers pay for time and certainty. [YT-KhFlD54nQrY@00:01:38] [YT-KhFlD54nQrY@00:07:43]
12. Tiers $10-$40 apart give top-10% buyers no way to spend more; use up to four tiers in clearly different buying buckets. [POD-0938@00:12:31] [LS-_ooj0V4hpgw@01:08:42]
13. Starting-price rules of thumb (services): consumer impulse $500-$600, consumer higher ticket usually $3,000-$10,000, small business $400-$800 a month cheap and probably $2,000-$3,000 mid. [POD-0938@00:30:32]

### Promises and truth
1. Promise only what your repeatable process reliably delivers (six figures a month was his ceiling); keep lowering promises until close rates fall, then stop. [POD-0020@00:05:29] [POD-230819-15@00:17:16]
2. Promising less and disclosing more will probably sell to about the same number of people but shifts the mix of buyers you convert. [POD-0825@00:09:43] [POD-0825@00:31:51]
3. Sell the product as a tool for building something, not a money promise, to cut "I did not make money" churn. [POD-0870@00:23:41]

### Guarantees
1. Results-based guarantee suits short front-end offers (4-6 weeks) to cold traffic, not long back-end contracts. [POD-0007@00:03:42]
2. Broad unconditional guarantees suit low-ticket consumer offers; higher-ticket and business offers lean specific; costly fulfilment will likely want a conditional or anti-guarantee. [POD-230819-11@00:22:41] [POD-230819-11@00:14:51]
3. Maximise net conversions: sales up 20% with refunds rising 1% to 5% still nets about +15%; if 10% back sells as well as 100%, give 10%. [YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc@00:17:36] [YT---9kOXNCUdE@00:31:39]
4. Strength is proportional to the risk reversed; guarantee what nearly scares you, then fix the delivery that scares you. [POD-0451@00:20:15] [POD-0451@00:24:09]
5. Thought experiment: would "free, guaranteed, or we pay you double" be easy to sell? If you are not confident you could deliver, fix the product, then consider peeling off one or two conditions to keep a compelling guarantee (he says maybe 90% yes). [YT-neTSqOAMgao@00:34:34] [POD-0418@00:16:10]
6. Do not guarantee what the buyer controls or what staff deliver without skin in the game; one such satisfaction guarantee brought about 25% refunds. [POD-0825@00:33:03] [POD-0807@00:02:41]
7. People want the outcome, not the money; in intangible markets guarantee a specific experience the customer describes. [POD-0850@00:12:18] [POD-0445@00:09:43]
8. Shrink guarantees as reputation grows: unconditional when starting, then conditional, performance pay, anti-guarantee. [YT-s7QA1TJKlbQ@00:08:55]
9. Build conditions from the first 30-90 days of customers who ascended and renewed, with the fewest stipulations (2-3). [POD-0451@00:22:34] [YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc@00:20:30]

### Scarcity and urgency
1. Keep it real: state true capacity, refuse sales after the cap or deadline, offer fewer spots than you could sell. [YT-_PCCqqv2pig@00:10:05] [YT-JDR-R--4HhM@00:22:08] [POD-0583@00:20:02]
2. A limit not communicated is worth nothing. [YT-4GQLJjH9-oA@00:31:17]

### Reference numbers

2. Naming or cover change alone: claims of 2-10x response, sometimes up to 5x buyers (his hedged claim), 3-4x conversion. [POD-230819-13@00:13:39] [POD-230819-11@00:35:44] [POD-0459@00:34:01]
3. Portfolio companies averaged 2.8x revenue and 4.7x profit after 24 months. [YT-VBoRLJimVzc@00:25:32]
4. Estimated impact of five service-business changes: about 5x price, 1.5-2x lower CAC (maybe 2x if lucky), "seven or eight X" overall. [YT-N5MExtki_VI@00:51:21]

## 3. Tactics and scripts

1. One-line statement: "I help [narrow who] get [good outcome] without [bad stuff], through [unique mechanism]." [YT-nIk3DedjxJM@00:18:03]
2. Narrow the avatar until three of age, gender, profession, problem, interest are set; narrowing has doubled to 5x revenue while cutting audience about 85%. [YT-nIk3DedjxJM@00:08:18] [POD-0531@00:10:30]
4. Splinter stack: peel apart what you already do, name each piece, present as bonuses; give extra service rather than money off. [POD-0464@00:10:57] [YT-qpQvdBFW_yI@00:09:55]
5. Over-stack first, then trim what early clients do not value (about 80% of commitments for the first five). [YT-RZbpSe9pdFs@00:04:18] [POD-0645@00:45:01]
6. Cut delivery time to half of rivals'; peel one element off a slow product and deliver it first; sell speed as a priced tier (+50% for 24 hours, +300% for 60 minutes). [YT-XwZH-lOKG9c@00:24:06] [POD-0742@00:27:17] [POD-0869@00:19:16]
8. Present core first as the anchor; stack bonuses only when the buyer hesitates ($2,000 core, $15,000 of bonuses). [YT-XwZH-lOKG9c@00:26:46]
9. Hold bonuses in reserve and reveal the one answering the stated objection, then ask again; give unused bonuses after purchase. [POD-0742@00:23:08] [POD-0451@00:02:30]
10. Each bonus must individually outweigh the price; one that needs explaining should go. [YT-5MjjpB8SPMo@00:06:35] [YT-YONIUpk-kAs@01:03:00]
11. Turn recurring buying obstacles into bonuses; keep a vault by recording workshops and interviews; bundle partner deals with a real price. [YT-unshZobTt6Q@00:27:44] [POD-230819-11@00:09:20] [POD-0284@00:03:13]
12. Guarantee ladders: 30-day unconditional plus 90-day conditional; milestone guarantees paid in credit or time; extension at least twice the original period. [YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc@00:19:18] [POD-0451@00:24:09] [POD-230819-11@00:25:53]
13. Tie the refund to behaviour you need (8 weeks, 2x fee, content due Fridays by noon); name the buyer's biggest fear and reverse it. [YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc@00:06:45] [POD-230819-11@00:33:37]
14. When you cannot control client behaviour, guarantee the service delivered; for a high-hard-cost project guarantee on time and on budget or give back profit (about 20%), not the price. [POD-230819-11@00:21:44] [YT---9kOXNCUdE@00:29:59]
15. Sell the guarantee as an add-on (about 10% extra) or as insurance. [YT-unshZobTt6Q@00:54:50] [POD-0540@00:05:09]
16. Test a guarantee on sales calls first, then move it into marketing. [POD-0418@00:15:04]
17. Close with best case versus worst case; with no guarantee, argue control over the result lets you guarantee more. [POD-0052@00:00:00] [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@02:02:29]
18. Honest scarcity: total cap, weekly intake cap, cohort cap; advertise fullness; limited releases about monthly; make leaving a top level final. [POD-0451@00:04:52] [POD-0583@00:18:19] [POD-0583@00:16:44] [POD-0583@00:21:38]
19. Name the avatar at sub-market level, rhyme the container word, use 3-5 components, test two or three names and keep the winner as control. [POD-230819-13@00:04:20] [POD-230819-13@00:06:08] [POD-230819-13@00:09:28]
21. Free offers: try the core offer directly first; free items should be cheap to serve and leave something to upgrade; store credit drew as many customers as cash back. [POD-230819-04@00:06:41] [POD-251114-18@00:01:36] [POD-250819-03@00:07:25]
23. Sell to goal: measure the gap, estimate the work that makes failure unreasonable, price that, make the guarantee conditional on the work. [LS-WeH2E1fGgaw@00:31:41] [POD-0474@00:11:20]
24. In one client case, replace hourly billing with a flat outcome fee plus small commission ($1,500 average to $5,800 flat). [YT-3yAiVjcImQ4@00:07:09]
25. Move payment earlier: half now, half at midpoint, prepayment sold with a guarantee only for prepayers. [LS-R7McLmx73Xw@00:28:33]
28. First customers: a free trade capped at five (use, feedback, review); a free beta of about 20 for testimonials; a required charity donation of $500-$1,000 fixed non-engaging free referrals. [YT-w7g08dVTwaE@00:03:12] [POD-0448@00:06:18] [YT-HsQeQM1jUeg@00:11:02]
29. Pick the first thing to sell from three circles (pain overcome, passion, profession) or his personal seven categories times physical or digital; real offers combine categories. [YT-AN2KpRBsmRY@00:10:27] [POD-0540@00:01:01] [YT-LVM89ik-7Kw@00:02:17]

## 4. Cases by kind of business

### Physical products and e-commerce
1. ButcherBox: a bug that gave free bacon in every first box became the core offer; "bacon for life" (about $4 a box) cut acquisition cost by more than it cost. [YT-orMbq2LtzKE@00:31:12] [YT-orMbq2LtzKE@00:32:29]

### Made-to-order and manufacturing
1. Nationwide balloon decor (about $2.2M, 18-19% net): productise into three packages, price on value, focus corporate. [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@04:39:23]
2. Permanent lighting can be leased on 1, 3 or 5-year contracts instead of a $6,000-$10,000 upfront buy. [POD-0861@00:04:26]

## 5. Changes of mind and contradictions

1. Top versus bottom of the equation: he says most people, himself included, focus on the top (about 70-75% of his career); now he works delay and effort. [POD-0181@00:12:26] [POD-0581@00:05:11] [YT-_qspvJAq34M@00:07:32]
2. Over-delivering: he moved from "over-deliver" to "set expectations, meet expectations"; elsewhere he still says set modest expectations and over-deliver. [POD-0633@00:26:13] [YT-EUW3rMp-Uvg@00:08:31]
3. Sales versus product: he now thinks his younger focus on sales and marketing was the wrong game, yet also warns people over-polish offers. [YT-DiQ3N8F1Hl8@00:10:36] [YT-U_s0ekwPK5g@00:09:53]
4. Zero-cost solutions are crowded so he favours high value with some cost, which he says he has probably changed from the books, while elsewhere advising free content and paid implementation. [YT-prj1bnTAM8A@00:21:34] [YT-oTQPxPFROck@00:03:13]
5. "Too expensive" cancel reasons: once dismissed, now collected and tagged. [YT--j8_YCWZ05Q@00:05:17]
6. Changing the offer every six months is a warning sign, yet his wrapper changed about 20 times in 4 years (the monetisation structure stayed). [YT-Uki3IUkUu7Q@00:11:19] [POD-0244@00:00:52]
7. Price tests: four price points converted the same and he chose by preferred business; elsewhere raising price lifted close rate or doubled profit. [YT-kloJJeiysxg@00:14:21] [YT-TooAB8Ow6cQ@00:20:55] [YT-ZuJryiwxjDw@00:18:56] [YT-_ArQlwPvGUA@00:00:00]
8. Programme length: six weeks fits the general population, twelve only a narrower buyer; yet duration is not a selling point. [POD-0070@00:06:22] [LS-6_CCutkM11g@02:41:38]
9. Unconditional guarantee: short front end only, versus the first 30 days of a longer programme, versus "nine times out of ten" Hormozi's reasoning is that a guarantee earns more. [POD-0007@00:03:42] [POD-0451@00:19:23] [POD-0418@00:15:04]
