# outreach

The canon already covers: Core Four, Warm Outreach Steps (list, personalise, 100 a day, ACA replies, "who do you know", five free slots), Three Problems of Strangers (List Building, Big Fast Value, More Times More Ways), the Nine-Word Email, Rule of 100, and the headline numbers (100 cold calls give about 4 engaged leads; about 20% warm reply; 2,000 emails a day gave about 4 customers daily). The recordings add: the Gym Launch outbound build in real figures (funnel per week, CAC, ramp time, the executive "intervention"), staffing tiers and daily dial targets, how long outbound really takes versus what he expected, volume and effort-gap benchmarks, many scripts and openers (mostly from the first-customers videos), list-quality tests, the organic "Rule of 300" variant, and a long tail of advice he gave to specific businesses on how to reach their buyers.

## 1. Rules and thresholds

Funnel and cost benchmarks (Gym Launch and portfolio companies; his figures unless named)

- Gym Launch cold-calling funnel per week: 500 calls, about 10 interested, 7 show up, 1 sale. [POD-0249@00:01:31]
- Outbound CAC there was about $400 per sale (about $1,600 a month per caller, 4 sales), against $2,000 to $2,500 in paid ads. [POD-0249@00:03:30]
- Outbound is worth scaling when gross profit per customer is above 3x CAC (so above $1,200 on $400 CAC); output is lists x activity x people. [POD-0249@00:07:01]
- A later BDR-model estimate: about $50 per shown appointment, 10% close of scheduled appointments, about $500 per close, against an LTV of about $40,000. [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:19:49]
- Niche high-ticket portfolio company: about 4% lead-to-engagement by email, about 1 customer per 100 attempts (presuming a third convert), over 30:1 return; 3:1 is the stated minimum. [POD-230819-10@00:26:23]
- Fund outbound with 10-20% of the ad budget, expect no return for about a quarter, then roughly 20:1; he says he has yet to see someone doing 100 reach-outs a day for six months fail to do well. [YT-XwZH-lOKG9c@00:50:16]
- He says outbound should in theory be done only when the ticket is at least about $15,000 (his cheapest offer is $20,000). [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:06:30]
- Outbound should run for the business, not the personal brand, and suits transactional or simple sales. [POD-0460@00:41:51]

Volume and effort

- Floors: about 100 calls a day, and 200 to 500 cold emails a day (200 minimum) before 5-10 meaningful replies mean anything. [POD-0249@00:05:42]
- BDR target: 100 dials a day, about 20% pickup, 1-2 shown appointments with SDS support. [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:09:56]
- Dial expectations the host (not Hormozi) cites: 200 a day at DirecTV; at least 30 by lunch for a sales rep. [POD-0515@00:05:00]
- Phone funnel in the book: 100 calls, 20% pickup, 25% take the magnet, about 4 engaged leads, roughly one per hour of calling. [POD-230819-10@00:25:36]
- Hand-doing 100 attempts takes about 5 hours (about 20 an hour); spend about four hours a day on the chosen acquisition method. [POD-0645@00:15:25], [YT-gD0X-PLax5I@00:06:20]
- People underestimate needed volume by 10x to 50x: 100 DMs over eight weeks instead of 100 a day; 10 calls every three days versus 200 a day is about 50x, not 2-4x. [POD-0515@00:03:15], [POD-0401@00:11:20], [YT-WrCt0R3FBFs@00:10:32]
- Beginners expect about 5 reach-outs per customer; about 200 is closer, and the first sale often pays about a tenth of the imagined price. [POD-0787@00:14:30]
- A beginner at cold calling should expect about 1,000 calls per deal; after 5,000 without one, do another 5,000. [YT-w9Zvk70uj5Q@00:02:53]
- Volume before tweaking: 100 conversations or 10,000 emails first; scripts stay 1-2 pages for phone and chat, half a page for email. [POD-230819-10@00:16:33]

Close and response rates

- Warm chain: 100 contacts, about 20 replies, about 4 take the free offer, about 1 pays (the last step is about 1 of the 4). [POD-230819-06@00:27:06]
- Warm outreach alone: 500 a week at 1% buying a $400 offer is about $104,000 a year. [POD-230819-06@00:28:13]
- A personal video or voice memo with the name and one personal line draws about 20 replies per 100. [POD-230819-10@00:27:10]
- In cold outbound a 0.5-1% close is normally good news; on a $50K-$100K B2B deal a 1% close on cold calls is fine, think revenue per dial. [YT-nrounb8NlFQ@00:37:19], [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@00:09:38]

Ramp time

- Expected 12 weeks, veterans said a year, it took almost a year. [POD-230819-10@00:04:27], [YT-oDK4g5na4Jw@00:38:39]
- Gym Launch ramp in the book: sales per month 0, 2, 4, 6, 10, 14, 20, 30 from September to May ($16,000 per sale). [POD-230819-10@00:03:26]
- Gym Launch ramp in the sales-team talk: 1, 2, 4, 6-8, 12, 16 per month, about 45 a month by month 14 (about 60% of company sales). [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:04:36]
- Each added outbound person adds a steady 4-8 sales a month, avoiding the lumpy cash of paid campaigns. [POD-0249@00:11:36]

Structure, offer and list rules

- Three tiers: BDRs (first touch), SDS (qualify and deliver the promised value), consultants (closers); roughly 2 BDR per SDS and 2 SDS per closer. [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:08:24]
- If outreach returns are strong, ten people at half your effectiveness still beat you alone, so delegate despite the quality drop. [YT-UR9dO1jZemE@00:04:20]
- Hire experience, not potential: cold outreach failed under an experienced outsider and an inexperienced insider and worked only with an experienced insider who knew every stat. [POD-230819-18@00:18:27], [POD-230819-10@00:25:36]
- If the cold offer is not pulling, add to what you give until saying no feels foolish; value must land in under about 30 seconds. [POD-230819-10@00:15:46]
- The free thing offered must be something the market truly wants; run it unscalably first. [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:14:32]
- He would not use a giveaway as the attraction offer for cold outbound; giveaways suit one-to-many channels such as content and ads. [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@03:36:36]
- For expensive offers aim cold outreach at interest first and sell in a later step; for low-ticket products use outreach to recruit affiliates instead. [POD-230819-10@00:07:07], [POD-230819-10@00:24:37]
- Sample-test every list source (a few hundred leads) before buying volume, and prefer hand-built lists for freshness. [POD-230819-10@00:08:42], [YT-aBPWCdaJJqA@00:00:14], [LS-ehmBlLNGgsA@00:41:53]
- Bought or software-built lists get tired because rivals use them too. [YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ@00:23:44]
- Begin charging only once referrals start arriving, then raise the price about 20% every five clients (80% off, 60% off, 40% off in blocks of five). [POD-230819-06@00:23:23]
- For beginners: start with outbound and keep a baseline of content (a post every two to three days on the outreach platform), because prospects look you up first. [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@05:39:36], [POD-0782@00:08:18]
- In small industries concentrated at the top, constant conference attendance is among the most effective methods. [LS--HbwUdqUcEs@02:09:39]
- Enterprise buyers are reached mostly by less scalable routes through a trusted champion; selling to them is the same conversation repeated roughly ten times up a chain. [LS-_ooj0V4hpgw@01:23:56], [LS-0RjIw9M1j28@01:10:00]

Diagnostics

- To someone wanting more clients, first ask whether they do 100 reach-outs or spend $100 on ads every day (per person working, for 90 days). [YT-UR9dO1jZemE@00:03:28]

## 2. Tactics and scripts

Starting lists and first customers

- Build the warm list from three sources: email contacts, followers you can DM, phone contacts; start on the platform with most people, finish it, move to the next. [POD-0571@00:00:59], [YT-w7g08dVTwaE@00:00:00], [POD-230819-06@00:09:36]
- "Declaration of business" post to your network, offering something free that helps them (his led to his first 20 paying clients). [YT-5cOwh-8scu8@00:04:29], [POD-0568@00:07:00]
- The "Rule of 300" for people without an audience: post, comment, then message those who reacted; four lukewarm sources are post likers, post commenters, people who liked your comments and people who replied to them. [YT-HsQeQM1jUeg@00:05:50], [YT-HsQeQM1jUeg@00:07:44]
- Rough yield: 100 thoughtful comments give at least 100 likes or replies to follow up; engagement compounds so that within 7-30 days outbound can be entirely engagers. [YT-HsQeQM1jUeg@00:09:24], [POD-0764@00:06:21]
- Free first-customer deal: cap at five, three terms (use it, give feedback, review if deserved); frame it as "you are helping me", never "you owe me". [YT-w7g08dVTwaE@00:03:12], [YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ@00:05:07]
- Free work pays four ways: customer, review, referral, or feedback that improves you. [YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ@00:04:32]
- Open by asking whether they know anyone who wants the result, not by pitching; describe a past client's struggle and result first. [YT-3ktgTBNlg7M@00:07:45], [POD-230819-06@00:13:40], [YT-w7g08dVTwaE@00:05:17]
- Ask a no-based question ("would you be opposed to...") and use yes/no questions where both answers have a prepared next step. [YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ@00:19:04], [YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ@00:18:02]
- Follow up up to three times (daily for three days or until reply; weekly for physical mail). [POD-230819-06@00:10:23]

Cold scripts and cadence

- BDR call: "we are calling gyms about common struggles and offering material from our private client work free", then book with a colleague; the SDS call gives about 30 minutes of real value and 15 minutes reserved to reveal the paid service. [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:12:34], [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:15:18]
- Email first, use the silence as the reason to call; expect 2-3 conversations before a high-ticket sale. [POD-230819-10@00:21:27]
- Give each rep a fixed lead allotment and an absolute appointment quota (70 leads, 7 appointments); cadence in the example: twice on day 1, twice on day 2, once on day 7. [POD-230819-10@00:28:40], [POD-230819-10@00:33:32]
- Re-run a worked list after 3-6 months (they missed it, the timing was wrong, or things changed); "no for now" gets a pulse check, meme or one-liner every 3-6 months. [POD-230819-10@00:22:13], [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@02:25:13]
- A meme was his highest-response message to ghosted pipeline (about 200 sent, roughly 5-10 re-engagements). [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@01:11:45]
- "I'm busy, call me later" means the reason is too small for the ask: enlarge the reason, shrink the time ask (60-120 seconds, not 15 minutes). [LS-QgmRlzh0Y7M@00:44:08]
- Scale email from 100 to about 5,000 a day with warmed-up multiple domains and AI personalisation, replies handed to closers round-robin. [LS-TLpLmhWqKEU@01:36:55]

Inbound-lead calling and text

- Fresh opt-in lead: text the lead's number automatically, call at once; he says this converts about 50% (stage unclear in audio). [POD-0017@00:02:58]
- Local appointment from a Facebook lead: upbeat tone, first name as a question, name the source, give a landmark, ask what time works, offer to text the address. [YT-aFoMYz_jWcs@00:11:00], [YT-StVqS0jD7Ls@00:43:43]

Partners, influencers, enterprise

- Name your ten biggest potential partners and spend real money to reach them (a gift, a visit), or do 100 reach-outs a day; send referral partners business first, aim for 10-20. [YT-XwZH-lOKG9c@00:55:13], [YT-h6y0nYVZgwE@00:13:32]
- DM every new follower with a filter ("here for entertainment or want help?"); each "help" is a lead (his example: 800 followers a month, 5% close). [YT-6Fg4VXjRphQ@00:21:30], [YT-3yAiVjcImQ4@00:06:05]

## 3. Cases by kind of business

Physical products and e-commerce

- Robotic cotton-candy machines ($15,000, 50% margin): about 1 sale per 100 cold emails; he asked what stops 10,000 a day with setters feeding one closer. [LS-TLpLmhWqKEU@01:30:37], [POD-0908@00:44:30]
- Energy drinks handed out cold, 1,000 a day for about $500 (his estimates): pays back if enough recipients become buyers. [POD-0438@00:01:36]

Made-to-order and manufacturing

- Custom-apparel middleman (about $100K a month, 35% gross margin): college orders average only $1,200-1,500 a year; construction firms look about 25x more valuable, so re-aim the list. [LS-eW04CvHKvMo@02:03:32]
- 3D-render studio with few, very large clients: trade shows, conferences, trusted advisers, long-term brand. [LS-_ooj0V4hpgw@01:23:56]

Wholesale and resellers

- Adult day centre: stop citing rivals' kickbacks; treat physicians as affiliates, launch then integrate in their offices. [LS-I0nCuqu7Cac@03:08:16]

Local and home services

- Residential trash hauler (loss-making, $642K): drop HOA bids, go all-in on door-to-door, restructure the offer so first-transaction cash covers bin, first-quarter service and commission; rep pays $50 a sale; details in the recording. [YT-Wr6n_zNKvMk@00:00:00]
- Pressure-washing and gutters ($810K, subscriptions door to door): keep stacking subscriptions, hire commission door knockers (8-10 sales a day, $50-100 a sale), no online ads yet. [POD-0809@00:05:45]
- Auto detailer with 18 clients: knock on doors with flyers, 100 doors every Saturday. [POD-0636@00:10:40]
- Janitorial: offer and cash terms matter more than a lead magnet; local PPC and door-knocking for small buildings, LinkedIn outbound to managers for large. [LS--HbwUdqUcEs@02:11:34]

Agencies and B2B services

- Part-time web hosting agency ($3,500 a month): charge at least $500 a month and spend its weekly hours on one-to-one outreach with free work first. [POD-0892@00:07:19]
- Lead-gen agency for financial advisors (about $20K a month): too little volume and proof; sign clients on a trial with penalty. [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@03:49:16]
- YouTube-growth service on cold email, income swinging month to month: scale cold email toward 1M, fix own YouTube, add ads. [LS-lidFtb1Ga0o@00:45:25]
- Instagram-DM systems agency: push cold outbound volume, take only buyers who have sold before, make the first 30 days turnkey. [LS-lidFtb1Ga0o@01:23:52]
- Transaction-coordination firm ($1M): add outbound to high-volume agents, screened by production volume, and raise prices. [LS-lidFtb1Ga0o@01:31:13]
- Enterprise lab-software founder ($250K a year): his "luck" is a 1-in-100 cold-call process; far more dials plus a waived-fee structure; about $500 per dial at 1% (his math). [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@00:06:28]
- Data and regulatory consultancy ($15M, down from over $30M) and cybersecurity compliance firm ($3.6M, demand constrained): conferences, partners, public LinkedIn thought leadership, no webinars. [POD-0884@00:36:17], [POD-0894@00:25:07], [POD-0894@00:30:17]

Coaching and education

- Online fitness coach, about 75 outreaches and 2-4 posts daily, 4 customers in 5-6 weeks (about 1% response): judged on track. [LS-9OsUfwTTslc@01:12:27]
- Fishing-guide coach stuck at $1,000 a month: market is small and poor; decommoditise his own charter, charge 2-3x, then teach better-off guides. [LS-I0nCuqu7Cac@01:43:31]
- Mortgage broker niched on pilots: stop recruiting generic realtors (lower LTV), do pilot content and a community; about 25 outbound messages a day did not convert. [LS--HbwUdqUcEs@01:18:11]
- Hormozi's first online clients: free 12-week offer with a charity donation, then paying clients; the ladder took about a year. [POD-230819-06@00:04:49], [YT-HsQeQM1jUeg@00:01:47], [POD-0533@00:16:06]
- Bookkeeper afraid to promote: 4 hours every evening of content plus about 10,000 outreaches, aiming for 10-20 clients in six months; also about 1,000 B2B partners (HR and recruiting firms) with kickbacks instead of 10,000 cold contacts. [YT-0_Gf5v8DEMY@00:32:14], [YT-0_Gf5v8DEMY@00:35:04]
- Person who says 100 daily outreaches for years left them broke: the numbers do not add up; likely a poor offer and no real iteration; work free for reps and proof. [LS-32fVmwvLmTM@00:19:24]

## 4. Changes of mind and contradictions

- He used to believe only paid ads scale; outbound scales by adding people, and meeting a recruit whose firm sold about $10M a month by outbound (about 5x his own revenue then) broke that belief. [POD-0249@00:00:00], [YT-hnmBXTyMFKI@00:02:25]
- He thought cold outbound suited mostly higher-ticket B2B or recruiting affiliates for consumer goods; he now sees it working for direct-to-consumer too (energy-drink and door-to-door examples). Against that, the earlier rule that in theory the ticket should be at least about $15,000. [POD-0438@00:00:50], [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:06:30], [YT-Wr6n_zNKvMk@00:00:00]
- Ramp expectations disagree across recordings: 12 weeks expected, about 12 months actual; 30 sales a month by May (book) versus 45 by month 14; "3 months to payback" versus four months with one sale and an executive intervention. [POD-230819-10@00:04:27], [POD-230819-10@00:03:26], [YT-okA9Yt2KZuk@00:04:36], [POD-0826@00:11:12]
- Free offers: the canon treats costly free work as a strong magnet; here he says a giveaway does not suit cold outbound, and elsewhere that more free service tripled take rates. These differ by channel (giveaway versus free service). [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@03:36:36], [POD-230819-10@00:14:19]
- Realistic close rates range widely: 0.5-1% as normal, about 4% engagement, 15% in one small affiliate email test, and 20 of 100 for personal video. [YT-nrounb8NlFQ@00:37:19], [POD-230819-10@00:26:23], [POD-230819-10@00:27:10]
