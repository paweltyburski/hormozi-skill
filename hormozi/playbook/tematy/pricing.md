# pricing

The canon already has Price to Value Discrepancy, the Virtuous Cycle of Price, pricing far above market, Implied Guarantee and performance pricing, supply and demand, the discount rules, Decoy Pricing, Feature Downsell, the Continuity Bonus, Pay Less Now or Pay More Later, financing and gross profit. The recordings add: close rate as a pricing signal, the profit arithmetic of raising and cutting, exact steps for raising prices (new and existing customers), capacity, peak and speed pricing, tier spacing, billing cadence, price points by buyer type, and many coached cases in trades, agencies, clinics, restaurants and e-commerce.

## Rules and thresholds

Close rate and the price test
1. Healthy close rate is about 30-40%; above the mid-40s is probably too high, above 50% likely underpriced, 70-80% usually means price could double or triple. [POD-0822@00:22:06] [YT-sBJppqCeFGI@00:03:07]
2. Ladder: 80%+ = probably 3-4x room in price, 60-80% = probably 2-3x, 50-60% = probably 1.5-2x, 40-50% = probably 1.25-1.5x, 30-40% fair; under 30% get better at selling before any cut. [YT-A_tx40lNpf8@00:00:46] [YT-ZuJryiwxjDw@00:31:52] [YT-A_tx40lNpf8@00:02:22]
3. Per his pricing guide, each 10 points of close rate above 35% leaves room for a 25-50% rise; two yeses in three suggests about 2.5x headroom; at ~80% close and full capacity, test double or triple, changing only the number. [LS-tnDC4ZJ0oy8@00:44:47] [POD-0894@00:06:08] [LS-I0nCuqu7Cac@04:05:03]
4. Keep raising while conversion times price (or units times gross profit) still grows; falling sales velocity alone is no reason to stop. Example: 10% at $10,000 earns $1,000 a call, 30% at $5,000 earns $1,500, so test low to high. [YT-41EvCgwPrDc@00:21:09] [YT-BMF2fWHyDrg@00:26:06] [YT-SmiOK8Yun4s@00:26:41]
5. Do not judge a rise on the first few calls; know baseline and wait for a meaningful number. [YT-oRMG_HpOAN4@00:24:10]

Profit arithmetic
6. Per 1% gain, price moves profit about 4x, churn 2x, acquisition cost 1x (other tellings differ: section 5). [POD-0139@00:02:41] [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@00:51:34]
7. Worked example (100 customers at $100 a month, $8,000 cost, 33% churn): doubling price lifts profit $2,000 to $12,000; doubling customers only to $4,000. Even if doubling price loses a third of sales, profit about triples. [POD-0443@00:01:32] [POD-0443@00:06:40] [YT-_ArQlwPvGUA@00:03:34]
8. Lower margin means bigger effect: at 20% net, +20% price doubles profit, +50% gives 3.5x, +100% 6x; at ~6% net a 6% rise roughly doubles it. [YT-Gy-RmpRif-I@00:00:00] [YT-BMF2fWHyDrg@00:50:26]
9. Cuts cost more than they look: at a 40% margin a cut needs double the units; a 20% revenue loss at 40% margin halves profit; at 15% margin a 15% discount is working for free. Before cutting price to cure churn, understand benchmarks and expectations. [YT-BZQtuK-ucDM@00:07:42] [YT-tdLLGKoBojo@00:45:02] [POD-0218@00:03:30] [POD-0270@00:04:43]
10. Services: Hormozi requires 80%+ gross margin in his own businesses (price 5x cost; small owners often make the 2-3x markup mistake). On $100 monthly cost: $333 gives 70%, $500 gives 80%, $1,000 gives 90%. [POD-0488@00:22:21] [YT-A_tx40lNpf8@00:36:12]
11. Targets: he wants about 97.5% gross rather than 80-90%; he'd target 30%+ net for a service firm at 13-14% and pays little attention to industry averages; home service about 30%, niche specialist 50%+. [POD-0442@00:01:42] [POD-0891@00:13:34] [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@00:48:20]
12. Value caps price: in his examples a fee of $250,000 for $1M of value created, or $10M for $50M of net worth, is reasonable; at most about 30% of value delivered; price may be up to 100x cost if the buyer still gets a bargain. [POD-0518@00:22:12] [LS-IG9IWQVmxqk@00:35:08] [POD-0581@00:01:56]
13. Cost-plus is almost always worst and removes the incentive to cut cost; price on willingness to pay and aim past the peak close rate. [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@04:47:12] [YT-BMF2fWHyDrg@00:25:11] [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@05:36:09]

Tiers
19. Each tier about 5-10x the one below, about 20% take it, each should roughly double revenue; after a $1,000 product the next rung is $5,000-10,000, not 1.5x. [YT-ZuJryiwxjDw@00:08:59] [LS-32fVmwvLmTM@00:56:26] [YT-ZuJryiwxjDw@00:17:23]
20. Tiers close together are basically one decision ($100 vs $120; $3,000 vs $4,000 a month); use up to four with a real spread. 21. An anchor works only if the rep truly sells the premium first. About 1 in 5 prospects will pay 5x or more. For an ordinary service a higher price can raise close rate by raising belief you can deliver; for risky personal-outcome buys, buyers read high price as lower risk. Billing and structure
22. Churn by cadence (ProfitWell study, as he cites): ~10.7% monthly, 5% quarterly, 2% annual; keep monthly until churn is low; match billing to the value cycle. [YT-41EvCgwPrDc@00:07:13] [LS-noE1MQPMvAc@01:22:12] [LS-_ooj0V4hpgw@00:39:46]
23. Annual plan typically about 16% below monthly (ten months for twelve), advised for one caller above monthly lifetime value ($59.99 at 18% churn is ~$300, offer $399). [YT-41EvCgwPrDc@00:24:45] [POD-0827@00:21:00]
24. Four-week billing gives 13 cycles, +8.3% revenue and ~+41% profit at 20% margin; a visible 3% processing fee adds ~30% profit at 10% margin. [POD-250819-07@00:18:33] [POD-250819-07@00:19:35]
25. Standalone-to-continuity price ratio sets the split: 1.33x gives 50% continuity, 1.66x 60%, 2x 70%, 2.33x 80%, 2.66x 90%. [POD-250819-07@00:10:26]

27. A bigger up-front fee means lower churn; buyers resist monthly fees more than one-time ones; a defined front end can typically be 3-4x a recurring fee; recurring stays sticky if its value is 3-4x its price. [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@02:34:56] [LS-TLpLmhWqKEU@00:48:57] [POD-0536@00:05:40] [POD-0931@00:13:47]

## Tactics and scripts

1. Raising sequence: survey customers (too high to consider, too low to trust, a stretch you would buy, a bargain), decide, tell the team, existing customers, then the list with urgency; allow two months to settle. [POD-0443@00:10:38] [POD-0443@00:23:20] [POD-0443@00:09:46]
2. Raise for new customers first, grandfather existing ones, announce the coming rise to clear the pipeline. [POD-0443@00:09:46] [POD-0583@00:29:34]
3. Existing-customer letter: centred on the customer, lists improvements, hand-signed, PS offering help if hardship; quote the rise per week or day; grandfather to a stated date; or a temporary discount equal to the rise for 3-6 months. [POD-0443@00:20:16] [YT-Gy-RmpRif-I@00:04:40] [YT-oRMG_HpOAN4@00:23:19] [YT-QTZsh3BgOwY@00:08:11]
4. Gym legacy members: meet each personally, release them from the old deal, hold the price, offer flexible billing instead. [POD-0029@00:02:57] [POD-0029@00:04:13] [POD-0029@00:07:21]
5. Old price only if they prepay; or tell past customers ahead and offer the old price if they buy now. [POD-0850@00:27:00] [POD-0820@00:39:22]
