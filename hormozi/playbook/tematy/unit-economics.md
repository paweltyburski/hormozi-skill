# unit-economics

The canon already covers LTGP:CAC (3:1 floor, 10:1+ target), CAC, Gross Profit (80%+ for services), Payback Period, the Three Levels of Advertising, the 30-day credit rule, the churn-to-lifetime formula, revenue churn and "fix the model before the ads". The recordings add: a required ratio that moves with manual steps, the "ceiling" arithmetic (new customers divided by churn), churn benchmarks by product type and tenure, price-lever multiples, test-budget rules, ways to compute CAC and LTV from bookkeeping, and many worked cases outside the canon's service frame (physical products, subscription boxes, clinics, home services, agencies, software, education). Several figures disagree between recordings; section 5 lists them.

## Rules and thresholds

Ratios and scaling
1. LTV on gross margin should be at least 3:1 to CAC as a rule of thumb; he says "our jobs" (possibly gyms) average about 5.5:1 on the front end alone. He personally ignores businesses below 10:1, and calls 10:1 "good enough for growth", ideally 20-40:1 but not needed upfront. [POD-0176@00:03:58] [POD-0345@00:10:24] [POD-0239@00:05:39]
2. The minimum ratio rises with manual steps in lead generation, conversion and delivery: all automated 3:1, one 6:1, two 9:1, three 12:1. New hires perform worse than the best one, so scaling people is lumpy. [YT-i7bLRKwKSms@00:15:01] [YT-A_tx40lNpf8@00:06:42] [YT-A_tx40lNpf8@00:07:42]
3. A service model is ready for more spend when the first 30 days return about 4:1 to 5:1 over acquisition cost; software about 1:1 within 3 months. [POD-0226@00:10:55]
4. Sustained maintenance periods run at 5-10:1; 30:1 up to 101-201:1 windows are short. Colder audiences lower the ratio but can raise total return (as an illustration, about 20:1, maybe 5:1, maybe 3:1 at 100x spend). [YT-reisEL_D7xc@00:29:11] [YT-reisEL_D7xc@01:19:30]
5. Absolute profit beats ratio: 3:1 on $1,000,000 earns more than 10:1 on $10,000; one $10,000 sale at $8,000 profit equals 400 sales at $25. [POD-0226@00:15:29] [YT-ZuJryiwxjDw@00:23:02]
6. Prefer paying more for a much bigger customer: $5,000 to acquire $45,000 beats $1,000 for $5,000. [POD-0477@00:23:37]

The 30-day cash window
7. Test a new ad up to twice the profit collected from a customer in 30 days (do not judge a campaign before spending 2x target CAC); kill it at 1x if it brings no leads. [POD-230819-12@00:36:26] [POD-0933@00:01:07] [YT-n6SHLmKcY0E@00:52:13]
8. Cash-test variants: 30-day net free cash above 2x the cost to acquire plus fulfil; 30-day gross profit at least 2x CAC; in the live launch, above twice (CAC + COGS). [POD-0436@00:02:24] [POD-251114-07@00:01:45] [LS-6_CCutkM11g@00:12:07]
9. Why 30 days: typically as long as most small businesses can handle on cash flow, matching the interest-free period of cards. Faster collection multiplies reinvestment (a 7-day cycle repeats about 4 times a month). [YT-i7bLRKwKSms@00:07:50] [YT-k-3PoOT4vOM@00:20:54] [POD-251114-08@00:02:50]
10. A day-one break-even rule caps the base at new customers divided by churn; relaxing to day 30 or 60 lifts the cap and reaches a 10-50x larger market. [POD-0198@00:10:11] [POD-0198@00:12:44]
11. Lower the barrier to entry only as far as retention and stacked recurring revenue absorb the lost upfront cash; a lead magnet may lose money if it lowers the cost of a paying customer. [POD-0103@00:01:33] [YT-7NMH1oAkgLY@00:03:44]

Ceiling, churn, retention
12. Largest recurring base = new signups per month / monthly churn; hypothetical max revenue follows from new customers per month and lifetime value. Compare today's revenue with it to see whether the business will grow, shrink or stay flat. [POD-0082@00:00:46] [POD-0345@00:01:05] [YT-QGcjweehrvU@00:15:54]
13. The lower churn goes, the more each point is worth: 10 signups a month gives 100 members at 10%, 200 at 5%, 333 at 3%; double signups and halve churn quadruples the ceiling. [POD-0082@00:08:14] [POD-0082@00:12:03]
14. Churn counts only the original cohort; new signups never enter it. A flat business has churn = new customers / current customers. [POD-0082@00:10:32] [POD-251114-11@00:03:23] [POD-0345@00:08:51]
15. Revenue retention: share of January 1 revenue still arriving December 31; for B2B he says you probably want above 80% a year on the starting group. Only voluntary churn signals a bad business; structural churn caps retention (about 70% a year best case at 3% monthly). [YT-uYds0zcAFWM@00:08:47] [YT-A_tx40lNpf8@00:43:38] [YT-3fsJFUvA6Ts@00:00:46] [YT-CoPs-Bk8M9Y@00:03:29]

Monthly churn benchmarks: advice to one caller's book summary app: under 3% to get big, ideally 80%+ annual retention; software 80%+ annual retention (under 2% monthly) was advice to a caller's fence app; services about 1% floor; paid communities 5-10% good, about 20% probably around average, 50% means price is too high; on Skool average monthly retention is about 80% (about 20% churn). 20. Keeping a customer three more months can cost about $60 of staff time against about $1,000 of acquisition; onboarding can add two to three months of LTV. [YT-yPDQCfrwh8E@00:04:48] [YT-afbP6sB_Atc@00:02:47]

Margin and price
21. Services: for his own service businesses he requires gross margin above 80%, 90-95% preferred, each step doubling profit per dollar of delivery; he also wants about 97.5%. Small owners often mark up 2-3x (50-67%); above 80% means 5x cost. [POD-0839@00:09:14] [POD-0442@00:01:42] [POD-0488@00:22:21]
22. Net margin cannot exceed gross; he doesn't pay much attention to industry-average net margins and would target 30%+ (home services minimum 30%, niche specialist 50%+). [YT-A_tx40lNpf8@00:33:54] [POD-0891@00:13:34] [LS-NcpyWPT90RE@00:48:20]
23. Lifting gross margin from 66% to 80% can turn a typical 12% net into 26%; a $20M business at 50% earns what a $100M one at 10% does. [YT-9xAMe0QBFhU@00:02:42] [YT-3fsJFUvA6Ts@00:07:19]
24. Per 1% improvement, price moves profit about 4x, churn 2x, acquisition cost 1x. At 20% net margin, +20% price doubles profit. [POD-0139@00:02:41] [YT-Gy-RmpRif-I@00:00:00]
25. A price cut on a 40% margin needs double the unit sales to hold profit; keep raising price while units times gross profit per unit keeps rising; judge a rise only after enough conversations. [YT-BZQtuK-ucDM@00:07:42] [YT-BMF2fWHyDrg@00:26:06] [YT-oRMG_HpOAN4@00:24:10]
26. When automation cuts delivery cost, keep the price; customers' price expectations adjust slowly. A refund rate above margin is a death spiral. [YT-9q5ojtkqsBs@00:20:58] [POD-0807@00:03:09]

Customers, hires, capital
27. Within an industry CAC is nearly a commodity; the firm with customers worth 5-100x more outspends the rest. If CAC grows faster than CPMs, suspect negative word of mouth; CAC generally rises over time. [YT-A_tx40lNpf8@00:46:38] [YT-Uki3IUkUu7Q@00:12:26] [YT-i7bLRKwKSms@00:17:22]
28. Referral levels he cites: 0.4 per Gym Launch client; 0.3 cuts CAC about 30%; engineer toward 1.1; 25-30% word-of-mouth customers is a strong sign. [POD-0267@00:00:48] [YT-Uki3IUkUu7Q@00:10:16] [POD-0678@00:13:39] [POD-0546@00:17:01]
29. Profit is a power law: top 20% of customers give 80% of profit, top 4% give 64%. Keep any customer under 20% of revenue; he personally prefers 5%. [YT-ZuJryiwxjDw@00:04:29] [YT-HKbFUWJwEG0@00:38:30]
30. Below about $1,000 lifetime value very few people make real money; a service that cannot afford $100-$200 per customer has a business problem, not a marketing problem. [YT-BSwJQsogah0@00:07:45] [YT--UvmyixFszw@00:00:00]
31. Value is profit times multiple: +$1M profit at 10x adds $10M value but about $0.5M take-home. Multiples: under $5M revenue 2-3x EBITDA, $10M-$100M might get 6-8x, over $100M 12-15x. [YT-LMlbWtUFa4E@00:00:50] [YT-al_WXFDbN8I@00:04:27]

## Tactics and scripts

1. Quick LTV: lifetime sales / customers ever sold; monthly price / churn; or, when flat, monthly revenue / new customers a month. [YT-FXzDLLdxsCk@00:00:46] [YT-FXzDLLdxsCk@00:02:16] [YT-FXzDLLdxsCk@00:04:50]
2. Quick lifetime gross profit: last year's gross profit / customers sold; max CAC one third of it, recovered in 30 days. Product LTGP = gross profit x transactions; service LTGP = gross profit / churn. [YT-reisEL_D7xc@00:29:58] [POD-251114-11@00:04:10]
3. Lifespan from one month of data: if 9 of 10 starting customers remain after 30 days, churn is 10%, average stay 10 months. [YT-jzKpAtzKQ54@00:11:44]
4. Be conservative: overstate CAC, understate LTV; compute CAC over a year, or trailing 6-12 months for lumpy revenue. [POD-0850@01:19:09] [YT-jzKpAtzKQ54@00:10:10] [POD-0850@01:17:58]
5. Build CAC from payroll: payroll / engaged leads x leads per customer; owners usually undercount (believed $200, actual $500). [POD-251114-24@00:21:38] [POD-251114-10@00:00:00]
6. Pull cash forward: upsell, liquidation offer, week-one special, ancillary products, affiliates, setup fee. [YT-Cr0KdqZ954c@00:09:26] [YT-QGcjweehrvU@00:13:47]
7. Offer annual prepay as an option: 10-15% take it, and 10% uptake roughly doubles upfront cash. Advice to one caller, not a general rule: with 18% churn (about 5 months x $60 = $300), make annual $300 or more, e.g. $399. [YT-oRMG_HpOAN4@00:47:02] [POD-0827@00:21:00]
8. Bill every four weeks (13 cycles, +8.3% revenue); add an annual renewal fee of 1-3x the monthly price. [POD-250819-07@00:18:33] [YT-pLhQOYMGa88@00:19:08]
9. Recover payments: charge on paydays and retry declines the same day (recovers about a third); waive the fee for a second payment method; take a backup card (he claims churn can halve). [POD-250819-06@00:12:23] [POD-250819-07@00:20:28] [POD-0186@00:02:24]
10. Track the month people leave and over-deliver the month before; count unrecovered declines as cancellations. [POD-0018@00:06:21]
11. Find the activation point by comparing early actions of retained and lost customers. Measure churn only after a trial converts. [POD-0167@00:09:23] [POD-0518@00:07:45] [POD-0902@00:21:11]
12. Raise price 20% every five customers until conversion times price falls. [YT-reisEL_D7xc@00:03:55]
13. Pick the front end by LTV:CAC times market size; if the best-ratio offer's market is much smaller, put the larger-ticket offer behind it. [YT-TooAB8Ow6cQ@03:14:33] [POD-0782@00:23:53]
14. Find which customers become permanent and advertise only to them even if cost per customer rises. [YT-k-3PoOT4vOM@00:37:05]
15. Size a referral discount from expected conversion (three names, one in three converts: up to about one CAC). Cap affiliate payouts by the target ratio. [YT-qpQvdBFW_yI@00:09:33] [POD-230819-17@00:11:53]
16. Fund ads on a credit card: if 30-day net cash exceeds CAC the customer cost nothing; raise the limit by early repayment or another card. [POD-0438@00:09:39] [POD-251114-13@00:00:48]
17. Test attribution by switching ads off; in one case Hormozi read an LTV:CAC saying the main channel loses money in a clearly profitable business as likely an attribution issue, though the agency not performing was the alternative. [YT-HGZOxBfnF-E@00:04:20] [YT-HGZOxBfnF-E@00:02:34]

## Changes of mind and contradictions

1. Minimum ratio: as a rule of thumb 3:1 or higher in most recordings against a floor scaling with manual steps (6:1, 9:1, 12:1), tagged as a change of mind; another version asks 20:1+ for manual models, at least 15:1. [POD-0176@00:03:58] [YT-A_tx40lNpf8@00:06:42] [YT-uYds0zcAFWM@00:12:22]
2. Stage targets differ: 10:1 personal threshold, 4:1-5:1 for services in 30 days, 5:1 for a $33M company, and for one caller's coaching funnel 7:1 sounded fine and 10:1 with a back end, so scale (not a general rule). [POD-0345@00:10:24] [POD-0226@00:10:55] [POD-0884@00:12:14] [LS-noE1MQPMvAc@01:19:08]
3. LTV as revenue or gross profit: some define it on gross profit, others compute it on revenue and say so. [POD-230819-03@00:04:00] [YT-BSwJQsogah0@00:02:33] [YT-aBPWCdaJJqA@00:20:03]
4. The 30-day test: canon compares 30-day gross profit with 2x CAC; recordings put COGS inside the bracket or speak of net free cash; elsewhere longer windows appear (a vertical SaaS with 12-month payback). [LS-6_CCutkM11g@00:12:07] [POD-0436@00:02:24] [POD-0870@00:19:08]
5. Lever sizes: price 4x, churn 2x, acquisition 1x per 1%; elsewhere price 6x against churn 2x, later "3x" (unclear in audio); the multiples differ between statements; doubling price is sixfold in one genie game, about threefold if a third of sales are lost. [POD-0139@00:02:41] [YT-41EvCgwPrDc@00:00:00] [POD-0443@00:06:40]
6. Churn goals: aim 1-2% monthly; services cannot go below about 1%; small-business clients hard to get under 10%, and obsessing past diminishing returns delays growth. [YT-3P1XjUvo1b4@00:08:25] [YT-XC_lklN9KmE@00:00:46] [POD-0226@00:00:55]
7. Gym Launch return quoted as 36:1 lifetime on about $3M, 25-27x in one conversation (relayed by the host via Dan Henry), and 100:1 in year one, with four windows in his life above 30:1. [YT-q32-l3Yoqg4@00:01:43] [POD-0430@00:02:32] [YT-A_tx40lNpf8@00:10:59]
