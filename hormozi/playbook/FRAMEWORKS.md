# Frameworks

Register of the named frameworks and models taught in the four courses (Offers = OC, Leads = LC, Money Models = MM, Scaling = SC). One canonical name per framework; other names are listed as aliases. Entries are ordered by topic (taxonomy order), then by how central they are. Citations give the atom ID and timestamp.

## Grand Slam Offer
aliases: GSO
topic: offers
what: An offer packaged so completely that prospects cannot compare it with alternatives and decide on value rather than price. Often the issue when conversion is weak.
parts: Right market; price; value (Value Equation); problem-solution list; delivery mix; scarcity and urgency; bonuses; guarantees; name.
key rules: Taught in this order: market, price, value, then the enhancers. Repackage what you already deliver instead of rebuilding it. Gains in response, close rate and price multiply. List every perceived problem and answer each one.

## Value Equation
aliases: —
topic: offers
what: Model of what makes an offer feel valuable; used to audit and improve every offer element and to classify objections.
parts: Dream outcome (up); perceived likelihood of achievement (up); time delay to first and final result (down); effort and sacrifice (down).
key rules: All four are perceptions, so results must be communicated. He now focuses more on driving time and effort toward zero than on a bigger promise. Fast beats free. Every perceived problem is one of four flavours: not worth it, will not work for me, too hard, too slow.

## Trim and Stack
aliases: —
topic: offers
what: Selection step that turns a long list of possible deliverables into a high-value, high-margin offer.
parts: List delivery options per problem; rate each on value and on cost; cut low-value items; keep high-value ones; check every problem is covered; cap high-cost items.
key rules: Low-value items go even when cheap. Typically no more than one or two high-cost items per solution set (his habit; keep accountability-type items when execution is the main failure point). Target 80%+ gross margin for services.

## Bonus Checklist
aliases: —
topic: offers
what: Rules for choosing and presenting bonuses so they raise perceived value and prompt a decision today.
parts: Always offer bonuses; benefit in the name; link to the prospect's problem; proof; picture of the result; stated and justified price; tools and templates over more training; one obstacle per bonus; solve the next need.
key rules: Present core offer and price first, then stack bonuses. Bonus value should exceed the core. Add scarcity or urgency to the bonuses. Give bonuses, not discounts, when re-asking for the sale.

## Unconditional Guarantee
aliases: money-back guarantee, two no-risk options
topic: offers
what: Refund for any reason within a period; removes the buyer's risk and works as a close.
parts: Reframe (buying is how you decide fully informed); time window; simple refund mechanism; confidence close.
key rules: Feature it prominently or reserve it for hesitant prospects. Refunds are rare with hands-on delivery (2 in 4,000+). Run the maths: more sales usually outweigh more refunds. Place some of the value after the refund window.
taught in: [OC-08@00:04:54], [OC-08@00:06:30], [OC-08@00:07:18], [OC-08@00:08:12], [MM-11@00:08:18]
related: Conditional Guarantee, Stacking Guarantees

## Conditional Guarantee
aliases: X-in-Y-or-Z guarantee
topic: offers
what: Guarantee that pays out only if the customer met stated terms and still missed the result.
parts: Ten structures: outsized refund; free service until result; modified service; credit toward another programme; owner's personal service; hotel and airfare; wage payment; release from contract; delayed second payment; first outcome.
key rules: State both the condition and the consequence. Make conditions the activation-point behaviours. Make use of your recommended vendors one of the conditions of a keep-serving guarantee, so their commissions pay for the free work. Avoid release-of-service where results are slow.

related: Unconditional Guarantee, Activation Points, Stacking Guarantees

## Scarcity
aliases: Scarcity and Urgency
topic: offers
what: Limiting quantity so prospects decide sooner.
parts: Limited seats or units; limited bonuses; never-again or one-of-a-kind items.
key rules: Base the limit on real capacity and say it (spots left, client cap, per-cohort cap). Physical bonuses make limits believable. When demand rises, cut supply and raise price. Naming a group after its cap also aids retention.

## Penny Gap
aliases: free vs one cent
topic: offers
what: Drop in take-up when a price moves from zero to even one cent.
parts: Free offer; smallest paid offer.
key rules: About 9x more people take the free version. Free leads close at the same rate as paid ones; filter with friction.

## Price to Value Discrepancy
aliases: selling dollars at a discount
topic: pricing
what: The gap between perceived value and price that makes people buy, stay and refer.
parts: Price; perceived value; the gap between them.
key rules: When value falls to or below price, customers cancel or never return. Widen the gap by raising value; price can only fall to zero. Lack of repeat business and referrals probably signals an average product, he says.
taught in: [OC-03@00:01:46], [OC-03@00:02:43], [OC-03@00:05:17], [OC-07@00:03:43]
related: Value Equation, Virtuous Cycle of Price, Goodwill (Price-Value-Cost)

## Virtuous Cycle of Price
aliases: Vicious Cycle of Price
topic: pricing
what: Explains why premium pricing reinforces itself and market-average pricing spirals into more-for-less.
parts: Client side: emotional investment, perceived value, results, lower demandingness, more revenue to fulfil. Business side: profit, self-perception, impact, service level, sales conviction.
key rules: Do not price from competitors' average. Price so far above the market that it reads as another category. Raise price where client effort drives results.

## Implied Guarantee
aliases: performance-based pricing, value-based pricing, pay-for-performance
topic: pricing
what: The customer pays only when the outcome happens; no downside risk for the customer and uncapped upside for the seller.
parts: Per unit (sale, show, pound); share of revenue; share of growth over a baseline; profit or gross-profit share.
key rules: Qualify prospects strictly because you front the cost. Use only if results are consistently good.

## Goodwill (Price-Value-Cost)
aliases: consumer surplus
topic: pricing
what: Links value, price and cost to referrals and profit.
parts: Value minus price = goodwill; price minus cost = profit.
key rules: Large goodwill produces referrers, zero produces silence, negative produces detractors. Raise value so price can rise with goodwill intact. Lead magnets force goodwill because the price is zero.

parts: After the yes, offer two options: payment plan without guarantee, or full prepayment with guarantee.
key rules: Ask only after the buying decision. Most choose prepayment.

## Money Model
aliases: Money Models
topic: money-models

## Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA)
aliases: Client-Financed Acquisition, Customer-Financed Acquisition, CFA
topic: money-models
what: State in which a new customer yields more gross profit within 30 days than it cost to win them, so customers finance growth.
parts: CAC; 30-day gross profit; reinvestment of the surplus.
key rules: A good lifetime ratio is not enough if the cash arrives slowly. Thirty days matches interest-free credit. Reach it with upfront fees and early upsells. After that, operations become the constraint.

## Four Types of Offers
aliases: Four Types of Offers (Attraction, Upsell, Downsell, Continuity), four prongs
topic: money-models
what: The building blocks of a money model, each with one job.
parts: Attraction offers (turn strangers into customers, liquidate CAC); upsell offers (maximise profit); downsell offers (convert those who said no); continuity offers (recurring payments, stable cash flow).
key rules: Not every business needs all four. Add offers in layers, not all at once; each adds operational drag. Several offers can sit in one type.

## Win Your Money Back
aliases: challenge with money-back, Make Everybody a Winner
topic: money-models
what: Attraction offer: the customer pays up front and earns the money back, as cash or credit, by meeting set criteria; works as paid onboarding.
parts: Goal (result, actions or both); criteria that are trackable, realistic, promotional and include sales appointments; credit toward a larger back-end offer.
key rules: Use where the customer must act and refunds are under 5%. Back end ~5x the deposit. Spread credit over a long commitment, never free months. At the midpoint, credit everyone regardless of outcome. Refund anyone who asks.

## Free Giveaways
aliases: giveaway, scholarship offer, Refer a Winner
topic: money-models
what: Attraction offer: a chance to win a grand prize generates qualified leads, who are then sold a partial prize.
parts: Grand prize and promotional offer; entry with contact details and eligibility questions; entry deadline; live announcement; promotional offer to all other entrants on a new deadline.
key rules: Prize = your main product at its stated value. Entry 3-7 days with daily benefit-plus-proof messages. Discount 10-30% of gross margin. Prize must match your price level. Cap entries to contact capacity. In recurring businesses spread the discount, no free months.

## Decoy Offers
aliases: free vs premium offer
topic: money-models
what: Attraction offer: a free or cheap basic version is advertised and then presented beside a much stronger premium version.
parts: Decoy (stripped of the costliest features); premium (more features, support, guarantee); side-by-side presentation.
key rules: Advertise the outcome, not the inclusions. 70-80% should choose premium; if not, widen the contrast. Lead with the premium or use the "free stuff or lasting results?" question. Give decoy takers a small extra and upsell later.

## Buy X Get Y Free
aliases: BOGO, buy one get two free
topic: money-models
what: Attraction offer that expresses a discount as free units or free time, so the customer still buys units at full price.
parts: Paid quantity X; free quantity Y (same item, different cheaper items, or extra months).
key rules: The more free relative to paid, the better it converts. Raise price or add a high-margin extra first. Fits one-time buyers and prepayment. Size beyond usual customer lifespan. Limit long prepay to ~10% of the recurring base and ring-fence the cash.

## Pay Less Now or Pay More Later
aliases: Pay Less Now, pay-later offer
topic: money-models
what: Attraction offer: start for $0 with full price charged later if satisfied, or pay a lower price now with bonuses.
parts: Pay-later (card on file, implied guarantee); pay-now (20-50% or more off plus bonuses, no guarantee); upsell.
key rules: Offer pay-now only after they accept pay-later. Pay-later price about 3x pay-now. Promise a measurable yes/no result; cancellation only if they did the work. Expect ~10% cancellations. Balance the two with the size of the pay-now incentive.

## Upsell Offers
aliases: —
topic: money-models

## Classic Upsell
aliases: You Can't Have X Without Y, No-Sale Sale
topic: money-models
what: Give or discount one item and sell the item that naturally must accompany it.
parts: Front item (free or cheap); companion item; assumed close ("you don't want anything else, do you?").
key rules: A free front item stays free; encourage, never require. Choose companions with little delivery work. 80%+ should take it. Offer an alternative solution to those who refuse the core.

## Menu Upsell
aliases: Unsell, Prescription Close, A/B Close, Card on File Close
topic: money-models
what: Four-step upsell for businesses with several products and many transactions.
parts: Unsell what they do not need; prescribe what they need and exactly how to use it; ask A or B; offer the card on file.
key rules: Unsell low-margin items to build trust for high-margin ones. Nudge the A/B choice. When out of stock, take payment and set a delivery date you can beat. Script the unsells for staff.

## Anchor Upsell
aliases: Price Anchoring, premium anchor
topic: money-models
what: Show a very expensive version first so the main offer looks like a bargain; suits fewer, larger sales.
parts: Present anchor; get the gasp; rescue (ask if they care about the premium's secondary features); present main offer; ask how they will pay.
key rules: Anchor at 5-10x the main price, sold in earnest. Vary only secondary features. Always have a premium option; some will buy it.

## Rollover Upsell
aliases: credit rollover, Gift Card Play
topic: money-models
what: Upsell that credits an earlier purchase toward a larger one.
parts: Who (lapsed customers, upset customers, competitors' unhappy customers, current customers); what (a pricier offer); how the credit is applied (up front, spread, waived fee).
key rules: Next offer at least 4-5x the credit. Credit the least that works; never whole lifetime spend. Spread credit across a commitment, not as free periods. Optionally one-time, take it now (his preference). Offer a redo before a refund.

## Downsell Offers
aliases: Downsells

parts: Payment Plan Downsell (change how they pay); Trial with Penalty; Feature Downsell (change what they get).
key rules: Never the same thing for less. Treat a no as information. Trade, do not pressure. Every qualified prospect should leave having bought something.

## Payment Plan Downsell
aliases: Seesaw Downsell, Layaway
topic: money-models
what: Same product at the same or higher total, on easier terms, stepped down only as the buyer refuses.
parts: 1 plan price, discount for paying in full; 2 financing, credit card or layaway; 3 half now, half on payday; 4 desire check (1-10); 5 three payments; 6 even spread; 7 free trial with card.
key rules: Reward prepay instead of charging interest. Bill on paydays. Below 8 on desire, switch to a feature downsell. Keep the prepay discount open ~30 days. Check that paid-in-full sales do not fall.

## Trial with Penalty
aliases: free trial with terms, Rebate
topic: money-models
what: Downsell: the product is free while the customer meets set terms; they pay small fees for what they skip.
parts: Offer after a no; take ID and card; get agreement to stay if it works; explain and initial fees; mandatory check-ins (the sales meetings); upsell by outcome (liked, hated, unused).
key rules: Terms = actions that produce results plus promotion plus check-ins. Per-item fees, not all-or-nothing. Do not bill non-starters. Never call it a penalty. Works only where results need customer effort.

## Feature Downsell
aliases: remove-a-feature downsell
topic: money-models
what: The only downsell that lowers price, by changing what the customer receives.
parts: Less quantity; lower quality; a component removed (e.g. the guarantee); a cheaper alternative or DIY version.
key rules: First remove something valued for a small cut (pushes them back to the original); then restore it and remove low-value items for a big cut. After two refusals check desire; 8+ means go to payment plans. Close with "fair enough?". Barter is the only cut without removal.
