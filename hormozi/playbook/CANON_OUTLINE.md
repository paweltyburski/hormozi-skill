## offers
- Grand Slam Offer: an offer built from every component (market, price, value, problem-solution list, delivery, scarcity, urgency, bonuses, guarantees, name) so it is judged on value, not compared on price. [OC-01@00:10:07], [OC-01@00:09:21]
- Rule: weak conversion, slow rep ramp or few yeses many times point to the offer, not to marketing or sales skill. [OC-01@00:02:43]
- Principle: repackage what is already delivered; gains in response, close rate and price multiply. [OC-01@00:06:01], [OC-01@00:08:35]
- Case: bootcamp gyms replaced a $99/month membership with a ~$600 six-week challenge with guarantees and bonuses; break-even became ~30:1 in 30 days. [OC-01@00:03:58]
- Case: lead-gen agencies replaced a $1,500 retainer with setup fee plus pay-per-show; 0.5:1 became ~11:1 in 30 days. [OC-01@00:07:00]
- Value Equation: value = dream outcome x perceived likelihood / (time delay x effort and sacrifice); all are perceptions and must be communicated. [OC-04@00:00:49], [OC-04@00:04:59]
- Rule: cutting time delay and effort toward zero may matter more than a bigger promise; fast beats free. [OC-04@00:08:31], [OC-04@00:06:00]
- Example: CoolSculpting sells at $2,500-$5,000 against $10-$20/month workout programmes for the same outcome, on likelihood, speed and effort. [OC-04@00:07:36]
- Problem-solution list: dream outcome statement (result, time frame, without the main fear); each customer step yields problems in four flavours (not worth it, will not work for me, too hard, too slow); each is reversed into a solution. [OC-05@00:01:32], [OC-05@00:05:14], [OC-05@00:09:16]
- Trim and Stack: rate delivery options on value and cost, drop low-value ones, typically keep no more than one or two high-cost items. [OC-06@00:15:41]
- Bonus Checklist: core offer and price first, then named, priced, proven bonuses worth more than the core; give bonuses, not discounts, to close today. [OC-07@00:05:29], [OC-07@00:01:33]
- Four Levels of Bonus Mastery: partner products as bonuses, up to exclusive terms plus commission. [OC-07@00:10:34]
- Guarantees: four kinds; a strong one can lift sales 2-4x. Unconditional Guarantee (2 claims in 4,000+ sales); Conditional Guarantee ("X in Y or we do Z", ten structures); Anti-Guarantee (all sales final, with a reason); Stacking Guarantees. [OC-08@00:01:41], [OC-08@00:07:18], [OC-08@00:10:29], [OC-08@00:03:15], [OC-08@00:20:49]
- Rules: a guarantee that doubles refunds still nets more sales (117 against 95 in the worked example); long conditional terms attract long-stay clients; give the guarantee a vivid name. [OC-08@00:08:12], [OC-08@00:24:01], [OC-08@00:22:18]
- Scarcity: limits quantity (seats, bonuses, one-off editions), always from real capacity; urgency limits time. [OC-09@00:06:08], [OC-09@00:06:54]
- MAGIC Naming Formula: magnetic reason, avatar, goal, interval, container word; programmes named in weeks, bonuses in minutes. [OC-10@00:03:17], [OC-10@00:06:26]
- Fraternity Party Planner: any stated reason to act now beats none. [LC-03@00:35:42]
- Perceived Likelihood of Achievement: eleven proof levers (own results, similar people, reviews, credentials, data, endorsements, new mechanism, guarantee, pain description, demonstration). [LC-04@00:22:03]
- Perceived Hidden Costs: non-money costs (chores, sacrifices, risk, delay); ask non-buyers why, then remove them. [LC-04@00:34:38], [SC-03@00:10:31]
- Penny Gap: free pulls ~9x more than one cent; free leads closed at the same rate in a 20-gym test; filter with friction, not a weaker offer. [MM-03@00:04:53], [MM-03@00:09:56], [MM-03@00:09:01]
- Rule: a refused free offer is unwanted, not believed, or shown to the wrong people; a believable small promise out-pulls a big one. [MM-03@00:05:43], [MM-03@00:07:09]
- Rule: with nothing to sell, give something away free first, to learn and to collect proof. [SC-03@00:01:31]
- Rules: answer all perceived problems, since the one blocking a given prospect is unknown; publish average results by month as factual expectations instead of best cases. [OC-05@00:11:03], [LC-11@00:20:40]

## pricing
- Price to Value Discrepancy: people buy and stay only while perceived value exceeds price; widen the gap by raising value. [OC-03@00:01:46]
- Virtuous Cycle of Price: premium price raises client commitment, results, delivery budget and seller conviction; pricing off the market average spirals into more-for-less. [OC-03@00:07:04], [OC-03@00:05:17]
- Rule: price far above the market so it reads as another category. Case: Gym Launch $16,000 minimum against a $5,000 competitor. [OC-03@00:11:41], [OC-03@00:12:41]
- Principle: price signals quality and filters for commitment; it is one way to ethically get the client invested where they must do the work. [OC-03@00:10:02], [OC-03@00:13:45]
- Implied Guarantee: performance pricing (per unit, share of revenue, growth or profit); needs strict qualification and reliable results. [OC-08@00:18:24], [OC-08@00:21:08]
- Rule: when demand rises, cut supply and raise price. [OC-09@00:09:36]
- Rules: solving a more expensive problem supports a higher price; selling the expensive hands-on version first anchors the cheaper one. Case: See's Candies raised prices by about 5-6% every year, faster than inflation. [OC-04@00:03:07], [OC-06@00:04:28], [OC-03@00:11:41]
- Rule: move from free to paid in 20% steps every five clients, then keep raising while capacity is full. Case: Gym Launch package $6,000 to $50,000. [LC-04@00:37:01], [SC-04@00:17:00]
- Pre-Paid Post Guarantee: guarantee only with full prepayment, offered after the yes. [LC-04@00:38:53]
- Goodwill (Price-Value-Cost): value minus price = goodwill (referrals); price minus cost = profit. [LC-11@00:14:20]
- Rules: free or full price for the core; discount only a broken-off, familiar component, by 50% or more; 10-20% off changes no behaviour. [MM-03@00:05:43], [MM-03@00:20:28], [MM-03@00:14:27]
- Four Ways to Display a Discount: percent, amount, equivalent, crossed-out price; rotate them. [MM-03@00:15:34]
- Rules: a low absolute price works only when the usual price is known; where no-shows are costly, take a small payment to get a card on file. [MM-03@00:18:02], [MM-03@00:13:13], [MM-03@00:19:55]
- Decoy Pricing: offer A, B and A+B, the bundle priced as the dearer single item. [MM-19@00:18:09]
- Rule: buyers refuse what costs too much today, not overall; quote the plan price and reward prepayment. Number: third-party financing most times adds ~35% revenue on average. [MM-23@00:01:27], [MM-23@00:02:19], [MM-23@00:04:35]
- Tactics: use the plan price as a price test and raise it while buyers accept; launch new offers low and raise in steps; quote recurring services as an annual price with a prepay discount. [MM-23@00:03:25], [MM-30@00:04:59], [MM-23@00:16:58]
- Numbers: billing every four weeks adds 8.3% revenue; a 3% processing fee is pure profit and can be traded for a second payment method. [MM-28@00:08:13], [MM-28@00:08:57], [MM-28@00:09:53]
- Rule: price for the narrow avatar who gets most value; a premium can fund the unscalable service that removes hidden costs. [SC-07@00:05:32], [SC-04@00:04:18]

## money-models
- Money Model: a deliberate sequence of offers that minimises CAC and maximises gross profit in the first 30 days; the fewest offers that do it, added one at a time. [MM-07@00:00:00], [MM-07@00:15:46], [MM-30@00:04:59]
- Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA): 30-day gross profit exceeds the cost to acquire and serve, so customers fund the next customers and operations become the constraint. [MM-01@00:05:35], [LC-09@00:15:46], [MM-06@00:02:49]
- Four Types of Offers: attraction (cover CAC), upsell (profit), downsell (conversion), continuity (stable cash). [MM-08@00:07:34]
- Case: gym ad costs rose 5x; a ~$200 supplement sale per new member covered acquisition; returns went from 4-5:1 to 31:1. [MM-01@00:08:57], [MM-01@00:10:23]
- Number: Gym Launch ran $500 CAC against $6,000 gross profit in 30 days and $48,000 in 12 months. [MM-07@00:10:36]
- Offer Stack: Gym Launch's sequence (free content, free call, $16,000 intensive on weekly payments, halfway continuity offer). [MM-07@00:12:08]
- Case: a rental-car counter turned $19/day into ~$400 with upgrade, insurance, fuel and late-return offers; old industries' models can be transplanted. [MM-07@00:00:56], [MM-07@00:09:50]
- Principle: buyers hold separate mental wallets, so a different product does not cannibalise the first; people who refuse the core often buy an alternative. [OC-10@00:03:17], [OC-10@00:01:43]
- Point of Greatest Deprivation: offer when the need is felt, not after satisfaction (supplements sold 48 hours after sign-up, not at it). [MM-05@00:05:15], [MM-05@00:04:25]
- Five Upsell Moments: immediately, 24-72 hours, first big win, halfway, last chance; move end-of-term upsells earlier. [MM-05@00:06:02], [MM-05@00:07:42]
- Principle: buyers purchase in bursts, so withholding further offers hands the sale to someone else; do not hold back because 1 in 20 may complain. [MM-05@00:00:45], [MM-07@00:09:04]
- Attraction Offers: free or discounted front ends; demand rises toward free. The six plays follow. [MM-10@00:00:00], [MM-10@00:00:50]
- Win Your Money Back: pay up front, earn it back as credit by meeting result or action criteria; ~78% succeed and roll into a larger offer. Rules: refunds under 5%, back end ~5x the deposit, credit spread over a year, never free months. [MM-11@00:01:52], [MM-11@00:03:31], [MM-11@00:07:29], [MM-11@00:10:42]

- Free Giveaways: grand prize = main offer; 3-7 day entry with qualifying questions; every non-winner gets a partial prize on a deadline; discount 10-30% of margin; prize must match your price level. [MM-12@00:02:54], [MM-12@00:12:05], [MM-12@00:16:36]
- Rules (giveaways): a second prize for the winner's referrer multiplies entries; cap entries at what the team can contact. Case: $300 tickets failed as the prize for a $15,000-$40,000 offer; $50,000 of equipment worked. [MM-12@00:17:40], [MM-12@00:18:35], [MM-12@00:16:36]
- Decoy Offers: advertise a stripped free or cheap version and present it beside the premium; 70-80% should upgrade. [MM-13@00:03:38]

- Buy X Get Y Free: a discount shown as free units; more free than paid converts best; cap long prepay at ~10% of recurring customers. [MM-14@00:02:24], [MM-14@00:05:45], [MM-14@00:09:00]

- Pay Less Now or Pay More Later: $0 with card and full price later if satisfied, or a third to half now with bonuses; ~10% cancellations is normal. [MM-15@00:02:04], [MM-15@00:06:19], [MM-15@00:10:17]

## unit-economics
- LTGP:CAC: lifetime gross profit against acquisition cost; 3:1 is the floor, 10:1+ the portfolio target. [LC-09@00:10:19], [LC-09@00:14:41]
- CAC: all acquisition cost (ads, payroll, commissions, software) per new customer; lowered mainly by a better offer. [MM-03@00:00:00], [MM-03@00:03:21]
- Gross Profit: price minus variable delivery cost. Number: services need 80%+ gross margin, netting ~30%. [MM-04@00:00:00], [MM-04@00:05:59], [OC-06@00:19:44]
- Payback Period: time for gross profit to cover CAC; shortened by upfront fees and early upsells. [MM-05@00:00:00], [MM-05@00:09:37]
- Three Levels of Advertising: LTGP exceeds CAC; gross profit within 30 days exceeds CAC; gross profit within 30 days exceeds twice CAC, where growth compounds. [MM-06@00:00:00], [MM-06@00:04:03]
- Rule: a 10:1 ratio is still a cash problem if the profit arrives a year later; the 30-day test matches interest-free credit. [MM-01@00:03:59], [MM-06@00:00:45]
- Principle: CAC differs ~1-3x between competitors, customer value ~10x; fix the model before the ads. Case: same unit sales, $17M against $300,000 profit. [LC-09@00:12:11], [MM-04@00:00:56]
- Case: a meal company looked 5:1 on revenue, ~1:1 on gross profit, and was closed. [MM-02@00:00:00]
- Rule: for organic and outreach, payroll / engaged leads x leads per customer = CAC. Diagnostic when CAC is above 3x the industry average: unqualified leads (advertising) or qualified non-buyers (sales). [LC-12@00:10:57], [LC-12@00:13:27]
- Formula: lifetime revenue = price / monthly churn; churn from 10% to 3% triples it. [MM-26@00:03:10], [MM-26@00:04:56]
- Rule: scale ads while 30-day cash covers CAC; mature companies may accept 3-5 months. [SC-08@00:11:30]
- Revenue Churn: revenue lost / starting revenue, tracked beside logo churn. Cost to Acquire a Permanent Customer: trials needed per customer who never leaves. [SC-07@00:27:24], [SC-12@00:18:08]

## Not covered by the canon
Material on these subjects is likely new, even when it uses canon vocabulary.
- verticals: nearly every case is a gym, Gym Launch (coaching and licensing for gym owners), supplements or an agency. Nothing on manufacturing, made-to-order or custom production, e-commerce, physical-product economics, distributors and dealers, tenders and quoting, or long multi-stakeholder B2B sales cycles; software appears only as short cases.
- unit-economics outside services: inventory, working capital, capacity utilisation, lead times and margins well below 80% are mentioned only as exceptions.
- pricing: raising prices for existing customers, passing on cost increases, quoting custom work and B2B price negotiation.
- objections-closing: beyond five concern types, three beliefs and a few closes there is no handling of individual objections, no negotiation method and no persuasion theory.
- sales-team: hiring profiles, full compensation plans, quotas and day-to-day sales management appear only as fragments.
- promotions-launches: seasonal campaigns, product launches and events beyond the refresh order, one launch sequence and the challenge or webinar format.
- product-delivery operations: production quality control, capacity planning, supply chain and written procedures.
- management-ops: meeting rhythms, KPI systems outside sales and service, delegation, maker versus manager time, leadership style.
- finance-exit: valuation mechanics, deal structures, the selling process itself, debt terms, investment criteria beyond one benchmark; tax and employment points are US-specific.
- strategy-positioning: moats, competitor analysis, choosing which business to start beyond the four market variables.
- operator-mindset: only persistence, work capacity and learning; little on habits, relationships or handling hard periods.
- scale: nothing above ~500 employees.
