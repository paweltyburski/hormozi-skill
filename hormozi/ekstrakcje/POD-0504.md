### POD-0504#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:47
claim: Entrepreneurs fail for one of four reasons: they do not know something is possible, do not know how to do it, cannot get others to do it, or cannot get themselves to do it.
detail: Acquisition.com uses this rubric to judge how much to bet on a founder, and a founder can use it for self-diagnosis.
numbers: The firm speaks to 600 entrepreneurs above $1M a year each month; 300 are above $3M and 100 above $10M (annual).
### POD-0504#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:33
claim: Hormozi's own gap was not knowing licensing existed: he had spent about two years flying out to fill gyms himself.
detail: He owned six gyms, then ran 30-plus gym turnarounds (fill a gym in under 30 days, collect the cash, move on) before a mentor said he was too good at it and should teach others, introducing licensing. The point is that the biggest threat is not knowing what you could do, and it hurts people from lower-income backgrounds most.
numbers: 6 owned gyms; 30+ turnarounds; ~2 years; fill time under 30 days.

### POD-0504#03 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:31
claim: To discover what is possible, spend time with and consume content from people far wealthier than you and your parents.
detail: Illustrative things he says outsiders do not know: insurance is not taxed in some structures, a mortgage broker can earn $5,000-$20,000 on a single loan, and he claims about 35% of average American income goes to interest payments, which he frames as a very high-margin fee. These are his claims and not verified here.
numbers: Mortgage broker earnings $5,000-$20,000 per loan; ~35% of income to interest (his claim).

### POD-0504#04 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:46
claim: To learn a skill fast, pay a practitioner two to three times their normal rate for one-to-one tutoring while you control the work.
detail: Wanting to learn Facebook ads, he approached agency owners who only sold managed services; one agreed to teach for $750 an hour, one hour a week. Rules: Hormozi holds the mouse and the tutor says where to click and why. In four weeks he learned what the tutor did, in the next four he explained it back, and then he stopped. Phrase the gap as "I do not know how to [problem]" to find the right seller.
numbers: $750/hour, weekly = ~$3,000 a month; about 8 weeks; total ~$6,000.

### POD-0504#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:47
claim: Getting people to act (sales, recruiting, leadership, management) is the highest-leverage meta-skill, and a founder's interpersonal ceiling is usually what caps the company.
detail: With that skill you can hire experts for websites, legal, finance, marketing and product. When assessing a founder the firm asks whether they can attract high-level talent and manage ever more skilled people, and often pairs founders with operators. The firm itself helps attract talent, for example former exit owners who would not join a $10M company.

### POD-0504#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:48
claim: Character traits such as discipline, loyalty and focus are skills built by repeatedly acting correctly in the specific circumstance that tests them.
detail: Method: name the circumstance where you fail (for example, Saturday mornings you should be working), define what counts as success there, and string days of success together as proof. Breaking vague traits into behaviours makes them trainable.

### POD-0504#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:22
claim: A founder who says "I was not born that way" about a trait is less coachable, which caps the business.
detail: Hormozi treats trait-as-fixed language as a signal of a low ceiling, because a coachable founder treats humility, focus, loyalty and honesty as learnable skills.

### POD-0504#08 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:19
claim: To judge what is possible, interview many people ahead of you and rely on the threads they all share; first-principles thinking is the harder alternative.
detail: Twenty people give twenty pieces of advice, but the few points that recur are the most reliable. First principles means reducing a problem to its basic physics and asking why it would not be possible. If you cannot reach those people, consume their content for indirect access.

### POD-0504#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:57
claim: Knowledge, not assets, is the owner's top asset: lack of knowledge costs as much as the missing result is worth, and wealth passed on without the knowledge is squandered.
detail: His family lost assets in the Iranian revolution but rebuilt comparable wealth in two generations because they kept the knowledge. If knowledge is not handed down, heirs statistically lose the asset.
