### YT--HJg4TYBgtI#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:46
claim: Ramsey Solutions grew to about 14 profit centers over roughly 35 years by being platform-agnostic: each new medium (book, event, website, podcast) is tried if it serves one mission, and old ones are kept unless they die on their own.
detail: The mission filter is common-sense education. The company has ~1,100 people on a 650,000 sq ft campus, with offerings from free apps and podcast to 25-person VIP events at the founder's farm. Roughly one new profit center every two and a half years, with some killed along the way.
numbers: ~1,100 staff; ~14 profit centers in ~35 years; EveryDollar app 50 million downloads; VIP events of 25 people.

### YT--HJg4TYBgtI#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: unit-economics | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:33
claim: Dave Ramsey says his company tests new ideas in a beta and judges them on ROI, customer acquisition cost, ROAS, conversion and margin; ideas that cannot scale get killed because of opportunity cost, not affordability.
detail: Ramsey said they can afford to try things, but the missed opportunity on something working at ten times the return means a loved-but-weak idea must die. Ramsey says they iterate a few degrees before killing, and stop when they lose hope for scale or margin.
### YT--HJg4TYBgtI#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:46
claim: Ideas are cheap and implementers are scarce, so anyone may pitch an idea but must defend it, and Ramsey estimates only about 10% of ideas survive contact with the market.
detail: Ideas feel great on a morning run and mostly fail when tested. Ownership is earned by making the case for resources.
numbers: ~10% of ideas work; 90% fail on exposure.

### YT--HJg4TYBgtI#04 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:09:37
claim: New initiatives bubble up from the head of a unit (who talks to customers), while orphan ideas go to a special-projects team that incubates them until proven, then makes them a profit center or hands them to an existing one with a new leader.
detail: The special-projects group is a mix of senior leaders from several disciplines and also fixes broken areas missing budget. The founder sits on it. 80-90% of new leaders come from inside the bench, often the person who originated the idea, because they will fight for it. He prefers passionate champions he can polish over lighting wet wood.
numbers: 80-90% of new-initiative leaders promoted in-house.

### YT--HJg4TYBgtI#05 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: Five S's of Advanced Compensation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:54
claim: Run each business unit as its own P&L with overhead charged to it, and pay its leader off the bottom line.
detail: Early on the web team sat as a separate island paid from a profit center's P&L, so leaders lacked accountability; about 20 years ago they moved to a matrix. Overhead (rent) is job-costed per unit. The operating board is half C-suite and half business-unit leaders. Unit leaders who are not accountants get accounting support rolled up under them.

### YT--HJg4TYBgtI#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:17:15
claim: Ramsey gives no equity to leaders because the end game is perpetual family ownership with no IPO, sale or liquidity event; leaders are paid like partners from the bottom line instead.
detail: Ramsey notes a planned exit might change things, and that giving some equity before going public is no big deal. He chose in advance never to sell, and prefers that nobody walks away holding stock. Hormozi says only that he and Leila have a similar perspective at acquisition.com. Stock sits with his children, with him holding 1%.
### YT--HJg4TYBgtI#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:20:44
claim: Dave Ramsey (not Hormozi) says everyone at his company works in person because communication quality, trust and speed go up, and an organisation moves at the speed of trust.
detail: Practices described by Ramsey: management by walking around, short "drive-by" personal interactions, and a subsidised cafeteria that costs several million dollars a year so that staff eat together at about half local prices. Some people left over it. Remote is fine for pure tasks. Hormozi adds research that in-person helps most in the first third of a career (about 18-35) through unstructured learning and more promotion chances.
numbers: Cafeteria subsidy: several million dollars a year; food about half market price; in-person upside strongest ages ~18-35.
### YT--HJg4TYBgtI#08 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:29:17
claim: Ramsey killed a branded debit card after several million dollars of development because unbounded fraud liability appeared in beta.
detail: It was on-brand and customers liked it. A partner click-and-mortar bank was needed. After a pandemic delay, a beta of about 10,000 users attracted fraud bots; contract terms made Ramsey liable for overdrafts. Because losses could exceed the sunk cost, he stopped. Lesson: they did everything right but did not know their exposure; kill when downside is unlimited.
numbers: Several million dollars sunk; ~10,000 beta users.

### YT--HJg4TYBgtI#09 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:35:00
claim: Keep investments and real estate outside the operating company in separate entities, with assets over $10 million in stand-alone LLCs and large assets in a trust the company rents from.
detail: Reasons are litigation risk containment and estate tax: the campus (about $600M, bought for cash) sits in his children's trust and the firm is a tenant, so the growth escapes estate tax. Partial-interest LLCs can reduce appraisal values for estate planning. Accounting is in-house; taxes, estate planning and nuanced litigation are outsourced, and a specialist zoning attorney is hired for rezoning.
numbers: Campus ~$600M; first land purchase $10M; value roughly doubled while held; LLC per property over $10M.
applies_to: Founders with large real estate or accumulated wealth.

### YT--HJg4TYBgtI#10 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:41:55
claim: Dave Ramsey describes a growth model with five stages (treadmill, trailblazer, a planning stage, pathfinder, legacy), each levelled up by a specific change, plus six drivers (personal growth, purpose, people, profit planning and others not named).
detail: Per Ramsey, Treadmill: solo, owning a job; level up by time blocking to work on the business and by making first hires and delegating. Trailblazer: 10-15 people running in all directions; level up by planning and a first layer of leaders. Pathfinder: systems, processes and planning in place. Legacy: succession, bench depth, family-office mentality. Stage names and order in the audio are partly muddled (unclear in audio). Companies cycled through the drivers 10-15 times.
numbers: Trailblazer stage ~10-15 people; drivers cycled 10-15 times; Ramsey's model is based on coaching ~10,000 small businesses over 30+ years.
### YT--HJg4TYBgtI#11 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:47:20
claim: A stage is defined by behaviour, not revenue: an HVAC owner with 20 trucks may still be working three layers below where he should be.
detail: Ramsey could not tie stages to revenue because industries differ; it is also why the owner is the thing holding the business back at every stage. Treadmill operators do not last ten years because they run out of breath.
