### POD-0184#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:23
claim: Pick a fixed balance and drain the business account down to it every month, even when only a small amount is above the line.
detail: Do it without exceptions, including a month with just $800 of excess. He says taking out as much as you can keeps you disciplined and mindful of where money goes. He gave an example of someone who reinvested during good times, was hit by bad times and had nothing to show for it, and says stacking cash in good times gives you a parachute.
applies_to: Single-location owner-operators.
### POD-0184#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:42
claim: The average gym nets about 12.5% margin, so every dollar of spend needs about eight dollars of new revenue to recover.
detail: A $4,000 equipment purchase therefore has to produce around $32,000 of added revenue to return the $4,000 of profit. At a 12.5% margin he says you need roughly eight times the investment in revenue to break even on it. Judge each spend by that revenue test.
numbers: 12.5% average gym margin (his CPA friend, who works only with gyms); $4,000 spend needs ~$32,000 revenue.
### POD-0184#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:42
claim: He says a gym will probably sell for around three to four times year-end profit, depending on the buyer, so $5,000 less profit would cost about $15,000 at 3x.
detail: Spending that lowers year-end profit hurts twice, once in cash and once in valuation. He says a lot of equipment purchases would likely be treated as equipment costs or upkeep rather than reinvestment, which takes from the multiple.
numbers: 3x to 4x annual profit; $5,000 less profit = $15,000 less price at 3x.
### POD-0184#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:32
claim: In his opinion, the only reason to open additional locations is to prove a concept for licensing or franchising, not to earn from multiple sites.
detail: Few people make substantial money owning several brick-and-mortar service sites. Each location adds overhead and uses the savings, and one hiccup can take down both. Otherwise the choices are run one site for monthly income, or build it to sell.
applies_to: Gyms and local service businesses.
### POD-0184#05 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:23
claim: Total all your liquid cash across every account each morning to learn the rhythm of your money.
detail: He did this until the company was handling over $100,000 of revenue a day. You see patterns, such as payroll, card and ACH deposit days, spot anything unusually high or low, and become less willing to spend what was hard to earn.
numbers: Practised until revenue passed $100,000 per day.

### POD-0184#06 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:41
claim: An owner who reinvested everything during three good years lost half his clients when a trainer left and had to close with nothing saved.
detail: Had he stacked cash monthly he would have had a parachute to switch opportunities and something to show for the work. Separately, a partner once moved company money offshore and he lost the money from several locations; he had not known the partner had been indicted for fraud twice.

### POD-0184#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:58
claim: In his belief, unless you make $100,000 a month in profit or more, he thinks you should consistently spend money on acquiring skills rather than on the business.
detail: His reasoning: if you are not making the money you want, it is because you lack a skill, a character trait or have a false belief. Sometimes that investment means paying for communities and access to people who can break that belief. He says skills usually do more for you than things bought for the business.
numbers: Threshold: $100,000 per month in profit.
### POD-0184#08 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:18
claim: Run internal sales plays to existing clients at least four times a year and take that profit out.
detail: These add-on or paid-in-full plays are pure excess profit and should not be put back in the business. Money kept is guaranteed to raise net worth, whereas invested money is not.
numbers: At least 4 plays per year.
