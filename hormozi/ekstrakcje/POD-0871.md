claim: Hormozi looks in the first week or two at whether his own time allocation changes noticeably, since an A player shows high activity and high alignment.

### POD-0871#02 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:12:56
claim: Coach people by turning vague labels into the specific observable behaviours that produce them.
detail: Steps: ask what exactly was observed that led to the label (lazy, confident, difficult); list every instance; then state the few behaviours to change as concrete asks. Example: "confident" breaks down to eye contact, nodding while listening, repeating back the last point, willingness to take risk. "Lazy John" became: reply in under five minutes and send meeting notes ahead of time. Telling someone "be more confident" means nothing to a six-year-old.
numbers: Reply-time target in example: under 5 minutes.

### POD-0871#03 | CASE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:14:09

detail: Several leaders had told him only to stop. ### POD-0871#04 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:07:48
claim: Define abstract words operationally so they can be trained: learning is same condition with new behaviour, intelligence is rate of learning.
detail: Other working definitions he gives: excuse is a statement made to avoid punishment; patience is working out what to do in the meantime; support is deliberately lowering another person's chance of failure; courage is shortening the time between perceiving risk and acting; authenticity is how you behave when there is no risk of punishment; resilience is how quickly behaviour returns to baseline. The point is to remove words that cannot be observed.

### POD-0871#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:05
claim: To explain a behaviour, look at what followed the last time you did it (the reinforcer), not at what "triggered" it.
detail: His own example: when his wife cried he first comforted her, then got angry; she became scared and stopped crying, so anger was reinforced. The same loop with a team: one outburst makes everyone afraid, information flows to him more slowly, and the business suffers. Remedy: have someone like the CEO who gets all the information, and reinforce the new behaviour (a thank-you when he stays calm) so it overpowers the old one.
