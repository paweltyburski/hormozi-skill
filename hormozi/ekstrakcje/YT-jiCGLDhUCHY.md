### YT-jiCGLDhUCHY#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:11
claim: Solve the one big domino (become worthy) rather than stacking small hacks to look better.
detail: He applies the big-domino idea to business, fitness and relationships: optimising a profile is like polishing an ad (spend five minutes), but the real work starts at the date. Hacks mostly manage perception and fail over time.

### YT-jiCGLDhUCHY#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:59
claim: Judging people accurately is a skill built by exposure, and you learn what you value by sampling widely and having bad meetings.
detail: Assessment is fine; calling people good or bad is the difference. Notice traits you like in coworkers to find what matters. His father said you rarely marry your type because then you would have a wife, not a type.

### YT-jiCGLDhUCHY#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:56
claim: Hormozi's personal preference: he needed a partner who sees his work as a give, and he wants to be judged on the same metrics he judges himself on.
detail: For him, a partner who felt he was taking by pursuing his goals would never work long-term, since he could never flip that balance. Mismatched metrics create a huge gap ("you could be doing more" versus "how can I possibly do more"). He and his wife are both harder on themselves than the other is.
### YT-jiCGLDhUCHY#05 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:09:37
claim: Advertise your downsides, because someone for whom they are neutral or positive is the perfect match.
detail: Rory Sutherland's idea: realtors should list a property's negatives, since a nightlife lover sees the noisy pub as a pro. The arbitrage is between what you accept and others dislike. He applies it to dating: state weaknesses, find those who like them, and you get a "steal".

### YT-jiCGLDhUCHY#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:37
claim: Everything cannot be a priority; rank what matters, state the trade-off and its cost, and do not complain about it afterwards.
detail: Same as strategy in business: if you cannot rank, no one else can. Keep number one non-negotiable and treat lower items as gravy. Make the price explicit before agreeing, as with buying expensive shoes and then protesting the price.

### YT-jiCGLDhUCHY#07 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:19
claim: In hard conversations, state the facts and tell the truth, especially about intentions.
detail: He disapproves of leaving someone waiting for something you know you will not give. He also defines loyalty as the ability to predict someone's behaviour, trading novelty for loyalty and exploring for trust.

### YT-jiCGLDhUCHY#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:21
claim: Choose a partner as in business: the same goal and values, complementary skills, so the sum exceeds the parts.
detail: A spouse is an asset or a liability; liabilities decline in value. Measure a relationship by how much better the two are together than separately. Do not change the rules on someone mid-game: one goal both give everything to makes efforts add up.
