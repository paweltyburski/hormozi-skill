### MZ-56qJcFfbt9I#01 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals, pricing | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A remodeler doing about $2M a year funds net-90 commercial jobs with residential work and loan-shark credit and aims for $100M in 13 months.
detail: Hormozi calls the timeline the real problem: trying to do in 12 months what is certain in 10 years makes every strategy fail. Cash-flow-constrained businesses are limited only by growth rate, provided gross margins are set properly.
numbers: $2M revenue; $100M target; net-90 terms; 10 years proposed.

### MZ-56qJcFfbt9I#02 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: finance-exit, sales-process | fw: What Would It Take | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:23
claim: Treat payment terms as part of the sale: closing and collecting are separate, so negotiate half down or billing in advance.
detail: Example: a guard-services firm facing net-30 norms moved to billing a quarter upfront and stayed three months ahead of cash needs. Ask in the sale what it would take to put half down. Competing on terms is a way to avoid being a commodity; with giant clients hunted as whales you may have to accept their terms.
numbers: net-30 industry norm moved to quarterly billing in advance.

### MZ-56qJcFfbt9I#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:08
claim: Solve growth against cash with a projection: cash, cost to get a customer, average customer value and growth rate; pick the rate where cash stays positive.
detail: It is a spreadsheet problem, not a real one: at one growth rate cash goes negative, at a slower one it is positive. The example picks roughly 20% growth as the sweet spot.
numbers: 30% growth gives negative cash; 20% growth positive (as example).
