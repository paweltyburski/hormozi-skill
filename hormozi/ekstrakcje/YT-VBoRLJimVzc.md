### YT-VBoRLJimVzc#01 | DEFINITION | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A millionaire here means net worth above $1M excluding the primary residence, and liquid net worth is a narrower measure than total net worth.
detail: Net worth is assets minus liabilities, so a $1M house with a $500,000 mortgage and no other savings is $500,000, not a millionaire. Liquid net worth counts only assets you can trade in and out of, such as cash, which differs from owning assets worth the same amount.

### YT-VBoRLJimVzc#17 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:32:33
claim: For capital-heavy businesses, split cash flow roughly between owner pay, growth and reserves, or use a watermark that leaves a fixed balance and distributes the rest.
detail: Back-of-napkin split used for brick-and-mortar chains: about a third to the owner, about 30% to growth (new locations), the rest to rainy-day reserves for the business and the owner (exact shares unclear in audio). Watermark method: cost of planned growth per month (for example two more locations, reps or developers) is subtracted from month-end profit; that sum stays in the account and everything above it is distributed. The watermark suits a target growth rate and is more flexible; the choice depends on risk tolerance.
numbers: Split about 33% owner, 30% growth, remainder reserves.
applies_to: Capital-intensive businesses such as multi-location chains
