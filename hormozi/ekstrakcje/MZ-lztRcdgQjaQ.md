### MZ-lztRcdgQjaQ#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Money Model | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A hair-extension educator sells to broke stylists whose top price tolerance is around $4,000; successful peers make education the front end and product the back end.
detail: Stylists pay about $4,000 to get certified, then roughly one in three keeps buying product indefinitely. Because product margin exceeds cut-and-colour margin, extensions become the stylist's core. Hormozi compares it to his own gym-supplement play, but notes gyms stayed service-first, while for stylists product economics fit.
numbers: $4,000 certification; 1 in 3 buyers stays on product forever; five-year picture: about 3,000 stylists at $500/month in product, around $1M/month.
applies_to: Training businesses with a consumable back end.

### MZ-lztRcdgQjaQ#02 | TACTIC | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:27
claim: Use education as "offset CAC": break even or lose money on training to acquire resellers who sell your product for life.
detail: Build the second product (back end) first, because US competitors with back ends will outspend a company without one. Sequence: step one back end; step two spend more to acquire customers with the new cash flow; step three enter the US market.
numbers: back end expected to take the business from about 5 to about 1.5-2 (metric unclear in audio).
