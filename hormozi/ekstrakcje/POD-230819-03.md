### POD-230819-03#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:43
claim: Maintenance is a myth: a company that does not grow is shrinking, because the market itself keeps growing.
detail: He uses the stock market's roughly 9% yearly growth as the baseline for standing still, and says that in a growing market you may need 20-30% yearly growth just to hold your position. Growth then comes from only three levers: more customers, higher average purchase value, more purchases per customer.
numbers: ~9% per year baseline growth; 20-30% per year needed in a growing market to avoid falling behind.

### POD-230819-03#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:35
claim: Revenue ceiling equals new customers per month multiplied by what each customer is worth over their life.
detail: Worked example: selling to 10 new clients a month, each worth $1,000 over their lifetime (average order value times number of orders), caps the business at $10,000 of monthly revenue. ### POD-230819-03#05 | DEFINITION | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:35
claim: A commodity is anything available from many places, so buyers decide on price; competition drives its margin down to just above survival.
detail: If a prospect compares you with another provider and concludes they are about the same, they buy the cheaper one, which is what he means by being commoditized. ### POD-230819-03#22 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Price to Value Discrepancy | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:41:12
claim: Cutting price is the simplest way to widen the price-value gap but most of the time the wrong decision; don't compete on price unless you can cut your cost to about one tenth of the competition's.
detail: Price can only fall to zero but can rise without limit, and a business's goal is making money, not maximum purchases. Raise the price only after raising the value, so the buyer still gets a large deal, for example $100,000 of value for $10,000. Quoting Dan Kennedy, Hormozi says being second cheapest has no strategic benefit, being the most expensive does.
### POD-230819-03#23 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Virtuous Cycle of Price | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:41:59
claim: Copying the market average produces a "more for less" offer, and the competitors being copied are usually barely surviving.
detail: The six-step habit: look at the market, see what rivals offer, take the average, go slightly below, offer slightly more, end up with more for less. Repeated entry pushes the market to perfect efficiency, where participants earn just enough to continue. The bottom 10-20% of operators are washed out each cycle and replaced by new entrants who repeat the pattern.
numbers: bottom 10-20% of operators exit per cycle.

### POD-230819-03#24 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: sales-process | fw: Price to Value Discrepancy | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:46:06
