### POD-0804#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Ascension Path | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A $30M personal-finance course business for women had a low back-end ascension rate, not a CAC problem.
detail: Paid Meta ads fill a free webinar that sells a $2,000 offer (or 12 payments of $200); no low-ticket self-liquidating front end. A $10,000 back end converts at 7% through an outsourced sales team. ### POD-0804#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: Ascension Path | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:15

### POD-0804#03 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: retention-ltv | fw: Ascension Path | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:38
claim: Build the ascension into onboarding: the onboarding call ends only when the customer books a goal-setting call, which leads to a setter and then a closer.
detail: Customer path: webinar, purchase, onboarding call (group to start, one-to-one earns more), goal-setting call with a would-be setter that re-sells and sifts those who can afford more, then a closed call inviting them to a higher level. Proof of the next booking is the exit condition, so there is a full through-line. Add several buy-now-pay-later options on the front-end sales calls.

### POD-0804#05 | CASE | topic: sales-process | also: retention-ltv | fw: Basic Sales KPIs | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:36
claim: A Dallas dental group ($3M, 5% margin) lost about $1M in a year to a 30%+ cancellation and no-show rate despite calls a week, three days and one day ahead.
detail: Five offices, three part-time, a 30-year-old business inherited from the owner's father. Their reminders sit too far out ("Monday morning is a different universe" to someone booking on Saturday). Hormozi's fixes follow in separate atoms.
numbers: cancellation/no-show rate a little over 30%; about $1M lost in a year.

### POD-0804#06 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:32
claim: Right after booking, ask "is there anything that could get in the way of you showing up?" (an integrity tie-down), and offer earlier appointment slots.
detail: The question shakes people out of autopilot; he cannot fully explain why it works but it does. Pulling appointments forward also raises show rates.

### POD-0804#07 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:23
claim: Use automated reminders far out and manual texts from an office phone at three points: the night before, the morning of, and 60-90 minutes before.
detail: The last three touches carry the real effort. Add personalisation so the reminder reads as meant for that person.
numbers: manual texts at 24 hours, morning of, 60-90 minutes before.

### POD-0804#08 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:15
claim: Create an incurred cost by letting the customer pick a small item in advance (colour, size, goodie-bag contents) and send a photo with their name on it.
detail: Works well for brick-and-mortar: a gym offers a shirt held in their size. For a dentist, patients choose the free items in the goodie bag. Seeing that you spent effort for them raises the felt obligation to show up.

### POD-0804#09 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:33
claim: A 10-month-old roofing company ($1.8M, 95% insurance work, $1.1M from repeat customers) asked which scaling model to pick; the answer was to nail the model first.
detail: Four salespeople produced the rest. Goals are $8M soon and about $70M then exit. Insurance deductibles are rising, so fewer homeowners fix roofs. Hormozi: do not decide with information that could be known; once the model is proven and returns on capital are clear, the path (own locations or franchise) becomes obvious.
numbers: $1.8M revenue, $1.1M from returning customers, 95% insurance.

### POD-0804#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:56
claim: Franchising is the most expensive form of capital and a sign of impatience; he prefers owning every location.
detail: A franchisor trades 8% of top line (roughly a 25-30% partner) for other people's money, but he has seen a graveyard of 20-location franchises that make little. Maintaining 20 franchises takes about as much effort as owning 20, with less return. Case: a teeth-whitening chain with 14 corporate stores and 18 franchisees was bought back to 32 owned stores within 12 months, removing the administrative headache.
numbers: 8% of top line as franchise fee; 14 corporate + 18 franchise stores became 32 owned in 12 months.

### POD-0804#11 | DEFINITION | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:47
claim: LTV:CAC is the micro version of a business's engine; return on invested capital is the macro version that includes equipment, leases and build-outs.
detail: Example: a $500,000 location that earns $500,000 in its first six months gives a 2:1 return on capital within a year. Also consider the cost of building the machine again and again.
numbers: $500,000 to open; $500,000 earned in six months; 2:1 return in a year.

### POD-0804#12 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: pricing | fw: Delivery Cube | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:12:41
claim: A music studio for neurodivergent kids ($500K, target $2M, underpriced, overpaying teachers, unused capacity) should move from private lessons to semi-private groups of four at a specialist price.

He will not run a brick-and-mortar service below 80%, ideally 90. Neurodivergent specialisation supports a premium price. Lead with a six-week beginner challenge with a discrete outcome (play a song), package roughly $600-$2,000 (audio reads "$600,000", unclear), then continuity.
numbers: 1-to-4 ratio; $200 per session; teacher cost $40-$50; margin 80%+; challenge six weeks; package $600-$2,000 (unclear in audio).

### POD-0804#13 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:18
claim: Sell semi-private lessons to parents as protection against a child getting attached to one teacher who might leave.
detail: Pitch: if the child bonds with a single teacher and that teacher leaves, the skill is linked to the person and the child quits after years. Mixed sessions and rotating teachers build attachment to the craft and the brand, not the teacher, and stop a teacher taking students private.

### POD-0804#14 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: pricing | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:40
claim: If you are capacity constrained, use three steps in order: raise prices, change the client-to-provider ratio, then delegate to others.
detail: Price first because it is fastest and needs no operational change; ratio change (one-to-four) improves leverage, cash flow and margins; delegation is the ultimate leverage. He also says the more stressed someone is, the lower their IQ, and problems that are hard under stress get solved in minutes with good sleep and a little time.
