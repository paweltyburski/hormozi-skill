### POD-0169#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: lead-getters | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Your customers are a distribution base: recommend non-competing products they already buy and take a tracked commission.
detail: Pick adjacent categories, closest to your core first (for a weight-loss gym: supplements and food, then apparel and equipment). Make the recommendations you would make anyway, but route them so they are tracked to you. Be transparent that the customer pays nothing extra for it. This is described as the only workable form of passive revenue.
applies_to: Businesses with an established customer base, gyms in his examples.

### POD-0169#02 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:05
claim: Recommending landing-page software to customers earned about $724,000 in commissions.
detail: The company did not build the software, it recommended a tool that worked well to customers and was paid affiliate commissions. He cites it as the proof for the distribution-base approach.
numbers: $724,000 total commissions.

### POD-0169#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:27
claim: Three of four Americans buy a dietary supplement, and the average gym or affiliate sold supplements to about 14 people a month.
detail: Those 14 monthly supplement buyers, he says, would cover the owner, four trainers and all their food. The supplement figure is from public statistics he cites; the 14 is his own average across gyms and affiliates.
numbers: 74% of Americans use at least one supplement; 14 supplement sales per month per gym.

### POD-0169#04 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:19
claim: Give the referred customer a discount so a commission turns into goodwill rather than a cost to them.
detail: His ready-meal partnership let gym clients buy at a lower price than normal while the gym earned the commission, so the customer saves on something they would buy anyway. He pitches the target as the revenue from supplements covering the gym's rent and the revenue from meals covering food.

### POD-0169#05 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:33
claim: Offering ready meals as a service makes the front-end sale easier by reducing the lifestyle change required.
detail: If prospects need to get only one meal right a day, the plan feels doable, so they are more likely to buy and to stay. Later meals can be removed to teach them to cook. Food is eaten by everyone, so, unlike supplements, it needs little selling once it is available.
numbers: Normal adults spend about 5.9 hours a week prepping and cleaning food (USDA, his citation); estimated about 9 hours for people changing their fitness lifestyle.

### POD-0169#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:25
claim: Covering trainers' food through the partner program is a staff benefit that also teaches them to look after customers.
detail: Trainers who are taken care of take care of customers, especially in service. Free food becomes a talking point for staff even when pay is modest.
