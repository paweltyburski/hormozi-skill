### YT-y4VoOKa1ywQ#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:45
claim: All business is arbitrage: you buy in one market and sell in another, and your profit is set by the size of the gap.
detail: A service firm buys labor in the labor market and sells it in the customer market. A supply chain is a chain of markets, each buying at one price and selling at another, with the owner in the middle.
### YT-y4VoOKa1ywQ#02 | DEFINITION | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:52
claim: Economic profit is revenue minus explicit costs and also minus the opportunity cost of using your resources in this market rather than another.
detail: Accounting profit is revenue minus all costs. Example: a paper mill that owns a forest should ask whether selling timber or sawdust would earn more from the same people, equipment and skills.

### YT-y4VoOKa1ywQ#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: Four Market Variables | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:27
claim: Hormozi describes how he looks at opportunities: compare arbitrage opportunities for the same skills, prefer the one with more arbitrage, and note that growing markets likely have a greater degree of arbitrage because demand is higher.
detail: Online fitness was his growing-market example. List all the businesses you could run and ask which suits your resources, skills, talent and team best, which also answers what you can be best in the world at.
### YT-y4VoOKa1ywQ#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:05
claim: Price should relate to what the market will bear, never to your cost; he abandoned early moral limits on how much he could charge.
detail: Big companies run on extreme arbitrage, such as media paid for by advertising, or telecom where a new user costs pennies. Hormozi says there is no cap except demand.
