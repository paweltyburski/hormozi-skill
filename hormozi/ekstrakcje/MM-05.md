### MM-05#01 | DEFINITION | topic: unit-economics | also: - | fw: Payback Period | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Payback period is how long it takes until gross profit from a customer exceeds the cost to acquire them — your break-even time.
detail: Also called the cash conversion cycle. The shorter it is, the easier it is to scale advertising: if the dollar comes back tomorrow, you can spend it again tomorrow. Slow payback forces businesses into marketing budgets to manage cash, a problem fast payback eliminates.

### MM-05#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Buyers buy in short, intense windows after a new decision or identity, so a business that holds back further offers simply hands those sales to a competitor.
detail: Owners feel guilty making several offers in a row; Hormozi says they are wrong and poorer for it. When someone starts a new lifestyle they enter a hyper-buying cycle and then go dormant. Example: a friend decided to lose weight and bought a bike, then within a day or two a helmet, shoes, pants, gloves and water bottles, and signed up for a race. The first bike shop could have sold all of it; she was buying either way.

### MM-05#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:20
claim: Letting customers know what else you offer is not hard selling — in his opinion, if you can solve their problem, you ethically should tell them and tell them what it costs.
detail: You sell 0% of people who do not know you have something. Make the offer; yes is fine and no is fine. If you believe what you sell solves problems, solve more of them.

### MM-05#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: - | fw: Payback Period | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:06
claim: With $160 CAC and a $100/month product costing $20 to deliver, payback is two months — calculated on gross profit, not revenue.
detail: Month one: $80 gross profit against $160 CAC leaves you $80 down. Month two adds $80 — break-even. People who use revenue think it is 1.5 months, which is wrong; always subtract cost of goods (a $10 meal costing $9 earns $1, not $10).
numbers: CAC $160; $80 GP/month; payback 2 months.

### MM-05#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: More Better New | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:20
claim: Shorten payback by making More, Better or New offers within the first 30 days, at the right moment.
detail: Upsells fail for four reasons: the wrong way, the wrong time, poor description, or the wrong person. Many owners have a strong upsell but present it when the problem it solves is not present.

### MM-05#06 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Point of Greatest Deprivation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:25
claim: Supplements offered right after a gym sale flopped; moved to a nutrition orientation 48 hours later, they sold.
detail: Right after signing up for weight loss, the customer felt the problem was solved — the upsell reopened a closed wound. At the orientation he reviewed their food, pointed out what they were missing, and so created a new problem that supplements solved.
numbers: Orientation 48 hours after signup.
applies_to: Gyms; any service with a natural follow-up step.

### MM-05#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Point of Greatest Deprivation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:15
claim: Sell at the point of greatest deprivation, not the point of greatest satisfaction.
detail: Right after a big steak dinner you do not want another steak, however good; a thirsty person wants the first cup of water far more than the second. Many upsells are pitched right after the customer is satisfied. Create or reveal deprivation, then make the offer.
quote: Sell at the point of greatest deprivation.

### MM-05#08 | FRAMEWORK | topic: money-models | also: sales-process, retention-ltv | fw: Five Upsell Moments | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:02
claim: There are five moments to upsell: immediately, at the next step, after the first big win, at the halfway point, and at the last chance.
detail: (1) Immediately, in the same conversation — works when solving one problem naturally creates the next. (2) Next step, 24–72 hours later — when you must guide them to a new problem (e.g. the nutrition orientation). (3) After a big win or milestone — a fully solved problem opens a new one (hit $10K/month → tax issues, no time, need employees); so engineer fast wins. (4) Halfway point — empirically very open to upsells. (5) Last chance — the exit interview. Four of five come before the halfway point, and most fall within the first 30 days, which is what shortens payback.
numbers: Next step at 24–72 h after the first sale.

### MM-05#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Five Upsell Moments | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:42
claim: If you upsell only at the end of the engagement, move the offer earlier — ride the open buying cycle instead of trying to reopen a closed one.
detail: At the end, the customer is satisfied ("I'm good with what you gave me"). Mid-stream, frame it as keeping the momentum going: add a service piece, get there faster, reduce risk, add support. Hormozi says moving the upsell forward has never failed for him.

### MM-05#10 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics, money-models | fw: Payback Period | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:37
claim: Shorten payback with upfront charges: first and last month today, or an initiation, onboarding, enrollment or activation fee.
detail: A fee is all gross profit, so it pulls cash forward. In the $160 CAC / $100-a-month example, collecting first and last month up front puts him ahead on the first transaction (he states +$80, half of the next customer's CAC; the given numbers work out to about +$20), so every two customers fund another without waiting for the recurring payment. Payback moves inside the first 30 days. This is one structure among many to come.
numbers: Payback from 2 months to what he calls "one and a half" months, i.e. cash back within the first 30 days.
