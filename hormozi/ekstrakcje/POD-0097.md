### POD-0097#01 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:49
claim: Spend thinking time, even three to four hours, finding the single big domino that makes other tasks done or irrelevant.
detail: Comes from a Tim Ferriss anecdote about his routine. As a business grows, what you personally need to do shrinks while the amount of thinking about what to do grows. Hormozi says his weeks contain a small amount of work, aimed at the biggest ball only he can move.
numbers: 3-4 hours a day of thinking about the business (Ferriss).

### POD-0097#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: Impact vs Investment Matrix (RICE) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:16
claim: Of a long list of ideas or requests, all of which would help, he says that a lot of times you can easily cross out about three quarters right away.
detail: Rank by return on time and attention. Saying no does not mean the idea is bad; nearly all would make money. Cutting the list lowers anxiety, which rises as life gets noisier.
numbers: A lot of times, about 75% of a list can be crossed out.
### POD-0097#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:28
claim: In calls with owners making $5M-$15M he asks where they are and what the main bottleneck is: lead gen, shows, closing, churn or fulfilment.
detail: He decides from numbers, not emotions, then tells them to ignore everything else. The conversations are astonishingly simple. An outsider can give permission to say no.
applies_to: Owners at roughly $5M-$15M.

### POD-0097#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:31
claim: If churn is the hole in the bucket, deliberately run acquisition at a lower level for about a month, fix retention, then ramp acquisition back up.
detail: Example: full acquisition might give 80 sales a month, a lower setting 40. If cash flow is adequate at 40, put attention on the leak. Strong acquisition can mask that the real growth limit is retention.
numbers: 80 sales per month at full pace versus 40 at reduced pace; fix period about one month.
applies_to: Businesses with decent acquisition and high back-end churn.
