### POD-0268#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:45
claim: Your plan B rises with your skill set, so build a ladder of worst-case incomes.
detail: Level one was ride-hailing by day and night work, which he estimated near $200,000 a year. After learning to sell, level two was a car dealership job at roughly $200,000 to $400,000. Level three was generating leads, working them, making offers and selling higher tickets. Skills, he says, are the only asset that cannot be taken from you.
numbers: Plan B incomes $200,000/year, then $200,000-$400,000/year via car sales (his estimates; he gave $100,000-$150,000 in POD-200609-01).

### POD-0268#02 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: outreach, verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:53
claim: After a failed venture left $1,200, he offered local gyms free customer acquisition, sold packages in their space, and took the profit after costs.
detail: He asked owners for no payment. He ran his own leads, worked them, and sold at the front desk; the owner needed only to supply a hat and t-shirt and deliver the service. He kept the profit after recovering his time and ad spend, and the owner kept the rest of the customer lifecycle. Works where lifetime value sits beyond the first package.
applies_to: Anyone who knows a local business's value proposition and can sell.

### POD-0268#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:44
claim: The deal structure depends on your cash and the business: the more risk you carry, the more of the reward you can claim.
detail: For one-time big jobs such as roofing, he would probably do some split, taking maybe 30% or half of the gross profit after recovering his costs. With truly no money, have the first client cover ad spend, be repaid first, then split profit; use that income to fund the second client's ad spend and keep more. With zero cash, first earn about $100 selling time, then get a phone and find local businesses.
numbers: Share for one-off jobs maybe 30%-50% of gross profit; start capital threshold $100.
### POD-0268#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:39
claim: Daily economics of the gym campaigns: $100 of ads bought about 20 leads, closing four at $500 gave $2,000.
detail: From the $2,000 he subtracted $100 for the next day's ads, leaving $1,900, and the same thing happened on day two.
numbers: $100/day spend; about 20 leads; 4 sales at $500; about $2,000 revenue per day.
### POD-0268#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: verticals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:25
claim: Results varied sharply by market: roughly $34,000 in 14 days at the first gym, about $11,000 at the second, then $121,000 in 20 days at the third.
detail: The second market was hard for reasons he could not pin down; the next gym after the third produced around $120,000 again. Once it worked he hired six salespeople to run six gyms a month and reached about $100,000 in his bank in roughly six weeks. He worked about 12 hours a day, six and a half days a week.
numbers: $34,000 in 14 days; about $11,000 in 10-14 days; $121,000 in 20 days; $100,000 cash in about six weeks; six gyms per month with six sales reps.
