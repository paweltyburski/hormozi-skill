### YT-mRlSb0O5QNU#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: An event-planning company (30 people, 12 full time) with rising deal sizes had invented prices, thin margins and weak lead flow.
detail: Utopia Experience in St. Louis. Audio-visual work is a large add-on and video is passed through at cost for convenience. The owner converts ten times better than his two end-to-end salespeople. No defined acquisition process. Hormozi's order: fix the offer, then price, then sales motion, then demand, since "mispriced" explains part of the demand gap.
numbers: Average deal $34,000 last year, $44,000 trailing 12 months; AV spend $20,000-$150,000 per event; 30 staff.

### YT-mRlSb0O5QNU#02 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:04
claim: The company's quotes were stacked invented line items: retainer, sponsor management, vendor management and venue procurement.
detail: Every package carried a one-time retainer, then sponsor management, vendor management (photo booth, video team), and venue and logistics fees. After a bad experience, guardrails were added: a higher tier when attendees, vendors or sponsors pass thresholds. Customers got long delays, and the company did not know profit per event.
numbers: Retainer $15,000; sponsor management ~$15,000; venue procurement/logistics $7,500.

### YT-mRlSb0O5QNU#03 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:55
claim: Cost out each delivery level with a dynamic sheet, then price at 5 to 10 times cost.
detail: Inputs are attendee headcount, variable costs that scale with headcount (sponsors, vendors), and fixed costs (e.g. a quarter of an employee who can handle only four events a year, about $15K). The sheet outputs a cost basis the customer never sees. Use the position inside 5-10x depending on how affluent the buyer is. Build it as an if-then spreadsheet or an AI-coded tool, so a salesperson can quote on the spot.
numbers: Price = 5x to 10x total cost; floor of 5x because costs may overrun.

### YT-mRlSb0O5QNU#06 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:05
claim: Offer a price-lock guarantee (price holds if scope doesn't change) and a 10% prepay discount.
detail: Fixes cash flow because event clients paid in instalments. Price lock: the quoted figure holds unless the client changes things, with a fudge factor built in: headcount within about 20%, same date and venue, same services. Framing line: either you get nickel-and-dimed, or we pick a price within your budget and it holds. If they break scope, discuss it; a client who adds scope already decided it is worth it. Discounts are paid for by the padding in the price.
numbers: Prepay discount 10% (up to 10-20% worthwhile); headcount tolerance ±20%.

### YT-mRlSb0O5QNU#08 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:38
claim: Where the event's audience is the planner's target customer, win those events and use them as the lead source.

### YT-mRlSb0O5QNU#10 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: sales-process | fw: Training | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:22
claim: Drive new reps with daily meetings (morning plan, end-of-day review) and role-play until the pipeline works, then go weekly.
detail: Morning: how many touches, which leads, what prep and personalisation. Evening: how conversations went and where the pipeline stands. Weekly one-on-one runs in three steps: personal check-in first, then short-term fixes in script and intro, then long-term goals and shadowing the owner's calls for those hitting KPIs. Role-play endlessly because feedback loops are how salespeople learn.

### YT-mRlSb0O5QNU#12 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Crazy Eight | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:49
claim: Early in the call ask "how many events do you run per year?" and bid for all of them.
detail: Hormozi added the question after "why did you book this call today?". A client with three events a year turns an $80K ticket into $240K. Commit to all three at one rate; if the first goes well, book the others. Result: one-year clients became three-year clients; offering more of what they just bought tends to always lift LTV.
numbers: Example ticket $80K to $240K with three events.

### YT-mRlSb0O5QNU#13 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:29:06
claim: Multi-event deals: 10% off for committing to all events, a pay-the-difference exit, another 10% for prepaying, pitched at tax and budget timing.
detail: Seed with the question about event count, then give 10% off if they commit to all three and, if they leave, they pay the difference (a "stick"). Another 10% for prepaying all. Frame for annual budgets: it is near tax season, so prepaying the event takes cash off the books. Works best in Q3-Q4 for events booked for the next year.
numbers: 10% off for commitment, plus another 10% for prepay.

### YT-mRlSb0O5QNU#14 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: Sales Aids | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:37
claim: Put a video sales letter before every call, including the follow-up and post-event calls, with an engagement tag.
detail: Before call one, call two and the post-event debrief, send a VSL covering the FAQs so the call is spent closing. Add a CTA such as "text me your headcount and date" to prove they watched. If they did not, play it live (about 7 minutes) while the owner steps away. The post-event version uses different examples and objections (will the price be the same?). This can turn two calls into a one-call close, with a quote by the next day.
numbers: VSL about 7 minutes; deliver quote within a day.

### YT-mRlSb0O5QNU#15 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: sales-process | fw: Riches in the Niches | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:33:16
claim: After narrowing to one avatar the company's top-of-funnel VSL made prospects feel known, and a bottom-of-funnel VSL reached silent decision-makers.
detail: The top VSL states the avatar, the pain and the threshold to work together; nine in ten prospects now have the same pain, so the answer can be templatised, which adds margin, scale and lower CAC. A second VSL targets the CEO or CFO who decides but may not join. The owner calls it "10 miles deep, an inch wide".
