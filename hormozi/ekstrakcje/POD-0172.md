### POD-0172#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Goodwill (Price-Value-Cost) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:18
claim: Revenue equals the goodwill you hold multiplied by how many times you ask for a sale.
detail: He says large companies beat small ones through a choreographed process that asks repeatedly, online and in person. Small owners who feel bad selling skip the asks, so they stay small.

### POD-0172#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Menu Upsell | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:26
claim: A new rental-car clerk added about $400 to his booking with a short script of choice-framed upsells.
detail: Her asks: a bigger car, insurance framed as standard versus minimum (both are upsells; "minimum" sounds like the safe choice), and prepaid fuel for a full tank he mostly would not use. He also notes the insurance cost about $20 a day against roughly $20 a month elsewhere, which he calls near-pure margin. The process was simple enough that anyone could run it.
numbers: about $400 added to the ticket; prepaid tank about 25 gallons at roughly $3 = about $75; insurance about $20/day.

### POD-0172#03 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Sell to Goal | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:37
claim: At sign-up, sell the member through to their goal (for example 36 weeks) instead of a membership or free trial.
detail: People are most committed and excited at the door. Selling the whole programme turns a trial prospect into a committed client, which he says lifts lifetime value to two to three times a typical gym.
numbers: about 36 weeks, about $2,000 sale; 2x to 3x the average gym's lifetime value.
applies_to: Gyms and coaching with a defined goal

### POD-0172#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Point of Greatest Deprivation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:22
claim: At a nutrition orientation after sign-up, about 80% buy at least two supplements and a third to a half take meals.
detail: Restate the goal and pain, onboard well, ask about their eating, then offer supplements and meals so they need not cook. Treat these as separate revenue streams, like fuel and insurance at the car counter.
numbers: about 80% buy 2 or more supplements; 1 in 3 to 1 in 2 take 1 to 3 meals a day (depends on the salesperson).

### POD-0172#05 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: Five Upsell Moments | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:15
claim: Hold a progress review at day 14 to 21 and upsell either way: more accountability if they struggle, a more advanced programme if they succeed.
detail: Review attendance and goals. If results lag, offer one-on-one accountability or private training with a deal for deciding that day. If results are strong, use the goodwill to offer the advanced tier.
numbers: day 14 to 21 (the second to third week).

### POD-0172#06 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: retention-ltv | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:45
claim: Run a quarterly campaign to current, past and lapsed customers selling a defined-period programme with continuity behind it.
detail: Examples are a 28-day or six-week challenge. It catches customers who declined earlier offers and reactivates old ones; the back end is a continuity service so they stay and pay more.
numbers: quarterly cadence.
