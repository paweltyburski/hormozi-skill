### MZ-cuNT2fjRrnE#01 | CASE | topic: offers | also: retention-ltv, verticals | fw: Perceived Hidden Costs | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A firm that builds apps for non-technical founders in 100 days or less delivers on time and budget, yet clients end exhausted.
detail: Founders do not know the workload in advance; by launch they feel drained ("SaaS fatigue"). The owner does not want to become an education company.
numbers: 100 days or less per build.
applies_to: Custom software or project-based services.

### MZ-cuNT2fjRrnE#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: - | fw: Perceived Hidden Costs | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:48
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: he thought the exhaustion of his software clients was a hidden-cost problem, since they sign up without knowing how much they will have to do.
detail: His suggested options were to set higher expectations up front of what clients must do, or to do more pre-work so the result appears more magically for them.
### MZ-cuNT2fjRrnE#03 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:48
claim: Offer a slower, gentler timeline option, such as 200 days, beside the 100-day sprint.
detail: A punishing sprint makes people dread the rest of the project. Hormozi also suggests telling the client honestly that the 100 days will be miserable.
numbers: 100 versus 200 days.
