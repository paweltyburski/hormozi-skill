### MZ-KG5QJ3FuwA8#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A cleaning firm serving homeowners and vacation-rental hosts is told to drop the weaker segment.
detail: Revenue $1.2M, goal $3M in 18 months. Homeowners are recurring, numerous and sticky; hosts are price-conscious and fewer. Hormozi: the decision is easy, let rentals die, since processes differ and two businesses dilute focus.
numbers: Homeowners ~60% of revenue, hosts ~15%; LTV:CAC 9:1 vs 6:1.

### MZ-KG5QJ3FuwA8#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:24
claim: While one avatar offers unlimited demand at 9:1, perfect it instead of adding a second service.
detail: Process gains could lift 9:1 to 12-14:1, which splitting attention prevents. Keep deferring the second-segment decision as revenue grows through $10M, $30M and $100M.
numbers: 9:1 improvable to 12-14:1.

### MZ-KG5QJ3FuwA8#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: Buy vs Build | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:18
claim: A yoga studio owner bought a chocolate factory to build a wellness empire, an illustration of premature diversification.
detail: Such businesses only attack adjacent verticals after maxing their addressable market, when buy-or-build becomes the question.
