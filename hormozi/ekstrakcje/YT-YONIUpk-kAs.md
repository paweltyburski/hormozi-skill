### YT-YONIUpk-kAs#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:05:00
claim: Mental toughness can be measured on four variables: how much bad it takes to change you, how little you change, how fast you rebound, and whether you rebound above baseline.
detail: 1) How much negative stimulus is needed before your behaviour changes at all. 2) Once it changes, how small is the change (an ice cream rather than heroin, cheating or drunk driving). 3) Resiliency: days or weeks to return to baseline. 4) Whether you return below baseline (lasting change), to baseline, or above it (adapted). Defining it means you can measure it, and if you can measure it you can improve it.

### YT-YONIUpk-kAs#15 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:42:14

### YT-YONIUpk-kAs#16 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: More Better New | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:46:45
claim: The more a business works, the less likely a change to any variable improves it, so the safest move is doing more of what already works.
detail: Analogy: tossing a handful of candies and hoping they land sorted; the taller the working stack, the more likely any different brick breaks it. A control that won 36 of 36 split tests is unlikely to be beaten by a variant. When nothing works, try many new things; once something works, deviate less. Many owners damage a working business over problems that have not happened yet; real threats usually give warning and let you adapt, as owners saw in COVID.
