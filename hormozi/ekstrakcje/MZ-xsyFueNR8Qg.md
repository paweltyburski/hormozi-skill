### MZ-xsyFueNR8Qg#01 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals, strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A nine-year-old wellness e-commerce firm with $5-10M revenue and no profit was told to shut down, not to be sold.
detail: Growth came from Meta ads with the owner unpaid to repay debt, while his brother's 3PL was thriving. Hormozi: there is no brand, so it is media arbitrage that nobody acquires. Priority: get equity in the 3PL in writing, and bring the two or three best staff.
numbers: Revenue $5-10M; 9 years old.

### MZ-xsyFueNR8Qg#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:22
claim: In e-commerce the sellable asset is a brand; no one buys media arbitrage (just running ads on products).
detail: To this caller he pointed to the lack of a big brand spokesperson and of collaborations with named people; he said she had just run ads and people bought.
### MZ-xsyFueNR8Qg#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: money-models | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:46
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: don't rush a monetization lever unless it will disappear; the vast majority only get better with time, and he suggested saving it to juice about six months before a sale.
detail: The caller's business was already at the brink of breaking, so pulling the lever now wasn't worth it unless there was urgency to prove profitability for an exit. In a sale process, buyers would see a skyrocketing.
numbers: About 6 months before a sale.
### MZ-xsyFueNR8Qg#04 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:36
claim: When a sales-led founder outgrows operations, step in briefly, hire someone who has done it, then return to selling.
detail: The 3PL grew so fast that ops could not keep up.
