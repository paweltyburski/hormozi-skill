### POD-0835#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:10:56
claim: Leila Hormozi's team speech (not Alex's) calls "sincere candor" a core value with three parts (tell the truth, tell it fast, intend to improve things) and explicit exclusions.
detail: In Leila's speech: leaders who withhold feedback because it is uncomfortable are failing; teammates who whisper instead of speaking directly are part of the problem; anyone defensive about feedback caps own growth. What it is not: cruelty, tearing people down, speaking to hear yourself, passive-aggressive or vague feedback, or waiting until too late. Clients come before politics and ego.
### POD-0835#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:16
claim: At new-hire orientation Layla states that the company will not accommodate newcomers; people mould into the existing culture, because it is what is winning.
detail: Culture is a spectrum: nobody is a 10 out of 10 fit any day, the question is how far one exemplifies it. Good ideas from new people are welcome, but they should not import corporate jargon or passive-aggressive habits. Success brings more frequent opposition, internally and externally.

### POD-0835#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:07
claim: "Be kind, not nice": niceness that avoids saying hard things to save face damages the team and the project.
detail: In a final-round interview a candidate said every interviewer had named the same pet peeve (nice, not kind). Contrast: "suicidal empathy", loving the person who is hurting you and so not stopping them. Layla's self-rule: be a "kind killer", very kind but a hunter. New hires often say they did not believe the culture would really be like this.

### POD-0835#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:03
claim: If a candidate asks about work-life balance in the interview, they are probably not hired, because the company wants people whose interest in the work continues outside hours.
detail: Reasoning: of two competing teams, the one whose people think about the work voluntarily wins. Be upfront in interviews that scaling quickly needs discretionary effort. He says he only needs a couple hundred such people. The other speaker (not Hormozi) adds that people with young children or caring duties should work at balanced employers. Example: a marketing teammate pays his own way to an international summit.
applies_to: Fast-growing companies with a demanding culture

### POD-0835#07 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:26:49
