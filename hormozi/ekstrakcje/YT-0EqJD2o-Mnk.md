### YT-0EqJD2o-Mnk#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: pricing | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:29
claim: Fixes are usually multi-step: if demand exceeds capacity, raise price to fund cash flow, which funds hiring, which funds redundancy.
detail: For an electrical contractor ($1.6M revenue, about $650,000 kept, goal $5M) who returns to the van whenever one crew member is out: he needs two or three backups per role. If the hiring constraint sits behind cash flow, then pricing is the real work, and second- or third-order changes are reversed back to the core problem. If one thing were the answer the owner would likely have seen it.
numbers: Revenue $1.6M; kept about $650,000; goal $5M.

### YT-0EqJD2o-Mnk#02 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: Conditional Guarantee | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:18
claim: Raise price 20-40% by making the job faster, more reliable and easier, backed by a guarantee with real teeth.
detail: Example guarantee: if we miss the time or budget terms, you get all my profit back. If you fail you return to your old price; every job you hit earns the premium. Competitors who only promise to be on time have nothing at stake, which exposes their lack of confidence. Extra cash lets you recruit the extra technician, then keep advertising to backfill.
numbers: Price increase 20% to 40%.
applies_to: Trades and contractors.

### YT-0EqJD2o-Mnk#04 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: outreach | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:11:27
claim: When a contractor depends on six or seven heating-and-cooling companies for work, start quoting jobs for competing companies to gain leverage.
detail: Those few "whales" hold all the power and call on short notice. Offering to quote jobs for other firms (many are stuck with whoever they use, not happy or unhappy) builds new customers and improves negotiation. The contractor has no marketing function at all.

### YT-0EqJD2o-Mnk#06 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:16:00
claim: Run a time study: log what you did every 15 minutes for a week, rank by revenue value, and delegate the bottom half.
detail: Use a phone alarm and a spreadsheet; at week end rank each activity by value and uniqueness, then see whether the lower half fits a current person with bandwidth or a new hire. Premium customers mean premium margins, so raise the bar and pay for better people. If half your time came back, the business could double.
numbers: Interval 15 minutes; one week.

### YT-0EqJD2o-Mnk#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-team | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:42
claim: Keep inbound and outbound sales as separate teams; inbound is a promotion that top closers earn.
detail: Outbound reps go back to the street. Inbound leads are the costliest, so only the best closers get them, ideally one person first, so you iterate on one variable at a time (offer, lead source, lead-to-talk process). Inbound pays a different commission structure (lead cost, lower per sale) but is steadier, less stressful and indoors. Never have too many "new" things at once.

### YT-0EqJD2o-Mnk#11 | CASE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: outreach | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:24:09
claim: A $1M junk-removal firm selling to commercial property managers should pick the channel that matches its skills, and any of them can work.
detail: Current growth is referral only; they are starting cold email, LinkedIn, social outreach and BNI networking. Hormozi rejects the premise that one channel will work and others will not: someone in each channel already wins. Choose the one with the most skill overlap. Here the owner is best in person, so find every local event of 20-50 people with similar avatars and move from a few events a week to several a day. That is the risk-adjusted move, frees cash flow, and then funds learning a second channel.
numbers: Revenue $1M, goal $10M; events: from a few per week to several per day.
