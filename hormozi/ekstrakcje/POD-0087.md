### POD-0087#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:26
claim: Do not open a second location until the first passes four gates: high net profit for six months, absent owner, growth during absence, and personal cash needs already covered.
detail: Gym Lords criteria: net profit of $20,000 a month for six months running; owner truly away (five hours a week, ten at most, not 40 hours of thinking and messaging); gym grew during that time; personal money needs already met. Savings piled up are no reason to expand, since opening drains them while liabilities double.
numbers: $20,000/month net for 6 consecutive months; owner time 5 hours/week, 10 maximum.
applies_to: Single-location gym or local-service owners considering expansion.

### POD-0087#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Owners usually open extra locations out of status, not arithmetic, and status cannot pay rent.
detail: What grows status (more locations) often works against what grows the bank account. Hormozi did the status route with his own gyms and calls it a mistake.
numbers: Owners often start scouting a second site about 12 weeks after a turnaround.

### POD-0087#03 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:37
claim: Typical multi-location slide: each new site dilutes owner attention, so profit per site falls until all sites roughly break even.
detail: Owner nets about $10,000 a month from one gym at a 25% margin. Attention splits, and a 25% sales drop is normal going from owner-operated to remote. The second site does about two thirds of the first, both near break-even, so a third gets opened. The fix is closing sites.
numbers: Single site $10,000/month net at 25% margin; second site about two thirds of the first's result; about 25% sales fall when going remote.

### POD-0087#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: verticals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:02
claim: Gym owners with three or four locations are, in his view, probably stressed and probably not making much.
detail: Owners with four locations calling in are, nine in ten, strapped for cash. Typical path: one site netting $10-15K, then two netting about $2,500 each, then three at break-even. Owners who consolidated earned more.
numbers: About 9 in 10 owners with four locations who call in say they are strapped; $10-15K net from one site falls to roughly $2,500 each across two.
### POD-0087#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:20
claim: In a fixed-cost business like a gym, the next dollar of revenue in an existing site carries a far higher margin than the first, so stacking in one site beats a second site.
detail: A gym at $30,000 revenue with $20,000 overhead nets $10,000. Another $10,000 of revenue might cost about $2,500 to serve. A second facility would need roughly $28-29K revenue to yield the same extra $7,500.
numbers: +$10,000 revenue at about $2,500 cost = 75% margin; second site needs about $28-29K revenue for the same $7,500 profit.
applies_to: Fixed-cost local businesses.

### POD-0087#06 | BENCHMARK | topic: verticals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:22
claim: Once gyms showed single sites reaching $83K, $100K and $120K a month of recurring revenue, owners believed vertical stacking was possible.
detail: A $100,000-a-month single gym beats two at $40,000 each, because more of its revenue falls through as margin.
numbers: Single gyms at $83,000, $100,000, $120,000/month recurring revenue; compared with 2 x $40,000.

### POD-0087#07 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:22
claim: Before expanding, ask whether this is really the best you can do and the best business you can possibly make.
detail: If the answer is no, improve the current business first; tie ego to making one site excellent, not to site count.
### POD-0087#08 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:15
claim: If two locations earn less in total than one did, the model was weaker than believed; in his opinion, cut back to one and refocus.
detail: Even with shared admin labour and software, two sites should earn about double. If not, close sites, refocus on one, and decide later from abundance.
