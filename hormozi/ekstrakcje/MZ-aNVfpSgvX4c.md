### MZ-aNVfpSgvX4c#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: lead-getters | also: product-delivery | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:51
claim: Affiliates activate only when product, incentive, timing and low friction all hold, and product quality matters most.
detail: If he had to order them: product first, incentive second, and timing and friction probably tied for third. An affiliate risks social capital by recommending, so a mediocre product fails. Affiliate strategy is rarely the reason something grows; Dropbox and PayPal were strong products wrapped in an incentive.
### MZ-aNVfpSgvX4c#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:52
claim: TikTok Shop works because friction and incentive are zero: creators already post and the commission is built in.
detail: Asking people to complete nine steps to become an affiliate kills activation. He notes shop products are physical only.

claim: Build a creator ad army from a school community of micro creators, paid from a pot proportional to ad spend.
detail: Invite interesting micro creators, commit a monthly ad budget to using only their ads, and split a pot among them in proportion to the spend their ads earn. The exact pot sizing was stated as $100,000 spend and a $10,000 pot (unclear in audio).

### MZ-aNVfpSgvX4c#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: retention-ltv | also: lead-getters | fw: Point of Greatest Deprivation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:17
claim: Ask for the referral when the customer is satisfied but not deprived; if they need the next thing, make the upsell instead.
detail: The two asks sometimes fall at the same moment, and when a solved problem exposed a bigger one, the upsell makes more sense. Steak example: a diner who loved the meal does not want another steak, so ask who else lacks it.
