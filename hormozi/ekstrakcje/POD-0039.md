### POD-0039#01 | CASE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:46
claim: Two gym partners got big results and still asked 20 questions to compare Hormozi with another coach before joining his top programme; he withdrew the offer.
detail: They ran Gym Launch twice, earning about $30,000 and then $40,000, and went from near closing to out of debt in two or three months. His offer to join the premium group was one time only, and when they later asked (about eight months afterwards) he declined again.
numbers: Results: roughly $30,000 first run, $40,000 second run; clients typically 2x to 4x revenue within months; the group then had 300 members (the pair would have been about the tenth).

### POD-0039#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:23
claim: When a client's response to a great result is abnormal, treat it as a red flag that their decision-making is broken.
detail: If your inputs produce unpredictable outputs, it becomes really difficult to create a successful outcome for them because your definitions of success differ. They won't be happy and you'll be miserable; give yourself the okay not to work with them and don't let it bug you.
### POD-0039#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:59
claim: A one-time offer must really be one-time, for three reasons.
detail: (1) Going back on your word loses clients' respect, since you ask them to keep theirs; (2) you lose self-respect, feeling you bend for money; (3) you can no longer say with conviction that it is a one-time invitation. He adds that radical honesty is the business principle that has carried him furthest.

### POD-0039#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: sales-team | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:07
claim: As volume grows you will meet difficult people; after about 20 walk-ins, one is nearly certain.
detail: Asked whether about 5% of people have serious issues, he agreed. Such clients absorb coaches' time and attention and can become a cancer in the business. Move on without self-blame, being truthful that it is not a fit. If everyone leaves you, though, the problem may be you.
numbers: About 5% of people have serious issues (the other speaker's figure, he agreed); one among about 20 walk-ins.
### POD-0039#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:22
claim: Judge a past decision by the quality of the process given the information you had, not by the result.
detail: A sound process with incomplete data is not stupidity; this view helps get out of regret.
