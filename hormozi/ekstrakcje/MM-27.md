### MM-27#03 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: money-models, sales-process | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:03
claim: A Gym Launch client closed 70–80% of walk-ins at full membership price by raising his challenge price and then giving the challenge free to anyone who joined as a member.
detail: Same ads and copy as everyone else; only the in-person presentation changed. Gyms want everyone on memberships, but nobody buys a membership outright, hence 21-/28-day transformations and six-week challenges as entry points (structured onboarding also gives a better experience). He found it by accident when a prospect couldn't afford the challenge — most sales innovations start that way. It also feels generous: a valuable gift plus member-only benefits.
numbers: 70–80% close rate on walk-ins.

### MM-27#04 | DEFINITION | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:24
claim: A continuity bonus offer gives the customer something great for signing up to continuity today — typically worth more than the first continuity payment.
detail: Gym: a $600–800 challenge free with a $200/month membership; newsletter: $24,000 of courses for trialling a $400/month subscription. Product bonuses: many small items or one big complementary product. Service bonuses: a defined programme, onboarding, setup or added features. Bonuses add good stuff; discounts remove bad stuff (anything given free can instead be discounted) — use both. Add urgency: only if they join now. The bonus can also be sold standalone or be available only with continuity.
numbers: Gym challenge $600–800 vs $200/month membership (first figure unclear in audio).

### MM-27#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:22
claim: Continuity alone brings little cash now, so stack it after cash-heavy offers and then sell a prepay — that keeps 30-day profit goals intact.
detail: Sequence: attraction offer (e.g., buy X get Y free, win your money back) → upsells → downsells, so you are already profitable. Then a soft continuity pitch with bonuses to get the yes and the first month's payment. Once committed, offer a discount for prepaying more months, pulling cash forward and stacking recurring revenue. Front-loaded cash funds more advertising.

### MM-27#06 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:08
claim: Continuity bonus examples for physical, service and digital businesses — one-time bonuses get them to buy, recurring bonuses get them to stay.
detail: Pet food at $59/month: every dog toy ever made free ($800 value) on signup, plus a new toy each month. Service: a $1,000 short-term accelerator free with a $100/month membership; VIP members also get first access to events, longer support hours, better reps and faster responses. Digital: 40 past newsletters valued at $15,800 for joining at $399/month after a 30-day free trial; pay today to lock $299/month for life plus early digital access and a monthly physical copy.
numbers: $59/mo + $800 toys; $1,000 accelerator with $100/mo; $15,800 back issues, $399 vs $299/mo.

### MM-27#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: paid-ads, offers | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:42
claim: Advertise the free bonus, not the membership — explain the continuity only once they show interest.
detail: "Join my membership" is weak; "get this valuable thing free" pulls. Advertise the $800 of free dog toys, not the dog food; the six-week challenge, not the gym membership. In person, present the $1,000 item, then reveal they can get it and more for less by joining.

### MM-27#08 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:42
claim: Choose bonuses like upsells: more of the same, something complementary, or an upgrade.
detail: More of the same: two years of past newsletters for joining a newsletter. Complementary: free nutrition services with a fitness membership. Upgrade: a free gold-tier membership, limited availability.

### MM-27#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:29
claim: Keep bonuses closely related to the core offer, or you attract the wrong customers.
detail: A free t-shirt to sell tech services draws the wrong leads; a free t-shirt to sell t-shirt printing fits. For tech services, a free "get out of jail" server fix fits naturally. Free dog toys attract dog owners for dog products.

### MM-27#10 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: product-delivery | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:29
claim: Build bonuses from things you already have or already do, and put a price on them.
detail: Past newsletters cost no extra time but carry high value. Onboarding must happen anyway, so price it and give it as a bonus — if you value it, they will. Defined programmes and challenges are mostly fancy onboarding. This way the business needn't change to deliver the bonus.

### MM-27#11 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:19
claim: Cross physical and digital: give physical bonuses with digital memberships and digital bonuses with physical products or services.
detail: Digital membership: a hat, shirt, tool or boxing gloves. Boxing gym membership: livestreamed sessions. Hormozi finds the crossover works exceptionally well.

### MM-27#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:07
claim: A good bonus usually cuts the cost of acquiring a customer by more than the bonus costs — even if some people take it and leave.
detail: Losses on bonus-and-run customers are made up on everyone else. If the bonus becomes too expensive, change the offer and test again, but adding a bonus usually nets more money long term.

### MM-27#13 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:07
claim: Value bonuses realistically — a bigger anchor sells more, but only if believable; ideally use products you have actually sold at that price.
detail: Made-up values fail to anchor and destroy trust. Best: "I used to sell this for $2,000 — here are the receipts — want it free when you sign up for $200?" Real prices make the bonus and discount ethical and credible.
numbers: $2,000 past product free with a $200 signup.

### MM-27#14 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:08
claim: Give status titles at tenure milestones, placing the first just past your typical churn point and spacing later ones further apart.
detail: Titles like silver, gold, diamond, double diamond, with small perks. Knowing an upgrade is coming pulls people through continuity. If most churn at two months, put the first title at 2.5–3 months, the next around month 8, then a year — like video-game levels that take longer as you climb. A friend found customers valued the title more than any other bonus and introduced themselves by it ("I'm double diamond"). Status is the cheapest thing you can give and often the most valued.
numbers: Milestones e.g. ~3, 8, 12 months.

### MM-27#15 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:10
claim: Free bonuses and steep discounts are interchangeable framings of the same continuity offer.
detail: "Become a member for $200 and get the $1,000 programme free" equals "get the $1,000 programme for $1 when you become a member for $200." Pick whichever suits the offer.

### MM-27#16 | SCRIPT | topic: money-models | also: sales-process, objections-closing | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:10
claim: Sell the bonus seriously as a standalone offer first, then ask if they want to know how to get it free, then stack member benefits with dollar values.
detail: Moves: (1) Sell the bonus's benefits as if it were the product — if you don't sell it seriously, they won't consider it seriously (00:20:43). (2) Use its high price as an anchor, like the anchor upsell. (3) "Want to know how you can get this for free?" (4) "Become a VIP member today and it's a free gift — or buy it alone for X. Which do you prefer?" (5) "On top of that, members get thing one, two, three," each with a dollar value. Buyer logic: $1,000 alone vs free with $200/month means breaking even around month five plus membership benefits. A $300 bonus against $200/month makes many just buy the $300.

### MM-27#17 | BENCHMARK | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:14:56
claim: In tested gym sales, people paid about a 33% premium to avoid continuity: a standalone price 1.33x the monthly continuity price split buyers 50/50.
detail: Standalone price (per-month equivalent) vs monthly continuity price → share choosing continuity: 1.33x → 50%, 1.66x → 60%, 2x → 70%, 2.3x → 80%, 2.66x → 90%, ~3x → almost everyone. Example: $400 for a six-week programme (~$266/month) vs $199/month membership splits about 50/50. Tested across many brick-and-mortar fitness locations; Hormozi bets it transfers, especially to services. The exact numbers matter less than the principle.
numbers: 1.33x/50%, 1.66x/60%, 2x/70%, 2.3x/80%, 2.66x/90%, 3x/~100%.

### MM-27#18 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics, pricing | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:17:19
claim: Set the gap between the standalone bonus price and continuity by how much upfront cash you need to cover CAC.
detail: Smaller gap → more standalone buyers (more cash now); bigger gap → more continuity, but each standalone sale brings more cash. E.g., 70% on continuity with 30% paying 2x still yields good upfront cash. For more cash, sell the bonus separately at 33–266% above the first month of continuity-plus-bonus; for half to buy standalone, price it 33% above. Small and local businesses that advertise especially need this. If unsure, start with the bonus available standalone plus month-to-month continuity (00:19:42).

### MM-27#19 | BENCHMARK | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Buy X Get Y Free | novelty: canon | ts: 00:18:10
claim: A bulk prepay upsell on continuity ("buy five months, get one free") lifts 30-day cash by 50% if only one in eight members takes it.
detail: The usual discount law applies: a richer deal (buy six, get six) gets many more takers and could double or triple upfront cash. This turns continuity into another upfront-cash play that offsets acquisition cost and funds advertising.
numbers: 1 in 8 taking buy-5-get-1 → +50% 30-day profit.

### MM-27#20 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:18:57
claim: Pair the bonus with a commitment — free only if they join for 3, 6 or 12 months — trading fewer takers for locked-in term.
detail: Example: buy the $500 item alone, or get it free with a six-month commitment at $200/month ($1,200 committed; $2,400 for a year). Fewer accept than with no-commitment continuity, but each yes is an extended term worth more than the $500.
numbers: $500 bonus for $1,200 (6 months) or $2,400 (12 months) commitment.
