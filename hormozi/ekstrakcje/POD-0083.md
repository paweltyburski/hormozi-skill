### POD-0083#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:02
claim: Casual check-ins with customers feel like service but are the owner's biggest time drain; service needs structured communication cycles.
detail: He talks only to favorite customers because it feels good, which hurts the business and opens lines that bounce back as service issues the owner should not handle. Structure makes service scalable, trackable and systemisable. Trainers do the same unless contact is their role.

### POD-0083#02 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:52
claim: Use a stand-in assistant to route messages until you have a real one.
detail: Tell staff you no longer check social messages. The assistant replies as himself or herself, thanks the sender, and routes by type (customer service, billing, tech). Interested prospects go to the service team, not the founder; a call with everyone would end the business.
numbers: Gym Launch had an eight-person service team (2018).

### POD-0083#03 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:32
claim: Delete the Facebook app and use the mobile browser, checking only notifications and never the news feed.
detail: The browser version is clunky, which removes the dopamine hooks that drag you back; within a day of reinstalling the app he is refreshing constantly. Notifications serve as the inbox.

### POD-0083#04 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:33
claim: Hand your phone to your first admin and use a new number to wall yourself off.
detail: He calls this his biggest productivity jump. Early customers had his number and expected personal replies; the number should go to someone who can give real attention. Even his mother got the new number only after months.
numbers: about nine months before his mother received the number.
