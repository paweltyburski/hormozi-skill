### YT-zBZHWrvjD8Y#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Goals are set as a science-style hypothesis in four steps: what problem are we solving, what is our hypothesis, what will we do, and what do we think will happen (did we do it and did it happen).
detail: The first three steps form the before phase: name the problem, state the hypothesis, then define one input (the activity) and one output (the expected change) with baselines. The after phase has two columns reviewed at quarter or year end: did the input happen, and did the output follow, with commentary.
### YT-zBZHWrvjD8Y#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A goal is only worth doing if it ties to getting more customers, making customers worth more, or raising enterprise value.
detail: If a department cannot say how its goal serves one of those three, it does not understand its job. IT examples: faster page loads bring more leads, and a better sign-on reduces churn so customers are worth more.

### YT-zBZHWrvjD8Y#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: sales-process | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:32
claim: Worked hypothesis: cutting lead response time raises the share of leads who schedule, but the result must be checked.
detail: Baseline input was about 37 minutes to call a lead, goal 5 minutes; the output goal was scheduled calls rising from 50% to 70%. The team only reached about 7 minutes and saw no improvement in scheduling, so there was no reason to push to 5.
numbers: Call time 37 min to 5 min target; scheduled rate 50% to 70% target; actual 7 min with no change in schedule rate.

### YT-zBZHWrvjD8Y#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: More Better New | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:12
claim: Big companies grow through many small improvements across the funnel, which is hard for copycats to see; early on, measuring a metric can itself be the quarter's goal.
detail: He sketches stacking gains: more opt-ins by 10%, scheduling by 15%, shows by 20%, close rate from 25% to 35%, which he says together about triples the business (his arithmetic is rounded; the product is nearer double). Systems like this matter most around $5M-$20M revenue.
numbers: Opt-in +10%, schedule +15%, show +20%, close 25% to 35%; range $5M-$20M top line.

### YT-zBZHWrvjD8Y#05 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: content-brand, hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:55
claim: Have every employee, down to individual contributors, run the same hypothesis format so they see how their work ties to growth.
detail: Example: a video editor's problem is getting more customers; hypothesis is doubling the number of shorts will bring more traffic from organic content. At quarter end check whether they made twice as many and whether link clicks rose. If clicks did not, form a new hypothesis such as story-based content.
