### YT-yPDQCfrwh8E#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: Activation Points | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:33
claim: Retention point example from TV: viewers who reach episode three of a hit series are about 87% likely to finish all five seasons; find the equivalent moment in your product and pull it forward.
detail: Break the outcome into small chunks with visible leading indicators of progress. Each reinforcement counts.
numbers: 87% completion after the third episode (as stated for Breaking Bad).

### YT-yPDQCfrwh8E#02 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: GROWTH Onboarding | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:40
claim: Send the client a daily note of what you did for them rather than a report every two weeks, to give 14 positive interactions in two weeks instead of one.
detail: The work stays the same; only communication changes, so the client perceives faster time to value.
numbers: 14 versus 1 interaction per two-week cycle.

### YT-yPDQCfrwh8E#03 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: Activation Points | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:00
claim: Gym Launch activation: a high-ticket sale by the client within seven days tripled LTV, so everyone was pushed to get it fast; later the measure was how quickly the client launched an email campaign for us.
detail: Because LTV went from about $17,000 to about $50,000, roughly $33,000 of mostly profit supports heavy resources to get that one action, such as sending the email on the call together with a tech helper.
numbers: LTV $17,000 to $50,000 when the action occurs; first high-ticket sale within 7 days triples LTV.

### YT-yPDQCfrwh8E#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:48
claim: Keeping a customer for 3 more months costs far less than buying a new one: about $60 of staff time versus about $1,000 of ad spend, roughly 17x more profitable.
detail: Staff time: two 30-minute calls a month at $20 an hour, over three months. Spend effort in the cheapest return bucket first, then move to the less efficient ones.
numbers: $60 labour vs $1,000 paid acquisition for the same revenue.

### YT-yPDQCfrwh8E#06 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: management-ops | fw: Customer Journey | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:34
claim: Replace aimless check-ins with scripted calls that name the problem customers usually have at that week of the program ("most people at this point are at A or B, where are you?").
detail: This reads as proactive and valuable, and builds confidence in the vendor. It requires knowing the activation milestone.

### YT-yPDQCfrwh8E#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:20
claim: Do not offer unlimited free community or access after the paid programme: it creates an endless liability for one-time income and removes the deadline that pushes clients to act.
detail: Install a known cost at the end so clients move faster. If you can use it whenever, you do it never.

### YT-yPDQCfrwh8E#08 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Problem-Solution Cycle | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:50
claim: Solve the promised problem first, then introduce the next one yourself, which you know better than the customer does (weight loss leads to loose skin, new wardrobe, then a six-pack program).
detail: Meet them where they are; offering the six-pack at the start loses people who only want their old weight back. Most businesses never solve the first problem well enough to earn the next sale.

### YT-yPDQCfrwh8E#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:03
claim: What is not measured is not improved, and metrics make incentives possible: tie individual and team rewards to the milestone you want clients to reach.
detail: The quality and quantity of data a team tracks predicts how good it is; this company tracked almost none. At $100,000 to $1M a year, missing pieces are normal.

### YT-yPDQCfrwh8E#10 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: GROWTH Onboarding | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:19
claim: Clients form a lasting impression in the first 24 hours after buying, so open and close four to six small promises in that window.
detail: Split one task with six checkpoints into six separate promises, each fulfilled. A touch-base "in four days" is far too slow.
numbers: 4-6 promises kept within 24 hours.

### YT-yPDQCfrwh8E#11 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: sales-process | fw: BAMFAM | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:04
claim: Onboarding fix, four parts: align sales and customer success with a formal handoff (book the next meeting from each meeting), pass the client's stated goal from the sales call to success, reset expectations to match the sale, then outline the steps.
detail: Success reiterates the buyer's own reason ("you want to retire your wife", or a wedding coming up). Re-sell the purchase with stories of similar people. Early cold feet and refund requests usually come from a gap between the first and second conversation.

### YT-yPDQCfrwh8E#12 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:59
claim: Install a CRM tracking time to value and churn automatically, watch leading indicators (missed gym visits, not logging in), and automate satisfaction surveys by rep.
detail: Churn is lagging and cannot be fixed directly. Rep-level scores show who drags the team down; knowing they will be surveyed also keeps reps prepared. Surveys at week 0, 2, 4, 8, 12 risk fatigue.
numbers: Survey points: weeks 0, 2, 4, 8 and 12 (possible).

### YT-yPDQCfrwh8E#13 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:10
claim: Pay customer-success staff commission weighted by progress on target metrics, since much of profit comes from retention.
detail: Order: CRM, then metrics, then target range, then commission scaled to position in the range: 100% of target pays 2X, 50% pays X, 0% pays nothing. Example: a client reaching week 12 of 16 counts as 75%.
numbers: 100% = 2X, 50% = X, 0% = 0; 12/16 weeks = 75%.

### YT-yPDQCfrwh8E#14 | BENCHMARK | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Ascension Path | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:19:34
claim: A paid wall at week 16 (pay to stay), rolled out gradually, got 10% of customers to ascend at once and 27% within 60 days.
detail: Little extra cost beyond the CRM and the success commission. Doubling LTV at the same CAC can allow about 10x the marketing, not just 2x.
numbers: Ascension 10% then 27%; backend $140,000 in 60 days.
