### YT-_ArQlwPvGUA#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Doubling the price lifts profit sixfold, while doubling customers only doubles it and doubling retention mainly raises LTV.
detail: Business: 100 customers at $100 a month, $8,000 cost, $2,000 profit. Doubling customers gives $4,000 profit; doubling price keeps costs, giving $12,000 profit; doubling retention eventually reaches the same revenue as the customer option, but allows a larger acquisition spend.
numbers: Baseline $10,000 revenue, $8,000 cost, $2,000 profit; price x2 gives $12,000 profit (6x); customers x2 gives $4,000.

### YT-_ArQlwPvGUA#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:34
claim: In his example, where doubling the price gives 6x profit per customer, selling a third fewer (which he says happens sometimes) still roughly triples the business's profit; he says to test price because it is the strongest of the three levers.
detail: He says that more times than not demand does not drop in the same proportion as the price rise. People test pages, scripts and offers but forget price, which he calls the strongest lever.
numbers: Price x2 with one third fewer sales: profit ~3x in his example (6x profit per customer).
### YT-_ArQlwPvGUA#03 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: Price to Value Discrepancy | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A $5M business doubled profit in under 8 weeks by raising price 50% after market research showed the market would bear it, with no product change.
detail: Research of competitors suggested the market could support a 50% higher price. More people bought, because a higher price signals value and fit the owner's brand.
numbers: Price +50%; profit doubled in < 8 weeks; business size $5M.

### YT-_ArQlwPvGUA#04 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:34
claim: Raise prices with a survey first, then internal communication, then customers, then new buyers.
detail: Step 1: survey existing customers on demographics, purchase trigger and four price-perception questions (too expensive, too cheap, a stretch, a bargain), plus which part of the value stack they would keep if only one remained and which they would not miss. Step 2: tell the team. Step 3: tell customers why (past, present, future). Step 4: tell the list with urgency, then the new price goes live. Expect fewer sales while the pipeline clears, and let it settle before judging.
numbers: Example: $1,200 for a 12-week program, test $2,000-$3,000.
