### POD-0834#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:26
claim: Sort decisions by reversibility; most are reversible, and the cost of waiting usually exceeds the cost of reversing.
detail: Uses Bezos's one-way versus revolving door idea. A leader's indecision slows chains of contingent decisions across the company; every decision taking 20-50% longer compounds. He admits his own past mistake of demanding too much data.
numbers: decisions delayed 20% or 50% longer, across contingent chains.

### POD-0834#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:08:52
claim: Decision matrix: if reversible and both options would probably get you there, pick one immediately, since speed outweighs a small edge.
detail: Take option B as 10% better than A: choosing A instantly rather than B after a week outperforms it by far, and speed across hundreds of decisions beats 5-10% gains. He calls the small word-swapping debates "happy to glad" (from government contracting proposals).
numbers: option B 10% better; delay of one week.

### POD-0834#03 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent, operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:14
claim: Reduce the fear of deciding by labelling calls openly as "my best bad decision" and promising not to punish quick mistakes.
detail: He roots slow decisions in fear of looking dumb. He tells the team he expects to be wrong sometimes, wants errors on flippable calls, and wants no griping when something is reversed. Teach decisiveness as a core skill and delegate decisions.
numbers: target about 80% right; 20% wrong is acceptable (his Musk-based framing); judge by decision batting average.
