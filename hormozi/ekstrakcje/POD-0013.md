### POD-0013#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: Leverage | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: If your time is worth more than the roughly $10-$15 an hour you could pay someone else to do the task, stop doing the task yourself and use the time for selling.
detail: He says most gym owners who still train sessions are doing it to save money. He asks them whether their time is worth more than $15 an hour, and if so, why they are selling it at that rate.
numbers: Hiring someone for the task: about $10-$15 per hour.
applies_to: Single-location gym owners.
### POD-0013#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: management-ops | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:18
claim: One $500 sale per week more than pays for the trainer who covers your afternoon sessions.
detail: Three afternoon sessions at about $15 each, loaded to about $20 with payroll taxes in California, is $60 per afternoon or $300 per week. So two weeks of the replacement's pay is $600; the owner needs to close just one or two $500 programmes in the freed time. If you cannot close two in a week, learn the skill first.
numbers: $20 loaded cost x 3 sessions = $60 per afternoon; $300 per week; one $500 sale covers a week.

### POD-0013#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: sales-process | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:55
claim: With daily leads, an owner who meets ten prospects in an afternoon and closes six on a $500 fixed-length programme earns about $3,000 for those hours.
detail: He contrasts that with four training sessions costing about $60 to staff. Newly hired staff also take about two weeks to receive a first paycheck, which gives the owner time to earn the replacement's pay out of the freed hours.
numbers: 10 appointments, 6 closes, $500 price, six to eight week programme = $3,000 an afternoon.

### POD-0013#04 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:30
claim: Outsource in this order: training first, then admin and billing, then sales, then promote the front-desk or sales person into a general manager, leaving the owner with marketing.
detail: Single-location owners should end up responsible for marketing and selling; learn each job first so that you can teach it your way, then hand it off. Each step is putting yourself out of that job.
applies_to: Single-location service businesses.

### POD-0013#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:21
claim: Never outsource offer, creative and copy; at most hand off the mechanical placement of ads, ideally to someone with equity or on staff.
detail: An agency will not keep tweaking until it works because it is not their business. He had never seen a friend who crushes it with an outside marketing company running everything; they have an employee or equity partner who only markets.
