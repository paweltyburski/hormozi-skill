### MZ-t7aSRUZXEMQ#01 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: verticals, scaling-constraints | fw: Cost to Acquire Talent | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A dental group needs orthodontists but pays nothing to recruit them, although each earns about $400k gross profit a year.
detail: Four offices, fifth opening; constraint is trained doctors who fit the culture, then leads. He would pay $50k, then $100k. Hormozi: use headhunters, agencies, specialist forums, pay community owners.
numbers: Revenue $15M, goal $50M; orthodontist gross profit $400k a year; offer $50k-$100k.

### MZ-t7aSRUZXEMQ#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: hiring-talent | also: unit-economics | fw: Cost to Acquire Talent | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:36
claim: With a six-year average tenure an orthodontist is worth $2.4M gross profit, so even breaking even at $400k could pay.
detail: Factor in risk of early departure. All businesses are demand-constrained, including for talent.
numbers: $400k x 6 years = $2.4M.

### MZ-t7aSRUZXEMQ#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:19
claim: A portfolio company paid ten recruiting agencies $10,000 per hire to build a 50-person in-house sales team that added $5M a month.
detail: Company grew from $2M a year to $10M a month in 18 months; outsourced sales were brought in-house in 90 days, hiring 80 and keeping 50. Total spend about $500k.
numbers: 10 agencies; $10,000 per hire; $500k total; +$5M a month.

### MZ-t7aSRUZXEMQ#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:53
claim: Pay for speed either in profit or in growth rate, and treat hires as enterprise value.
detail: Five orthodontists at $50k each cost $250k, add about $1M EBITDA and at a roughly 10x group multiple about $10M of enterprise value. Costing nothing so far is why they are stuck.
numbers: 5 hires x $50k = $250k; extra EBITDA about $1M; multiple about 10x; about $10M value.
