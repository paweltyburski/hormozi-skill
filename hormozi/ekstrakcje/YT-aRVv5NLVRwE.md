### YT-aRVv5NLVRwE#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A dollar invested early can be worth much more than a later one: his illustration is that $1 extra at age 25 might be $90 by 70, only a third as much if invested at 35 and a ninth as much at 45.
detail: His claim is that compounding on excess cash in early career outpaces growth in earning power, though not that all income should go into an index fund. The point is that dollars put to work earlier get disproportionate returns.
numbers: $1 at 25 = ~$90 at 70 ("might be"); at 35 = ~1/3 of that; at 45 = ~1/9 (his illustration, not verified).
### YT-aRVv5NLVRwE#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:38
claim: Youth multiplies the reputation, press, mentors, network and capital access that any win earns, and the multiplier fades around age 30.
detail: Skills also compound because early repetitions build advantage (Bill Gates started coding young). Example: he wrote a $500,000 check to an 18-year-old who had built an e-commerce business doing $30M a year and wanted to build a tool for such stores; the age made the achievement more convincing. The kid had found his content at 15, so it took three years.
numbers: $500,000 investment; $30M/year e-commerce business; multiplier decays by ~30.

### YT-aRVv5NLVRwE#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:09
claim: Early success is a stepping stone: each business funds and informs a larger next one.
detail: His ladder: getting fit, then personal and online training clients, then one gym, then several gyms, then Gym Launch, whose sale funded the family office. The steps are fixed; the choice is how soon you start.

### YT-aRVv5NLVRwE#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:01
claim: The young have more energy, fewer obligations and fewer location constraints, so they can go where the reps are and reinvest more aggressively.
detail: Go to the places where your field's learning happens (politics in DC, finance in New York, media in LA) before kids and fixed routes tie you down. It is harder to leave an inherited path later, and the most adaptable player wins a new system. Model the rule (the richest got rich young), not exceptions such as Colonel Sanders starting at 63.

### YT-aRVv5NLVRwE#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:18
claim: Reached goals feel reasonable only because the work was already done, so success requires doing work that seems unreasonable at the time.
detail: Example: his 315-pound bench press seemed obvious once he had done 275 for five reps and 295 for three. Winners differ in actions, risk tolerated and pain endured over long periods with uncertain payoff, not in goals. New goals get planted before old ones are finished, so there is no final win.
