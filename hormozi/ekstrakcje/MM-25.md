### MM-25#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: offers, objections-closing | fw: Feature Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A friend raised his close rate from 25% to 75% by offering his $4,000 programme for $2,000 without the guarantee — many then chose the full-price version.
detail: When prospects said they couldn't afford $4,000, he offered the same thing minus the guarantee at $2,000. Seeing the price gap made the guarantee's value obvious, so many re-closed themselves on the expensive offer. No payment plan or discount involved.
numbers: Per 100 prospects: before 25 bought at $4,000; after 35 bought at $4,000 and 40 took the $2,000 version (25% → 75% close).

### MM-25#02 | DEFINITION | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Feature Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:46
claim: A feature downsell lowers the price by changing what the customer gets: less quantity, lower quality, a cheaper alternative, or an optional component removed.
detail: It is the only downsell that actually lowers price — payment plans and trials change terms. Formula: take something away, lower the price, and in effect ask "how about now?"

### MM-25#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: pricing, offers | fw: Feature Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:32
claim: Every feature has a price and a value; buyers weigh money saved against value lost, so which feature you cut and how much you drop the price decides which offer they take.
detail: Remove something they love and cut the price a little → a worse deal, so they re-upsell themselves to the original. Remove something they don't care about and cut a lot → a great deal, so they take the downsell. People see the value of what was removed only once they see the price gap. Hence remove features from highest to lowest value.

### MM-25#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: product-delivery | fw: Feature Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:19
claim: Quantity and product-quality downsells: fewer units or sessions, older or cheaper materials.
detail: Product quantity: a one-month supply instead of three. Service quantity: two sessions a month instead of four (also shorter duration or less time). Product quality: older versions, less reliable or lower-status materials — vinyl instead of leather seats (00:03:05).

### MM-25#05 | FRAMEWORK | topic: product-delivery | also: money-models, offers | fw: Feature Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:05
claim: Services have many quality levers — each can be dialled down for a downsell or up for a premium tier — so quantify your own service standards on every one.
detail: Levers: time availability (set office hours vs any time; some days vs all; 9–5 vs 24h); length (15- vs 60-minute calls); location (one vs all sites); cancellation (reschedule fee vs free); response speed (minutes/hours/days, e.g., overnight instead of five minutes); delivery speed (queue, priority, same day, next week); service ratio (one-to-many, one-to-one, many-to-one); channel (tech/chat/video support); provider (owner, veteran, junior); live vs recorded; in-person vs remote; DIY/DWY/DFY; expiration (forever vs limited uses or times); personalisation; guarantee length, coverage and conditionality. Customers need not see the list; you peel items back when downselling.

### MM-25#06 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: - | fw: Feature Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:53
claim: You can remove a whole feature rather than reduce its quantity or quality, framing the saving as passed on to the customer.
detail: Example: instead of priority chat, email and calls, keep chat and email and drop calls — "you'll still get your answers, it saves us time, and we pass the savings to you." The guarantee removal in the opening case is the same move.

### MM-25#07 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: product-delivery, verticals | fw: Feature Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:53
claim: After every service downsell fails, offer a do-it-yourself product that solves the same problem — though most service businesses can usually skip this.
detail: Chiropractor: home tools (massage tools, foam rollers, mats) instead of adjustments. Painter: paint plus daily rental of a spray machine. Acquisition.com: a two-day workshop instead of the team buying and growing your company. Same as the supplement-store "no-sale sale": a nutrition consult, then a DIY at-home programme. DIY opens new problems the customer may later pay you to solve (00:06:57).

### MM-25#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: money-models | fw: Feature Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:57
claim: Never negotiate price; stay a helpful guide hunting for the best deal for them — that gives you unlimited shots on goal.
detail: "Totally understand, let's figure out something that works for you" offends no one. A transactional "if not that, then this" feels icky. Keep the back-foot, let's-find-a-fit frame through every downsell.

### MM-25#09 | FRAMEWORK | topic: money-models | also: pricing, objections-closing | fw: Feature Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:37
claim: Standard order: first remove something valuable for a small price cut to push them back to the original; if that fails, restore it and remove things they don't value for a big cut; keep going until they buy.
detail: Step 1 is a "hip check": e.g., drop the guarantee and take $4,000 to $3,500 — many re-upsell themselves. Step 2: treat it as a real price issue — put the valuable feature back, remove low-value features, cut the price a lot. Continue removing and lowering until they buy; something beats nothing. Early on you won't know customer preferences; as you serve one avatar, learn what they value most and standardise the removal order (00:07:48). Hormozi especially recommends feature-downselling the guarantee.
numbers: Example step 1: $4,000 → $3,500 without guarantee.

### MM-25#10 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: offers, objections-closing | fw: Feature Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:23
claim: Name only your cheapest package "the minimum", so a prospect who rejects everything can be asked whether they want nothing more than the minimum.
detail: You can name tiers (first/business/economy-style), but Hormozi names just the cheapest. "Minimum" implies they need at least that (from his rental-car story). If all packages are rejected: "So nothing more than the minimum package then?" Very effective, though not always.

### MM-25#11 | DECISION_RULE | topic: objections-closing | also: money-models | fw: Feature Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:23
claim: After two feature changes in a row are refused, check desire on a 1–10 scale; at 8+, switch to payment-plan downselling.
detail: Same temperature check as in payment plans. If they like a feature combination but still can't afford it, ask whether they can't afford it in general or just today; if today, go into payment plans (half now, when do you get paid…) (00:17:06). A lower score means reconfiguring the offer.

### MM-25#12 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: money-models | fw: Feature Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:23
claim: Close each downsell with "Deal?" or "Fair enough?" — framing it as a barter makes reciprocity hard to refuse.
detail: Structure: "I'll meet your terms and remove this; now you meet my price. Fair enough?" After two or three such trades it is hard for anyone to say "no, that's not fair." Hormozi demonstrates the wording and tonality on his podcast The Game, episode 202 — recommended for sales teams doing downsells.

### MM-25#13 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Feature Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:33
claim: Boost DIY downsells by inviting non-buyers to a free orientation and selling the DIY version at the end.
detail: "Even though we're not working together, I still want to help — come to a free orientation tomorrow." At the end, offer the low-cost DIY product solving the same problem, e.g., a home-painter orientation that sells daily machine rental and paint. It takes little of your time and they still pay.

### MM-25#14 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models, unit-economics | fw: Feature Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:24
claim: In Acquisition.com's portfolio, a low-to-mid-priced segment had the second-highest LTV because companies proactively moved under-using customers to cheaper plans — those customers almost never left.
detail: Usually the highest-paying customers have the highest LTV, but this segment's churn was tiny. Companies contacted customers using, say, four of six services and offered just those four at a lower rate. Customers not consuming the product cancel much faster (Hormozi has extensive gym data), so align price with what they use. They switch from "might cancel" to "these people are honest." Doing it on a cancellation call is a last chance; reaching out first earns the goodwill.

### MM-25#15 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: lead-getters, money-models | fw: Feature Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:14:46
claim: The only way to lower price without removing anything is to change what they give you: price plus barter, usually paid in advertising.
detail: Example: $500 offer, prospect can do at most $400 — accept on condition they leave reviews on every site, give testimonials, post daily and introduce two referrals now; $100 is traded for advertising worth more. It also protects pricing integrity: when a $600 member asked why Susan paid $400, Hormozi offered Susan's terms (cleans the gym daily, referred three friends, posts on Facebook daily) and he chose to keep paying $600.
numbers: $500 → $400 for reviews, testimonials, daily posts, 2 referrals; Susan $400 vs $600.
