### LC-12#01 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: sales-team, scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: After missing an outbound goal two quarters running, Hormozi found a hiring funnel needing 40 screening calls per entry-level caller; switching to group interviews beat the goal within six weeks.
detail: Questioning chain: missed → need more calls → need more callers → hires arrive about as fast as others churn. HR showed churn beat industry average, so intake was the problem: 40 screens → 10 → 4 passed on → 1 picked. Fix: group interviews, pass everyone with social skills and work ethic, train the rest.
numbers: 40 → 10 → 4 → 1 hire; goal hit within 6 weeks.

### LC-12#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: scaling-constraints, sales-team | fw: Internal Core Four (Recruiting Mirror) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:18
claim: A business acquires on both sides — customers and employees — and scaling one forces you to scale the other.
detail: More leads need more salespeople, which need a pipeline of salesperson leads. Treat recruiting as an acquisition funnel.

### LC-12#03 | DEFINITION | topic: lead-getters | also: hiring-talent, finance-exit | fw: Lead Getters | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:18
claim: Lead-getting employees are staff you train to get leads the way you did — ads, content, warm or cold outreach.
detail: More of them means more engaged leads and less work for you, and a business that grows without you — far more valuable because it can be sold without you.

### LC-12#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:07
claim: Two owners with identical $5M revenue and $2M profit differ in wealth: the 80-hour "no one does it like me" owner has a high-paying job; the 10–20-hour owner also holds $10M+ of equity.
detail: The first's net worth is his cash; the second's is cash plus a business that doesn't need him. Acquisition.com looks for the second; Hormozi calls this possibly the most lucrative point in the course.
numbers: $5M revenue, $2M profit; owner-independent ≈ $10M+ equity.

### LC-12#07 | FRAMEWORK | topic: hiring-talent | also: lead-getters | fw: Internal Core Four (Recruiting Mirror) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:12
claim: Recruiting uses the Core Four under other names: warm outreach = asking your network, cold outreach = headhunting, content = role posts from business and personal pages, paid ads = promoted job postings.
detail: There are only four ways people tell others about anything. If you can get customers, you can get talent the same way.

### LC-12#08 | FRAMEWORK | topic: hiring-talent | also: lead-getters | fw: Internal Core Four (Recruiting Mirror) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:19
claim: The lead getters mirror too: customer referrals = employee referrals, affiliates = owners of communities of skilled people, employees = in-house recruiters, agencies = staffing firms and headhunters.
detail: An affiliate might run a community of video editors or salespeople and send you candidates for pay. Hormozi's realization: being "good at customers but bad at talent" makes no sense — both are advertising, one for a job opportunity.

### LC-12#09 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent, lead-getters | fw: Document Demonstrate Duplicate | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:22
claim: Train lead getters by document, demonstrate, duplicate — repeating feedback until they can critique themselves, which is mastery.
detail: Document: do it and checklist it; tip — video yourself and write the checklist afterwards. Demonstrate: do it in front of them. Duplicate: they do it in front of you and get feedback.

### LC-12#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: Document Demonstrate Duplicate | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:35
claim: For entry-level lead-getting hires: if one who has been properly trained is still below expectations three weeks in, cut them.
detail: Entry-level advertising work is simple; it mainly needs meta-skills like grit, which take longer to teach than the task. Hire for grit and teach skills. Quick cuts keep culture high and show the team no one freeloads.
numbers: ~3-week window.

### LC-12#15 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: sales-process | also: unit-economics, paid-ads | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: canon | ts: 00:13:27
claim: When CAC is more than 3x the industry norm, ask whether the engaged leads actually have the problem you solve, plus the budget to pay for it
detail: To find the norm, search "industry average cost to acquire a [category] customer". Within roughly 3x, acquisition probably isn't the issue — if things still struggle, the business model is at fault. Beyond 3x: leads that are not qualified point to an advertising problem; qualified and buying but in small numbers is still advertising (volume); qualified yet not buying points to sales. Applied to each acquisition.com investment, which stops owners blaming staff for problems they created.

### LC-12#16 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: scaling-constraints | fw: A Players | novelty: canon | ts: 00:15:04
claim: Buy or build talent: buying typically costs more but reaches money faster, and as a company scales he has observed it tends to buy more talent than it builds (not a must).
detail: You won't train someone into the CFO of a $100M company. Learning on your dime means mistakes you pay for — "cheap lawyers cost a fortune"; buy nice or buy twice. This lesson's process is for frontline lead getters, not director level and up.
