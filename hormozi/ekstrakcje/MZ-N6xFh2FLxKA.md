### MZ-N6xFh2FLxKA#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: If the product has a clear enterprise use case, hunt whales, not minnows.
detail: The three things to settle are who you sell to, what you charge and how you reach them. Once the buyer is enterprise, price follows from that choice.
applies_to: B2B sellers with long sales cycles.

### MZ-N6xFh2FLxKA#02 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: In an enterprise price conversation, open with the biggest number you can imagine, since you can come down but cannot go up.
detail: The worst outcome is quoting a figure the buyer accepts instantly and then knowing you could have asked for double.
numbers: example of quoting $250,000 and regretting not asking $500,000.

### MZ-N6xFh2FLxKA#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: pricing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:47
claim: Ask "at what price would I not care about the contingencies?" to see whether messy contracts are really a pricing problem.
detail: Heavy concessions on one-off contracts came from charging a price the seller did not care about. The caller said a $10,000 monthly deal is not worth the friction of enterprise selling, while a $1M annual one is. Hormozi judged the seller one deal away from fixing cash flow.
numbers: Caller: $10,000/month not worth it; $1M/year worth it.
