### MZ-ppTHkazcE2Q#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A 40-unit glamping resort owner at ~$2M wants $20M and must choose between scaling the current model and shifting to men's retreats.
detail: Units cap revenue. Hormozi's own pick would be doing more of the current model, but the owner is building a personal brand on retreats and has lead generation available. Because he wants to host them and does not plan to sell the company, the retreat shift is accepted.
numbers: Revenue just under $2M; profit just under $500K; 40 units; goal $20M.

### MZ-ppTHkazcE2Q#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: To judge an organizational change, ask what actually changes in my day-to-day.
detail: For the retreat shift the answer was: leading the retreats personally, hiring and training a sales team, building funnels, and running ads; he said you'd probably need ads to hit the volume, even with some organic reach.
### MZ-ppTHkazcE2Q#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:37
claim: Selling two-day retreats at about $5,000 on existing units could reach $20M and reverse-engineers to $36M at higher cadence.
detail: Hormozi walked the math the day before. Attendees per retreat are not stated here (unclear in audio).
numbers: $5,000 per two-day retreat; $36M a year at two retreats a week.

### MZ-ppTHkazcE2Q#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:37
claim: Whether you plan to sell the company changes the right growth path.
detail: Going from 40 to 400 units is the high-likelihood, sellable bet; the retreat business is harder to sell and "has more hair on the deal". If no sale is planned, the sale question matters less.
### MZ-ppTHkazcE2Q#05 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:27
claim: Ramp a new event business by cadence: one a quarter, then a month, then a week, then two a week.
detail: Scale ads in step with the cadence, and have guests book their next visit before leaving to create repeat revenue.
