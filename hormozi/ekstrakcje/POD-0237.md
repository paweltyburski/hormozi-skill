### POD-0237#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:52
claim: Teams usually need reminding more than teaching, and the most advanced operators never skip the basics.
detail: Gaps are more often things already assigned and not done than things missing. Examples of basics: always follow up with leads, call within five minutes, always email back, onboard in the right sequence. Simple scales; complex breaks.
numbers: Contact leads within 5 minutes.

### POD-0237#02 | DEFINITION | topic: management-ops | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:38
claim: "Happy to glad" changes are edits that change nothing, such as swapping one word for a synonym.
detail: The term comes from construction contracting. Hormozi warns that stories of a tiny tweak tripling a business are rare; most changes do nothing, and often the existing step was never being executed in the first place.

### POD-0237#03 | CASE | topic: sales-process | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:46
claim: Gym Launch found its team was not following up with people who submitted an application but never booked a call.
detail: These prospects could not work out the scheduling step and were simply left. The company then started working those applications. Lesson: a polished acquisition system fails if its basic follow-up is not run.

### POD-0237#04 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:36
claim: When a metric underperforms, first ask basic questions: is the headline curious, does the creative resonate, would anyone want this offer, and what are the basic steps?
detail: He says nine times in ten there are basics undone, and today looks for those before anything advanced.
numbers: About 9 in 10 problems are basics not done.
