### MM-18#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Classic Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A fourth-generation furrier lured customers with free fur earmuffs, then sold summer coat storage and conditioning — about $1.4M a summer.
detail: Storage cost about $200 per coat for roughly 7,000 coats. It was summer, so staff handed over the earmuffs and took them straight back for storage too, then asked "You don't want anything else, do you?" — an assumed close covering storage, conditioning and the earmuffs. Many were existing customers, so it works internally too.
numbers: ~$200 × ~7,000 coats ≈ $1.4M; ~$30 to store earmuffs.

### MM-18#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: You Can't Have X Without Y | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:15
claim: The classic upsell rests on one idea: you can't have X without Y.
detail: No burger without fries, no fur coat without storage and conditioning, no car without servicing. Your upsell is the next natural thing they need (00:11:00).

### MM-18#03 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: money-models, sales-process | fw: No-Sale Sale | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:15
claim: The no-sale sale: after presenting the add-on, ask "You don't want anything else, do you?" — the reflexive "no" confirms the bundle.
detail: People are conditioned to say no to salespeople; "anything else" implies "in addition to what I just offered", so they say yes by saying no.
quote: You don't want anything else, do you?

### MM-18#04 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals, objections-closing | fw: Classic Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:12
claim: Gym prospects who refused every service got a free at-home program and nutrition consult — and there bought supplements at a higher ticket than members.
detail: To salvage goodwill: "I still want to help you for six weeks — here's a thumb drive of home workouts; come back in two days for a free nutrition consult." Many accepted; at the consult the gym closed the same percentage as with members but at a higher ticket. They wanted results without work — a "magic pill" — so offer that solution (while still advising proper eating). Offering something else turned no-sales into sales.
numbers: Avg supplement ticket $200 (service buyers) vs $300 (non-buyers).

### MM-18#05 | DEFINITION | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: Classic Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:46
claim: A classic upsell gives one thing free or cheap and monetises the thing that must go with it.
detail: Variants: A free in exchange for buying B; A free while encouraging B; A free as long as they keep using B. Works for one-time and recurring services (if recurring margins cover both), with free or discounted fronts (00:08:57), on the front end, back end or with existing customers, and is easy even for weak salespeople (00:12:09).

### MM-18#06 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: - | fw: Classic Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:32
claim: Lemonade: give a free cup, then sell lemonade ice cubes and a stainless-steel to-go mug with refill discounts.
detail: Serve it not ice-cold so the ice cubes (cold without dilution) are an upsell; the mug includes a $1 children's-cancer donation and 20% off refills. Every product has a natural upsell.

### MM-18#07 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Classic Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:18
claim: A storage-facility owner showed that a "free month of storage" is worth about $127 once the required lock, boxes, hand trucks, insurance and larger units are sold.
detail: The owner (a gym member across the street) asked Hormozi what a free month costs; his front desk answered "$127". Upsells: a lock — only the facility's own fits, $47; boxes on site; hand-truck rental; insurance — $500 damage included, $1M coverage for $10/month more at ~90% margin ("your stuff must be valuable if you pay to store it"); and bigger units, since people underestimate space and need room to reach things at the back.
numbers: ~$127 per free month; lock $47; insurance +$10/month at ~90% margin.

### MM-18#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: - | fw: Classic Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:05
claim: Customers will buy the companion items anyway — the only question is whether from you or from someone down the street.
detail: As with the rental car and the storage unit, whatever customers must buy to use their purchase is revenue you either capture or hand to another business.

### MM-18#09 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: verticals, unit-economics | fw: Classic Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:05
claim: Vertical examples: supplements at weight-loss orientation, recovery products after free physio, and free coaching tied to required software.
detail: His gym sold supplements at the nutrition orientation, then nutrition plans; supplements needed no delivery, gave instant profit and usually covered acquisition cost before service began. Physical therapy: four free treatments, then orthotics, bands, braces, oils and kinesio tape. Education: free group coaching and community as long as they use your software — the Skool model.
numbers: Orientation supplement sale typically covered CAC.

### MM-18#10 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Classic Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:57
claim: A weight-loss chain charged about $5/month for doctor-supervised services and made its money on roughly $5,000 of supplements and food sold on day two.
detail: The near-free service just drew people in; profit came from supplements, bars, shakes and food.
numbers: ~$5/month services; ~$5,000 supplements on day two.

### MM-18#11 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:57
claim: Pay top performers outside the information business to teach you their craft — few people ask, and it is cheap.
detail: Hormozi paid that chain's top salesperson $500/hour for two hours to explain exactly how she sold supplements; she thought he was crazy. It made him millions.
numbers: $1,000 total → "millions".

### MM-18#12 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: Classic Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:00
claim: If the front item is free it must stay free — never require the upsell — so pick something cheap to give but genuinely valuable.
detail: Strongly encourage the add-on instead of mandating it (e.g., the thumb-drive workouts).

### MM-18#13 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Classic Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:03
claim: Uptake depends on how essential the add-on feels and how frictionless buying it is — make it feel required without making it mandatory.
detail: If supplements are core to results, the likelihood of buying is very high. Mandatory is shady; ethically the add-on should genuinely improve results (weight-loss add-ons: ongoing service, supplements, prepared meals, hormone treatments). Prefer add-ons with little operational drag so they offset acquisition cost immediately or become a main profit stream.

### MM-18#14 | BENCHMARK | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Classic Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:13:18
claim: 80%+ of buyers should take a classic upsell; around 30% means it isn't presented as assumed enough.
detail: It must "go with" the purchase, like fries and a Coke at McDonald's. Give the main thing away and profit on what goes with it.
numbers: Target ≥80% uptake; ~30% = not assumed.
