### YT-ZfaZYFx89UU#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:42
claim: Fortunes come from four types of leverage: labor, capital, code and media; the first two need others' permission, the last two do not.
detail: Labor: buying other people's time; the hardest to manage once you have 50-500+ staff. Capital: other people's money, for example borrowing 100% to buy an asset that doubles in five years, so returns on your own cash are effectively infinite (Buffett, Munger). Code: software where another user costs nothing. Media: owned attention that can be sold repeatedly at no extra cost, gross margin near 100%.

### YT-ZfaZYFx89UU#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:04
claim: Stacking several leverage types (other people's money pays labor that writes code later monetised as media) compounds returns.
detail: Google is both code and media. Hormozi says the biggest fortunes use two or three types. You need not code: a blog, YouTube channel or podcast creates media with no cash cost.

### YT-ZfaZYFx89UU#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: verticals | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:06
claim: Hormozi's earnings jumped when he switched from a flown-in done-for-you service to licensing media, which has zero incremental cost.
detail: Previously he flew out with a sales team to fill brick-and-mortar locations (service, sold time); then moved to licensing media. Margins rose sharply and the cash became the base for building assets.
numbers: At 27 he had $1,036 after selling his gyms and losing the proceeds in a venture; about two years later he had over $17M in the bank.

### YT-ZfaZYFx89UU#04 | DEFINITION | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:46
claim: Money is a transfer medium; wealth is assets that earn without your time, so practitioners must become owners to get rich.
detail: Poor people do the wrong things, rich people the right things, wealthy people own the right things. Example: lawyers, doctors and accountants trading time cannot get super wealthy until they own a practice of many practitioners working for them.
