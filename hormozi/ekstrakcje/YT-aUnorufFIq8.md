### YT-aUnorufFIq8#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: sales-team, hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:46
claim: STAR system: when someone has not done a task, check in order whether they knew you wanted it, what exactly, how to do it, by when, and whether something blocked them, before judging motivation.
detail: Each earlier question offers a non-personal explanation, so the final question about motivation lands only after other causes are removed. Fixes are in order: write it down, define the behaviour or outcome, train, set a deadline, and clear obstacles.

### YT-aUnorufFIq8#02 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:46
claim: Put every request in writing, because an unwritten verbal request is not proven to exist.
detail: Their internal saying: if it isn't written, it never happened. People remember conversations differently, and a written record, even an email afterwards for the timestamp, lets everyone point back to the same thing. Without follow-up, people conclude the task was not important.

### YT-aUnorufFIq8#03 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:40
claim: Define what you want in behaviours or outcomes (format, medium, due date), not labels such as "be nicer" or "stop being creepy."
detail: Clarity takes two or three extra minutes and saves the other person hours. Example: a complaint that a colleague is hitting on someone could describe a friendly wave or something serious; only by asking what exactly happened can you tell. People rarely know what doing a good job looks like because nobody taught them.

### YT-aUnorufFIq8#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:02
claim: Everything is trainable, but not everything is worth training; break "can't do it" down step by step to find the missing step.
detail: Attitude terms like "be kind" hide roughly 500 behaviours, aptitude perhaps 20, so attitude takes longer to train, not impossible. Ask which step they cannot do (open the file, find the data, format it). Usually a person knows eight of nine steps. Some skills are prerequisites, expected on arrival, like English 101 before 201.
numbers: Attitude ~500 underlying behaviours vs aptitude ~20 (his estimate).

### YT-aUnorufFIq8#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: Document Demonstrate Duplicate | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:38
claim: The more skilled the person, the vaguer the instruction can be; the less skilled, the more specific.
detail: Example chain: to a seasoned operator you can say "build a business around this"; to a good marketer, "run a campaign"; to a junior, "write these emails." Chunk the task down until it matches the person. For the training itself he uses the document, demonstrate, duplicate sequence (leads book, pages 207-208).

### YT-aUnorufFIq8#06 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:57
claim: To set a real deadline, ask when it will be done, how long the work itself takes, and what else they have between now and then.
detail: The answers show real workload, and you can say "do that first" when their other task matters more. Hormozi says that, in his opinion, it makes you a way better boss and gives you a better idea of everyone's workload in real time.
### YT-aUnorufFIq8#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:32
claim: Make end of day the default deadline; an end-of-week organisation is far slower than an end-of-day one, and an end-of-month one slower still.
detail: He claims a company defaulting to end of day moves about seven times faster than one defaulting to end of week, and an end-of-month company moves at about one thirtieth of the pace. He links this to why some firms grow many times faster.
numbers: End of day vs end of week ~7x; vs end of month ~30x (his claim).

### YT-aUnorufFIq8#08 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:55
claim: When the excuse is circumstances, test their agency: how hard would it have been to remove the obstacle yourself?
detail: Example: asked for an omelet, the chef says he has no eggs; ask why three days passed without getting eggs. Circumstances are the answer people reach for because it protects the ego. Judge whether the obstacle was real and whether they tried to solve it.
### YT-aUnorufFIq8#09 | DEFINITION | topic: management-ops | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:07
claim: Motivating someone means temporarily raising the relative value of an outcome for them; persuasion and sales are the same act.
detail: He tells of his mother, who tried for 30 years to lose weight, until a doctor said she would not see her grandchildren. Find what drives the person (reduce pain, add reward) and line up the task with what they want. Reframe a salesperson's boredom as a focus skill still to be learned.
numbers: ~30 years of failed weight-loss attempts (mother's case).

### YT-aUnorufFIq8#10 | SCRIPT | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:26:19
claim: To address under-performance, state that output is below what you believe they are capable of, then list the STAR causes and ask which applies.
detail: Order: ask what they are doing today; say production looks low compared to their potential (a compliment embedded); ask whether it is not knowing that, what, how, when, something blocking them, or motivation; invite them to help find the gap. Both sit on the same side of the problem, avoiding defensiveness.

### YT-aUnorufFIq8#11 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:49
claim: If someone lacks motivation after the other causes are cleared, let them go, including relatives you keep only because nobody else would hire them.
detail: Plenty of other firms employ mediocre staff. He singles out keeping a sister, her husband or a cousin on payroll.

### YT-aUnorufFIq8#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:35
claim: Turn "no one can do X like me" into the question "how do I find someone who can?", because beliefs stated as facts are only rules you made.
detail: Strategic problems are mostly "how" problems. Rules owners accept, such as "we cannot charge more" or "we cannot pay that much", come from copying an industry where most people earn little.

### YT-aUnorufFIq8#13 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: hiring-talent, strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:23:55
claim: Doing what everyone does leads to commoditised price and no profit; Henry Ford paid better than rivals to attract the top talent.
detail: Average owners model other owners, do it slightly better for slightly less, and end up running near non-profits. Paying above the market is one differentiator to capture the top 10% of talent.
numbers: Average business owner earns ~$50,000 a year (his figure).

### YT-aUnorufFIq8#14 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:08
claim: Organisations drift to the mean unless leaders hold the standard; he asks leaders to think of themselves as drivers who push work forward.
detail: Using a Bezos one-pager built from Dawkins, standing out takes energy and the environment pulls everything toward mediocrity. A driver asks where something is, how to do it faster and what to take off the person's plate.
