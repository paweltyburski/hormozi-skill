### POD-0059#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:22
claim: An owner must understand all five core functions: generate leads, nurture them, sell, fulfill, and retain or ascend customers.
detail: Agencies claim owners should stick to training clients; Hormozi calls that demeaning, since the work is learnable. Even at 10 locations with a full-time traffic manager, the owner needs enough knowledge to challenge numbers. Outsourcing a function you cannot judge keeps you bouncing between agencies.

### POD-0059#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:50
claim: Before taking operating advice from a gym marketer, ask whether they ever ran gyms and how that went.
detail: Many gurus sold their advice after closing or selling a gym that did not work. Their own product usually did not grow, so they sell on a false premise.

### POD-0059#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:22
claim: Agencies tell owners ad platforms are too complex but hand your account to a newly trained hire paid little.
detail: The usual agency cycle: campaign works in month one, weaker in months two and three, while a 12-month contract leads to a dispute and a bitter exit. He has not seen anyone stay with an agency beyond a year. Hormozi had run Facebook ads for eight years and says the platform changed little.
numbers: hire paid $15 to $20 per hour, trained about two days; clients leave within about a year.

### POD-0059#04 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:34
claim: Gym Launch began as a rev-share company: an eight-person sales team flew to gyms for 21 days and he kept all revenue.
detail: Average about $50,000 of sales per location in 21 days, from which he paid flights, hotels, cars, ads, payroll and commissions. Still profitable, but he stopped for three reasons: scaling would need a 200-person sales team; heavy logistics; and incentives clashed, since he signed as many members as possible while some gyms could not fulfil 100 sign-ups and the owners did not grow.
numbers: 8-person sales team; 21 days on site; about $50,000 sales per location; 200-person team needed to scale.

### POD-0059#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:11
claim: If a firm offers rev-share with no upfront cost, it sees profit you are missing, so learn the skill and keep the revenue.
detail: They fund ads, make a positive return and still pay you a share; ask whether you would earn more owning all that revenue. Entrepreneurs must accept risk.
