### POD-230819-18#01 | CASE | topic: operator-mindset | also: paid-ads | fw: Track Lose Print | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:56
claim: At a small event for eight-figure entrepreneurs a $3M-a-month advertiser said he no longer learns from courses but by spending a set share of revenue on tests, which freed Hormozi to test aggressively.
detail: The advice: reserve a fixed percentage of the advertising budget for new campaigns, channels, pages and wild ideas, with no return expected, as an education cost. Winners are rare but large. Hormozi tripled his ad budget the following week, accepting that he would either make more or learn.

### POD-230819-18#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: paid-ads | fw: More Better New | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:31
claim: Gym Launch revenue climbed from $400,000 (June 2017) to $780,000 (July), then to $4M a month by stacking tests, and the portfolio later exceeded $16M a month.
detail: Sequence: tripled ad spend, then cost per customer rose, so new audiences were tried (most failed, one hit) and revenue reached $1.2M and $1.5M. He found no follow-up of engaged leads; email and phone calls failed but text messages worked, giving $1.8M. More paid-ad volume and higher production value gave $2.5M; the affiliate programme added about $1.5M, passing $4M.
numbers: $400,000, $780,000, $1.2M, $1.5M, $1.8M, $2.5M, then +$1.5M to above $4M a month; portfolio above $16M per month.

### POD-230819-18#04 | CASE | topic: paid-ads | also: operator-mindset | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:17
claim: A 300-flyer test at his first gym produced one angry call, while his mentor tests with 5,000 and runs 5,000 per day on winners.
detail: The mentor, a tanning-salon chain owner, said 0.5% response is decent and 1% a winner; at 300 flyers 0.5% is about one and a half people, too few to read. Hormozi concluded effort and volume must never be the reason something failed.
numbers: 300 flyers versus 5,000-flyer tests; 0.5% decent, 1% winner; he did about 1/17 of the mentor's test size and about 1/1,500 of the effort needed to make flyers work.

detail: Advertising is an inputs and outputs game: effort in is engaged leads out. A person who reached 100 people over six weeks and got one customer had done roughly a forty-second of the required work.

### POD-230819-18#07 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: Open to Goal | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:57
claim: His daily structure: wake 4-5am (so sleep early), start work at once with no rituals, no meetings until noon, and a full uninterrupted block of about eight hours on advertising.
detail: He claims no magic in early rising, only in the long stretch of undisturbed work; firefighting and conversations come after the block. If overwhelmed, throttle back a few hours and build up. The leads book itself was written in that block. Total workday exceeded 12 hours.
numbers: Wake 4-5am; no meetings before noon; about 8 hours deep work; more than 12 hours total.

### POD-230819-18#08 | TACTIC | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: management-ops | fw: Advertising Checklist | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:33
claim: The one-page plan has five steps: pick lead type, pick Rule of 100 or Open to Goal, fill the checklist, repeat daily until you can pay someone, then make employees the new lead type.
detail: Checklist fields: who, what (offer or lead magnet), where, to whom, when (first eight hours), why (target number), how (warm, cold, content, ads), how much, how many follow-ups/retargets, how long (100 days or until goal). It takes about five minutes. Avoid long plans that are never used.
numbers: Checklist time about 5 minutes; 100 days.

### POD-230819-18#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: retention-ltv | fw: Seven Levels of Advertising | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:26
claim: At Level 4 (consistent referrals, 25%+ of customers), stop scaling ads and fix the product until referrals are steady, then rebuild the advertising team.
detail: He noticed ads were off yet referrals kept arriving every week, so he used customer feedback to update the product every two weeks and added a strong referral incentive. Most people let the product slip here and never recover.
numbers: Referrals at 25% or more of customers; product update every 2 weeks.
