### POD-0200#01 | SCRIPT | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:59
claim: Announce hard changes by restating the shared mission, naming what changed, then offering options and asking the team to help choose.
detail: Steps: 1) confirm everyone still wants the same goal; 2) confirm circumstances changed and spending now exceeds income; 3) lay out the options and say which you lean toward; 4) ask what they prefer. Present only options you would support. Letting people share the decision gives them ownership, whereas a verdict handed down breeds resentment and factions because they lack the information you had.

### POD-0200#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: management-ops | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:28
claim: His crisis options for a gym team were two weeks off per month, cutting half the staff, or all working at half pay with the owner taking zero first.
detail: Each option is framed as a way to keep the vision alive until the business can recover. Starting with the owner's own pay cut signals shared risk; he says he would lean toward taking zero if the team takes half.

### POD-0200#03 | CASE | topic: sales-team | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Gym trainers demanded a 60/40 revenue split; the owner answered by walking them through the margin and his risk.
detail: He showed that the gym netted only about 12% on revenue, had lost around 20% of revenue in the pandemic, and that the owner earned nothing while carrying the risk, including a personally guaranteed $400,000 lease. Staff are paid regardless, while the owner can lose his home. The split between pay and keeping doors open was framed as roughly 20% to the trainers and 80% to run the business (unclear in audio). Understanding, not agreement, is the aim.
numbers: 12% margin; ~20% revenue drop; $400,000 lease signed personally; 60/40 split requested.
applies_to: Owner-operators of gyms and similar small service businesses.

### POD-0200#04 | SCRIPT | topic: retention-ltv | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:05:33
claim: When clients protest a trainer's dismissal, thank them, ask who has more stake in their experience, and explain you cannot share the information behind the decision.
detail: Sequence: acknowledge their concern; ask whether anyone has more reason than the owner to want them happy; thank them for caring; explain that legal reasons (such as theft or a member complaint, given only as hypotheticals) prevent disclosure; ask whether you may know more than they do; say that with the same facts they would likely decide the same; promise a replacement they will like. He says employers cannot explain dismissals in virtually every US state.
