### YT-xQLQoGD6Sjg#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Dalio Learning Loop | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:56
claim: A good decision can have a bad outcome, so judge the decision separately from the result.
detail: Decision-making is the owner's most valuable asset (picking market, price, mentors, marketing). Hormozi did not buy Bitcoin, following Buffett's logic that speculation lacks intrinsic value or production, and it later rose. That does not make the rule wrong.

### YT-xQLQoGD6Sjg#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: Dalio Learning Loop | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:17
claim: Switch your decision framework only if it would have won across all your past decisions, not just the one that hurt.
detail: Adopting the framework that would have caught one missed winner means applying it to every deal where it would have lost. He attributes this to Ray Dalio's Principles.

### YT-xQLQoGD6Sjg#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:05
claim: Do not judge past decisions with information you did not have; revisit your method only for repeated calculation errors.
detail: He re-examines old bad outcomes by asking why he decided then, and often would choose the same again. Only a repeated mistake from poor calculation justifies tweaking the decision process.

### YT-xQLQoGD6Sjg#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:57
claim: Even the best batter fails most times, so a sound swing should not be reset after each miss.
detail: A batter with a .350 average still makes outs about two of three times. Keep the swing, take enough at-bats, and let the occasional home run outweigh the misses. Rule alongside it: never bet everything on one outcome you cannot afford to lose.
numbers: .350 batting average (about 65% non-hits).
