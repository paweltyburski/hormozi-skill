### POD-0188#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: Waived Fee Offer | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:16
claim: The bigger the gap between the up-front fee and the later recurring bill, the stickier the subscription.
detail: A tanning-salon chain owner found the stickiest plan was a high down payment with a recurring rate about half the normal rate. He ran 4,000+ memberships.
numbers: $100 down plus $10 a month, versus the normal rate of about $19 a month; 4,000+ members.

### POD-0188#02 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:16
claim: Use the initiation fee to pay the salesperson, because enrolling someone has almost no cost.
detail: At a large gym chain the rep keeps whatever initiation or enrollment fee they collect as commission, since no one would pay commission on a $25-a-month contract. The fee is nearly pure margin and recovers acquisition cost.
numbers: Example monthly contract: $25.

### POD-0188#03 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: promotions-launches | fw: Four Ways to Display a Discount | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:34
claim: Pair a large percentage discount on the first month with a one-time fee so the real discount is much smaller.
detail: Example: 88% off a $200 first month is $24; adding an enrollment fee makes the first month about $124, a true discount of roughly 30% (as he states it), though the advertised 88% is more attractive. Disclose the fee on the offer and thank-you pages to stay compliant. People still join straight into a recurring plan.
numbers: $200 month x 12% = $24 plus fee (about $100) = ~$124 first month.

### POD-0188#04 | SCRIPT | topic: pricing | also: sales-process | fw: Waived Fee Offer | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:38
claim: Justify the one-time fee as an onboarding fee: starting a client costs far more of your time than keeping them.
detail: Structure: (1) say beginners must be taught eating, cooking, shopping, the booking system and the workouts, which takes one-to-one time; (2) say that once they are up to speed they cost far less; (3) show that the savings are passed on, so they pay less each month than if the effort continued.
numbers: Example: $1,000 up front then $200 a month; savings of about 80% on ongoing cost.
