### YT-Kk6Vdfdwmxw#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: paid-ads | fw: Create Flow Monetize Add Friction | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Five levers raise lead quality: tighter targeting, more steps, a paid or call-first offer instead of a giveaway, an application, and value given first.
detail: Each lifts quality but typically cuts volume, so you don't have to use all five; he wants to apply as few of these filters as he can, and tunes them against the monetization structure.
### YT-Kk6Vdfdwmxw#02 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:47
claim: Narrow targeting by zip code, income or profession, accepting that rich segments are contested, dearer and smaller.
detail: Rivals target the same zip codes, so cost per lead rises and volume falls.

### YT-Kk6Vdfdwmxw#03 | TACTIC | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: paid-ads | fw: Penny Gap | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:48
claim: A straight "book a call" offer yields fewer but better leads than a free giveaway, which gives volume that needs a strong follow-up mousetrap.
detail: More steps before reaching you also select for pain and commitment.

### YT-Kk6Vdfdwmxw#04 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:35
claim: An application with paragraph answers qualifies better than checkboxes, and can ask whether they would invest at least half your price.
detail: Ask for their goal, problem and unknowns. Example: asking if they will spend at least $500 on a $1,000 offer.
numbers: $500 minimum on a $1,000 offer.

### YT-Kk6Vdfdwmxw#05 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:09
claim: A five-minute ad that teaches something usable draws more qualified leads than a gripping ad with no substance.
detail: Value first builds authority before the prospect arrives.

### YT-Kk6Vdfdwmxw#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:16
claim: Add quality filters when volume is high and quality poor, or the team cannot handle the flow; he is willing to sacrifice a third to half of throughput then.
detail: Example: for an overloaded national call campaign, he would be fine doubling lead cost to fence the flow. Otherwise, personally, he tends to start with the widest net and put the load on the back-end model.
numbers: 33-50% throughput loss accepted.
### YT-Kk6Vdfdwmxw#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:16

detail: Without such funnel-return numbers for a trial or lead magnet, marketing decisions are harder.
numbers: Per $1 spent: $1 (day 15), $3.50 (day 30), $7 (day 60).

### YT-Kk6Vdfdwmxw#08 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Money Model | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:58
claim: Seven-figure gym owners moved to an online fitness model at the same average price with unlimited lead flow at about a fifth of the cost per lead.
detail: The model took three or four iterations to tune; the same wide-net approach was used for the gym, supplements and Gym Launch.
numbers: Cost per lead ~1/5.
