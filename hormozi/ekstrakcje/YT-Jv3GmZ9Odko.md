### YT-Jv3GmZ9Odko#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:50
claim: Business strategies are simple; speed differences come from execution and recognising patterns in people.
detail: Two lawn care owners, one at a billion dollars of value and one barely above $5,000 a month, run essentially the same strategy: find what people want, charge a lot, deliver so well they tell others.
numbers: Example gap: billion-dollar lawn company against about $5,000 a month.

### YT-Jv3GmZ9Odko#02 | DEFINITION | topic: hiring-talent | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:17
claim: "Ideal scene" is knowing what a good outcome in a role looks like; not knowing it is an "ignorance tax" paid in time, which outside experts remove.
detail: Two ideal scenes are needed: the order in which heads of marketing, sales, HR, finance, customer success, product and legal should be hired, and what an excellent person in each role looks like. Once you have seen an excellent sales director, you can find and copy the pattern.

### YT-Jv3GmZ9Odko#03 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:56
claim: After going 0 for 7 on assistants over a decade, Hormozi found a fit because he finally knew what proactive looked like.
detail: The working pattern: the assistant keeps a prioritised stack of tasks with time estimates and fills any gap in his day (13 or 18 minutes) with a matching task; when he finishes early she supplies the next one.
numbers: Seven failed assistants over about 10 years.

### YT-Jv3GmZ9Odko#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Peter Principle | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:39
claim: Leila Hormozi says that making your mom who can do some maths the controller, or your top salesman the sales director, is not necessarily a good idea.
detail: Leila says the portfolio companies she works with are mostly e-learning, coaching and service businesses. She says basic maths is a requisite but not all of them, and a top salesperson may be only a strong individual contributor and a weak leader. She says a mis-hire costs time twice, to onboard and then to find out and offboard, while the department does not grow.
### YT-Jv3GmZ9Odko#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: A Players | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:31
claim: Leila Hormozi says that in her own experience every A player she has had was recognisable as one within the first couple of days, and she has never had someone who was not an A player up front become one.
detail: Leila says she has had A players who later stopped being A players. She says her tolerance for non-excellence keeps decreasing so she does not waste time figuring out whether someone will help grow the business.
numbers: Leila: every A player she has had was known within the first couple of days.
### YT-Jv3GmZ9Odko#06 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:17
claim: Take hiring advice from someone who has built several such departments and scaled them for a long time, not from owners a step or six months ahead.
detail: People one step ahead often announce a great new hire and replace them four months later. Experienced builders know the real requisites of a role.
