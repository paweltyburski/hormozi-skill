### MZ-s0ZfItLRkK0#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Constant novelty extends lifetime value for consumable products, because people try everything of a brand and then stop buying if nothing changes.
detail: More options also raise total consumption. Limited flavours around staples keep customers returning, and consumers do not care about the operational complexity behind them.

### MZ-s0ZfItLRkK0#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:47
claim: Cookie and frozen-yogurt chains rotate most of their menu weekly or monthly while keeping a few staples.
detail: A cookie brand sells five new cookies of six each week in one box, a frozen-yogurt chain changes six of eight flavours weekly, Oreos run limited flavours, and a vertically integrated bar brand drops a new flavour weekly. He suggests ice-cream stores and breweries (six rotating beers) as untapped.
numbers: 5 of 6 cookies new weekly; 6 of 8 yogurt flavours new weekly; frozen-yogurt chain 1-2 new flavours per month (as he recalled).

### MZ-s0ZfItLRkK0#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: product-delivery | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:30
claim: Variety works only with a short consumption cycle and operations able to deliver it; keep staples plus a limited rotating set that a customer can finish.
detail: Match rotation to the buying cycle (weekly drinkers, weekly rotation). Too many drops overwhelm and lose the "limited" appeal. He thinks it suits high-velocity, quickly consumed, impulse purchases like snacks, apparel, food and drink, and not services like agencies.
numbers: He thinks roughly four to six, or four to eight, varieties alongside staples.
### MZ-s0ZfItLRkK0#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:09
claim: Mystery purchases need a low margin of error; surprise works for cookies, less for hoodies.
detail: Buyers pay for the value of a surprise, which means different things to different people. A random cookie or yogurt flavour is hard to get wrong, while a hoodie that is off-style goes unworn.
