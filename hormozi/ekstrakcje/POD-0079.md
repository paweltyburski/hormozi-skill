### POD-0079#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:37
claim: An owner's income follows the skill they hold, climbing from training sessions to selling, then managing sellers, then marketing.
detail: The market pays about $15 an hour for running training sessions, so an owner doing them is capped. His programme teaches sales plus marketing so the owner becomes the "rainmaker" instead of waiting for someone else to make it rain. He positions this as the reason his price is high and the results larger.
numbers: Training sessions about $15 an hour.

### POD-0079#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:47
claim: Agencies and rev-share firms sell dependence on a skill that is easy to teach; ad-runners are typically staff paid $15-$20 an hour who learn in about a week.
detail: Their pitch is that marketing is too complicated for the owner. The information, not any difficulty, is what is withheld. Worst case the owner pays and the vendor does not care; best case results come but the owner needs them again. Agency growth is limited by the owner's own skills, so their companies stay smaller.
numbers: Ad-runner pay $15-$20 an hour; training about 1 week.

### POD-0079#03 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:11
claim: The origin of the programme: in 2016, as an owner of six gyms aged about 25-26, he was invited to speak at a funnel-software event and over a hundred gym owners asked to buy his method.
detail: He was the only brick-and-mortar owner ever to speak there, the other speakers being information marketers. He had nothing to sell at the time, and the demand later became the programme. His argument: a vendor who never ran a gym teaches less than one who scaled their own.
numbers: 6 gyms; 100+ owners asked to buy; year 2016.

### POD-0079#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:52
claim: He claims the average gym adds about $250,000 a year in revenue through the programme, against a $16,000 price.
detail: Used to argue that the price is small against the return and that the choice is dependence on an agency or learning the skill.
numbers: ~$250,000 added revenue per year per gym; $16,000 price.
