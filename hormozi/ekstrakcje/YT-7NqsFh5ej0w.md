### YT-7NqsFh5ej0w#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Hire in reverse order of the value a role creates, replacing first the tasks that are cheapest on the market.
detail: Compare the dollars per hour you generate in a task with what it costs to replace it; the net gap decides the order. Early hires act as extensions of you. These are rules of thumb: a strong seller delays the sales hire, a weak one takes it earlier.

### YT-7NqsFh5ej0w#02 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:54
claim: Typical order: admin help, customer support or success, first salesperson, marketing assist (setter, prospector or content videographer), fractional bookkeeper, then an IT person.
detail: Support takes over repeated tasks such as returns, billing changes and common customer questions. A strong closer may put a prospector first so more time goes to closing. Admin and support can swap order. Teams then branch out under marketing, sales and delivery.

### YT-7NqsFh5ej0w#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:27
claim: Before about $100,000 a month of revenue, he says a fractional bookkeeper, someone for taxes and a lawyer who is not in-house are probably sufficient.
detail: The bookkeeper's job early on is basic accounting so you know what you earn and can control expenses; he says the role will probably come in-house later.
numbers: Rough threshold ~$100,000/month revenue ("probably").
### YT-7NqsFh5ej0w#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:27
claim: Freed-up time must be reinvested in income-generating work, otherwise margins fall.
detail: Some owners replace all their tasks and then add no new value with the time they bought back.

### YT-7NqsFh5ej0w#05 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:22
claim: Grow early hires into leads of their own teams (admin into director of operations, salesperson into sales manager), but expect the individual-contributor to manager step to fail often.
detail: Experience lets you spot people with the talent a later role needs and hire them early, since a top executive from a large firm is unaffordable and uninterested at your stage.

### YT-7NqsFh5ej0w#06 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: hiring-talent, management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:05:09
claim: Starting a business means taking on several kinds of debt (financial, technological, management or cultural, life), and you choose which to incur first and repay later with interest.
detail: No proper CRM now means a costly migration later; no bookkeeper means messy finances to fix at scale; weak people or values mean cultural debt. IT is often the next hire after the first five, managing the CRM, sites, logins and onboarding access.

### YT-7NqsFh5ej0w#07 | EVOLUTION | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:07
claim: He once dismissed funded startups as not real entrepreneurship; he now sees funding as swapping financial debt for other debts, which lets him skip growth stages.
detail: He says his later companies grew bigger and faster because having finances meant incurring fewer other debts; with money you can sometimes buy a bigger CRM upfront, and the steps skipped would otherwise set him back roughly a quarter or a year, such as from inexperienced managers.
### YT-7NqsFh5ej0w#08 | BENCHMARK | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:16
claim: Marketing, sales and delivery are the teams that tend to grow as a company grows, while finance, IT and HR need fewer people per customer or employee.
detail: He pictures teams hanging off marketing, sales and delivery as the company scales; IT, finance and HR end up being built over time.
