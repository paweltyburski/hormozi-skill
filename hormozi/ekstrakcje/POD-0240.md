### POD-0240#01 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:48
claim: Compute your true hourly rate from profit, not revenue, divided by the hours you actually work.
detail: Take the last 12 months of profit and divide by 2,000 hours (a 40-hour week). If you really work 60 or 80 hours, the rate drops further. Use the figure first to see that tasks you consider beneath you may not be.
numbers: $50,000 profit: $25 an hour at 40 hours a week, about $17.50 at 60, about $12 at 80.

### POD-0240#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:37
claim: Outsource any activity someone else can do for less than your own hourly rate, working up from the bottom.
detail: The order he describes is a rough ladder, not a firm sequence: low-level fulfilment first, then probably administration, above that maybe finance (billing and collections), then sales, then marketing and prospecting. Your tasks should rise in value over time.
### POD-0240#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-team | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:29
claim: Keep selling yourself until you have margin to absorb the dip, and outsource non-revenue work first.
detail: With six gyms he still sold daily because he made about $500 an hour selling, more than anything else he did. The most expensive part of bringing in a salesperson is that person not selling at first. Leave sales until there is enough padding.
numbers: His selling rate: about $500 an hour.
### POD-0240#04 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:16
claim: Convert purchases into hours of work to keep spending honest.
detail: As a teenage smoothie shop worker at $6.75 an hour, he saw coworkers spend $10 to $15 on lunch from about $30 after-tax pay for a six-hour shift, so half a shift's pay went on lunch. Ask whether you would work four hours for what you are about to buy.
numbers: Shift: 6 hours, about $40 gross, about $30 after tax; lunch $10-$15.

### POD-0240#05 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:18
claim: Run a time study: log every 15-minute block on a printed sheet to see which tasks to outsource.
detail: It takes minutes of writing and pays back in efficiency; Hormozi does one about once a quarter or twice a year. Review which activities sit below your hourly rate.
numbers: 15-minute increments; repeat about quarterly.
