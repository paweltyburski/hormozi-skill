### MZ-2D1HPz9mQus#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: Riches in the Niches | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A former CFO (unicorn tech company and billion-dollar construction firm) considers a turnaround consultancy and fears a construction-only niche.
detail: He offers strategy and finance consulting to small and mid-size companies and worries about boredom and being pigeonholed. Hormozi's answer is to specialise where he has the record.

### MZ-2D1HPz9mQus#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: pricing | fw: Riches in the Niches | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:34
claim: A niche brings more customers, not fewer, because relevance raises response and allows niche prices.
detail: Case studies match the buyer, deals become a shoe-in, and messaging pared down gives higher response. Example: with a gym track record, advertising to all businesses brings fewer gyms than advertising to gyms.

### MZ-2D1HPz9mQus#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:34
claim: Every business at the top level looks the same: the founder with five to seven direct reports.
detail: Reports run marketing, sales, HR, legal, IT, finance and customer success. So the day-to-day converges whether the company is software, a PE firm or whitening stores, and boredom is not a reason to avoid a niche.
numbers: Five to seven direct reports.

### MZ-2D1HPz9mQus#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:33
claim: Focus on the area where you hold a massive track record and know how to win for clients.
detail: Do not expect to avoid boredom later by staying broad; growth erases that difference.
