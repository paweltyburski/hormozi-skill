### POD-0758#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:46
claim: If the logical fix fails or is too costly, try a psychological one that works on how people perceive the problem.
detail: Examples: mirrors in elevators instead of expensive faster lifts; a countdown dot map at UK stations showing the wait for trains. A known wait is more tolerable than an unknown one, since people can plan around it. He had a short saying on this in his offers book.

### POD-0758#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:17
claim: Hard periods end in one of three ways: you quit, the pressure goes away, or you grow skills so it stops being a threat.
detail: Entrepreneurship hurts mostly because the length of the hard period is unknown. He retells a mouse swimming study: a mouse rescued before it drowned and put back swam for about 16 hours instead of about 30 minutes, because it expected rescue. He adds that this is his own gloss and may not hold up. Knowing "this hard will pass" has helped him.
numbers: About 30 minutes baseline vs 16 hours (about 30x) in the story, as he remembers it.

### POD-0758#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: operator-mindset | fw: More Better New | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:25
claim: Typically max out volume first; sometimes, especially at a stagnation point, when you cannot do more, the counterintuitive move that has led to growth is deletion rather than adding something new.
detail: Typical stagnation response is a new initiative, which dilutes what you sell. He thinks "over-delivering" should mean doing fewer things better. Like pruning a tree so it grows taller, cutting has led to growth at more companies than he can count.
### POD-0758#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:47
claim: After a decade he found he can run about one big new initiative a year, and the company grows faster the less he tries to break it.
detail: He has many more ideas for Acquisition.com, but one big thing a year fully loads the team, given a strong operator. Meanwhile he practises patience as using the wait productively.
numbers: About one major initiative per year.

### POD-0758#05 | CASE | topic: sales-team | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:40
claim: Building the Gym Launch outbound team took 12 months, as a book had said, not the 12 weeks he assumed.
detail: He believed that with hard work he could beat the author's timeline. Some things take nine months however many people work on them; treat new products, processes and hires like pregnancies, not one-night stands.
numbers: Outbound team: 12 months to become a material share of the business; his guess had been 12 weeks.

### POD-0758#06 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:13
claim: While a long project matures, do the pre-work that will be needed afterwards ("dig the well before we're thirsty").
detail: He lays track in advance so that when the partner's track meets his, things connect and run fast. His division: he is the zero-to-one person; his wife is the one-to-infinity person who runs it and, he says, makes the money.

### POD-0758#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: unit-economics | fw: More Better New | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:27
claim: Most of the money is in doing more of what already works, which is the best risk-adjusted way to grow.
detail: The strategy is proven, so doing more has little risk. Examples: one salesperson to 20, one support rep to 20, first inventory order to six months of stock, ad spend from $1,000 to $100,000 per day. Entrepreneurs prefer novelty, but "more" also takes less of his time.
numbers: Ad spend example $1,000 to $100,000 per day; sales and support staff 1 to 20.

### POD-0758#08 | EXAMPLE | topic: content-brand | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:11:30
claim: Familiarity to you is not familiarity to the market: Ford's marketing head had not yet run the ad Ford was tired of seeing.
detail: After about three months of seeing a painted car ad in the office, Ford asked when it would stop running; it had not yet run. Owners worry about repeating themselves while the market does not even know their name.
numbers: About three months of seeing the unrun ad.

### POD-0758#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: content-brand | fw: Leverage | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:44
claim: Look for the one lever that makes thousands of other steps irrelevant.
detail: In 2019, starting YouTube, he reasoned that a massive trusted business brand would give proprietary deal flow so he would not need to be the best investor or have the most cash. He sets priorities to make that one thing true.

### POD-0758#10 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:27
claim: Ask which one thing makes the most money and imagine all the rest disappearing; if growth would be far easier, stop the others.
detail: Doing less means doing fewer things so more goes into the one with the highest return for what you put in.
