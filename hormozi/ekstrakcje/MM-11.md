### MM-11#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Win Your Money Back began as a gym owner's improvised deal with a hesitant client and became Hormozi's top-earning offer once he ran it on ads as large-group training.
detail: In a gym mastermind, owner Danny described a prospect who said no, then proposed paying $500 for one-on-one training, refunded if he lost 20 lb in eight weeks, with Danny free to use his photos. The client succeeded; advertising his before/after brought about 13 more customers. Hormozi tested a six-week one-on-one version, then made two changes that turned it into a "money nuclear bomb": running paid ads for it, and delivering it as large-group training. Free and paid versions with credit-back and money-back guarantees came later, after one to two years of iteration.
numbers: $500 deposit; 20 lb in 8 weeks; ~13 customers from one before/after.

### MM-11#02 | DEFINITION | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:52
claim: Win Your Money Back: set a goal, tell the customer how to reach it, and if they qualify they get their payment back as cash or store credit.
detail: It works as a high-cash-flow, choreographed onboarding — normally a cost, here a revenue generator. Benefits he lists: up-front cash, more people saying yes, better results, more long-term customers, and customers advertising for you.

### MM-11#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:52
claim: The customer can bet on a result, on doing the required actions, or on both.
detail: (1) Results: hit the outcome (make $X/month, get Y customers, lose Z lb) by any means and win. (2) Actions (00:02:40): do what you are told — attend all sessions and calls, log progress, take pictures, do homework — regardless of result. (3) Both: follow directions and hit the goal; useful because customers often lack the skill to reach the goal alone, so the directions get them there.

### MM-11#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv, content-brand | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:40
claim: Choose required actions that get results, advertise the business, and put the customer in your sales appointments.
detail: The two original purposes are results and advertising; he adds a third — mandatory attendance at feedback/transformation sessions and check-ins, so every upsell opportunity is a required step. Since you give the money back, some criteria can legitimately serve you.

### MM-11#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:31
claim: The money comes from customers who succeed and then buy more, not from forfeited deposits.
detail: Of the people who miss the goal, many wouldn't necessarily want to become customers anyway. Successful customers credit their deposit toward a bigger offer — the more results you deliver, the more you make. He also put referral customers on the program, because participants were more invested and got better results than on any other program.
numbers: 78% of participants hit their goal.

### MM-11#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: product-delivery | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:05
claim: Use it where customers must act to get results; skip it when you do all the work.
detail: Fits service businesses, software that needs self-onboarding (data entry, profile setup) and anything people start and quit. Poor fit: e.g., roofing — the customer has nothing to do. Works with new, current and past customers (00:07:29).

### MM-11#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: product-delivery, offers | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: canon | ts: 00:18:54
claim: Only run it if your refund rate is under 5%; otherwise fix the product first.
detail: Above that, a money-back mechanism invites too many refund claims.
numbers: Refund rate < 5%.

### MM-11#08 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: verticals, outreach | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:36
claim: Rehab and marketing examples show criteria combining required actions with a measurable result.
detail: Rehab — "Free Kick That Habit 28-Day Blueprint": attend all sessions, post in the group weekly, journal daily in the app, attend the feedback and transformation sessions (the upsell appointments); result: pass a urine test at day 28. Marketing (00:06:25) — "Free Five Clients" five-day challenge: send 100 outbound messages a day, attend daily training, post in the group after homework; result: five closes; both required.
numbers: 28 days; 5 days, 100 messages/day, 5 closes.

### MM-11#09 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:25
claim: For subjective outcomes such as pain relief, use action-only criteria.
detail: 28-day one-on-one pain program: attend all sessions, do home stretches, post a weekly picture, attend feedback and transformation sessions. No pain criterion, because people with money at stake may claim "still 10/10 pain".

### MM-11#10 | FRAMEWORK | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:05
claim: Good refund criteria are easy to track, realistic and result-producing, advertise the business, and include sales appointments.
detail: The criteria make or break the offer. (1) Easy to track — train customers exactly; best if already tracked automatically (phone steps, word count, dated photos). (2) Realistic — require what your best customers do (attend, work out, watch videos); if it looks too easy it is probably realistic. (3) Advertise — public posts with your copy and tags. (4) Sales appointments — for referrals, testimonials and before/after photos, and upsells.

### MM-11#11 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:09
claim: Set action criteria by copying what your best performers did, keeping only the fewest common behaviours, and repeat.
detail: Start with a guess, see who got results and what they did, require that of everyone, then re-check the top performers and update. Keep the few things they all did, not everything they did.

### MM-11#12 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: content-brand, lead-getters | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:55
claim: Requiring public workout posts and before/after photos with your copy and tags turns each cohort into free local ads and leads.
detail: Each workout post followed his script, tagged the gym and linked to the offer; at the end came the before/after. Friends' comments ("what gym is that?") became leads. When asked why he would refund people, he said: I want you to stay and bring friends, and results make you do both.
numbers: 3 workouts/week × 6 weeks = 18 posts each; 100 people ≈ 1,800 posts.

### MM-11#13 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:29
claim: Have a back end about 5× the deposit so the credit is at most a 20% discount; if everyone enters this way, raise the back-end price 20%.
detail: Without something to credit toward, the offer fails. With every customer arriving via the offer, the discount is a known cost you can price in — which is why he put even referrals on it. Repeated at 00:14:11.
numbers: Back end ≈ 5× deposit; credit ≤ 20%; +20% back-end price.

### MM-11#14 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: offers, objections-closing | fw: Unconditional Guarantee | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:18
claim: The offer could be advertised as legally "free" because of an added unconditional service guarantee, per his US counsel.
detail: Two guarantees: performance (do the work, get the money back) and service (if by the end you don't feel you got $500 of service, full refund — even at day 41, hour 23). The service guarantee closed many: "if you don't think it's worth it you won't stay anyway." Check your own jurisdiction.

### MM-11#15 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: objections-closing | fw: Unconditional Guarantee | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:04
claim: Refund anyone who asks: he says unhappy customers are where the trouble comes from, and holding on to their money is not worth the risk or the hassle.
detail: Give it back and go get another customer. Almost no one used the service guarantee; the two who did were people he had pushed too hard to buy.
numbers: 4,000+ sales over 5 years, 2 service refunds (00:19:40).
quote: Don't take blood money.

### MM-11#16 | SCRIPT | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:56
claim: Sell the program as the kickstart of a long-term goal: the money is external motivation that builds an internal habit.
detail: (1) Ask: short-term or long-term goals? (2) Position the program as the kickstart. (3) Toothbrush analogy: mom made you brush as a kid; now you do it automatically. (4) The money is that external push. (5) "21 days makes a habit — we doubled it to six weeks." External gets you going, internal keeps you going.

### MM-11#17 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv, pricing | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:42
claim: Never apply won credit as free months; spread it across a year-long commitment so customers pay from day one.
detail: "Three months free" nearly killed his business: non-paying members stopped coming and quit at the first real bill; paying even a discounted amount kept them showing up — skin in the game. Customers may legally use the credit as they wish, so present the spread option first and explain why. If someone insists on free months, they sign up for the full year first. Math repeated at 00:15:50.
numbers: $200/month ($2,400/year) − $600 credit = $150/month for 12 months.

### MM-11#18 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: canon | ts: 00:16:52
claim: Make every check-in required for the refund and attach a specific offer to each of the three appointments.
detail: (1) Orientation — before photos; sell supplements. (2) Mid-program check-in — offer a year of membership. (3) Transformation feedback — after photos; repeat the membership offer to non-buyers and offer prepayment to buyers. Upsell detail is in the Rollover Upsell lesson.

### MM-11#19 | SCRIPT | topic: money-models | also: sales-process, objections-closing | fw: Make Everybody a Winner | novelty: canon | ts: 00:17:16
claim: Halfway through, make the long-term offer as if they already won, crediting the deposit whether or not they hit the goal.
detail: Never mention this up front. At the midpoint, ask about the short-term goal, then the long-term one ("down 12 lb — but you want to lose 50, right?"), confirm it's about lifestyle, then: "I'll credit this program toward the longer one whether you hit the short-term goal or not." Removing their risk of losing the bet makes it a risk-reduction sale; anxiety drops and they stay. Same move at 00:04:21.

### MM-11#20 | SCRIPT | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv, objections-closing | fw: Make Everybody a Winner | novelty: canon | ts: 00:18:03
claim: "Let the losers win": credit the deposit even for people who refused the upsell and failed, and re-offer the long-term program.
detail: "You started — that's the biggest victory. You didn't hit your goal, but you met ours: finishing. To show we're in it for the long haul, I'll credit your whole deposit toward staying." The upsell becomes something they're grateful for. The aim is never failure; it's finishing and momentum. Rule: never let people stop paying you — roll credit into a more expensive offer.
