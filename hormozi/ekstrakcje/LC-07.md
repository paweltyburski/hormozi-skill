### LC-07#01 | DEFINITION | topic: outreach | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Core Four | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Cold outreach is privately contacting strangers one-to-one through any channel that reaches a specific person — email, call, DM, SMS, voicemail, door knock.
detail: Third of the Core Four (after warm outreach and content); the private counterpart to reaching cold audiences. It is platform-independent: it worked 100 years ago, works now, and will keep working when platforms change.

### LC-07#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: Core Four | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: He recommends starting with warm outreach before cold: until you are used to simply talking to people (for example offering friends something free and asking who they know), begin with warm.
detail: Cold builds two extra layers on top of warm — the advanced version. Get used to simply talking to people first.

### LC-07#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: outreach | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Three Problems of Strangers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:34
claim: Cold differs from warm in one thing — trust — and strangers create three problems: you can't contact them, they ignore you, and they aren't interested.
detail: The job is to bridge "stranger danger" as fast as possible. Solutions, which structure the lesson: 1) no contact → build a list (scrape, buy, elbow grease); 2) ignored/distrusted → personalize and give big, fast value; 3) too few chances to pitch → raise volume (automate delivery, automate distribution, follow up more times in more ways). Recap at 00:22:26.

### LC-07#04 | FRAMEWORK | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: List Building (Scrape, Buy, Elbow Grease) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:34
claim: Build cold lists three ways — scrape with software, buy from brokers, or assemble by hand from groups — easiest to hardest, and the harder the method, the fresher the list.
detail: What is easy for you is easy for competitors: a $99/month unlimited-leads tool has likely been hit by many senders (you can still succeed). A list hand-built from many groups is far less likely to have been solicited by many people, so it might be far more responsive, especially if those people have seen you give value in the group — why the methods work together.

### LC-07#05 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: List Building (Scrape, Buy, Elbow Grease) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:22
claim: Scraping: subscribe to as many lead-scraping tools as you can, run your customer criteria in each, test a few hundred contacts per tool, and buy more only where the data holds up.
detail: Find tools by searching "outbound lead scraping tool" or "database lead scraping". Keep a tool only if contacts are current, leads respond, and they really match the claimed profile.
numbers: Test sample ≈ a few hundred contacts per tool.

### LC-07#06 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: List Building (Scrape, Buy, Elbow Grease) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:22
claim: Brokers: give several list brokers your criteria, test each one's sample list, keep buying from those that perform, and when one runs dry buy everything and ask who else they know.
detail: Brokering lists is their whole business, so good ones can be more nuanced than software; quality varies by broker.

### LC-07#07 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: content-brand | fw: List Building (Scrape, Buy, Elbow Grease) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:35
claim: Elbow grease: join the groups where your audience gathers and, for each qualifying member, collect a way to contact them outside the group.
detail: Use the directory and profile links. Messaging inside the host platform risks losing a membership that feeds you leads, so post value in the group and contact people off-platform. Give more to the community than you take. Group-scraping software now exists and is worth using. If you have more time than money (00:05:12), start here — it costs only time.

### LC-07#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: outreach | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:12
claim: Interest groups are the warmest cold audience: members are already problem-aware and looking for a solution.
detail: They are "micro-engaged" — aware of a gap between where they are and want to be — so you skip making them problem-aware and just intercept them with a solution. One of Hormozi's favorite places to fish.

### LC-07#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: Big Fast Value | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:58
claim: What to say to strangers comes down to two moves — personalize, and give big, fast value — and if you can win cold audiences this way, treat warm audiences the same way.
detail: Personalization carries over from warm outreach; big fast value is added because strangers don't know or trust you. Cold is the hardest audience to earn trust from quickly, so the same approach gets even more engagement from warm audiences (a tip he says is not in the book).

### LC-07#10 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: Big Fast Value | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:48
claim: Personalize by acting like you already know them — each real detail buys extra seconds of attention.
detail: Make cold feel like a friend reaching out. A local area code raises pickups; their name buys a couple of seconds; something about them buys more. Action: research each lead and include a short snippet about their business, needs or background. Generic messages get deleted; tailored ones get read because people are interested in themselves. Pay people or software to do the research if needed.

### LC-07#11 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:35
claim: Rewrite every script below a third-grade reading level — this alone raised email responses about 50%.
detail: Run phone, text and email scripts through a free reading-level checker. Readers give you a fraction of a second and stop at the first word they don't understand.
numbers: ~50% lift in email response rate.

### LC-07#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: outreach | also: leads-fundamentals, offers | fw: Big Fast Value | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:56
claim: Strangers give you seconds, so give away the farm: the lead magnet must blow their minds in about 30 seconds, not tickle interest.
detail: Time granted scales with familiarity: family gets minutes, acquaintances less, strangers seconds. If outreach isn't working, make the free thing so good people feel stupid saying no ($100M Offers). Provide the biggest, fastest value you can afford.
quote: So good people feel stupid saying no.

### LC-07#13 | CASE | topic: outreach | also: leads-fundamentals, verticals | fw: Big Fast Value | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:10
claim: Gym Launch tripled outbound response rates by switching the free offer to something other companies actually charge for.
detail: After months of outbound, the breakthrough was what was given away on the call. Give away what others charge for — not merely something you think is good enough to charge for — so prospects have a value anchor and know it costs businesses money.
numbers: 3x outbound response rate.

### LC-07#14 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:00
claim: Keep scripts short and start: phone/chat scripts a page or two, cold emails rarely over half a page, a first DM two or three sentences.
detail: The work is the reaching out, not the script. Your first 100 cold calls teach more than 100 books on cold calling. Sample scripts are provided at acquisition.com.

### LC-07#15 | FRAMEWORK | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: More Times More Ways | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:17
claim: To get more chances to pitch, raise volume three ways: automate delivery (making the message), automate distribution (sending it), and follow up more times in more ways.
detail: Output = people reached × response rate (~00:13:14). Automation buys volume at a lower response rate; personalization buys response rate at lower volume; the ideal is both.

### LC-07#16 | PRINCIPLE | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: More Times More Ways | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:17
claim: Live, personally made messages respond best; recorded or templated ones scale but respond worse — choose by whether you have more time or more money.
detail: Live: a live call, a voice memo saying their name, a handwritten card — recipients sense the time invested ("scaling the unscalable"). Recorded: voicemail drops, templated emails/texts, pre-recorded video — make once, send to all. Automate as far as you can without hurting response rate; trade toward recorded as money grows. Gary Halbert's test (00:14:12): if one letter had to earn your living, you'd handwrite it and stamp it yourself — the high-response extreme.

### LC-07#17 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: More Times More Ways | novelty: canon | ts: 00:15:33
claim: Distribution can be automated separately: a hand-made message can still go out via power dialers or blasts of 1,000 emails, texts or voicemails.
detail: Manual = you dial or click send each time. Each element can be automated independently; generally (less so with AI) you trade personalization for scale.

### LC-07#18 | DECISION_RULE | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: More Times More Ways | novelty: canon | ts: 00:15:45
claim: In his opinion, the fewer targets you have, the less you should automate: a smaller and more valuable target universe calls for more personalization, a larger one allows more automation.
detail: A few thousand US hedge fund managers (high-value accounts) → handwritten letter plus a personal gift. Weight loss for ~160M US women (~100M of age) → heavy automation is fine. Small or costly-to-build lists (~00:13:14): personalize so you don't burn them.

### LC-07#19 | PRINCIPLE | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: More Times More Ways | novelty: canon | ts: 00:16:36
claim: The ideal is maximum personalization at maximum volume, and with tech you do not always have to sacrifice personalization for scale.
detail: He says personal data gets more accessible every day; if you can set up tech for both personalization and volume, it is an effective combination. Caveat (~00:13:14): expect an AI arms race over whether a name-personalized message is genuine; stay on the cutting edge.

### LC-07#20 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: More Times More Ways | novelty: canon | ts: 00:16:36
claim: Follow up more ways (several channels) and more times (rapid repeats): quick repeated contact signals importance and triggers curiosity.
detail: People prefer different channels (Hormozi ignores calls, answers DMs). Example cadence: email + text + call, repeated a few hours later, and again. This beats the usual single call with no voicemail followed by "it doesn't work". They may just have been busy. Act like you're actually trying to reach them.

### LC-07#21 | DECISION_RULE | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:18:28
claim: If someone asks you to stop, stop — turning neutrals into yeses is more efficient than turning noes into yeses, and reputation follows you.
detail: Try hard because you want to solve their problem, but be respectful.

### LC-07#22 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: More Times More Ways | novelty: canon | ts: 00:19:12
claim: After working the whole list multiple times in multiple ways, wait three to six months and start again from the top.
detail: People may not have seen it, lacked time to reply, or their circumstances changed. Ignore your head trash about re-contacting.
numbers: Re-work interval 3–6 months.

### LC-07#23 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: outreach | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:19:12
claim: A new outreach rep should copy the best rep exactly at double their volume and change nothing until beating their numbers.
detail: If the top rep makes 200 calls, make 400. Volume makes up for lack of skill — "volume negates luck"; twice the reps, good in half the time. Replicate before you iterate.

### LC-07#24 | SCRIPT | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:20:16
claim: "Calling in reference to" voicemail: their first name, your first name, "calling in reference to [a company in their world]", callback number.
detail: Name a client or competitor they know (targeting Adidas with Nike as a client; for a gym owner, the gym across the street). Pause slightly after your name so they search memory rather than hang up. Why it works: short, relevant via third-party trust, and an open loop people want closed — they call back, making it inbound. Don't say "this is [name] from [company]", don't name your company, never skip follow-up.

### LC-07#25 | BENCHMARK | topic: outreach | also: unit-economics, management-ops | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:23:11
claim: Cold calling benchmark: 100 calls a day at a 25% pickup rate yields about 4 engaged leads — roughly one per hour of calling.
detail: Cost per engaged lead is your time or the rep's wage. The pickup-to-lead rate is stated as "25" but the math implies ~16% (unclear in audio). Track this from day one: Hormozi's first two cold-outreach attempts failed because the hires never tracked metrics; the third succeeded because that person did.
numbers: 100 calls/day → 25 pickups → 4 engaged leads; ~1 engaged lead/hour over ~4 hours.

### LC-07#26 | BENCHMARK | topic: outreach | also: - | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:23:11
claim: A strong outbound-email system at a portfolio company runs about a 30% open rate with about 10% of opens replying.
detail: Real numbers from an email-only outbound operation acquisition.com tuned; opens are the email equivalent of pickups.

### LC-07#27 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: outreach, management-ops | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:24:10
claim: Give each rep a fixed weekly lead allotment at about 70% of capacity and hold them to an absolute quota.
detail: Too many leads get wasted; reps should treat leads like their children. Capacity 100 → give ~70. Equal allotments allow absolute quotas, e.g., 70 leads → 7 appointments. Founders should work their own leads early to learn what one is worth.
numbers: ~70% of capacity; 70 leads → 7 appointments.

### LC-07#28 | FRAMEWORK | topic: outreach | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Core Four | novelty: canon | ts: 00:24:57
claim: Cold outreach plan: you send a hook plus a lead magnet or offer, via any private channel, to your built list, every day, at least 100 a day.
detail: Who: you. What: hook + lead magnet/core offer with big fast value. Where: any private channel. To whom: scraped, bought or built list. When: daily, 7 days a week. Why: turn leads into engaged leads. How: calls, voicemail drops, email/text blasts, DMs, video/voice messages, direct mail, handwritten cards. How much: 100+/day. How often per lead: 2–3 touches a day for the first few days, then once daily for 7 days. How long: as long as it takes.

### LC-07#29 | PRINCIPLE | topic: outreach | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:25:44
claim: Seven reasons to build cold outreach: no content or ads needed, invisible to competitors, reliable, few platform changes, simpler compliance, a more sellable business, and hard to copy.
detail: 1) No dependence on a face or content. 2) It is private: Hormozi found a gym-space company doing $10M/month from cold outreach he had never heard of — which convinced him to take it seriously. 3) Funnel numbers run like clockwork and the pipeline stacks — leads from 6–18 months ago return. 4) Call and mail rules barely change, unlike algorithms, CPMs and ad bans. 5) Only legal compliance, not platform rules (tobacco, firearms, weight loss, adult products). 6) Sellability (separate atom). 7) Competitors can't see it, so the same script, follow-up and offer can run for 20 years.
numbers: Unseen competitor ≈ $10M/month from cold outreach.

### LC-07#30 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: outreach | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:28:07
claim: A business fed by an outreach team rather than a founder-rainmaker is more sellable; ~50% of Gym Launch's sales coming from cold outbound was a main driver of its sale multiple.
detail: A buyer seeing 20 people cold-calling daily knows an owner change won't stop acquisition; a single rainmaker is a large risk. Outbound gave Gym Launch a second channel as strong as paid ads and a clear lever — double the team, double sales — which the buyer roughly did.
numbers: ~50% of sales from cold outbound at sale.

### LC-07#31 | CASE | topic: outreach | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:29:39
claim: Fully automating scraping, writing and sending let one VA send 2,000 emails a day for a portfolio company, yielding 40 engaged leads and about 4 customers daily and pushing it past $1M a month.
detail: Response rate dropped with personalization removed, but volume compensated. It broke the company through the "100 customers a month" ceiling. It joined acquisition.com at $250K/month and now has LTV over $20K per customer.
numbers: 2,000 emails/day (~60K/month) → 40 engaged leads/day (~2%) → ~10% close → 4 customers/day; start $250K/month; LTV > $20K.
