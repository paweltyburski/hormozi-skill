### MZ-A46-I4vKbkI#01 | CASE | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals, scaling-constraints | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A gym-growth software company at $4M (target $10M in two years) has strong economics, so the only question is what breaks at higher volume.
detail: Metrics: profit about $2.2M, CAC ~$1,000, Meta ~70% of acquisition, $500 a month average (plus annual plans), churn ~3.5% monthly, about 35 sales a month, ~$35K ad spend a month, lifetime ~$12K revenue with ~20% delivery cost, so roughly 12:1. Hormozi's math: existing velocity alone gives modest growth (his arithmetic about $4.8M is unclear in audio); scaling needs about $300K a month of ad spend.
numbers: Revenue $4M; profit ~$2.2M; CAC ~$1,000; churn ~3.5%/month; 35 sales/month; LTV ~$12K; ad spend ~$35K/month; LTV:CAC ~12:1.

### MZ-A46-I4vKbkI#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: product-delivery | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:47
claim: When profitable and fulfilment is near its limit, and you are not in a rush, Hormozi would build capacity first, because being out of capacity means either profit or reputation takes a hit.
detail: Here the website-build team of one plus VAs was straining. Hormozi: double the VAs now (cheap), so the team is tuned and you feel bullish about spending; hesitation is "pulling punches". Building capacity keeps ethics high.
### MZ-A46-I4vKbkI#03 | EVOLUTION | topic: verticals | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:33
claim: He says he sold Gym Launch because he could not see how to make it bigger and had grown resentful, though he now believes a $400M exit was possible with few changes.
detail: He lost love for the avatar; clients who blamed him despite not doing the work caused resentment. Early on he assumed his firm was better since it did not "burn" people, later learned only about half of clients do the work. He started at 20, entered gyms at 26, around ten years in.
numbers: Possible exit ~$400M (retrospective); "half a billion or a billion" now seen as possible.
