### YT-lIC8fYbrkII#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:46
claim: If an employee is not doing what you want, assume it is your fault and check three things in order: do they know what to do, do they know how, and do they know why it matters.
detail: What = communication, How = training, Why = motivation. Hormozi took the first two from Andy Grove's High Output Management and added motivation from experience. At each step ask what you can do: document it, supply a course or call, or explain the larger purpose.

### YT-lIC8fYbrkII#02 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:46
claim: Rule: if it is not written, it never happened, so follow every verbal directive with a message in Slack, Asana or email.
detail: Include what is expected and by when, so you can later answer whether they knew what you wanted.

### YT-lIC8fYbrkII#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:44
claim: Employees lose motivation when they cannot see why their role matters or do not buy into the company mission, so tie every function to the bottom line.
detail: Most common causes: feeling like a cog, which produces busy work. Managers should explain what happens if the function did not exist and what a good job produces (IT enables decisions, marketing brings business in, service prevents chargebacks). Culture and training are what scale decision-making.

### YT-lIC8fYbrkII#04 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:57
claim: Usually two of the three are present; the missing one shows the pattern.
detail: Knows why and has the skill but not what to do: a lot of times is paralyzed or works on things that are not priorities. Knows what and why but not how: usually pretends to be busy out of shame. Knows what and how but not why: typically the worst case, tends to check out and is usually probably job hunting.
