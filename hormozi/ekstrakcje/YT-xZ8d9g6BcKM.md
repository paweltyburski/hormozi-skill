### YT-xZ8d9g6BcKM#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: verticals | also: unit-economics | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A typical frozen yogurt store sells about 500 eight-ounce cups, about $2,100 a day, yet nets the owner little.
detail: Annual sales run $715,000-$800,000 but net margins are only 10-15%; he quotes about $328 a day owner pay and about $93,000 a year take-home for the average Menchie's owner. Fruit spoils, machines break, and a prominent lease is needed.
numbers: About $2,100 a day from 500 cups of 8 oz; $715K-$800K a year; margins 10-15%.
applies_to: Retail food franchises
### YT-xZ8d9g6BcKM#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit, verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:18
claim: Franchisors set fees so franchisees earn just enough to keep going, taking most of the upside off the top.
detail: A royalty of about 6% of revenue is about 60% of take-home at a 10% margin. Franchisors aim for 20-25% return on capital versus about 10% for stocks. Bulk buying should save costs, but franchisors often mark supplies up.
numbers: Royalty about 6% of top line.

### YT-xZ8d9g6BcKM#03 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:23
claim: Buy a failed business's whole fit-out from a business foreclosure site at a fraction of cost.
detail: He bought his first gym equipment this way for $13,000 by refreshing the listing weekly. Three failed yogurt franchise stores were listed, the dearest at $20,000. Someone has usually failed at your business before; motivated sellers part with stock at about 5-10 cents on the dollar.
numbers: Gym equipment $13,000; yogurt store at most $20,000.

### YT-xZ8d9g6BcKM#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: pricing, verticals | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:23
claim: In a yogurt cup the base is the profit and toppings are the cost, so the owner wants as much yogurt in the cup as possible.
detail: A cup is about 75% yogurt, 25% toppings by weight. Yogurt costs about 8 cents an ounce and sells at 50-60 cents; toppings cost 10-40 cents and sell at 25-60, and fresh fruit can break even or lose.
numbers: Yogurt cost about $0.08/oz, price $0.50-$0.60/oz.

### YT-xZ8d9g6BcKM#05 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:54
claim: Selling by weight instead of cup size lets customers set their own spend, and they blame themselves, not the price.
detail: A shopper who fills a cup and sees $7 feels they took too much; a fixed $7 item makes the shop feel expensive. Let customers choose price by usage wherever possible.

### YT-xZ8d9g6BcKM#06 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:54
claim: Running out of small cups lifted a yogurt chain's sales an estimated 20-30%, showing the power of the default option.
detail: With only medium and large left, customers took the bigger size as normal; chains then dropped small cups, since a small portion in a huge cup looks silly.
numbers: Estimated sales lift 20-30%.

### YT-xZ8d9g6BcKM#07 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:59
claim: Order a self-serve line with the highest-margin item first and the costliest last, so the customer's capacity is used on profit.
detail: Yogurt first, dry toppings next, fresh fruit at the end when the cup is nearly full. Buffets put salad first and seafood last.
