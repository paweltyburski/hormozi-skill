### YT-LMlbWtUFa4E#01 | EVOLUTION | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: His view moved from earning a billion a year, to earning it over a lifetime, to owning something valued at a billion.
detail: The value of what you own can rise (e.g. a 10x multiple on profit growing by $1M adds $10M of value) without being taxed as take-home income.
### YT-LMlbWtUFa4E#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:50
claim: At a 10x earnings multiple, a $1M profit increase adds $10M of value but only about $0.5M of after-tax take-home.
detail: A business with $5M profit is worth $50M; at $6M it is worth $60M, untaxed until sale. So raise profit in the core business instead of building side income.
numbers: Profit $5M to $6M; value $50M to $60M; take-home after ~40% tax ~$3M to ~$3.5M.

### YT-LMlbWtUFa4E#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:38
claim: A billion-dollar company needs roughly $70-100M of EBITDA at about 15x, around $1.3-1.5M of profit a week.
detail: At a 30% margin that is several million of revenue a week; he rounds to about $300M a year (his arithmetic is loose).
numbers: EBITDA $70-100M; ~15x; ~$1.3-1.5M profit per week; 30% margin; ~$300M revenue.

### YT-LMlbWtUFa4E#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:42
claim: He personally would not pursue an opportunity capped at around $100M a year; to think big, look for one not limited by your time, serving a very big market, with very high gross margin, and probably a very long time horizon.
detail: A goal sized at $10M forces larger increments than a six-figure goal: 1,000 sales of a $10,000 offer means three sales a day, hence nine calls a day at a one-in-three close rate.
numbers: 1,000 x $10,000 = $10M; 9 calls a day at 33% close.
### YT-LMlbWtUFa4E#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:02
claim: Why buyers pay 10x earnings: 20% yearly growth plus a loan turns a $50M purchase into a very large gain.
detail: Profit growing 20% a year from $5M reaches about $12.5M in year five, worth about $125M at 10x. Buying with $15M down and $35M borrowed, partly repaid from cash flow, he calls roughly $120M of gain (rough arithmetic).
numbers: Price $50M (15 down, 35 loan); year-5 profit ~$12.5M; value ~$125M.
