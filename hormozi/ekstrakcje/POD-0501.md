### POD-0501#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:56
claim: Someone ahead can spot the few missing links in your skill chain, which you cannot see; think of skills as a bridge where every brick is needed.
detail: Having 20 of 28 bricks, people keep reinforcing known ones by buying more courses on copywriting or landing pages while the gap is ads, selling or product. You pay for lessons in time or money, usually with whatever you value least. He repeats that ignorance costs the gap between your income and a million a year.
numbers: Example: 20 of 28 "bricks".

### POD-0501#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:51
claim: Never ask a successful person "how can I provide value?"; do the forethought yourself, because they want time, convenience and value first.
detail: Offering to do anything for free (fetch coffee) costs them more attention than paying someone. Empathise with what the other person wants and reverse what they would do for you.

### POD-0501#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:39
claim: Hormozi has offered speaking fee times two, sometimes $250,000, and up to $350,000 (refused) for one hour with a person who sees reality more clearly.
detail: If you cannot buy their time, you must trade for it. He argues people who earn more have a more accurate view of reality.
numbers: $250,000 per hour typical high offer; $350,000 maximum, declined.

### POD-0501#04 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:52
claim: First route to people above you: build a platform with attention, because distribution is a currency they lack.
detail: Examples: Ray Dalio goes on podcasts of far smaller people, and Joe Rogan pays guests in attention, not money. Any genre works if you have enough eyeballs.

### POD-0501#05 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:27
claim: Second route: barter a base skill by doing about a day of full work first, as if for a paying client, before the call.
detail: Identify problems and create the solutions in advance, walk them through it, and they will want to give back. Do not prepare them for it, and downplay the effort. This stacks goodwill (IOUs) worth more than money; his wife teased that he was always on "another favour call".
numbers: About a day of pre-work, not 10 minutes.

### POD-0501#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:01
claim: Rules for an IOU: never say they owe you, and always make the redemption a question.
detail: Pretending they owe it shows you gave for the wrong reason. Redeem with something like "I hope the earlier work is going well; do you know anything about X?", then schedule. Ideally follow up with more value before asking, and ask on the call; if they give nothing back, you probably stop giving to them. You may hold the IOU and use it later.
### POD-0501#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:10
claim: Givers move faster than matchers and takers; most people are matchers or takers, so expect to be exploited more often than repaid, but the few giver-to-giver relationships are worth it.
detail: Expect matchers and takers to return half, a third or 20% of what you gave; if their skill is the missing link, that is still worth it. Givers who recognise givers try to outgive each other for life.
numbers: Typical return: 50%, one third, or 20% of what you gave.

### POD-0501#08 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:30
claim: Trade a skill for a skill, then trade two skills for a third, and go deep with a few valuable people rather than shallow with many.
detail: Business cards and ten-minute meetings are useless; five valuable people given significant time beats 100 people given 10 minutes. Early on, go deep with everyone because you cannot yet judge who is valuable.
numbers: 5 people with deep time versus 100 people with 10 minutes.

### POD-0501#09 | EXAMPLE | topic: sales-process | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:53
claim: Hormozi's barter skills were fitness plans, local ads and, most valuable, fixing a company's sales process, delivered as complete finished work.
detail: For fitness he made a full meal plan, grocery list and preparation instructions. For sales he reviewed a company's whole funnel and follow-up sequences, recorded a call with one of their reps, and offered to train the team. Clients then offered help (for example flying out to record YouTube ads with him). Sales fixing paid far more than training, so it was traded most often. Once, instead of paying a consultant for paid-ads help, he cashed in a favour banked earlier.
