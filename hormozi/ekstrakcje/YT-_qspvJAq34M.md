### YT-_qspvJAq34M#01 | CASE | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:50
claim: His answer to his father's doubt about $42,000 programs was to reframe it as a trade that fits each part of the equation.
detail: He asked whether his father would pay $42,000 if it made him $239,000, it took 11 months, it needed about 15 hours a week and he paid as the money arrived. The father said yes. The questions asked (return, how sure, how long, what he must do, how he pays) map onto the components of value.
numbers: $42,000 price; $239,000 return; 11 months; ~15 hours a week.

### YT-_qspvJAq34M#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:18
claim: A paid expedited DMV service beat the free government version by selling speed.
detail: A friend runs 14 such locations in New Mexico charging about $50 for a service that is free from the government. He draws the rule that when you compete against free, compete on speed. A similar example is paying extra for first in line on Lyft.
numbers: 14 locations; ~$50 versus free.

### YT-_qspvJAq34M#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:11:55
claim: Precision Nutrition's growth came from redefining the dream outcome of their certifications as belonging to a community of similar people.
detail: Dr. John Berardi explained that buyers did not want continuing education; the certification was a socially acceptable reason to join people like them. Restating the outcome accurately was a breakthrough for the business.

### YT-_qspvJAq34M#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:50
claim: The time you spend on fulfilment should not be in your value equation, since a surgeon doing the 10,000th operation spends less time but is worth more.
detail: Buyers pay for the perceived likelihood of the outcome, not for how long delivery takes.

### YT-_qspvJAq34M#05 | EVOLUTION | topic: offers | also: operator-mindset | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:32
claim: He began by promising bigger outcomes and piling up testimonials, and later focused on the bottom of the equation; the biggest moats sit there.
detail: He calls raising the dream outcome the amateur starting place. Amazon, Netflix and Uber won by reducing delay, effort and sacrifice, the harder work and the harder thing for rivals to copy.

### YT-_qspvJAq34M#06 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: product-delivery | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:19
claim: Score two offers side by side with a yes-or-no on each component to see why one sells at ten or forty times the price.
detail: Personal training scored 1 of 4 (outcome yes; likelihood, speed and low effort no) and liposuction 4 of 4. The result: one needs an hour of persuasion to sell a $1,000 package, the other sells $40,000 over the phone in under 15 minutes.
numbers: $1,000 package vs $40,000 over phone in under 15 minutes; 1 of 4 vs 4 of 4.

### YT-_qspvJAq34M#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: pricing | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:07
claim: The dream outcome sets the price category, and men tend to pay more for money-making than weight loss because it raises status.
