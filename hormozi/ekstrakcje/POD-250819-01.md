### POD-250819-01#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Classic Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:12
claim: A storage owner's "free first month" earns about $127 per customer through a basket of add-ons sold on the spot.
detail: The unit really is free, but the owner sells a heavy-duty lock for $47 (the only kind that resists bolt cutters), then boxes, tape, labels and markers for the other roughly $80. Moving help goes through an affiliate who pays a kickback; dollies, hand trucks and straps are rented for a fee. Free insurance of $500 can be raised to $100,000 for $10 a month, but only with his lock.
numbers: Free month, average ~$127 collected; lock $47; insurance upgrade $10 a month, $500 to $100,000.

### POD-250819-01#02 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: product-delivery | fw: Classic Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:45
claim: Find add-on offers by asking, step after step, what else the buyer will need next, then source it in-house, through an affiliate or as a rental.
detail: The owner walks through the buyer's next needs one by one (lock, boxes, moving, protection). Needs he cannot serve himself become affiliate commissions, and one-off tools become rentals, since buyers dislike buying things they will use once.

### POD-250819-01#03 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:32
claim: Offer the next size up by default, because customers underestimate how much they need.
detail: In storage, everyone owns more than they think and rents too small a unit, so the owner always quotes one size larger. The customer gets the space needed and the owner earns more.

### POD-250819-01#04 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:22
claim: Hormozi opened gyms at full capacity on day one by selling memberships in the empty building, funded only by a small lease deposit.
detail: Put down about $3,000 on a lease, run a few days of ads and sell customers inside the empty gym. The cash from sign-ups pays for more ads, equipment, paint, flooring, furniture and signage. He opened a new location about every six months with no debt.
numbers: ~$3,000 lease deposit; one new gym every ~6 months; no debt.

### POD-250819-01#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models, verticals | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:14
claim: His front-end gym funnel turned $5 leads into about $680 of gross profit per paying customer within 48 hours.
detail: A free six-week challenge ad until about 20 leads a day. About half the leads show up, and about half of those buy a $600 programme, so about 25% of leads pay. Supplements add roughly $80 per customer. At $5 per lead, that is roughly $1 spent for $34 returned (implied CAC about $20 per customer).
numbers: 20 leads/day; 50% show; 50% of shows buy $600; ~25% of leads pay; +$80 supplements = ~$680; $5 per lead; ~34:1 in 48 hours.

### POD-250819-01#06 | BENCHMARK | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:00
claim: The gym back end credited the $600 toward a one-year membership, converting two thirds into recurring revenue.
detail: A few weeks after sign-up he told members they could get the $600 back as credit if they joined for a year. Two thirds of sign-ups converted, so about $3,000 of lease deposit became a full gym with about $20,000 a month of recurring memberships, all within 30 days, then repeated at the next site.
numbers: 2/3 of sign-ups convert to year memberships; ~$20,000 per month recurring; ~$3,000 down; 30 days.

### POD-250819-01#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:46
claim: When your skill is far larger than your opportunity, sell the method to peers instead of running the operation.
detail: After a $25,000 one-hour call, the marketer said he had a level 10 skill set and a level 2 opportunity and should teach other gym owners instead of running gyms. Over nine months he closed the newest gym and sold the other five. He then did 30-plus on-site turnarounds before switching to licensing.
numbers: $25,000 for a one-hour call; 9 months to exit the gyms; 30+ turnarounds before licensing.
