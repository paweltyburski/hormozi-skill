### YT-pWbSl7d0tEc#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:35
claim: Customer concentration is a deal killer: if a few big customers are most of revenue, buyers discount heavily or walk.
detail: Example: with ten large customers, losing two or three could wipe out a long-established business. It is worse when those relationships exist because of the owner personally.

### YT-pWbSl7d0tEc#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:35

detail: Many owners do not know how they get customers; they just come. Related killer at 00:09:58: single-channel dependence, where if that one channel shut off, buyers fear going to zero.

### YT-pWbSl7d0tEc#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:13

### YT-pWbSl7d0tEc#11 | DEFINITION | topic: finance-exit | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:58

### YT-pWbSl7d0tEc#13 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:34
claim: Pick a banker who has recently sold your type of business at your size.
detail: A bank that only does very large deals (e.g. $500M+) knows buyers who won't want a $10M-revenue company, because for them the check is too small. Recent comparable deals mean current contacts.
numbers: Example mismatch: bank doing $500M+ deals versus $10M top-line company.
### YT-pWbSl7d0tEc#14 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:40
claim: Find bankers by networking for introductions, send a one-page teaser to get a call, then meet four to six and negotiate fees.
detail: You first sell them on representing you; interested ones fly out and pitch you. Ask about similar recent deals and their buyer network. Hormozi had no investment-banking contacts and found this slow.
numbers: Four to six banker meetings.

### YT-pWbSl7d0tEc#15 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:43
claim: Bankers' fee terms to negotiate are the upfront fee, targets, ratchets, break-up fee and minimums.
detail: Upfront fees are asked when they doubt a sale, which is an argument against them. Ratchets raise the percentage above price thresholds. Minimums mean no payment below an offer level, which he included so bankers stay motivated.

### YT-pWbSl7d0tEc#16 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:39
claim: Negotiate the banker's fee as a percentage of cash you actually receive, not of enterprise value.
detail: Example: selling a 10% stake of a $1B company yields $100M cash, but a 4% fee on the whole enterprise value would take $40M of it.
numbers: 4% of $1B EV = $40M against $100M received.

### YT-pWbSl7d0tEc#17 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:27
claim: Write down the price you would accept, and why, before talking to buyers.
detail: Offers and letters of intent can inflate expectations. Selling is emotional because it is part of your identity. Also give the banker the specific selling points only you know; you will sell the company better than they do.

### YT-pWbSl7d0tEc#18 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:33
claim: Bankers, like estate agents, want a deal closed more than the best deal, so be willing to walk away and say "if I don't get this point, the deal is off."
detail: Institutional counterparts say no to everything until you threaten to leave, then they move and your banker negotiates harder. You win by having options and a business you are happy to keep.

### YT-pWbSl7d0tEc#19 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:58

### YT-pWbSl7d0tEc#29 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: management-ops | fw: Prep for Sale | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:29:13
claim: Due diligence covers quality of earnings, legal compliance, customer and employee interviews, background checks and employment contracts.
detail: Buyers check that numbers are real, that you own the company, and whether lawsuits or compliance gaps exist; an acquaintance lost two nine-figure deals over non-compliance. They interview past customers and loyal staff to see if the business runs without you, and check contracts for staff who leave with you.

claim: Accept fundamental warranties (you own the company and trademarks) but negotiate out indemnities for things beyond your control.
detail: Fundamental breaches can make you liable for the whole price. Be ready to walk on the rest.

detail: Rolled equity extends the non-compete for as long as you are a shareholder. Argue categories (makeup is not skincare). Changing one defined word reshaped his agreement. Honesty about plans works better than guessing.
