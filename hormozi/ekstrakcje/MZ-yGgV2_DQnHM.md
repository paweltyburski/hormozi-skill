### MZ-yGgV2_DQnHM#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:23
claim: A baseball arm-health programme sells the same product to individual athletes and to teams, which blurs messaging.
detail: Revenue was $830K last year, goal $5M. Teams make about 40% of the business. Hormozi advises going all-in on teams as the main revenue driver, while keeping player-focused branding so players ask their teams to buy.
numbers: $830K revenue; teams ~40% of revenue.

### MZ-yGgV2_DQnHM#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:00
claim: The caller said individual customers have an LTV of $360 against a CAC of about $120, and team customers an LTV of $6,000-$8,000.
detail: The caller said the individual plan is $45 a month or $450 a year. He said the team data is still early (about three years in) and he had just started running team ads, so his team CAC is not clear yet.
numbers: Caller's figures: individual LTV $360, CAC ~$120, price $45/month; team LTV $6,000-$8,000.
### MZ-yGgV2_DQnHM#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:48
claim: Team customers are stickier because players age out while the team stays and keeps buying.
detail: Even if team CAC were 3x higher it is unlikely to be 20x higher, so the team sale is the better compounding game. Small businesses often start consumer, land an enterprise account, then should do more of it.
