### POD-0775#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: More Better New | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:47
claim: Optimisation versus order of magnitude is a separate axis from more/better/new: small funnel gains compete with step-function moves that make the gains irrelevant.
detail: Inside the funnel (after the click) everything is conversion optimisation, which is capped. Big step changes usually come at the front (media: 10x clicks, brand, giveaway) or the back (product: new price tier, new service line, much higher LTV), and these then let you spend more at the front.

### POD-0775#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: paid-ads | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: CRO | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:27
claim: Once something works, new changes mostly lose: 14 of 16 split tests on a free-trial sales page failed to beat the control.
detail: The better things get, the more likely a change from what works is not an improvement, and there is typically a decreasing incremental return, since you can only go to 100%. A new thing has about the same failure odds as the many prior attempts.
numbers: 16 tests run to beat the first control; 14 failed (about 88%), on a school's free-trial sales page.
### POD-0775#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: sales-team | fw: More Better New | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:07
claim: Choose between "more" and "better" by comparing effort for the same return: with one salesperson, hire a second; with twenty, a 20% lift equals four hires.
detail: One rep plus a second doubles throughput, even if the new one is half as good (+50%), versus a 10-20% lift from training. At 20 reps, a 20% training lift equals going from 20 to 24; then ask if that effort beats recruiting, hiring and onboarding four people. If the team's performance is low because it is mostly new people, it can make sense to tighten and improve first.
numbers: 1 rep to 2 = about 2x; 20 reps with 20% lift = 4 extra hires.
### POD-0775#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-team | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Impact vs Investment Matrix (RICE) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:10
claim: Weigh options by risk-adjusted return (reach, impact, confidence, cost): in his example a team closing 10% could probably triple sales, while one at 50% probably has a low chance of a lift.
detail: Going from 10% to 30% close with the same team might beat tripling headcount in effort; going from 50% to 60% is probably low likelihood. Likelihood of improvement falls as the baseline rises.
numbers: 10% to 30% close = 3x sales; 50% to 60% = 20% lift, low likelihood.
### POD-0775#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: paid-ads | fw: CRO | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:27

detail: Hormozi, who has earned heavily from conversion work, agreed with the logic: obsessive efficiency squeezes the same fruit instead of finding more fruit.

### POD-0775#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: paid-ads | fw: CRO | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:29
claim: Conversion is capped at 100% but traffic is not, so beyond a point improving traffic has more headroom than improving conversion.
detail: An e-commerce page at 2% will not go to 50%, yet traffic can grow 25x or 100x.
numbers: Example: 2% conversion cannot become 50%.

### POD-0775#08 | CASE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:23
claim: An entrepreneur spent about six months studying tax savings and realised that growing the business would have earned more than the tax saved.
detail: Reframe: instead of cutting a 30% tax on $100,000, ask what would make $1M or $10M a year, at which point paying 37% on $10M no longer matters. Questions get solved, so beware solving ones not worth answering.
numbers: 30% tax on $100,000 income leaves $70,000; 37% on $10M still leaves about $6.3M.

### POD-0775#09 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:17:53
claim: Ask what one thing, if true, would change everything about the business; then ask whether finishing everything else on the list would equal it.
detail: If not, put zero more attention on incremental items and all effort on making that one thing real. The answer is typically media or product, sometimes technology, and sometimes it is not on the to-do list at all. Repeat per function: finance, IT, people, product, marketing.

### POD-0775#10 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:44
claim: Save order-of-magnitude bets for media or product, not the funnel: a 10x in clicks, or a $1,000 product replaced by a $100,000 one, is realistic; 5x from CRO is not.
detail: Conversion work earns its place by unlocking media. For an e-commerce store, several steps (call team for abandoned carts, scarcity, bonuses, seasonal pushes, payment options) may add up to about 2x and unlock LTV that lets you spend 5x on advertising.
numbers: 20x revenue from a $1,000 to $100,000 product; 5x CRO unlikely, about 2x realistic.

### POD-0775#11 | BENCHMARK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:08
claim: For a small business, Hormozi would call growth below about 20% "slow-ish"; if stuck there, more optimisation might not help and the business might benefit from a big move.
detail: Big moves named: big brand association, big giveaway, offer change. Offer changes are step functions that touch pricing, finance, delivery, advertising and sales together.
numbers: In his view, growth under 20% (period unspecified) is slow-ish for a small business.
### POD-0775#12 | DECISION_RULE | topic: paid-ads | also: scaling-constraints | fw: CRO | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:03
claim: Maybe do an intensive CRO sprint if the limit on the business has been your ability to spend money to get customers; otherwise, he suggests looking for the one big thing to ship.
detail: The sprint he mentions is a three or six month period of aggressively testing several things per week. If you may have already done a lot of testing on pages, copy and follow-up, ask what would make 10x or 50x more people see your stuff, or what is 50x more expensive or has 50x the LTV that you can sell to a large percentage of customers.
numbers: Sprint: 3-6 months, 3-4 tests per week at a time.
### POD-0775#13 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:56
claim: Fast feedback from small wins is addictive and can blind you to the one change that makes them irrelevant.
detail: Example: when a creator with a huge audience launches a product, audience and brand decide success, not page optimisation.

### POD-0775#14 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:00
claim: Five things done in sequence finish faster than five done at once, because task switching kills productivity and sequencing multiplies team urgency.
detail: A company's resources can all be pointed at one thing, as people are not machines. Exception: a screaming problem that threatens the business's existence becomes number one.
numbers: Team urgency rises about tenfold when work is sequenced.
