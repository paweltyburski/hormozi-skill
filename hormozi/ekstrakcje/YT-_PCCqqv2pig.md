### YT-_PCCqqv2pig#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:47
claim: Reciprocity effects scale with the size of the gift: in a restaurant study, one mint with the bill raised tips by 3%, two mints by 14%, and a second mint added after a pause by 23%.
detail: Gift size should match the person you want to repay you. A store giving every visitor a cold drink pays about $5 a day and needs only one extra sale a week to profit. The point: cheap gifts are everywhere in business and can be used immediately.
numbers: Tip increase: 1 mint +3%; 2 mints +14%; mint, walk away, return with a second +23%. Drink cost ~$5/day.

### YT-_PCCqqv2pig#03 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: Lead Magnet Steps | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:05
claim: Get a small public commitment about identity first, then cite it when making the ask.
detail: Ask "are you the type of person who takes action and invests in themselves?" and, after a yes, reuse it at the close ("since you are someone who..."). It works because people act consistently with a self-image they stated aloud. A neighbourhood study: a small window sign for slow driving first, then a large yard sign two weeks later, got far more yeses than asking for the large sign cold. Lead magnets and front-end offers use the same small-yes logic.

### YT-_PCCqqv2pig#04 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: offers | fw: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:38
claim: Use a physical wall of proof: print and frame every five-star review for a floor-to-ceiling lobby, and fill the rest with before-and-after photos.
detail: For brick-and-mortar locations, screenshot all online reviews. Social proof works because people take cues from others.

### YT-_PCCqqv2pig#05 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:24
claim: When a prospect doubts a result is possible for them, point to the proof in the room and ask how much proof they would need and when their requirement becomes unreasonable.
detail: Sequence: gesture to the wall of reviews and photos; ask what level of proof they would need to believe; then ask at what point their demand for proof exceeds the amount that exists. This attacks the belief that it will not work for them or that he cannot do it.

### YT-_PCCqqv2pig#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: ACA | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:24
claim: People like those who flatter them, even when they know the flattery is paid for, and there is no ceiling on genuine flattery.
detail: In a study, subjects who knew strangers were paid to compliment them still liked them more. He applies ACA in conversations because being listened to is what people value most.

### YT-_PCCqqv2pig#07 | EXAMPLE | topic: sales-process | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:10
claim: Hormozi kept his energy and rapport up during 20-plus in-person consults a day with playful tricks: a mini trampoline by the desk and, earlier, a cartwheel before each handshake.
detail: Prospects found him goofy and more closed, because they were buying the feeling of the conversation. It also fights fatigue in front-facing roles.
numbers: 20+ consults per day in person.

### YT-_PCCqqv2pig#08 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:41
claim: Authority lowers the filters people apply to a message, so the same advice is accepted faster from a trusted source; show diplomas, awards and a track record.
detail: At his gym he printed certifications for himself and every trainer, with real external ones plus internal ones (he notes US fitness certifications are not regulated). Visitors rarely asked which. Show the record of what you have done.

### YT-_PCCqqv2pig#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: promotions-launches | fw: Scarcity | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:14
claim: Frame limits as loss ("you don't want to miss out") because people feel losses more than gains, and use limits of time, supply and one-time offers.
detail: Luxury brands send stores an unannounced quantity of items so staff cannot say more are in the back, controlling scarcity centrally; a few stores kept one-in, one-out lines after COVID because lines signal demand. Amazon's "23 left" works the same way.

### YT-_PCCqqv2pig#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: offers | also: promotions-launches | fw: Scarcity | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:05
claim: Real scarcity increases sales, so state your true capacity openly rather than inventing limits.
detail: If you can handle 20 customers a week or hold a set stock, tell buyers.
numbers: Example capacity: 20 customers per week.
