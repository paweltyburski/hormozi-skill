### POD-0545#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: paid-ads | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:51
claim: When shut out by regular processors, he accepted a high-risk processor at about 8% processing plus a 10% reserve and a monthly cap of $50,000.
detail: A shutdown counts like a bankruptcy mark on payment processing. He had 60-80 contracts a day that he could not run. His fix was using the cap in stages: run $50,000 on January 29, another $50,000 on February 1 when the month reset, then add two more processors for $50,000 each, with more added two weeks later. February ended with about $30,000 profit.
numbers: ~8% fee, 10% holdback; $50,000/month cap; $3,300/day team cost; February profit ~$30,000; 60-80 contracts a day.

### POD-0545#06 | CASE | topic: product-delivery | also: retention-ltv, verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:23:48
claim: One gym owner resented a launcher taking $100,000 in a month (when an owner earns ~$36,000 a year) and told members to refund and pay him half, causing $150,000 in refunds.
detail: The model had the launcher keep the first payment and the gym deliver afterward. Since the launcher held the money, refunds hit his account. It became a vicious cycle: he sold more to cover refunds, which produced more refunds. He admits he did not understand the flaw then.
numbers: Average gym owner take-home ~$36,000/year; $100,000 taken in one month; $150,000 refunds.

### POD-0545#08 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:26:51
