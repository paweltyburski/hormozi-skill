### YT-sGv2BTUCcCM#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Triage every business first as supply constrained (customers, not enough capacity) or demand constrained (capacity, not enough customers); you cannot have both at once.
detail: Demand problem: empty tables, open calendar slots, stock on shelves. Supply problem: waiting lists, turning people away, cannot make product or take clients. Test: if you doubled ads, outreach or content tomorrow, would sales double or would you create a mess? A mess means supply; doubling means demand.

### YT-sGv2BTUCcCM#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Price to Value Discrepancy | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:17
claim: If supply constrained, raising prices a lot (50% to 100%) is one of the primary paths to fixing it, and the freed time can then be used to add capacity.
detail: Case: a fully booked consultant works 60 hours, books three months ahead and keeps making more content. Even if 30% of clients leave after a doubling, he earns more and works with fewer people. Then increase delivery ratio (one-to-many) or hire and train others. He could double income with 15 fewer hours.
numbers: Raise 50-100%; 30% client loss still nets more; 60-hour weeks.
applies_to: Service providers at capacity.
### YT-sGv2BTUCcCM#03 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: paid-ads, product-delivery | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:36
claim: A scheduling-software company with a loved product but only 5% of spend on marketing has a demand problem.
detail: Competitors spend 20-30% on marketing. Hormozi says he swung between "marketing is everything" and "product is everything"; hold the product steady, move cash and talent to getting customers, and choose a Core Four method. Products also often improve by deleting, and constant change can erode service quality.

### YT-sGv2BTUCcCM#05 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:43
claim: A steakhouse full on Friday and Saturday but empty midweek should price by day, not buy more ads.
detail: Waits of 90+ minutes on weekends. Options: raise weekend prices, sell a pay-to-skip-the-line option, stay open later (close at 11 instead of 9). With 10% margins a 20% price rise triples profit on those days; he states this lifts the whole business profit about 1.5x if the two days are half of revenue (arithmetic as stated). Higher weekend prices push some demand into the week.
numbers: Margin 10%, price +20% = profit x3 on those days; stated 1.5x total.

### YT-sGv2BTUCcCM#06 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:15
claim: Frame a peak-time rise as an off-peak discount: weekdays 20% less instead of weekends 20% more.
detail: The net effect is the same, but people accept the discount and pay normal prices on busy days.
numbers: 20% off peak instead of 20% on peak.

### YT-sGv2BTUCcCM#07 | CASE | topic: product-delivery | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:11:02
claim: A fitness-equipment store whose sales jumped 4x in COVID but kept running ads worsened shipping times from two weeks to eight; fix supply, not demand.
detail: Reviews turned bad and service was swamped. Fixes: sell non-stock items to buyers (a warranty, affiliate or white-label apps, insurance, services such as flooring) to raise cash, then pay suppliers about 20% more to be first in line and add redundant suppliers. These are short-term cash plays, not a new business.
numbers: Sales 4x; shipping 2 to 8 weeks; pay supplier about 20% more.
applies_to: Physical-product businesses with supply limits.

### YT-sGv2BTUCcCM#08 | CASE | topic: sales-process | also: unit-economics, verticals | fw: Basic Sales KPIs | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:15:11
claim: A fully booked plumbing firm with thin profit should teach technicians a two- or three-line upsell script, rehearsed each morning.
detail: Technicians finish jobs fast but rarely offer upgrades, memberships or ask about other units; overtime at time and half compresses profit. Role-play daily and track the share upsold to a second job; at 50% revenue rises 50% with similar cost. Technically demand constrained because more money per customer funds hiring.
numbers: Upsell 50% of jobs raises revenue about 50%.

### YT-sGv2BTUCcCM#10 | CASE | topic: sales-process | also: scaling-constraints | fw: CRO | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:20:02
claim: A commercial-building software firm stuck 8 years closed only about 2 of 500 demos; fixing close rate beat doubling the sales team.
detail: About 10 demos a week; leadership planned to double sales. Hormozi aimed for 100 deals from 500 demos (50x). Leaks can sit in opt-in to schedule, schedule to show, or show to buy. Root cause: presenting to building owners who pay, while managers decide and gain nothing (principal-agent). Re-script to raise the manager's status. Retention was already 98%.
numbers: 2 of 500 demos; target 100 of 500; 98% revenue retention; 8 years stuck.

### YT-sGv2BTUCcCM#12 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: offers, paid-ads | fw: Price to Value Discrepancy | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:24:00
claim: A course creator at break-even should test a higher price, even when demand constrained, to afford more ads.
detail: Raise 1,000 to 3,000; sales may drop 15-20% yet revenue nearly triples. Add a 10,000 tier taken by about 10% for another 1,000 per sale, so average revenue per sale goes from 1,000 to about 4,000. Delivery is not the constraint.
numbers: 1,000 to 3,000 price, 15-20% unit drop; 10% buy 10,000 tier; 1,000 to 4,000 per sale.

### YT-sGv2BTUCcCM#15 | CASE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: paid-ads, lead-getters | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:28:43
claim: A well-built app with slow user growth needs awareness: store-listing conversion, paid acquisition, affiliate banners and referrals.
detail: Founders blame the product, but it is a demand constraint. Higher store conversion raises ranking, and the store itself favours converters. Options: optimise listing, buy installs, banner ads on apps serving the same market, a referral programme. Shifting effort could grow the user base 20x to 100x in a year.
numbers: Growth 20x to 100x possible in a year (as stated).

### YT-sGv2BTUCcCM#16 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: operator-mindset | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:30:17
claim: The constraint ping-pongs between demand and supply, and businesses stall when the founder keeps working the old one.
detail: Solve demand and supply bites; solve supply and demand returns. Solving a constraint so well that you create the next, without switching attention, is why founders get stuck for years.

### YT-sGv2BTUCcCM#17 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: scaling-constraints | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:31:50
claim: Four yes/no questions help show whether you likely have a supply problem: could you serve more customers at your standard, are you turning away or waitlisting people, are top slots or products always sold out, do you run out of inventory or face long shipping delays (physical products).
detail: A yes to any of them likely means a supply problem. If all are no, focus on demand.
### YT-sGv2BTUCcCM#18 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Document Demonstrate Duplicate | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:32:36
claim: Beyond price, there are three ways to lift service capacity: technology, training, or more people.
detail: Technology lets one person do about five times the work; training turns a B-level person into an A-level doing five times the work; hiring doubles B-level people. Also raise service ratio so one person delivers for three.
numbers: About 5x output per person from technology or training.
