### YT--is6Z2T2h8o#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: scaling-constraints | fw: A Players | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Every entrepreneur has one underlying problem, people: finding them, recruiting them, managing them and growing them.
detail: From roughly $10-30M ops take over, and by $30M it is all about talent. This was Hormozi's own bottleneck crossing $30M to $100M. Founders must accept the business should not need them, even if they like being needed, while keeping strategic vision.

### YT--is6Z2T2h8o#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:42
claim: Entrepreneurs are eternal optimists about their teams; if you cannot name someone on yours as weak, you have rose-coloured glasses on.
detail: The same owner calls a team great one year and horrible the next. It became real for him only when teammates grew divisions without him, in ways he had not thought of. An earlier step was crying when someone else made his first sale after he had done every sale for about a year and a half.
numbers: ~1.5 years doing every sale himself.

### YT--is6Z2T2h8o#03 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: product-delivery, verticals | fw: A Players | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:18
claim: A newly promoted brand manager asked to find new revenue emailed 20 apparel manufacturers, tried every sample, rejected them as not good enough for customers and returned with a vetted alternative and a logistics plan.
detail: She had spotted demand from social-media messages. She proposed using a manufacturer plus third-party fulfilment and asked only how many pieces to order as a baseline. The B-player version: three Google results, no samples, logo on a shirt. The difference is quality that people reorder, which raises customer LTV, plus continued care about customer experience.
numbers: 20 manufacturers contacted; $30 T-shirts in the B-player excuse.

### YT--is6Z2T2h8o#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:58
claim: At every level the leader should have the least tolerance for anything but excellence, and if a subordinate cares more than their supervisor, the roles should reverse.
detail: Standards dilute as they pass down, so the top must set them highest. Most people hire people worse than themselves, so a manager two levels removed has a large effect on front-line quality. His principle: if someone cares more about customers than he does, they should run the company.

### YT--is6Z2T2h8o#05 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: A Players | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:37
claim: It is worth five or even twenty extra interviews to find an A player, who will grow whatever they touch, since some people come "with batteries included" and development cannot replace selection.
detail: Culture follows the people on the team. He cites a Chick-fil-A people leader to the effect that competitors lose in the draft, not the championship.
numbers: 5 to 20 extra interviews.

### YT--is6Z2T2h8o#06 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:48
claim: To judge a team member ask what you would be doing in their position, what initiative you would take and what questions you would ask; an A player asks questions you had not thought of.
detail: Once you have seen an A player you can use them as a template. The skill develops with experience.
