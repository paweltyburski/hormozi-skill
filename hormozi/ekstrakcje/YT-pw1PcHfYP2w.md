### YT-pw1PcHfYP2w#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:06
claim: Buyers pay for how likely future sales are, not for a technology label.
detail: Value is a discounted view of future sales, driven by market size and the probability those sales happen. Insurance earns good multiples because customers stay; a service that never loses clients and sells more to each has a high multiple too. Software is just one vehicle to that retention.

### YT-pw1PcHfYP2w#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:06
claim: Software is judged on annual churn; around or above 10% a year makes it just a service business.
detail: He measures churn yearly for software, not monthly. The exact threshold is garbled in the audio ("less than or above 10%"; unclear in audio), so treat 10% as approximate.
numbers: ~10% annual churn threshold (unclear in audio).

### YT-pw1PcHfYP2w#03 | DEFINITION | topic: strategy-positioning | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:16
claim: Product-market fit means customers buy, use and tell others.
detail: If people reach the software only because you sold the service and they cancel when the service ends, it is a service. Customers must pay for it themselves, use it on their own and refer others.

### YT-pw1PcHfYP2w#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:16
claim: Software sold at 10x, 20x or 50x revenue is rare; most software fails or sells for parts.
detail: The big multiples go to products with strong growth, word-of-mouth and no user losses. He says software bolted onto a service, where people cancel when they cancel the service, is just a service.
numbers: Cited multiples 10x, 20x, 50x top line.
### YT-pw1PcHfYP2w#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Buy vs Build | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:16
claim: Acquirers check real usage, so software tacked onto a service will not fool them.
detail: They look at time on screen and daily users. A mastermind relabelled as a software tier does not pass. If a real competitor exists, it can copy a weak product quickly and offer a better experience for less.

### YT-pw1PcHfYP2w#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:54
claim: Founders who split attention between an info business and software damage both through sunk cost and ego.
detail: Venture investors expect two of 100 bets to carry the portfolio; as a single bet the odds are low. Competitors are pure software people, and one pays development costs while neglecting the main business, then keeps going because of public commitment.

### YT-pw1PcHfYP2w#07 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:06
claim: Partner with an existing software company as an affiliate instead of building your own.
detail: With serious traffic you can negotiate large shares and avoid the running costs.
numbers: Affiliate share about 20%, 30%, sometimes 40-50%.

### YT-pw1PcHfYP2w#08 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: retention-ltv | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:13
claim: Instead of building software, make the service stickier and grow customers through proven channels.
detail: Options listed: stickier service, acquiring similar companies, affiliates, referrals, paid ads, outbound, serving more customers and expanding the addressable market. He describes people with a $2M profit business hoping to sell a bolted-on software for a 20x top-line multiple; he says customers complain when the software is not the best one on the market.
numbers: Example: $2M profit business, 20x top-line multiple hoped for.
applies_to: Info and service businesses
