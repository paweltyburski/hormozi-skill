### YT-WsYgWC7NmO8#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:48
claim: A private equity partner focused only on two levers in a quarterly review: building technology into the business and accretive acquisitions.
detail: He saw those as the route to selling two portfolio companies for $250 million or more, and treated everything else as routine blocking and tackling. Lesson: most things do not matter, a few matter greatly, so cut the rest and you can afford mistakes elsewhere.
numbers: Exit goal $250 million or more.

### YT-WsYgWC7NmO8#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:35
claim: A smaller slice of a bigger pie beats all of a small pie, so share ownership or profit with leaders.
detail: Profit-sharing plans and bonus pools tying leaders to the vision returned far more than they gave up. The richest people usually own a minority of their companies.
numbers: Examples cited: Bezos about 10% of Amazon; Musk about 20% and 17% of SpaceX and Tesla (assignment unclear in audio).

### YT-WsYgWC7NmO8#03 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:32
claim: The Panda Express founder owns about 2,600 locations 100% with no franchisees; it took 45 years and much of the growth came in the last five.
detail: He named two keys: customers must love the product and big things take time. Hormozi concluded he should stop chasing fast wins and ask what will matter in 10 years.
numbers: About 2,600 locations, 45 years, about 600 opened in the latest year.

### YT-WsYgWC7NmO8#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:04
claim: Under-delivering creates negative word of mouth, which quietly raises acquisition cost faster than platform costs rise.
detail: Brand is built by making promises and exceeding them. People blame pixels and platforms when negative word of mouth is the hidden drag, and it spreads faster than the positive kind.

### YT-WsYgWC7NmO8#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:01
claim: Brand gives premium pricing power and a moat against cost shocks that kill commodity sellers.
detail: Illustrations: plain coffee versus a branded cafe, a plain white t-shirt versus ones with a sports or luxury logo. Extra margin lets a branded firm survive supply-chain or cost increases.
numbers: Roughly 4-5x for coffee, 10x with a sports logo, 100x with a luxury logo (his illustration).

### YT-WsYgWC7NmO8#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:56
claim: The largest portfolio companies have the simplest models; keep the unit, offer suite, customer journey and customer type as simple as possible because scale adds complexity on its own.
detail: Simplicity lets sales, service and success teams all understand the offer. If you cannot keep it simple you may not understand the business. Avoid risking the core for a side bet.

### YT-WsYgWC7NmO8#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:06
claim: Projections of new initiatives and threats are usually too extreme, so estimate conservatively in both directions to stay unemotional.
detail: Surprises, not plans, produce outsized results. He thought he would lose companies many times and lost none in 10 years.
numbers: Companies lost over about 10 years: 0.

### YT-WsYgWC7NmO8#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:33
claim: Top talent is attracted only to opportunities big enough to hold their ambitions, so define a vision large enough, and make room for future hires.
detail: Quoted from a major investment firm founder. A local dry cleaner is fine but will not attract the best, unless you truly aim to change how clothes are cleaned and can stay on it for years.

### YT-WsYgWC7NmO8#09 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:36
claim: Growing 10% a quarter compounds to about 40 times in a decade, so set goals you can surely hit rather than doubling in 90 days.
detail: His portfolio goals became more conservative over time. Ask what 10% a quarter for two years would give. Most founders set unrealistic goals, not trivial ones.
numbers: 10% per quarter, about 40x over 10 years, e.g. $1M a year to about $40M.

### YT-WsYgWC7NmO8#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:28
claim: Take a company-sized risk only when staying put threatens the company more than acting.
detail: Some founders at $3-5 million want a big risky move, and he says it is usually emotions distorting reality. Far more businesses are destroyed by a founder's excitement over a non-core opportunity than by disruption. He says about 80% of the portfolio is not tech.
numbers: About 80% of the portfolio is not tech; he gives $3M-$5M companies as examples of founders who call wanting a big move.
### YT-WsYgWC7NmO8#11 | EXAMPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:14:55
claim: A dictation software maker released a version 2.0 with no new features, only better accuracy, and was praised for it.
detail: Growth often comes from boring work: shorter time to result or to value, better customer health score, matching segments to the product suite.

### YT-WsYgWC7NmO8#12 | CASE | topic: offers | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:15:56
claim: A portfolio company wanted a new high-ticket upsell line; a survey found 85% of customers wanted more of what they had, 15% the new product.
detail: Hormozi pushed to survey rather than trust the team's enthusiasm. Lead with data, not ego, and build what the customer wants even if boring.
numbers: 85% wanted more of the existing offer; 15% the new product.

### YT-WsYgWC7NmO8#14 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:17:29
claim: His recipe chains: make promises, keep them, build brand, use brand to price above market, spend the extra profit on better people and channels, share the pie, and give it time.
detail: Each step funds the next; profit supported by brand pays for hiring and adding acquisition channels.
