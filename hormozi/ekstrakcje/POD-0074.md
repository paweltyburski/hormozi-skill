### POD-0074#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:35
claim: If your tasks pay less per hour than you want to earn, you lack a higher-value skill, not a bigger budget.
detail: A gym owner coaching all sessions for three years who moves into selling meets two people an hour and closes one or two at $500 to $600, so about $500 to $1,000 an hour versus $15. One sale covers a week of sessions.
numbers: $15/hour coaching vs $500-$1,000/hour selling.

### POD-0074#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: hiring-talent | fw: Document Demonstrate Duplicate | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:25
claim: Climb the skill ladder: coach, then sales and marketing together, then document the sale, teach it, then lead and own marketing.
detail: When you are bored selling you have unconscious competence, so document it and transfer it, since teaching selling scales more than selling. Then own marketing, vision and running the team.

### POD-0074#03 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:17
claim: Hand off sales gradually: document first, have a hire shadow, take a third then half then three quarters of the sales, and build cash before moving fully.
detail: Stay longer than you want in the "boring zone". Stack cash (he gives $15,000 net for three months as a trigger) so a dip does not force you back.
numbers: share handed off 1/3, 1/2, 3/4; example: $15,000 net/month for 3 months.

### POD-0074#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:52
claim: Leadership is selling the team on the business daily and weekly, because their attention gets pulled away.
detail: Employees must be sold repeatedly; that is why compensation and communication cycles exist. Practise it like selling.

### POD-0074#05 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:38

detail: If everything drops when you step away from training, front desk or sales, leadership is the missing skill.
