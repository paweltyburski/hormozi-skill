### YT-52tcB5FopAg#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:48
claim: A business sale rarely delivers the enterprise value in cash; the richest owners tend to buy and build rather than sell.
detail: After six exits he lists the leakage: transition obligations, earnouts, seller financing, rolled equity, bonuses and fees, transaction costs, then about 20% tax. He notes the urge to sell comes in the most painful periods, and recommends first trying to remove the pain, for instance by hiring the one or two people needed, instead of selling the whole company.
numbers: about 20% tax on proceeds; six businesses exited.

### YT-52tcB5FopAg#02 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:18
claim: Borrow against business equity (recapitalisation, minority or majority debt) to take cash out and de-risk without selling.
detail: Like refinancing a rental property: loan proceeds are not income, so no income tax, and the owner keeps the upside. His $1M-business example: take a $700,000 loan (leaving 30%), compared with a sale netting about $560,000 after tax, then both grow to $2M value; he quotes net worth of $3.3M versus $2M, though the arithmetic is unclear in audio. Public-company CEOs often live on asset-backed loans, and a private-equity partner told him his debt-free companies should borrow and redeploy. Refinance periodically as value grows.
numbers: $1M business; $700,000 loan; $2M later value; $3.3M vs $2M net worth (as stated, unclear).
