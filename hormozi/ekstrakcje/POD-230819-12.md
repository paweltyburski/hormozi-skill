### POD-230819-12#01 | CASE | topic: paid-ads | also: promotions-launches, verticals | fw: Track Lose Print | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:55
claim: The first Facebook ad for a gym, a plain all-caps text ad, sold 19 people a $299 challenge and launched a promotion that spread across the industry.
detail: In 2013 a gym owner who doubted ads gave Hormozi $1,000 to test, with profit split if he made more. The ad sought five local residents for a free six-week challenge in exchange for before-and-after photos. Leads came within hours; he booked them, sent a reminder text an hour before and sold on conviction rather than skill. The owner paid him more than his share, and the challenge became the dominant gym promotion for seven years.
numbers: $1,000 spend; 19 sales at $299 (~$5,700); owner's check $2,500; promotion taught to over 4,500 gyms, used in variants by 10,000+; at least $1.5B revenue.

### POD-230819-12#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: Puddles-Ponds-Lakes-Oceans | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:35
claim: As ad audiences widen, the spend-to-return ratio worsens but total profit grows, so widen until it stops being profitable.
detail: Example: spending $1,000 to make $10,000 leaves $9,000 profit; spending $100,000 to make $300,000 leaves $200,000. Start profitable in a small puddle, then pond, lake, ocean. Risk rises with spend, as does reward.
numbers: $1,000 to $10,000 (10:1, $9,000 profit) vs $100,000 to $300,000 (3:1, $200,000 profit).

### POD-230819-12#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Four Problems of Paid Ads | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:45
claim: A paid ad is a funnel from the whole world down to the needle: pick the platform, target, repel everyone else in the creative, then give the remainder one next step.
detail: Unprofitable ads are usually unprofitable because the right people never saw them. The ad itself should push away the wrong audience, not just attract the right one.

### POD-230819-12#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: paid-ads | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Four Problems of Paid Ads | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:21
claim: Choose a platform you have used as a consumer, can target on, know the ad formats of, and can afford the minimum spend on.
detail: Four tests: you have gotten value from it as a user; you can target people interested in you; you know how to format ads for it; you can pay the minimum ad spend. Then start by studying ads there before making one.

### POD-230819-12#05 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: - | fw: Lookalike Audience | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:39
claim: Fill a lookalike seed list to the platform minimum in order of quality: customers, then warm outreach, then cold outreach.

### POD-230819-12#07 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: - | fw: Ad Assembly Process | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:46
claim: Record about 10 main ads and 30 or more new openings weekly, then mix them into 300 variants.

### POD-230819-12#15 | DECISION_RULE | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: Track Lose Print | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:36:26
claim: When testing a new ad, allow spend up to twice the profit collected from a new customer in 30 days, and shut it off at 1x if it produces no leads.
detail: Example: $100 of profit in the first 30 days means up to $200 of spend, provided leads arrive; none by $100 and he kills it. He has lost on more ads than he won, but kills fast and scales winners hard.
numbers: 2x first-30-day cash per customer; kill at 1x if zero leads.

### POD-230819-12#16 | EXAMPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: Track Lose Print | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:36:26
claim: Ten $100 test ads can lose nine times and still win: scaling the one 5:1 winner 100x turns $10,000 into $50,000.
detail: Nine ads lose their $100 each; one returns $500 on $100, leaving a $500 net loss on the batch. Then spend $10,000 on the winner for $50,000 back. You may lose nine or 99 times in a row before the big win.
numbers: 10 ads x $100; 9 lose; winner 5:1; scaled to $10,000 spend and $50,000 return.

### POD-230819-12#18 | DECISION_RULE | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:39:23
claim: Reverse the budget from the customer goal, and Hormozi usually pads it by about 20% because ads get less efficient as they scale.
detail: If capacity is 100 customers a month at a $100 CAC, the base is $10,000; padded 20% gives $12,000 over 30 days, or $400 a day. Commit to it even if the number scares you.
numbers: 100 customers x $100 = $10,000; +20% = $12,000; $400 per day.
### POD-230819-12#19 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:43:05
claim: Worked CFA example: a $30 CAC on a 3.3:1 membership breaks even inside 30 days once a one-in-five upsell is added.
detail: Membership $15 a month, $5 delivery, $10 gross profit, 10-month life gives $100 LTGP, so 3.3:1 on $30 CAC. Alone it breaks even after about three months. A $100 upsell at 100% margin taken by one in five adds $20 average, giving $30 in the first 30 days, i.e. free customers. He credits this with passing $1M per month within 12 months without outside funding, in every company he started over seven years.
numbers: $15 price; $5 cost; $10 monthly gross profit; 10 months; $100 LTGP; $30 CAC; 3.3:1; $100 upsell, 1 in 5 take it; +$20 average.
