### POD-0242#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:46
claim: A chiropractor spent $1,200 on ads and collected about $9,000 from first visits before selling any $2,000 back-end package.
detail: The funnel produced 196 leads, 126 scheduled, 100 showed (about 51% of leads), and 64 of the 100 who showed bought the $130 first visit. At a 30% average take-up of the $2,000 package, about 20 sales would bring roughly $40,000 more; he quotes about $49,000-$50,000 all in on $1,200 of spend. Figures as stated: 84% show rate (unclear in audio) and $14 cost per lead (unclear in audio, since $1,200 over 196 leads is about $6).
numbers: $1,200 ad spend; 196 leads; 126 scheduled; 100 showed; 64 paid; ~$9,000 collected; about 9:1 before back end.
applies_to: Chiropractors and other appointment-based professional services.

### POD-0242#02 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Penny Gap | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:33
claim: Give the technology step away free but charge for the professional's assessment: a scan normally $260 is free, while the first visit is $130.
detail: Free beats discount, but separating the free item from the paid service keeps the value of the scan visible and keeps the visit a real sale. A scan alone means nothing without professional interpretation. Every show then becomes at least a $130 customer, versus $19 or $29 first consults.
numbers: Scan list price $260 (free); first visit $130.

### POD-0242#03 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:07
claim: Close the $130 first visit on the phone by taking a card on file with a $50 no-show fee, and charge only when the patient arrives.
detail: This makes the visit feel low-risk to buy yet secures commitment, and 64 of the 100 who showed on the call bought.
numbers: $50 no-show fee; $130 charged at the visit; 64 of 100.

### POD-0242#04 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:59
claim: Send leads to a funnel and a phone consultation with the front desk's full availability, not lead ads and not straight to an in-person booking.
detail: Funnels got about 2.5x more people to show than lead ads. Direct in-person offers in these niches get only 10-15% to show, and prospects cancel on the call. Phone consults let the front desk offer maximum availability.
numbers: Show rate 51% of leads (funnel and phone) versus 10-15% direct in-person; 2.5x vs lead ads.

### POD-0242#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:21
claim: Someone unwilling to pay $130 will not buy a $2,000 package, so let the front desk screen them out on the phone.
detail: This weeds out poor customers early and spares the practitioner's time. The opt-in sat at the bottom of the page, after the benefits, so those who opted in were somewhat more qualified.

### POD-0242#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-process | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:28
claim: For professional practices, pick in-person or phone scheduling by availability, and if the client will not follow the playbook, do not take them as a customer.
detail: Walk-in availability allows in-person; if not, use the front desk by phone or the funnel fails. Because you act as a performance partner, not asking for a retainer, the client must make these changes. The line "funnels don't work" is treated as a skill problem to fix through offer, copy and benefits.
