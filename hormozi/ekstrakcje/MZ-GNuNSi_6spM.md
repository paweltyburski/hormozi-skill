### MZ-GNuNSi_6spM#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:09
claim: Skills compound when combined outputs exceed what each gives alone, but people underrate it because the upside of an unlearned skill is an unknown unknown.
detail: Example: a rapper who adds beats, promotion, recruiting and deal-making ends up running a label. Hyper-successful people he knows are intensely curious and extract information from anyone, regardless of their income.

### MZ-GNuNSi_6spM#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:07
claim: Marketing results are not random: about 15 rules explain most of the outcome and the rest is unknown variance.
detail: He follows roughly 15 rules and has been wrong so often that he has no ego about it; a last-second email can beat a planned one. Large audiences give measurable answers (5.2% versus 7.2% click-through), so a loss is a wrong answer, not luck. The known rules control perhaps 60-70% of probability.
numbers: ~15 rules; 5.2% vs 7.2% click-through example; ~60-70% controllable (rough).

### MZ-GNuNSi_6spM#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: Many-Sided Die | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:45
claim: Staying in the game raises luck surface area; given a choice between perfect aim and unlimited shots he picks unlimited shots.
detail: Persistence means being present when lucky breaks come, which define careers; trying to time them rarely works. Most efforts do not matter and a few matter hugely, and you cannot tell which in advance (Ogilvy: wasted 90% of ad spend, unknown which).
numbers: 90% of advertising spend wasted (Ogilvy quote).

### MZ-GNuNSi_6spM#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:34
claim: Each learned skill raises the floor of your worst-case outcome.
detail: In his early twenties plan B was driving a car service or stripping. After learning to sell, the floor became car sales at $400-500K a year; after learning ads and lead generation, multi-million a year. After losing everything twice he restarted by offering a business owner customers for free in exchange for keeping a cut of what he closed.
numbers: Car sales floor ~$400,000-$500,000/year; marketing floor "multiple millions" a year.

### MZ-GNuNSi_6spM#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:37
claim: People fail to compound because they restart a "new character" when stuck on level four, chasing many rabbits.
detail: Losing feels bad, so after a long stall at one level they start something new, breeze through levels one to three again for fast feedback, and then stall again. Fix: keep fighting the same level.
