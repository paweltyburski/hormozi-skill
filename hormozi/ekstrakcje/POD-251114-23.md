### POD-251114-23#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: Continuity Discount Offer | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:38
claim: Separate recurring and upfront charges so advertising can show a huge discount on the first period.
detail: Because buyers expect to pay something, fewer are surprised than with free. Follow local advertising rules. Fees can be waived or charged at the salesperson's discretion.
numbers: Service: first month $100 plus $1,900 setup, $2,000 in all. Programme: $3,000 in three $1,000 months; first month at 88% off ($120) plus $1,000 setup, $1,120.

### POD-251114-23#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: pricing | fw: Big Head Long Tail | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:54
claim: According to John, his tanning salon's $100 sign-up on a $10 plan had next-to-no churn, unlike $19-down plans.
detail: Hormozi says the higher the barrier to entry, the higher the barrier to exit. If they leave and want to come back, they have to pay it again.
numbers: $100 sign-up / $10 monthly versus $19 down / $19 monthly (per John).
### POD-251114-23#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: verticals | fw: Waived Fee Offer | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:41
claim: An online weight-loss coaching business charged a large start fee and a small monthly fee, and clients stayed over two years.
detail: Typical fitness clients stay about four months.
numbers: $5,000 to start, then $267 per month; average tenure over 2 years versus 4 months.

### POD-251114-23#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: Waived Fee Offer | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:41
claim: When customers must do work (forms, attendance, behaviour change) to get results, it more often than not makes sense to charge a one-time setup fee.
detail: Create the fee by choosing a name, a price and a reason, then charge, discount or waive it; explain the reason to every customer. In general, the larger the upfront fee, the higher the stick rate.
