### POD-0627#01 | CASE | topic: sales-process | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:52
claim: Five years of 20+ daily one-on-one gym consults trained his ability to speak and pitch clearly.

### POD-0627#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:41
claim: A prospect who said he never wanted to look like Hormozi was first answered with ego, then reframed as a long journey he had not yet shown the determination for.
detail: Hormozi admits the ego reply ("don't worry, you never will") came first and the sale-minded reframe second: transformation takes a very long time, and measuring the person he could not yet see the commitment. Training was designed around what clients wanted to look like, not around how he trained himself.

### POD-0627#03 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:25
claim: Arriving earlier makes an event snowball, because a visible line is the signal that something is happening.
detail: In his fraternity days, passers-by judged parties by where the line was, so being early started the line, which attracted more people. He argued to his house that earlier arrival would make parties about five times bigger, not twice. A $1,000 half-scale ice-sculpture helicopter worked the same way as visible spectacle.
numbers: Predicted 5x party size from arriving earlier, versus 2x assumed.

### POD-0627#04 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:11:07
claim: To make a deal, start from the one outcome you cannot lose and work backwards to the partner most likely to accept and where you hold the most leverage.
detail: His house had been thrown off campus, and the one non-negotiable was keeping a house there. He listed every place a deal could be done, picked the likeliest acceptor, then worked step by step. More than one goal (also a great house, also good terms) makes decisions harder because you serve several masters.

### POD-0627#05 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:26
claim: His fraternity merged with a dying one that had a house but no members, trading headcount for a building.
detail: The other side's worry was culture clash (music styles) and voting power. The pitch to them was that the two groups drew from different pools of attendees, so combined events would be bigger and more diverse. Votes were weighted so each of his members counted for a quarter of theirs, but peer pressure meant the larger group still got most of its agenda.
numbers: About 100-120 of his members versus about 30 of theirs; each of his votes worth one quarter.

### POD-0627#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:24
claim: Build programmes around what the customer can sustain for life: if they will stop, six months of results accomplishes nothing.
detail: He told trainers that a powerlifter, bodybuilder or kettlebell style imposed on everyone is pointless if clients return to old habits. Getting people to walk ten minutes a day for the rest of their lives is a win. Test: how little is needed that they will actually persist with?
numbers: 10 minutes of walking daily (his illustration of a minimum sustainable habit).

### POD-0627#07 | TACTIC | topic: other | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:26:26
claim: To change a behaviour pattern, change the small set of repeated items that make up most of it, not everything.
detail: Most people eat about ten meals on rotation, so swapping two or three breakfasts, lunches and dinners changes the whole pattern. He eats the same breakfast and lunch to hit protein, leaving dinner flexible for business and social meals while tracking approximate calories.
numbers: About 10 meals make up about 90% of what most people eat (his estimate).

### POD-0627#08 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:29:14
claim: When entering a new category such as deal-making, read many books on one narrow topic, then interview practitioners with every question and merge notes into a working thesis.
detail: Before deal-making with Equizzer.com (spelling unclear in audio) he read eight books in a week, then phoned people who do deals for a living. He says books do work, but only the ones that serve the thing you must do; otherwise he learns through doing and through his partner relaying what she reads.
numbers: 8 books in one week on deal-making.

### POD-0627#09 | DEFINITION | topic: content-brand | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:34:20
claim: Branding is a teaching process that associates something unknown with things people already know, so that it changes behaviour at scale.
detail: A brand started as a mark burned onto cattle to change how others behave towards the animal. The same shirt with no logo versus with a recognised logo sells at very different rates and prices, the logo carrying the learned meaning.
numbers: Same T-shirt moves from about $5 to about $500 with a luxury logo (his illustration).

### POD-0627#10 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:32:36
claim: Build a brand around an ideal by repeatedly pairing it with things you agree embody it, and by being selective about what is not it.
detail: Show and discuss the ideal next to co-signed examples (hot-or-not for the brand) until the audience shares the meaning, then put the mark on a product. Do not define it too tightly; point to examples by yes and no rather than a dictionary definition. Many more things are off-brand than on-brand.

### POD-0627#11 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:36:37
claim: A brand is like a bouquet: a single rotten element or off-brand piece changes how the whole thing is perceived.
detail: The meaning comes from the combination, so one bad association outweighs many good ones, and audiences notice off-brand items. You must know both what the brand is and what it is not.

### POD-0627#12 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:38:54
claim: Acquisition.com first asks whether it can add substantial value, and favours brick-and-mortar service chains with simple operating units.
detail: Examples named: photography studio, teeth-whitening, nail salon and gym chains. He likes services for the jobs they create and because training and culture, meaning rules of behaviour that can be explained to many people, let a service business scale profitably. Retail is not a focus.

### POD-0627#13 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:40:28
claim: A target should have at least three, ideally five or more, locations with good unit economics, so you can tell a system from a charismatic founder.
detail: With one location you cannot tell a good model from a good operator. Each location should profit by itself and the way it makes money should make sense, and the overall market must be large enough.
numbers: Minimum 3 locations, ideally 5+.
