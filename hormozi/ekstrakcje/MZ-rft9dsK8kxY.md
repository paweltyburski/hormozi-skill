### MZ-rft9dsK8kxY#01 | CASE | topic: management-ops | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A warehouse property developer in Brisbane (profit on cost about 20%, investor returns 40-50%) is slowed by doing everything himself.
detail: About $200M of projects sit in the pipeline over four or five years, but timelines slip and he takes no new projects. Staff stay two to three years and his time goes to hiring and onboarding.
numbers: Profit on cost about 20%; investor return 40-50%; pipeline about $200M.

### MZ-rft9dsK8kxY#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:20
claim: For one-off roles he buys more than he builds: he pays a recruiter's premium for proven talent; for repeated roles he wants an in-house training "university".
detail: In a services firm the arbitrage is buying talent cheaper, adding your method and charging more. Cost to train one is nearly the same as to train five, so raise training effectiveness and lower its cost.
### MZ-rft9dsK8kxY#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: Document Demonstrate Duplicate | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:14
claim: Train with seven steps per skill: observable behaviors, modelling, elicit trials, observe, immediate correction, praise, repeat until it sticks.
detail: A skill is a chain of actions with a repeatable outcome. Explain less and make them try more; observe real output, not stated intentions. He says few training systems he audits have this.
