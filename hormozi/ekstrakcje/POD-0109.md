### POD-0109#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:58
claim: The cash cushion that feels safe scales with the business, so "enough" keeps moving.
detail: When he owned gyms, comfort meant cash equal to three times the yearly revenue. He hit that number, but the business needed to get there was 10 times bigger, and its comfort number was larger again.
numbers: Comfort target 3x annual revenue of the facilities; business 10x larger by the time he reached it.

### POD-0109#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:33
claim: At a 20% margin a $1M business needs about four years of saved profit to cover one year of costs; at 50% it needs one.
detail: $1M revenue, 20% margin: $200,000 profit against $800,000 costs, so four years (he first said five). At 50%: $500,000 profit covers next year's $500,000 costs. If half of profit is spent personally, the time doubles to about eight years.
numbers: $200,000 profit vs $800,000 costs = 4 years; 50% margin = 1 year; spending half doubles it.

### POD-0109#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:19
claim: A $10M business at a 20% margin makes about $2M but carries about $666,000 a month in costs, so $2M profit is only about three months of overhead.
detail: Explains why larger owners still feel financially stressed.
numbers: $8M costs / 12 = about $666,000/month; $2M profit = about 3 months.

### POD-0109#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:23
claim: Low-margin businesses never feel secure; aim for the highest margin possible to reduce operational drag.
detail: Selling at $1,000 with $900 of cost leaves $900 of costs weighing on you if anything goes wrong. He feels about 12 weeks from bankruptcy in his own head.
numbers: Example: $1,000 sale with $900 cost = 10% margin; his felt cushion about 12 weeks.

### POD-0109#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:44
claim: Bill Gates described a goal of holding one year of company costs in savings, and said Microsoft had only just reached it.
detail: Reported as about $17B (his recollection of the interview).
numbers: About $17B reserve = one year of costs.

### POD-0109#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:04
claim: A full year of cash reserve is rarely used in practice, because under cash losses you would cut staff and costs quickly.
detail: He still computes the number in his head but calls it irrational. He also notes that selling the business removes overhead but owners tend to start again to find the pressure back.
