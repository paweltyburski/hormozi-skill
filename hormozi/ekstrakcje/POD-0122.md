### POD-0122#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:35
claim: Forecast by working backward from the goal through each funnel rate to daily activity, using current numbers.
detail: Order used: target members; net monthly gain needed; add attrition to get gross continuity sign-ups; divide by front-end-to-continuity conversion; add the drop between sign-up and start; convert to weekly (divide by 4, not 4.3, for slack); divide by close rate for sit-downs; by show rate for appointments; by schedule rate for leads; then multiply by cost per lead for budget. He says his predictions are usually very accurate within a small margin of error.
applies_to: Any funnel with measured stage rates; he uses it for staffing and applications too.
### POD-0122#02 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:35
claim: A gym with 100 recurring members wanting 300 needed 36 gross new members a month and about 60 leads a week.
detail: She had just added 50 members in a month. Plan: net +20 per month for 10 months. Attrition 6% a month (about 16 members at 270). Needed sign-ups per week: 15 after 66% convert from front end and 10% drop before starting. Close rate two thirds gives 22 sit-downs, 50% show rate gives 44 booked, 75% booking rate gives 60 leads, at about $12 per lead.
numbers: 6% monthly attrition; 66% front-end to continuity; 10% no-start; 2/3 close; 50% show; 75% scheduled; $12 per lead; 60 leads per week; 36 gross members per month.

### POD-0122#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: paid-ads | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:18
claim: For that gym, one third of ad sets were winners, so 12 winning ad sets at $10 a day meant making 36.
detail: Ad budget came to roughly $3,600 a month and about four hours of ad creation a week (he says 'probably maybe more'). Daily workload: about five sit-downs per day. Timing of ad creation per week versus total set count is slightly unclear in audio.
numbers: 1 in 3 ad sets wins; $10/day per winner; 12 winners out of 36; ~$3,600/month.
### POD-0122#04 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:02
claim: Check capacity before setting a member goal: sessions per day times class size times days.
detail: Her limit worry was 200 members, but 22 per session, eight 45-minute sessions a day, gives 176 per day on Monday, Wednesday, Friday and again on Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday, about 350 at perfect attendance. A 300 goal was therefore easy. Set goals long and hit them early.
numbers: 22 per session; 8 sessions/day; ~176 per day; ~350 total.

### POD-0122#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:24
claim: Forecast on the current case, not the best case, because small rate changes swing lead needs dramatically.
detail: Example: scheduling falls from 75% to 50% and show rate from 50% to 25%, and weekly leads needed rise from 60 to about 190. Improvements will come, but growth breaks systems, so fixes only offset the new breakage.
numbers: 60 to 190 leads per week after two rate changes.

### POD-0122#06 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:24
claim: Do percentages in your head with tens, fives and ones.
detail: Move the decimal for 10%, halve it for 5%, then add 1% steps; for example 17% of 240 is 24 plus 12 plus 4.8, giving 40.8.
