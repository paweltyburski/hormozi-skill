### POD-0078#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: paid-ads | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:27
claim: Keep raising marketing spend until something else breaks, in order: leads exceed what you can nurture, then the salesperson's calendar is full, then the gym runs out of capacity.
detail: Spend as long as acquisition cost is positive or negative (you earn on acquiring). When leads overflow, first widen the pipe with automated lead nurture, then raise spend again until the sales calendar is booked, then check whether fulfilment can keep up. If you wish for more people in the door, you most likely just need to spend more.
applies_to: Owners whose ads are profitable.

### POD-0078#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:33
claim: A business owner already owns the investment: put surplus cash back into the proven ad engine before chasing a 10% passive return.
detail: His example: of $200,000 earned, 10% invested yields $20,000 a year, but spending that $20,000 on ads returning five, ten or twenty to one beats it and he controls it. His own investing is index funds he does not think about, with spare cash spent on marketing. Real estate is a separate game that would take about three years to learn and is often zero-sum. He quotes the view that diversification hedges ignorance, and says spending on a channel you know is not risky.
numbers: $200,000 profit; 10% = $20,000; ad returns of 5:1, 10:1 or 20:1 versus ~7-12% passive return.

### POD-0078#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:48
claim: Owners spend too much effort future-proofing and chasing new platforms; instead of finding the next thing, double what works now.
detail: He set a challenge to double ad spend for a month, noting that across his gyms and over a thousand client gyms monthly revenue tracks marketing spend in proportion when the money goes into a working channel. Gary Vaynerchuk's regret about not spending enough on Google ads when it was hot is the cautionary tale; he reported four to six more golden years for Facebook (said in 2018).
numbers: Facebook "four to six more golden years" (2018 claim); doubling ad spend for one month.

### POD-0078#04 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: - | fw: Track Lose Print | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:46
claim: One way to scale ad spend is duplication: copy the winning ads rather than raising the budget on an ad.
detail: Example: five ads at $20 a day; duplicate the three winners to reach about $160 a day, then duplicate one again for about $180. This is how he scaled spend for his own gyms. He mentions a rival school of raising budgets 10-20% every 24-48 hours, which he does not use.
numbers: 5 ads x $20/day; ~$160/day after duplicating winners; ~$180/day after again.

### POD-0078#05 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: management-ops | fw: Track Lose Print | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:36
claim: The method he uses for Gym Launch is to start at about three times the intended daily budget and prune losing ads every 48 hours.
detail: To reach $1,000 a day he would launch 30 ads at $100 a day each (about $3,000), cut about 10 within 48 hours (about $2,000), cut about five more after another 48 hours (about $1,500). He sometimes ends above target while still profitable, which tricks him into spending more. He splits spend across several ad accounts because he believes high spend on one account brings junk traffic and lower page conversion. He says no one outside the platform knows how it works, so trust data.
numbers: 30 ads x $100/day = ~$3,000, then ~$2,000, then ~$1,500 (target $1,000).
