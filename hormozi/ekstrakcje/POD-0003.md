### POD-0003#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:14
claim: He says he spends probably a quarter of a million dollars a year or more on courses and events to know what works, including a $100,000 mastermind last week limited to eight-figure-plus businesses.
detail: Rivals copied his programs, meal plans, contracts and ad copy, sometimes with their own logo on them. He used to be angry, then decided the defence was to keep learning faster than the copiers.
numbers: Probably $250,000+/year on courses and events; $100,000 for one mastermind last week.
### POD-0003#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: promotions-launches | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:09
claim: Layla Hormozi says that in a change, first adopters usually get about two thirds of the gains, normal adopters roughly the last third, and late adopters basically nothing.
detail: Layla says copies of their systems appeared seven months to a year later. In their market the window is maybe a year at most. Late copiers spend money chasing a tiny sliver of the market.
numbers: Layla: first adopters usually about two thirds of gains; normal adopters about one third; window in their market maybe one year.
### POD-0003#03 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:14
claim: Source new tactics from other industries (for example real estate marketing), then retest them in your own market several times before judging.
detail: He watches what works elsewhere, asks whether it can be modelled for gyms, and runs it once, twice and a third time; he says a typical winner appeared on the third attempt. Applying ideas to an industry he knows well, with a $15-an-hour employee executing, was the point.
numbers: Winning attempt often on the third try.

### POD-0003#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:13
claim: A failed channel test usually means the offer or format needs small tweaks, because every platform ends with a person giving brief attention.
detail: He quotes a mentor's line that marketing always works, like airplanes always fly once the equation is right. His examples of premature verdicts: TV, radio, direct mail, Facebook or Instagram "do not work for our industry." He also noted new placements such as Instagram Stories were priced at cents on the dollar early because few used them.
