### MZ-X3N-pEpgsAk#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:01
claim: Pick a few "big rocks" that will not change over decades (behaviour under pressure) and ignore the long list of pebbles.
detail: He says people should hold about three musts. The same idea is applied to partners: surface preferences fade, so judge by foundations.
numbers: About three must-haves.

### MZ-X3N-pEpgsAk#02 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:31
claim: State your non-negotiables (long work hours, daily training) on the first dates so a mismatch surfaces early.
detail: The smaller the gap between how you present and your baseline, the better long term. After meeting, triage quickly.

### MZ-X3N-pEpgsAk#03 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:18
claim: Use inversion: list the reasons people fail (for marriage: money, children, and many small preference clashes) and handle them before they occur.
detail: He and his wife took a divorce course first. Default reading rule he calls rounding up: when a remark can be taken two ways, assume the good one.

### MZ-X3N-pEpgsAk#04 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:28
claim: Ask for the specific behaviour you want ("can you do this instead of that"), and thank people in terms of the effort, which reinforces more than general praise.
detail: A change of wording from "I am proud of you" to "thank you for working so hard" landed far better. Feedback that has to be repeated zero times is, in his words, his wife's superpower. When a change has a cost, have a trade-off conversation.

### MZ-X3N-pEpgsAk#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:12:20
claim: When his wife had to choose between running the company and cooking his meals, he chose that she run the company and hired someone to cook.
detail: The principle: a person cannot keep adding duties, so ask what each person will trade, as with an employee. The rarer skill goes to the higher-value role.
