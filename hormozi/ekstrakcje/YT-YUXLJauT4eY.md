### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: promotions-launches | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:21
claim: Gymshark ran a 12-week holiday pop-up rather than a four-day one, starting before and ending after competitors.
detail: Hypothesis from the Shopify president: the early start let them learn which products would sell in late November (testing from October 1), so the sales period used proven winners. Gymshark also hid "Easter eggs" in its ads under a "There Will Be Signs" campaign, which led fans to study the marketing itself and raised impressions. Gymshark is an online brand using a physical New York pop-up.
numbers: pop-up length 12 weeks.

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#02 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: promotions-launches | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:29
claim: Rebuild the site for the sale: three header links only (women's, men's, accessories sale), best sellers first, and the discount revealed with a code.
detail: Narrow the choice during the one week wallets are open. Supporting evidence: a supermarket test with 24 jam varieties versus six, where more people tried the 24 but the six sold two to three times more. Gymshark showed retail prices on the page and revealed the savings at checkout through the code; Hormozi had no split test for it. Same pattern at BarkBox and QVC: banner for "today", top four products, three or four categories.
numbers: 24 jars vs 6 jars; six jars sold 2-3x more buyers.

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: promotions-launches | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:36
claim: If a few days produce what a month normally does, give the preparation the effort of an extra month a year.
detail: Sites built just for the day (banner urgency, first-time offer, best products first) show that peak days deserve dedicated work. Any timely occasion (New Year, Boxing Day, Halloween) can be handled with a temporary theme or reskin that says it is only for now.

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#04 | TACTIC | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:45
claim: BarkBox gave discounts through pet quizzes and challenges, so the coupon also collected data for personalisation and remarketing.
detail: The quiz asks the pet's name and age, so the buyer receives a personalised offer and the company builds a database. The lower action threshold also makes the first step easy; people trade information for a discount. Hormozi likes this idea and uses it for his own scaling roadmap form. Mischief is named as the leader in challenge-based commerce, with stunts such as a PT Cruiser hidden with its keys in the ignition.

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#05 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:47
claim: Make a crazy first-order offer when you know customer LTV, and point the page toward the subscription you really want.
detail: BarkBox offers the first box for $7 and puts the subscription call at the bottom left with a Black Friday incentive. Hormozi's point: new-customer attention peaks in this window, so a bold first-time offer is justified by LTV. Each store also had a "start here" keystone offer, with breadcrumbs placed along the path.
numbers: first box $7.

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#06 | EXAMPLE | topic: sales-process | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:51
claim: Accelerated checkout (Shop Pay, Apple Pay) has raised mobile conversion, and mobile is over half of retail traffic.
detail: People browse on phones but used to resist typing a card number; one-tap checkout removed that friction. The top banner is the most valuable real estate on a store, especially on mobile.
numbers: mobile above 50% of traffic (as stated).

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:16:06
claim: Do not make a buyer leave the video or page to purchase; QVC built one-click checkout inside on-demand video.
detail: Product thumbnails appear while the host presents; buying does not stop the viewing. A four-page or even one-page checkout away from the video adds friction. Hormozi adds that buyers enter "hyper buying cycles" (decide to run a 5K and then need shoes, shorts, an arm band) and a seller who does not offer the rest hands the sale to another retailer.

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#08 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:20:47
claim: A startup (BK Beauty) partnered with incumbents like Maybelline and L'Oreal to run their TikTok ads instead of competing with them.
detail: The pitch: "we do TikTok ads better than you, so collaborate". The brand became the top seller among TikTok Shop brands, runs traditional TV too, and judges channels only by ROAS and CAC against lifetime value. Hormozi adds that big companies fear small ones, since big firms struggle to place large bets, so collaboration can even pay for the startup's ad budget.

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#09 | TACTIC | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:23:18
claim: Native platform integrations outperform off-platform links relative to views.
detail: He set up a zero-dollar "product" buy box on X that sends buyers to his email list, which converts better per view than his other platforms because it is native. Early adopters of a platform's new feature (TikTok Shop, early Spotify podcasts) get its resources and distribution; the platform gets the innovation.

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#10 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:25:46
claim: Small firms can do what does not scale: a coffee-machine company's CEO sent a personal note and the buyer retold the story.
detail: A buyer of a premium pour-over machine received a note from the CEO who had researched him in five to eight minutes; he has since told the story about ten times and expects to have sold about ten machines through it. Larger rivals cannot do this, so creativity can offset their capital advantage.

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#11 | CASE | topic: promotions-launches | also: money-models | fw: Scarcity | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:27:16
claim: Burger King sold a 12-item collectible advent calendar that sold out instantly and drew a new kind of customer.
detail: Items ranged from figurines to bread and sunglasses; it launched on 22 November. Priced to echo the brand's 1954 founding with about $100 of product inside. It appealed to pop-culture fans rather than burger buyers, like Ferrari or Porsche merchandise bought by people who cannot buy the car. Burger King also ran an offer for a Whopper within 500 feet of a McDonald's, geolocated, to drive app downloads.
numbers: price $19.54; about $100 of product value; 12 items.

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#12 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:29:02
claim: Put untested products in an advent calendar next to best sellers to test next year's range with scarcity and earned media.
detail: Fly By Jing's calendar mixed best-selling sauces with demos (such as a Szechuan sauce); it sold out in 2024 using only email, social and text. The company learned what fans like before a full launch. Hormozi calls this "double dip or triple dip": PR, shares, sales, rewarding hardcore fans and data from one move.

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#13 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:33:07
claim: Grow the pie instead of slicing it: target people who never bought the category rather than your competitor's customers.
detail: Shopify investors worried the addressable market was too small because they counted existing retail small businesses; the market was bigger once new merchants entered. A gym's biggest competitor is the couch, Netflix's is sleep. Selling into a new market avoids price competition because you are the first love.

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#14 | CASE | topic: content-brand | also: lead-getters | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:36:03
claim: ButcherBox bought a live cooking-class company and gave free classes with no call to action, then eventually pointed to ingredients to buy.
detail: Buying a media asset that lives on sponsorships means you stop paying for ads and own the placement. The classes were high quality and the ask came later, once the audience was ready. Hormozi's rule: the give before the ask is almost directly proportional to sales at the ask.

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#15 | EXAMPLE | topic: content-brand | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:38:24
claim: A Levi's video showed one pair of jeans on one man in several settings so different buyer types could see themselves in it.
detail: Four or five avatars (hipster, workman and others) appear in one video; viewers who do not identify with one will with another, and the video cross-sells related items. It widens the addressable market of a broad category.

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#16 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: content-brand | fw: Puddles-Ponds-Lakes-Oceans | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:44:29
claim: Win by being the "mayor" of a tiny dot: a founder obsessed with one niche beats a catalogue seller.
detail: Examples: a founder known for black T-shirts, trending niche brands in blankets and pajamas, and Fire Belly Tea, a high-end tea shop that works as a small business after a mass tea brand failed to sell high-end tea. A niche can expand beyond its original market when buyers prefer your version. Large firms refuse small opportunities (priority seven when they do the top three), leaving room for 10-person companies.

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#17 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:41:35
claim: Buyers prefer purchasing from the people behind the product and from any channel; companies should be channel-agnostic and default global.
detail: Black Friday 2024 takeaway from the Shopify president: personality-led brands win, there is no online versus offline for the buyer, and Gymshark (an online brand from the UK) succeeds in physical retail. Cross-border orders have grown; a US business can ship to Canada, which adds about 40 million people.
numbers: cross-border orders about 20% of orders (as stated); Canada about 40 million people.

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#18 | DECISION_RULE | topic: paid-ads | also: content-brand | fw: Free Giveaways | novelty: new-detail | ts: 01:01:57
claim: A brand stunt such as giving away a car will probably return more than putting the same money into ads (delayed ROAS).
detail: His illustration: with a $1M/month ad budget, spending $50,000 on a giveaway will probably return more than spending 5% more on ads. He says that at this point the story and emotional connection matter a lot.
numbers: $1M monthly ad spend; $50,000 giveaway equals 5% of that.
### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#19 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 01:07:26
claim: Marketing and sales are one continuum, and acquisition is education: a change of behaviour under the same conditions.
detail: Moving a customer from ordinary tea to a higher-end tea is education. Example: an accountant who posts 60-second videos each time the tax code changes becomes the obvious choice when someone needs an accountant, and can move faster than big firms bound by compliance and committees.

### YT-YUXLJauT4eY#20 | EXAMPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Many-Sided Die | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:56:25
claim: Separate failing from being a failure; the president cites nine failures before a big success and a billion-dollar acquisition later sold.
detail: His rule: do not repeat a mistake twice. Shopify bought a logistics business for over a billion dollars and sold it because it was a "side quest" away from the main quest. Early ventures included slippers and poker chips. Cost of failure is low now, like many lottery tickets, and the real competitor is distraction.
numbers: nine failures before the first big success; acquisition above $1 billion.
