### POD-0002#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: verticals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Ascension Path | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:00
claim: He is a bigger advocate of adding a high-ticket tier after the gym is full rather than opening another location.
detail: He says that with six gyms behind him he would now grow more slowly and add high ticket much sooner. First fill or crowd the low-end offer so demand justifies a premium tier. Owner-operators should launch it personally; multi-site owners hire a strong trainer to run it.
applies_to: Gyms with a full base (about 250 to 300 members).
### POD-0002#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:19
claim: 60 members at $400 per month earn about three quarters of what 250 members at roughly $150 do.
detail: His example: 250 recurring members brought about $32,000 per month (stated figure; transcript says "$1.50", read as $150), against all-in overhead of about $18,000 to $20,000 per month. A 5x price needs about one fifth of the people for the same revenue and is easier to service.
numbers: 250 members about $32,000/month; overhead $18,000-$20,000/month; 60 at $400 = $24,000 (about 75%).
### POD-0002#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:45
claim: Customers decide on price in big jumps, so a $135 and a $155 tier are one decision, and higher tickets leave more room to charge.
detail: Move to a tier several times higher ($350 to $400). Once a buyer seriously considers a $2,000 package, price becomes movable and $2,500 might have sold; the extra $500 feels small by contrast but is large in margin.
numbers: $2,000 vs $2,500 package; tiers at $120-$160 treated as the same decision.

### POD-0002#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:55
claim: Price the front end above the ongoing membership to recoup ad cost, then drop buyers into a lower recurring rate that feels like a deal.
detail: A 12-week programme at $2,000 works out to roughly $650 to $700 per month; afterwards a downsell to $399 to $499 per month feels like a discount. Charging the full price for the same service after a cheap start leaves acquisition cost unrecovered and feels like a hike.
numbers: $2,000 for 12 weeks (about $650-$700/month); follow-on $399-$499/month.

### POD-0002#05 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: promotions-launches | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:24
claim: Match offer length to the target ticket: six weeks into a lower tier, 28 days or 12 weeks into a higher recurring tier.
detail: A 28-day programme priced near the membership is effectively an early recurring signup; a 12-week package can command $700 per month versus $400. A middle-ticket offer of $500 to $600 can feed a $150 to $200 membership. Rotate offers so the market does not saturate.
numbers: 6 weeks for middle to low ticket; 28 days or 12 weeks for high ticket; $700 vs $400.
