### YT-MLkFOaxIdxM#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery | fw: 80/20 Customer Analysis | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: An owner with three businesses and three product lines each had about 80% of revenue from 20% of the offers; Hormozi advised cutting the rest.
detail: The owner added things because "extra money was sitting on the table". Eliminating everything but the main line would remove roughly 80% of his time spent on operational drag and complexity of other teams. Hormozi's usual first move in a business is to get the owner to do less and the company to do fewer things.
numbers: 80% of revenue from 20% of offers; about 80% of the owner's time spent on non-core items.

### YT-MLkFOaxIdxM#02 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:46
claim: A digital marketing owner at $4-5M a year wanted to add general business coaching because clients asked; Hormozi said that is the distraction strategy exists to refuse.
detail: Hormozi calls such easy money "the woman in the red dress": a short-term win that pulls you from the main game. Strategy is knowing what you say no to. He turns down about two DMs a day offering $10,000 for an hour of his time.
numbers: Client revenue about $4-5M a year; Hormozi gets about two $10,000-an-hour requests daily.

### YT-MLkFOaxIdxM#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:33
claim: Founders hit a ceiling in beliefs, skills or character and jump to an adjacent easier ladder instead of fixing the limit, so they never climb higher.
detail: Each rung needs all three. When growth gets hard, the easy win on another ladder gives a dopamine hit but tops out below the first ladder's limit. Hormozi sees this as the number one entrepreneur mistake and one he made.

### YT-MLkFOaxIdxM#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:56
claim: Temptation grows with size: a side deal of $1,000 a month is easy to refuse, one of $50,000-$100,000 a month is not.
detail: At about $1M revenue and $250,000 net, a pitch for an extra $1,000 a month gets "no thanks"; a pitch for an extra $50,000 or $100,000 a month tempts you. Repeating one successful action patiently with compounding beats it. He says "Simple scales, fancy fails".
numbers: Example business: $1M revenue, $250,000 net; temptations $1,000, $50,000, $100,000 per month.

### YT-MLkFOaxIdxM#05 | EVOLUTION | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:28
claim: Hormozi thinks he started the supplement company (Prestige Labs) too early and believes it basically flatlined his growth for about two years.
detail: Going from one business to two before he could run the first hands-off forced him to build new character traits. He now treats the portfolio as the one thing.
numbers: By his own estimate ("I think"), growth basically flatlined for about two years.
### YT-MLkFOaxIdxM#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:44
claim: The test for moving from CEO to owner: the company keeps growing while you put in under five hours weekly for half a year and no rescue is needed.
detail: Until you pass that test, refuse new ventures. Being CEO without daily tasks is still a job, yet your attention returns only once someone else runs the company. Afterwards a new initiative means putting together a team, or buying a stake in an existing firm, rather than launching another business yourself.
numbers: Under 5 hours a week for 6 consecutive months.
