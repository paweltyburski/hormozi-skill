### POD-0105#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:58
claim: Pair each new goal with something you will stop doing, because attention is always full.
detail: Adding goals on top of a full plate cannot be sustained above 100% capacity. Instead identify the items on your plate that do not advance the goals and remove them; the goals then fill the space naturally.

### POD-0105#02 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:48
claim: If a department head has too many goals, the answer is a hire or delegation, not prioritising.
detail: Their department heads had 10 to 15 goals each and led teams of 15 to 20 people. An owner overloaded with tasks usually has not made the one hire that should take them over.
numbers: Departments of 15-20 people, 10-15 goals each; a department of 20-30 people is compared to a $3M-$10M business.

### POD-0105#03 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:39
claim: Block your whole calendar, then cut everything except a few meetings you must attend.
detail: They removed nearly all meetings, kept about four, and used the freed attention for priorities like going public. When in doubt, cut. Meet with the head of each department instead of attending the department's meeting.
numbers: 4 meetings kept.

### POD-0105#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:04
claim: Sitting in on a director's meeting removes their authority and lowers everyone's checking.
detail: Everyone looks to the founder for the final say, people think less creatively, and each side assumes the other is catching errors. Coach the head in a separate session instead; if you are in the meeting more than you coach, you are using a bandaid where stitches are needed.
