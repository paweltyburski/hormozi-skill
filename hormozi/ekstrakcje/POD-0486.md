### POD-0486#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Investing rules are written as if-then decision rules judged over many decisions, not by any single outcome.
detail: A single deal can blow up; the test is whether using the same decision process repeatedly would earn more over a long horizon. The 24 rules are his collected set. His stated audience for investment partnerships is companies of $3M to $100M.
numbers: Target companies $3M-$100M.

### POD-0486#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:00
claim: If you cannot buy it twice, and realistically probably three to six times, don't buy it once, because you are probably taking too big a chunk.
detail: He once planned to put about a third into one investment and was told his biggest allocation might be about 10%.
numbers: Biggest allocation might be about 10% of capital; realistically probably able to repeat the buy 3 to 6 times.
### POD-0486#03 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:32
claim: A specific worry can usually be covered by buying insurance for it.
detail: Insurance exists for almost anything. Annuities pay a guaranteed income until death for fear of running out of money; key-person insurance covers someone's death; specialists insure their hands. Name the concern, insure it, and move on.

### POD-0486#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:19
claim: If a deal looks like a grand slam, get a second opinion.
detail: A deal that looks perfect probably hides something you cannot see, so he sends such deals to other people automatically.

### POD-0486#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:50
claim: If you cannot say how you could lose money in a deal, do not do it until you can.
detail: Find all the ways to lose, not one. The unknown can never be eliminated, but it must be reduced as far as possible before committing.

### POD-0486#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:07
claim: If you feel rushed or pressured, pause, and treat pressure as a reason to walk away.
detail: Pressure to hand over money or lend grows as you get richer. He credits this rule with preventing many mistakes.

### POD-0486#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:40
claim: Never proceed with only one deal on the table; keep backup deals.
detail: With no alternative to compare against, you talk yourself into the only option, as with a dating pool of one. Wait until you have many options.

### POD-0486#08 | DEFINITION | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:22
claim: Buying with the intent to sell is trading or running a business, not investing, and the skills differ.
detail: Investing means very long horizons, which is why few legendary investors are in their 20s. Business operators and investors each often lack the other's skill, so do not apply one rulebook to the other.

### POD-0486#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:05:19
claim: In a partnership, if you do not agree, do not move forward, and the cautious partner must truly balance you.
detail: He is the optimistic yes-man; entrepreneurs have to be, since success odds are low. A partner who just gives in adds nothing. He does not override even with decision authority, and applies the same rule in his marriage, talking until agreement.

### POD-0486#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:06:28
claim: If a deal is complicated or above your head, pass.
detail: If you're barely catching on, you might as well be gambling; pretending you understand makes it doubly bad. Full understanding lowers the risk because you understand the variables.
### POD-0486#11 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:07:13
claim: When you feel fear of missing out, slow down.
detail: He calls this a decision-making variable; practising these if-this-then-that principles like muscles preserves your ability to make better decisions over time. Separately, if you miss something because you skipped it for lack of diligence, nine other things you also skipped might have lost you money.
### POD-0486#12 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:07:35
claim: No diligence, no deal: without the information needed, do not decide.
detail: Applies to any purchase, not just formal investing; do not buy on hype, an email or a call.

### POD-0486#13 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:08:20
claim: No model, no deal: demand the math and projections, then test their assumptions.
detail: Ask which assumptions the forecast rests on, whether they are reasonable, what information you can add, and what must be true for the outcome to occur. This makes the judgement objective instead of story-driven.

### POD-0486#14 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:08:52
claim: A counterparty who is unresponsive or misses their own stated response time is a reason to walk away.
detail: Slow replies are acceptable if the time promised is met (say two days, deliver in two days); not acceptable is silence or promising tomorrow and answering two days later. Hormozi judges people quickly on how meticulous and quick their responses are, and expects that what they say will happen does happen.
numbers: Example: promised reply in 2 days and delivered in 2 days is fine.
### POD-0486#15 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:10:44
claim: Hormozi's personal rule, adopted more recently: no neutral moves, only do something if it builds his reputation.
detail: Reputation is what you take with you forever, yet it can be lost and irreparably damaged, while money can be recovered. Continuing to reinvest in reputation gives compounding returns in relationships and financial returns.
### POD-0486#16 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:11:31
claim: No track record, no deal: choose performance over charisma, for investments, hires and vendors alike.
detail: Past performance is the best predictor. A model, openness to diligence and responsiveness do not replace it. He prefers lucky generals; someone else will back the unproven person and he can join the next deal.

### POD-0486#17 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:12:22
claim: If something has worked repeatedly for you, do more of it rather than seeking variety.
detail: Compounding comes from repetition. After three good hard-money loans, make a fourth instead of switching to crypto day trading. Own results show where you have an edge, but do not assume you are smarter than you are, since luck plays a part.
numbers: Example: 3 successful loans, then a 4th.

### POD-0486#18 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:13:11
claim: Always under-borrow, because any return multiplied by zero is zero.
detail: Debt is leverage on the way up and debt on the way down. Over a long horizon the downside happens, so repeated over-leveraging eventually wipes out earlier gains. Lower returns that survive beat higher returns that end in zero.
numbers: Example: 30% returns on 9 of 10 leveraged deals, then total loss on the 10th.

### POD-0486#19 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:14:44
claim: Ask basic questions, define terms and make the other side draw it out; if you cannot draw it back, you do not understand it.
detail: Many people cannot define their own terms. He opens calls by asking the presenter to explain as if he were a dog who does not speak English. Showing less knowledge costs nothing, while knowing more than they assume is an advantage, and obvious questions often expose weak spots.

### POD-0486#20 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:15:33
claim: At $10M to $30M of investable assets, only write a check if you also hold part of the general partner (GP) slot.
detail: A mentor's rule. Limited partners supply capital; GPs run the deal and earn an extra share. A large enough check lets you negotiate a slice of the GP so you share in the control of operations. He and his wife apply it.
numbers: Applies from about $10-30M investable assets.

### POD-0486#21 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:16:36
claim: A bad person makes a bad deal whatever the paperwork says, and mild unease alone is a reason to decline.
detail: Bad partners find a way to cheat regardless of terms. His wife's judgement of people has never been wrong in their relationship; his own optimism has. You should say no far more often than yes.

### POD-0486#22 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:17:15
claim: No contract, no deal: contracts set expectations, and avoiding the talk means avoiding the hard questions.
detail: Walk through what happens if the other side loses your assets or fails to deliver, or if you fail. If it cannot be discussed up front, it will not be discussed when it happens. He says everyone who insisted on handshake-only deals turned out to be shady.

### POD-0486#23 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:19:07
claim: Negotiate the contract as if it is everything, then live as if it does not exist.
detail: A contract is only as good as the signer: a personal guarantee from someone with no assets guarantees nothing. As the service provider, operate as if the customer is on a week-to-week basis even with a year-long term, staying above your obligations; defensive in drafting, offensive in delivery.
