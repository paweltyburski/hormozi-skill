### POD-250819-06#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: Downsell Offers | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:46
claim: A car salesman cut the same insurance from $5,000 to $400 and lost the buyer's trust, and the car sale with it.
detail: The buyer first refused on price and finally on distrust, wondering whether the car price was padded too. Lowering the price of the same thing closes one sale but makes the customer doubt every later price. Offering different, cheaper insurance would have kept trust.
numbers: Same insurance cut from $5,000 to $400.

### POD-250819-06#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: money-models | fw: Downsell Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:34
claim: A no means no to this offer, not to you, and a downsell is a trade: give something, get something.
detail: Find what they like and dislike, then offer more of the former and less of the latter with a matching price. Asking an alternative (small, juice or coffee after refusing a large soda) is better service, not offence.

### POD-250819-06#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: Downsell Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:20

detail: Cutting price on the spot is discounting, not downselling, and customers compare notes. If they only dislike the price, you can offer a payment plan. He treats unequal prices for identical goods as an ethical issue too, at least for himself.
### POD-250819-06#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:21
claim: A payment plan loses most when someone who would have paid in full takes the plan and cancels early.
detail: Plans make money when they bring extra buyers who complete payments, and lose it when buyers cancel before profit. Use them only where you know the bet is in your favour.

### POD-250819-06#06 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:05
claim: Ask whether they want you to set the payment terms or to decide themselves; when they choose, send them to a credit card.
detail: People usually prefer to choose. Pointing them to a credit card lets you take the money now while they pay the card company over time. Layaway is the lowest-risk option for you, since goods are released only after full payment.

### POD-250819-06#07 | SCRIPT | topic: sales-process | also: pricing | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:43
claim: Half-now offers start with "when are you next paid?" and, failing that, "what is the most you can put down today?"
detail: Take whatever amount they offer today and schedule the rest on their payday. Pay-cycle schedules (every two weeks) lift 30-day cash far more than monthly instalments.

### POD-250819-06#10 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:23
claim: The seesaw version frames big monthly payments as the bad option and uses the down payment as the lever.
detail: Ask whether they want giant or tiny monthly payments; they choose tiny; then say a prepayment removes monthly payments with a big discount. If they cannot afford it, say the more they put down, the lower the monthly rate. If they still refuse, check their interest and move your chair to their side of the table.

### POD-250819-06#15 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:16:42
claim: Adding a trial downsell turns 3 sales from 10 prospects into 6.

### POD-250819-06#18 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:11
claim: Do not sell a trial to someone who will not put a card down and do the work.
detail: Hold their ID, ask which card they want to use, and if they balk say it is how it has always been done. A refuser who then declines is shown out, since such people complain more and convert less.

### POD-250819-06#19 | SCRIPT | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:22
claim: Ask "if this gets you the result, will you stay long term?" and frame the trial as habit-building, not a quick fix.
detail: A no means there is no point offering the trial. If they want more explanation, say you cannot ethically promise a quick fix; the free trial builds the habits, and long-term results require staying on afterward. Then collect the card before explaining fees.

### POD-250819-06#20 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:12

detail: Explaining before the card draws more resistance. Use a "this is how we've always done it" attitude. The separate initials force reps to explain the clause. Then require check-ins, which are the upsell moments.

### POD-250819-06#21 | DECISION_RULE | topic: product-delivery | also: sales-process | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:44
