### YT-K8MFC9t7snY#01 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: He estimates Gym Launch missed about $900M of value because it sold one-time licences instead of keeping gyms paying.
detail: About 5,000 gyms bought the licence. If all 5,000 were still paying, he says the company would be worth over $1B, versus roughly $100M now. It took him about seven years to internalise this lesson.
numbers: 5,000 gyms; ~$1B hypothetical vs ~$100M actual; ~$900M missed.

### YT-K8MFC9t7snY#02 | DEFINITION | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:33
claim: Recurring means a subscription; reoccurring means customers reorder repeatedly without one, and either way you want to be in the reorder business.
detail: Facebook sells ad impressions without subscriptions, yet buyers return to purchase again and again. One-off merch drops have no compounding vehicle.

### YT-K8MFC9t7snY#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Formula: lifetime revenue = price / monthly churn | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:35
claim: Small steady sales with no churn compound into large recurring bases.
detail: One sale per week for 10 years gives about 500 paying customers each month; 10 new customers a month who never leave gives 600 after five years. If you sell 100 or 200 a month but lose them, you must resell the same number just to hold level.
numbers: 1 sale/week = ~500 customers in 10 years; 10/month = 600 in 5 years.

### YT-K8MFC9t7snY#04 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: retention-ltv | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:18
claim: Ask: if you could no longer market and new customers could only come from referrals of current ones, how differently would you treat them?
detail: He uses this question with portfolio companies to push them to improve the experience and the product before scaling.

### YT-K8MFC9t7snY#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:41
claim: A company with 10x the lifetime value of rivals at the same acquisition cost wins every bidding contest for the next customer.
detail: When customers do not leave you can afford any marketing and can outspend everyone, so he prefers to raise retention first and then put money behind marketing. Most people do the reverse: sell hard, burn goodwill and launch a new product.
numbers: 10x LTV at equal CAC (a company he was about to invest in).

### YT-K8MFC9t7snY#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:41
claim: When customers do not consume the product, blame your onboarding and product, not customer laziness; plateaus should lead to fixing the product, not more marketing.
detail: Fix the product by surveying customers, studying results and making outcomes faster and easier, until referrals appear. Amazon fixed problems until service tickets became rare.

### YT-K8MFC9t7snY#07 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:42
claim: In B2B, add collateral or control the money flow to make leaving harder; the party that handles the money has leverage.
detail: He cites a payment processor that collects your money upfront so it does not worry about clients' cards.
