### YT-t7o8dtUWPQg#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:35
claim: Seven disciplines an entrepreneur needs, rated as a self-audit pyramid: foundational, physical, professional, emotional, relational, financial, philosophical.
detail: Foundational is reading, writing and maths, the only one schools teach well and the gateway to the rest. Physical covers food, movement and sleep (without superstition). Professional depends on your skill set or career path; some schools teach it decently (e.g. accounting), though accountants say real education begins after graduating. Emotional is naming your own feelings and gaps. Relational is communication and influence, via likeability and trustworthiness rather than hacks. Financial and philosophical follow. Ask where you are lacking in each.
### YT-t7o8dtUWPQg#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:26
claim: Financial education has two stages: first spend well below what you earn, then learn to multiply the money.
detail: Wealth is a ratio of earnings to spending, so cutting spending raises wealth. After that comes making money work for you, which he says is where he currently spends his learning time. Self-test: can you read a balance sheet and a cash flow statement?

### YT-t7o8dtUWPQg#03 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:20
claim: Replace "I am bad at X" with "I have a deficiency in X, not yet", and use language patterns to predict who will grow.
detail: Statements like "I am not good with money" or "I do not play well with others" harden into identity. Reframe them as a gap you can fix ("not good at this yet, and that's okay"). Hormozi says that listening to the I-am, I-do, I-must wording of employees and client businesses tells him quickly how likely they are to grow.

### YT-t7o8dtUWPQg#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:09
claim: Improving every other discipline does not make you feel better unless you know why you do it; values are the base of well-being.
detail: Hormozi ranks the philosophical bucket (values, what it means to be human) as the most important despite listing it last. Changes in how much he enjoys life came from shifts there, not from more money. His own purpose is learning, and he learns best by teaching, which is why he makes free videos.
