### YT-sPkMHh8zTMI#01 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:51
claim: To find what works, survey the top 1% performers and extract what they all do.
detail: For Gym Launch he phoned every gym with the lowest churn held for six months or more. Each named about 30 tactics; five appeared in all of them. He added two more, and the seven together halved churn.
numbers: top 1% of gyms by lowest churn, sustained 6+ months; monthly churn fell from 11% to 6%.
applies_to: Licensing or consulting firms with many comparable locations.

### YT-sPkMHh8zTMI#02 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: objections-closing | fw: Fee or Feedback | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:36
claim: Tell customers at signup that cancelling requires an exit interview, then use the call to listen, validate and save them.
detail: Each cancellation teaches one hole to plug. On the call, let them vent and be more upset about the problem than they are. Offer a credit of what they paid toward staying, with an exit if still unhappy. Some turn out to be on the wrong tier: credit the lower-tier payments toward the higher one.
numbers: about half of the callers could be saved or upsold (his recollection); upsell tier 2-3x the price.

### YT-sPkMHh8zTMI#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: Revenue Churn | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:45
claim: If half of leavers upsell to a plan 2-3x dearer, revenue churn disappears even if logo churn stays.
detail: Revenue churn is the revenue lost, not the customers lost. Saving or ascending half the leavers outweighs losing the other half.

### YT-sPkMHh8zTMI#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:05:31
claim: Recurring service businesses stall at roughly 100 to 150 customers because of Dunbar's number unless relationships are systematised.
detail: Without a system an owner can hold about that many relationships in mind. Scheduled reach-outs approximate a personal relationship at scale.
numbers: cap about 100-150 customers.

### YT-sPkMHh8zTMI#05 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:17
claim: Assign each trainer about 40 customers and contact each every 14 to 21 days, checking on the person rather than the product.
detail: That is 10 to 15 contacts a week. Do not sell in it; sell the membership and use reach-outs as unadvertised over-delivery. It is easier to quit a membership than a relationship, and customers with an issue go to the person they already know, so feedback arrives faster. It must be systematised, not automated: obvious automation gets ignored.
numbers: 40 customers per trainer; every 14-21 days.

### YT-sPkMHh8zTMI#06 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: retention-ltv | fw: Seven Ways to Ask for Referrals | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:51
claim: Hold member events open to friends, so they pay for themselves in sales, and always announce the next event at the current one.
detail: A bring-a-friend event doubles attendance and gives a strong offer to the guests. After an event churn rises as people feel they got enough value, so the open loop of the next date is the fix. Referrals are worth more than cold customers, so pay the most for them.
numbers: referral customers worth 2-3x a cold customer.

### YT-sPkMHh8zTMI#07 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: retention-ltv | fw: Hinge Method | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:42
claim: Send a handwritten card about every six weeks that reminds members of the next event and ends with a P.S. asking them to text a photo of it to a friend in a group message.
detail: The friend gets in free by showing the photo. The card shows care, gives a themed benefit, and lets the member give something to a friend.
numbers: card cadence about every 6 weeks.

### YT-sPkMHh8zTMI#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:01
claim: Cancellation correlates most with lack of consumption; churn is a lagging indicator, so build an early-warning metric.
detail: Gym attendance falls in steps (three visits, then two, then one, then zero) before cancelling. A gym membership never used is never worth it; one used daily draws no complaint. Define the leading indicator for what you sell.

### YT-sPkMHh8zTMI#09 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:45
claim: When attendance dips, reach out at once, and escalate contact attempts for customers, the reverse of nurturing leads.
detail: Missing one of three sessions triggers outreach in the same week; they feel someone is watching and return. Leads get fewer touches each day; customers get more: one on day one, two next, three after.
numbers: contacts 1, 2, 3 on successive days.

### YT-sPkMHh8zTMI#10 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: GROWTH Onboarding | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:31
claim: Onboarding must set expectations and reconfirm the buying decision in the first 24 hours, when cold feet and chargebacks occur.
detail: Resell why they bought, add reasons they did not mention, and give a roadmap and walkthrough. Low-price products can use automated or group onboarding even at $100 a month. Expected gain: one to two extra months of recurring revenue.
numbers: first 24 hours critical; +1 to 2 months of recurring revenue.

### YT-sPkMHh8zTMI#11 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:19
claim: Spread expectation-setting into many small set-and-meet cycles rather than one big list.
detail: For an agency, announce today's task and report it done tomorrow: seven positive feedback cycles in a week for the same work. Customers are distracted, so repeat so they assemble the whole picture over time.
numbers: 7 cycles per week.

### YT-sPkMHh8zTMI#12 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: Activation Points | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:17:50
claim: Activation points differ by business: a custom URL for page-builder software, five pounds lost in week one, the first five clients for a trainer app.
detail: Find them by asking the longest-staying customers what they did early, or by surveying. Software users who switched to a custom domain stayed about five times longer; trainers who got five clients never cancelled. Gym Launch's was a $2,000 sale in the first seven days.
numbers: custom URL: about 5x longer retention; trainers with 5 clients: no cancels; $2,000 sale in 7 days.

### YT-sPkMHh8zTMI#13 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: lead-getters | fw: Ascension Path | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:20:08
claim: Use unlockables: bonuses released when customers activate, give a testimonial, refer, or buy the next level, or simply when they reach a tenure milestone.
detail: Place the first milestone just past the month where churn currently happens, then stretch later ones further apart and make rewards bigger (for example months 3, 8, 15). A cited study said buying something else is the strongest retainer. Track average tenure monthly and push it up, for example 3.7 to 4.2 to 4.7 months.
numbers: example milestones at months 3, 8 and 15.

### YT-sPkMHh8zTMI#14 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: Revenue Churn | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:23:07
claim: Halving churn doubles lifetime value and, at a 3:1 ratio, doubles the allowable spend per customer, which lifts the ad spend ceiling.
detail: $100 a month at 10% churn is $1,000 lifetime value (10 months); at 5% it is $2,000. Allowed CAC at 3:1 rises from $300 to about $600-650, so a daily spend cap of about $1,000 might become about $100,000. One of his companies lifted LTV 1.9x ($2,000 to $3,800), going from about $2M a month to about $2M a week (his statement).
numbers: LTV $1,000 to $2,000; CAC allowed $300 to $600+; company LTV $2,000 to $3,800.

### YT-sPkMHh8zTMI#15 | DECISION_RULE | topic: retention-ltv | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:25:31
claim: Implementation order by return for effort: onboarding, unlockables, exit interviews, attendance tracking, reach-outs, then events with cards together.
detail: Onboarding is one added step after the close. Unlockables are a one-time setup. Attendance tracking takes more work but would probably rank first or second in actual effect on retention.
