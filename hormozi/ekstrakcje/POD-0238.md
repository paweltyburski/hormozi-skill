### POD-0238#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: The Size of the Pie fallacy: the real market is likely 100 to 1,000 times larger than the small circle you watch, so a new rival rarely takes your customers.
detail: People live in a few groups and news sources and assume everybody knows each competitor. Most buyers know neither you nor the rival. The cost of the fallacy is not lost customers but lost attention taken from serving the customers you already have.
numbers: His estimate: market 100x-1,000x larger than perceived.

### POD-0238#02 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:14
claim: When he copied a rising local gym competitor's tactics, it converted worse and the rival earned less than he did.
detail: He modelled everything the newcomer did to beat him, and months later (six plus) found the competitor was making less. Meanwhile the fear had consumed his focus. In the licensing business a similar "hot new guru" appeared, and reacting by changing direction confused his customers; the newcomer was gone about 16 weeks later.
numbers: Rival gone after about 16 weeks; competitor discovery about six months later.

### POD-0238#03 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: lead-getters | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:32
claim: A gym-software company he had barely heard of runs no ads, only a large cold-calling and cold-email sales team, and did about $10 million a month.
detail: He found it was bigger than his own business, roughly three times his size, without seeing its ads or hearing it mentioned in his groups. His lesson: the part of the market you can see is one channel (Facebook), and others such as search, display, organic, email, outreach, cold calling and direct mail remain open.
numbers: About $10M/month (unit as stated); about 3x his own business size.

### POD-0238#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:01
claim: Respond to competitors by over-delivering to current customers and adding channels over time, not by reacting.
detail: Even a local radius of about seven miles holds many businesses advertising on the same platform, so one more gym ad does not halve results. His reactivity fell as he watched challengers who attacked him disappear within about nine months.
numbers: Local ad radius about 7 miles; challengers who attacked him disappeared within nine months.
