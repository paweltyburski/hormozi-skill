### YT-7YMjZgr7sHM#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:49
claim: Spend long analysis only on decisions that are irreversible and have downstream consequences.
detail: Examples: who to marry, where to live, what to work on, big strategic changes. One such decision took him about 12 months and had nine-figure consequences for his net worth.
numbers: About 12 months for one major decision.

### YT-7YMjZgr7sHM#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:52
claim: If your answer changes day to day while the data does not, you are deciding emotionally.
detail: He treats ping-ponging with unchanged information as a leading indicator of a bad decision, and says mistakes love a rushed decision.

### YT-7YMjZgr7sHM#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:19
claim: Reduce emotional influence on a big decision with four steps: rest, eat, separate, need nothing.
detail: Decide when well rested; well fed (stress and hunger are processed similarly, so food raises resilience); away from the usual workspace because it carries emotional anchors; and from a state of having enough, so the outcome does not matter to your ego. Emotion still plays a part but secondary.

### YT-7YMjZgr7sHM#04 | DEFINITION | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:20
claim: Two biases to fight: confirmation bias and conviction bias.
detail: Confirmation: emotion picks a preferred answer and logic collects supporting data. Conviction: the more you want an outcome, the more you blind yourself to its real likelihood.

### YT-7YMjZgr7sHM#05 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:51
claim: Use inversion to check your setup: list how you would make a terrible decision and avoid it.
detail: A bad decision comes when tired, hungry, rushed, anchored among stressors, and needing the outcome. Reverse each (attributed to Charlie Munger's inversion).
