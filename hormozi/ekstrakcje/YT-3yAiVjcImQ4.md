### YT-3yAiVjcImQ4#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Until a business reaches 1M a year, sell one product to one avatar through one channel.
detail: The case: an attorney losing 100,000 a year. He launched courses and software to give back; the software alone lost money, and the two together (courses and software) took most of his time while services were the cash cow. Hormozi fix: stop the draining side projects, reposition the cheap education as free to funnel into services, and reprice services for the best avatar.
numbers: Threshold 1M a year; case loss 100,000 a year.
### YT-3yAiVjcImQ4#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:03
claim: Do not average prices across avatars: an A client may spend 10 times a C client, so serve the A avatar first and price for them.
detail: Averaging leads to charging 2x rather than 10x and sacrificing margin and scalability. Close rate was very high because he said yes to everyone, which signalled underpricing; Hormozi saw an easy double or triple for the right avatar.
numbers: A client may spend 10x a C client; potential price 2-3x.

### YT-3yAiVjcImQ4#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: "Help everyone" is a failure to say no; you can only help more people if you make money.

Repeats the conversation with his wife on how profit enables helping more people. Hormozi also says software with a code-only edge will not earn a 10x multiple.

### YT-3yAiVjcImQ4#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:05
claim: Find where leads really come from and lubricate existing behaviour instead of building a new funnel.
detail: Leads watched YouTube, then found Instagram and sent DMs on their own, but videos had no CTA and the profile did not invite a DM. Fixing this left an estimated 20,000-50,000 a month on the table. He already converted DMs well, so the aim was to help the existing path.
numbers: Estimated 20,000-50,000 a month left on the table.

### YT-3yAiVjcImQ4#05 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:05
claim: Put the CTA at about the 30% mark of videos and repeat it in the description, pinned comment and channel profile.

### YT-3yAiVjcImQ4#06 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: sales-process | fw: Lead Scoring | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:05

### YT-3yAiVjcImQ4#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:09
claim: In one client case, he replaced hourly billing with a flat fee tied to the outcome, plus a small commission on deals.
detail: He charged 250 an hour with an average client value of 1,500 (about six hours). Hourly rates are judged against other hourly rates, so he becomes a commodity. Fix: a flat 5,800 up front plus a commission on deal value (some deals worth hundreds of thousands). A good close rate suggests underpricing. Flat plus performance signals confidence to the prospect, while 100% commission is attractive but risky when clients may fail.
numbers: 250 per hour and 1,500 average value (about 6 hours) to 5,800 flat fee.
### YT-3yAiVjcImQ4#08 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: Anchor Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:31
claim: Offer an A or B choice instead of yes or no, where B is a high anchor that makes A look like a steal.
detail: Here B was 25,000 with zero commission, making 5,800 plus commission the obvious pick. Other A/B framings: chocolate or vanilla, Monday or Tuesday start.
numbers: A 5,800 plus commission; B 25,000, no commission.

### YT-3yAiVjcImQ4#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:51
claim: Do not charge for a sales call as a paid consultation; qualify first, show a presentation, sell the service, then deliver.
detail: He billed 250 for the first call, an hour of his time that only sold more hours. High-intent buyers want proof of a process, and how you sell signals how legitimate you are.
numbers: 250 charged for the first call.

### YT-3yAiVjcImQ4#10 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: Sales Aids | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:38
claim: Build a 5-7 minute VSL that answers the questions prospects ask on calls, in their words.
detail: Cover who, what, where, when, why, then each frequently asked question with the answer. It qualifies leads and lifts conversion; no secret skill needed.
numbers: VSL length 5-7 minutes.

### YT-3yAiVjcImQ4#11 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: BANT | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:57
claim: Pre-qualify with BANT questions on the booking form, with a timing dropdown, and prioritise buyers who say today or tomorrow.
detail: Budget: can you afford 5,800? Authority: anyone else involved? Need: what happens if you do not solve this? Timing: when do you want to start (today, tomorrow, a few weeks)? Origin of BANT is IBM in the 1960s.

### YT-3yAiVjcImQ4#12 | FRAMEWORK | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:13:47
claim: At the sell step use a four-angle persuasion grid: more good with you, less bad with you, more bad without you, less good without you.
detail: Written as plus plus, minus minus, plus minus, minus plus. It gives four angles for the S of CLOSER and also helps aggravate the pain of not acting. Concerns already answered in the VSL are handled the same way if they come back.

### YT-3yAiVjcImQ4#13 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Downsell Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:33
claim: Add a hands-off do-it-yourself downsell at 2,000-3,000 rather than a tiny price, so the 5,800 core stays the best path.
detail: With the 25,000 anchor on one side and a downsell not much cheaper on the other, the middle looks best. It monetises DIY buyers who were unqualified for the service.
