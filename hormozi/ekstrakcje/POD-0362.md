### POD-0362#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: sales-team | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: An outbound cold-calling rep brought in about 50 deals a year at roughly $40,000 each, so about $2M of production per rep, and six more reps would be about $12M.
detail: This is the basis of the roughly $10M figure (he estimates 10) for two quarters of missed hiring. The goal was set in two quarters with no action, and was set again in the third quarter's meeting.
numbers: 50 deals/year; ~$40,000/deal; ~$2M/rep/year; 6 reps ~$12M.
### POD-0362#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: Internal Core Four (Recruiting Mirror) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:21
claim: Employees churn like recurring customers, and high-churn roles (customer service, tech support, front-line sales) need a talent acquisition system sized to replace losses.
detail: With a team of 10 and 10% quarterly churn you lose one a quarter, so one hire a quarter only breaks even. At 20% to 25% churn you lose two or more. Here HR sent about one candidate a week and the manager needed about five interviews per hire.
numbers: 10% quarterly churn on a team of 10 = 1 lost; 20%-25% = 2+ lost.

### POD-0362#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:54
claim: The funnel needed about 20 attempts per hire (4-5 phone screens per interview, 4-5 interviews per hire), all one-on-one; the fix was group interviews of about 10 sent straight to the manager.
detail: HR runs the ad and schedules candidates on the manager's calendar, so HR stops being the constraint. Earlier version in a similar video quotes different counts.
numbers: ~20 attempts per hire; group of 10.

### POD-0362#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:41
claim: Match the hiring process to the role: front-line, high-volume positions need a faster, higher-volume pipeline because onboarding is shorter and losing someone costs less than for management.
detail: Senior roles take longer to vet and onboard, so a slower process fits them.

### POD-0362#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:42
claim: A business grows to its constraint, not its potential; adding potential while the constraint stays put leaves output flat, and the missed goal means you must dig one or two levels deeper.
detail: Outbound production stayed flat two quarters because new hires only matched churn. The same applies if doubling creative or reach-outs fails to double results: another unseen constraint exists, and the CEO's skill is asking the hard questions to find it.
