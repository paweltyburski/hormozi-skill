### YT-4GjwtnA76ig#01 | CASE | topic: offers | also: unit-economics, paid-ads | fw: Implied Guarantee | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:16
claim: Changing only the offer of an ads agency (from a retainer to a setup fee plus payment per show-up) lifted cash return on ad spend 22-fold with the same ads.
detail: Old: $1,000 down and $1,000 a month. New: $4,000 one-time, no monthly fee, pay again only when prospects show up, so an implied guarantee. With the same $10,000 ad spend and 300,000 reached, response rate rose 2.5 times, booked calls went from 40 to 100, shows from 30 to 75, and close rate rose 2.3 times.
numbers: Old: 40 booked, 30 shown, ~5 sales, ~0.5:1. New: 100 booked, 75 shown, 28 sales, $112,000 collected up front from $10,000 spend = 11.2:1; price 4x, response 2.5x, close 2.3x.

### YT-4GjwtnA76ig#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Price to Value Discrepancy | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:38
claim: Price can only fall to zero but value can rise without limit, so widen the gap by raising value, and sell in a vacuum where the buyer has no comparable.
detail: Competitors that follow the market average are often unprofitable (he sees many P&Ls), so averaging them is copying the mediocre. Price low only if a cost-driven culture was in place from day one.

### YT-4GjwtnA76ig#08 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: Virtuous Cycle of Price | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:30:29
claim: In a blind taste test, one wine shown at three prices was rated by its price, so price itself is a component of perceived value.
detail: Buyers called the cheap one cheap, the mid-priced one medium and the expensive one great, though all were the same. The goal is to be priced so high the buyer thinks it is another category.
