### POD-0112#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:49
claim: Condense CLOSER for a team to two questions: is the promise valuable enough for the price, and do I believe you can deliver it.
detail: A "no" means either they do not see it as valuable or they do not believe you. In B2B the promise is easy ("pay us 10 grand, we make you 100") and belief is the weak point; in B2C weight loss the promise value is harder and conviction medium-hard.
numbers: example: $10,000 in, $100,000 back.

### POD-0112#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:39
claim: Selling is directing attention onto the problem, because attention makes it more important.
detail: He compares it to election polling where topics the news cycle emphasises rise in importance. Prospects often cope by ignoring the problem; dig into pain and how many life areas it touches.

### POD-0112#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:22
claim: Ask a chain of "why did you come today" questions: why did you sign up, schedule it, drive through traffic, find parking.
detail: Each shows how much it matters to them, possibly more than they realise.

### POD-0112#04 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:15
claim: Stack value to the extreme with a vivid promised land: years of life, children spared the same struggle, freedom to eat cake.
detail: The programme is only a vehicle. Describe the outcome (eating what you like, no more diet hopping, going out and looking great), then ask whether it is worth, for example, $200 a month.
numbers: example: $200 a month.

### POD-0112#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:29
claim: Name the offer in the buyer's terms: a six-week "big booty bootcamp" sells, a six-week squat and deadlift seminar does not.
detail: The training is the same; the phrasing lets the prospect see why it matters.

### POD-0112#06 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:16
claim: Add a damaging admission: warn them that reaching goal weight will change their identity and how others treat them.
detail: Steps: ask since when they have been overweight; ask whether they identify with it; warn it will be hard. The candour makes the pitch credible.

### POD-0112#07 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:19
claim: If they laugh about their weight, say "don't laugh, it's not funny: laughter is how you cope with something you know you need to do."
detail: From Nick Elliott. He calls it a throat punch: the tone shifts and the prospect takes the conversation seriously. Then close with "what card do you want to use?"

### POD-0112#08 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: product-delivery | fw: Unconditional Guarantee | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:19
claim: Rebuild why a past diet failed into three gaps your programme fills, then add a refund guarantee and ask for the card.
detail: With a Jenny Craig example: 1. little check-in (monthly) means no accountability; 2. bought food, so they never learned cooking (given fish, not taught to fish); 3. no resistance training, so metabolism did not rise. Then "you can have cake and your size two jeans", a refund offer if they feel unserved within the eight weeks, and "what card?".
numbers: monthly check-in; eight-week window (example).

### POD-0112#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:26
claim: If they accept the value but doubt delivery, point to the big wall of past clients; then, if they might feel like a special snowflake, ask why they feel that way.
detail: Usually it is a past failure they do not understand; explain why it failed and why this time differs. Sellers also become numb to transformations; remind yourself the day is life-changing for the client.
