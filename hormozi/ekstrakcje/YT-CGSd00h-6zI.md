### YT-CGSd00h-6zI#01 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Sell the outcome of tutoring (a higher score, entry to a better school), not the tutoring hours.
detail: He ranks standardized-test prep as the most valuable, since a better school changes network, recruiters and earning capacity.

### YT-CGSd00h-6zI#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:55
claim: Semi-private teaching with eight students earns about 4.5x what one-on-one earns per hour while each pays less.
detail: One-on-one at $60 an hour for four hours yields $240. Eight students at $35 yields $280 an hour, about $1,100 for four hours. Students take turns on problems, so personal attention remains.
numbers: $60 x 4 hours = $240; $35 x 8 x 4 hours = $1,120 (he says $1,100); 8x students for about two-thirds the price.

### YT-CGSd00h-6zI#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:41
claim: Anchor the price on the outcome's value to the family and prefer prepayment so income is not tied to hours.
detail: The pitch rests on the extra yearly earnings from a better school; $35 a session is an affordable middle point.
numbers: Example earnings gain cited: $100,000 more per year.

### YT-CGSd00h-6zI#04 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:34
claim: Sell a target score and justify it with the input math: more practice problems lead to higher scores.
detail: He estimates about 1,000 problems adds roughly 20 points, then converts the goal into problems and hours needed. Research he cites shows scores track problems solved.
numbers: 1,000 problems = about 20 points (his estimate).

### YT-CGSd00h-6zI#05 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: lead-getters | fw: Warm Outreach Steps | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:33
claim: Offer five to ten free sessions that combine assessment and pitch, with homework charged if skipped.
detail: Start with one easy win, show the method, then ask for feedback, a testimonial and a referral in exchange for the free work. Billing missed homework gives skin in the game and better scores.
numbers: 5-10 free sessions.

### YT-CGSd00h-6zI#06 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:19
claim: Once more than ten people want it, start charging, discounting the next cohort and posting testimonials as hooks.
detail: DM people who engage with a better offer. Also approach sports teams after practice; word of mouth spreads through the team.
numbers: Next 10 students at half price.

### YT-CGSd00h-6zI#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: product-delivery | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:17
claim: Paying a teacher $50 an hour against $280 an hour of group revenue keeps service margin above 80%.
detail: His target is cost per unit under $56. Templatize how students are pushed to do more problems.
numbers: $280 revenue, $50 teacher cost, 80% margin means cost under $56.

### YT-CGSd00h-6zI#08 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:11
claim: Build two pipelines, students and teachers, by asking each teacher for friends who would earn twice as much in half the time.
detail: With four teachers in parallel the owner earns about $3,600 a day after $800 of pay. He claims about $3,300 a day equals $1M a year (basis unclear in audio).
numbers: 4 x $1,100 = about $4,400 less 4 x $200 = about $3,600 a day.
