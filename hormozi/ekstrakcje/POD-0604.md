### POD-0604#01 | CASE | topic: content-brand | also: lead-getters | fw: Agency Learning Agreement | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:11:32
claim: He built YouTube through two agencies in turn: a basic one for cadence, then a premium one to learn top-creator craft, then took it in-house.
detail: Phase one, a basic agency just trimmed podcast episodes and added an intro, thumbnail and headline, and committed him to three videos a week; the aim was basics and a regular cadence. Phase two, once quality demands outgrew the small agency, a premium agency taught topics, thumbnails, intros, cuts and its decision process. About six months in the team matched it and fees dropped to consulting. Same play for other platforms, paid ads and cold outbound.
numbers: Basic agency about $3,000-$5,000 a month; premium about $20,000 a month; three videos a week; about six months per phase.

### POD-0604#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: content-brand | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:10
claim: Content agencies come in two tiers that differ in price by 3-5x.
detail: Those who just produce the output cost less; the pros who work with the biggest creators cost far more. He pays little to the first because he lacks the context to judge, and moves up after three to six months of consistent posting and audience learning.
numbers: One brand on one platform: about $3,000-$5,000 a month (basic) versus $15,000-$30,000 (advanced).

### POD-0604#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:56
claim: Agencies tend to put their best staff on new clients and their cheapest on old ones, so learn the checklist and hire the cheap labour yourself.
detail: His breakthrough: if the low-wage worker on his account can be taught the routine in a couple of days, he could pay someone around $20 an hour directly and learn the checklist. Agencies are also one client among many, whereas an in-house team has more time and more brand context, so only the expertise gap needs filling.
numbers: In-house helper about $20 an hour.

### POD-0604#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:59
claim: Practising agencies usually know what works now better than coaches who sell old successes.
detail: He gets his best education from people doing the work daily, even though they dislike selling education.

### POD-0604#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: lead-getters | also: management-ops | fw: Agency Learning Agreement | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:46
claim: Hormozi starts every agency relationship with a purpose and a deadline, expects to pay twice for a while, and moves to a lower-cost consulting agreement once his team can do the work well enough.
detail: He tells the agency he wants to learn, will pay a bit more than the base fee if needed, and will keep paying for consulting afterward. During the overlap he pays both the agency and his own team. He had consulting agreements bridging gaps at the end.
### POD-0604#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: lead-getters | fw: Prep for Sale | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:03
claim: Relying on outside agencies for cash flow lowers enterprise value, because they can cut you off or raise fees as they see your profit.
detail: A business that depends on six agencies is less reliable than one with the skills in house, and in-house capability de-risks a future buyer. The real gain from an agency is the skill to generate customers for life, not just the customers made during the engagement. Agency fast wins keep momentum, then the team sustains it after the agency's attention drops to junior staff.

### POD-0604#07 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: objections-closing | fw: Agency Selection Checklist | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:50
claim: Interview many agencies, including the most expensive ones you won't hire, to calibrate depth and avoid being sold hot.
detail: Hearing which nuances the top agencies raise makes you a more informed buyer, who then stays longer. If you have more money than time, buy speed; if not, learn it yourself and use first-platform cash to pay for the next. "Selling hot" means overpromising to get the sale.
