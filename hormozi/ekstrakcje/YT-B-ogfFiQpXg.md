### YT-B-ogfFiQpXg#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:09
claim: For the concierge practice, he replaced a $500 member credit with bring-a-friend treatments worth $1,000-2,000 and a standing question at every visit: who do you want to bring next time?
detail: A $500 credit means little to wealthy members; a real treatment makes referring socially positive. Only for referrals, never advertised. A top membership could include five such treatments; at each booking (always setting the next meeting) staff ask who comes along, backed by evidence that people do better together. Same person not twice; marginal cost is near zero, so converting a third is great. Spouses already join this way.

### YT-B-ogfFiQpXg#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: promotions-launches | also: lead-getters | fw: Free Giveaways | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:02
claim: For the practice's newsletter he proposed a quarterly P.S. offering members' friends and family a draw for a year of premium VIP service, with every entrant getting something.
detail: Low-pressure and a favour to the friend. A giveaway lets you state the full price openly, so there is no sticker shock; when entrants come in, one wins and the rest are offered the $5K or $10K tiers. He expects the quarterly draw alone to bring in a lot of business.

### YT-B-ogfFiQpXg#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: promotions-launches | also: sales-process | fw: Sales Aids | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:53
claim: The practice's main acquisition play: bring the scan machine to an existing local event every week, raffle a year of scans, and sell during the 20-minute scan with a VSL, closing right after.
detail: Target health, wealth or business events with older high-net-worth people, even cigar nights. Test 1: scan plus VSL, straight to a close. Test 2, if it feels rushed: book a later appointment with a VSL beforehand. Try many events; the ones that work go permanently into the annual calendar (e.g. as sponsor). Giving away one ~$20K package barely matters with 475 patients; marketing means giving things away and knowing the math. His math: 300 attendees, a third scanned, 25% closing gives ~25 sales, ~$2.5M LTV per event.

### YT-B-ogfFiQpXg#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: promotions-launches, paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:36
claim: For high-trust sales, in-person events and workshops work better than almost anything.
detail: His scalable version for the practice: Meta ads to a one-day or half-day health workshop with ~$500 of scans in the ticket, then in-person closes with a VSL between (ads, pitch one, schedule, pitch two). Charge something, e.g. $99, to filter out tyre-kickers and get a card on file, which eases the close. He likens it to diabetic dinners. Ads bring lower-quality leads to sift but reach the whole market.

### YT-B-ogfFiQpXg#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: verticals | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:55
claim: Physicians rarely scale practices because early time-for-money work makes them think in small numbers and a comfortable income makes them risk averse.
detail: He says the ratio of physicians to scaled practices is extreme compared with other fields. Typical blocks he sees: comfortable but not rich, which works like handcuffs; risk aversion; fear of marketing, selling and pricing; reluctance to invest in more education; holding work too close ("no one does it like me"); ego, surgeons worse. Hence a $55K agency loss or a $50K event sponsorship looks big when one event might return far more.
