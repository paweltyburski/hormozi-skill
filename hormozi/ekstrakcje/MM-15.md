### MM-15#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: promotions-launches | fw: Pay Less Now or Pay More Later | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A speed-reading trainer sold "double your reading speed in three hours or it's free": $0 down and $300 billed after, a $97 pay-now option, and an eight-week intensive pitched at the end.
detail: Registrants put a card down for $0 and were billed $300 afterward unless their speed hadn't doubled (email to cancel); attendance was required. The next page offered $97 now plus free recordings. He tested speed at the start and halfway, had people raise hands, and helped live those who hadn't improved. At the end he pitched an eight-week intensive. Revenue: $97 buyers, $300 later-payers, and the back end.
numbers: $0/$300 later; $97 now with recordings; 3-hour training; 8-week back end.

### MM-15#02 | DEFINITION | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: Pay Less Now or Pay More Later | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:04
claim: Pay Less Now or Pay More Later lets buyers pay full price later with an implied satisfaction guarantee, or a discounted price now with bonuses.
detail: Pay-later combines delayed payment with a satisfaction guarantee, removing the buyer's risk, so you can advertise "free" ($0 down). Almost anyone agrees to pay later if satisfied, so weak salespeople can sell it, and you get the card on file. Once they accept pay-later, offer pay-now with a hefty discount and bonuses; the card makes paying easy. Works online and in person. To exploit it fully, have something more, better or newer to sell next (00:03:49).

### MM-15#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: Pay Less Now or Pay More Later | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:19
claim: Make three offers in order: pay later (free today, full price afterwards), pay now (discount plus bonuses), then an upsell.
detail: (1) Pay later — present the core value as free; take a card, let them use it, and charge full price unless they qualify to cancel. (2) Pay now — the same thing at a third to half the price, with bonuses, in place of the guarantee. (3) Upsell — the larger back-end offer. Reading example (00:05:33): $0 then $299 afterwards; $97–149 now with recordings and notes; eight-week upsell.
numbers: Pay-now discount 20–50%, sometimes ~67%.

### MM-15#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics, pricing | fw: Pay Less Now or Pay More Later | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:36
claim: Get just enough pay-now buyers to cover acquisition cost, then let as many as possible take pay-later at about three times the price.
detail: The goal is balance, not pushing everyone one way. Set the pay-now discount to bring in the cash needed to break even. Pay-later money arriving within 30 days still counts, since it lands before your ad-spend card bill (00:06:19).
numbers: Pay-later ≈ 3× pay-now; break even within 30 days.

### MM-15#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: pricing, offers | fw: Pay Less Now or Pay More Later | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:36
claim: People agree to pay more later than now because a conditional, delayed payment prices in guaranteed delivery.
detail: Paying today carries the risk of not getting what was expected; "pay me only after you get a six-pack" ties price to the outcome, so you can charge far more. The discount and bonuses needed to move people to pay now reveal what delay and guarantee are worth to them (00:02:50).

### MM-15#10 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: offers, retention-ltv | fw: Pay Less Now or Pay More Later | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:44
claim: Promise a simple yes/no result you can deliver within the time frame, measured before and during, so it's easy to track and hard to refute.
detail: E.g., "I found my first deal", "my reading speed doubled". If you can't deliver in the window they'll ask not to be billed. Measure baseline and progress — for shoulder pain, have them rate it before and after. Objective measures matter more the bigger the pay-now/pay-later gap, since some will take the value and try not to pay.

### MM-15#11 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: Conditional Guarantee | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:31
claim: Make the guarantee conditional: customers can cancel the later bill only if they did what creates the value.
detail: The reading course required attendance; miss the workshop and you can't claim. Use attendance, appointments or submitted data — ideally what produces results — which is easy to justify: "you won't double your speed unless you show up."

### MM-15#12 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: pricing, offers | fw: Pay Less Now or Pay More Later | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:17
claim: Balance the options by adjusting pay-now incentives, and expect about 10% of pay-later buyers to cancel — more signals a problem.
detail: Too many choose pay-later → deepen the pay-now discount and/or improve bonuses; too many pay now → the reverse. Some cancel regardless; treat ~10% as a cost of business. Above that (e.g., 20 of 100): you promised too much, conditions are too lenient, the price is too high, or you're selling to the wrong people or those who can't afford it (00:15:35).
numbers: ~10% expected cancellations.

### MM-15#13 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv, pricing | fw: Pay Less Now or Pay More Later | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:02
claim: Subscriptions: free trial at the higher rate, then a thank-you-page offer to skip the trial and lock in a lower rate for life.
detail: Pay-later is a free trial converting at the full rate after 30 days; pay-now is a lower rate today kept forever, plus bonuses. You still advertise a free trial while the thank-you offer liquidates acquisition cost. Continuity lessons expand on this.
numbers: $400/month trial rate vs $300/month locked for life.

### MM-15#14 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: sales-process, promotions-launches | fw: Pay Less Now or Pay More Later | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:49
claim: At events and consults, seed your next offer early by mentioning what "everyone" wants to buy, without pitching the room.
detail: The trainer could have opened: "Everyone wants to know when my next intensive starts — I'll cover it at the end; first let's double your speed." Hormozi found this by accident in nutrition consults ("everybody wants to know which supplements to buy — we'll get there"); framing it about others implies everyone buys, and more people bought when asked. He then did it deliberately.

### MM-15#15 | SCRIPT | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Pay Less Now or Pay More Later | novelty: canon | ts: 00:13:34
claim: Get agreement to pay-later terms and the card first, then offer the pay-now upgrade as an afterthought.
detail: In person, present both after the estimate; online, use the thank-you page; by phone, take the card for the trial first. Then: "Oh, I forgot — want to save $200?" Explain that people who skip the trial get VIP seating and recordings for $200 less, and those bonuses won't be offered again. "Want to do that?" Better bonuses mean more yeses; pay-now buyers get discount and bonuses instead of the guarantee.

### MM-15#16 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Pay Less Now or Pay More Later | novelty: canon | ts: 00:15:47
claim: During delivery, give extra attention to anyone who hasn't yet got the result — fixing it live prevents later cancellations.
detail: The reading trainer worked personally with those who hadn't doubled their speed. Tipping them over the line is usually easy, especially in person, and saves a lot in cancellations.
