### POD-0101#01 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:20
claim: At about $250,000-$300,000 a month from a kitchen table, a peer said the results implied a $100 million business and told him to push harder.
detail: The company was three people at roughly 90% margin when he presented a plan for a supplement company at a mastermind. A top affiliate marketer said that if the gym results were real, a $100 million business was sitting there, and that someone bigger would take everything unless he lean in when it felt easy. Monthly revenue then rose each month over the next ones.
numbers: $250,000-$300,000 per month at start; then 475, 750, 1,000, 1,300, 1,500 (thousands per month, presumably revenue).

### POD-0101#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:26
claim: When a campaign starts working, double down instead of coasting once the month is covered.
detail: He compares it to a top investor putting everything into outsized returns. Assume someone is trying to take your position, and prepare for coming market changes. Use outsized returns from a winning channel to fund weak areas such as missing leadership.

### POD-0101#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:52
claim: Self-induced pressure (bills) creates limited urgency; market-induced pressure creates constant urgency.
detail: Staying focused on the customers' unsolved problems keeps a sense of inadequacy that drives pace. He asks his team why things are not faster or why some people have problems. Related: income limits come from beliefs about what you deserve, and his wife's line is that success is only earned, never deserved.
