### POD-0585#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Puddles-Ponds-Lakes-Oceans | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:19
claim: Niche advice is right when starting, but ask about total addressable market once you are growing; Hormozi saw he was in a pond while peers had oceans.
detail: At a meetup of eight owners with about half a billion in combined revenue he asked whether skills were his gap and concluded it was market size. Two paths existed: bigger chain gyms or down-market personal trainers. He also wanted to stop being known as the gym guy.
numbers: Eight owners, at least $500M combined yearly revenue; 50,000 micro gyms in the US, about 20,000 already contacted when he stepped down.

### POD-0585#02 | CASE | topic: content-brand | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:41
claim: After he switched his podcast and videos from gyms to general business in September 2019, his audience started growing, even though he had posted for two to three years.
detail: First YouTube videos came about a year later. The general business market is far bigger than the gym market. Staying could have given deeper expertise as an edge.

### POD-0585#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:31
claim: Choose a business with a compounding mechanism; his lesson from Gym Launch was that media compounds while a call force requires adding people.
detail: He could have built 1,000 cold callers, but the same effort in media yields more. An audience grows by a percent each month, so a larger base adds more people for the same rate.
numbers: About 10% more people find him each month (as stated).

### POD-0585#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:53
claim: He decided to quit consulting after weighing an MBA against using his savings to start a business and learn from it.
detail: At 22 he compared an MBA (two years of lost income, about $120,000 debt; median graduate pay about $120,000) with using his $50,000 savings to build a business to $10,000 a month. Reading 20 self-help books left life unchanged and some contradicted each other. It took six months between wanting to quit and quitting.
numbers: MBA debt about $120,000; savings $50,000; target $10,000/month; 6 months deciding.
### POD-0585#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:35
claim: His GMAT score rose from about 630 to 730-740 by doing practice problems four hours a day for 16 weeks, after a study showed score tracks problems done.
detail: He used it as a model of input-to-output, which suggested a test-prep business idea; a partner took his prepared material, so he chose fitness. A first paid $100 nutrition lunch showed him people pay for his knowledge.
numbers: 4 hours/day x 16 weeks; score about +100 points.

### POD-0585#06 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:15:36

detail: He gave up a white-collar track toward $100,000+ for a trainer role at about $12 an hour. His view: starting something new often needs a step back, and learning is worth more than short-term pay.

### POD-0585#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:18:00
claim: Hormozi recommends offering free work to someone only one or two steps ahead of you, because they probably have a small supply of free helpers.
detail: Top figures like Elon do not need unskilled free labour, and Hormozi says he probably gets about 50 such offers a day. Your time is not valuable to them just because it is cheap; you must provide value.
numbers: Roughly 50 free-work requests per day (his estimate: 'probably').
### POD-0585#08 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: content-brand | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:38
claim: Do the useful work first without asking, for example edit and send clips of their podcast daily, rather than offering to work for free.
detail: An offer makes them manage you, whereas action shows initiative, skill and the ability to see a need. After 10, 50 or 100 days straight the person notices. Make an offer so good they feel foolish refusing.
numbers: Persist 10, 50, 100 days.

### POD-0585#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:22
claim: A senior person's time has an opportunity cost, so approach someone's lieutenant first and trade up step by step.
detail: The host's illustration: if an hour of his time is worth $25,000-35,000, an hour a week needs about $1M a year of value to break even and about $10M for a return. Prove yourself to a leader's team, then get closer.
numbers: Example hour valued at $25,000-$35,000; $1M a year to break even; $10M for a return (host's figures).

### POD-0585#10 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:23:16
claim: To find what to offer, do more or better of what they already do, or fill gaps they ignore, and show it instead of saying it.
detail: Examples: clips for a podcaster; turning his existing material into emails for a list that gets about 1,000 new sign-ups a day but only two emails a year; running a platform he skips (Pinterest); pre-vetted deals for an investor. Do not criticise their work from a small account.
numbers: About 1,000 sign-ups/day; about 2 emails in the last year.
