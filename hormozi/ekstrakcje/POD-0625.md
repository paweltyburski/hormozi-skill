### POD-0625#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:13
claim: About 80% of the investment decision weighs the founder, because the market and model can be changed but the founder cannot.
detail: Three elements are looked at: who is served, how the business is structured and who the founder is. Basic filters: services, education or coaching type business, US based, at least $3M revenue a year, and a market he does not dislike.
numbers: 80% weight on the founder; minimum $3M annual revenue.
applies_to: Acquisition.com investment screening.

### POD-0625#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:00
claim: A founder is made of beliefs, skills and traits, and only traits are hard to change from outside.
detail: Limiting beliefs (such as "we can't charge more") can be broken, and missing skills can be added through team members. Founder traits are where most of his focus falls when evaluating a business, since they are very difficult to change.
### POD-0625#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:04
claim: Any sign of ego or posturing is an immediate pass and is the most common reason they reject founders.
detail: He accepts that entrepreneurs need confidence, but even a hint of posturing or takeaway selling ends the conversation, even if the founder is successful.

### POD-0625#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:04
claim: Lack of focus, at macro level (many businesses) or micro level (wandering on a call), disqualifies a founder.
detail: Building something big takes years, so he looks for discipline, which means the ability to say no. A founder with six businesses is a no; so is one who cannot hold a train of thought in a call, since communication would be impossible and implementing change very difficult. Fix: remove items from your calendar and say no to future ones.
### POD-0625#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:17
claim: He declines founders who are emotionally reactive because he would end up as a therapist rather than an advisor.
detail: Swings between hot and cold days also make it hard to build a team. Poor focus and reactivity often travel together, but not always.

### POD-0625#06 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:06
claim: In diligence he looks for strife in the leadership group; unsupportive spouses are usually a problem, a resentful co-founder with smaller equity could be a problem, and a very complex cap table can be one too.
detail: Interpersonal drama, misalignment or resentment is usually a red flag, and a cap table with many voices means it will be a pain to get things done. It is not impossible to work with, just not what they choose.
### POD-0625#07 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:14
claim: A founder praised his operator as the best in the world, but a call with the operator showed a train wreck, which made the founder's judgment suspect.
detail: A poor judge of people makes it hard to hire for scale. His cure: lean on people with good track records, learn what good looks like and have several people test your hypothesis about a hire.

### POD-0625#08 | EXAMPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:03
claim: His wife is a far better judge of people and sensed a well-performing portfolio company was hiding something, which proved true.
detail: After the call she said they were not telling something, and a major issue emerged; the founder was embarrassed. He admits his own early picks (partners, teammates, over-trusting) were bad and that she balances this.

### POD-0625#09 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:50
claim: To reduce ego, recall how wrong you were five years ago and aim to be less wrong rather than more right.
detail: Talk less and listen more; imagine your future self in ten years thinking you were a fool.

### POD-0625#10 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:50
claim: To control reactivity, reframe to life-and-death, never decide while emotional and lengthen the gap between stimulus and response.
detail: Ask "will I die?" then return to real scale. When angry he avoids talking to teammates and sleeps on it. An hour or two rarely matters in business, but what you say lasts.

### POD-0625#11 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:28
claim: Fix founder dynamics by defining roles with little overlap, confirming everyone is comfortable with pay, and airing interpersonal issues.
detail: These are the clean-ups he expects before investing.
