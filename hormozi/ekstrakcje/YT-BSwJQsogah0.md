### YT-BSwJQsogah0#01 | TACTIC | topic: scaling-constraints | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:47
claim: Break an annual revenue goal into a precise weekly target, because fuzzy targets are not hit and weeks are steadier than days.
detail: $1M a year is about $83K a month, $21K a week and a little over $3K a day. Daily numbers are too volatile until roughly $10M a year, so work in weeks when starting out.
numbers: $1M/year = ~$83K/month = ~$21K/week = ~$3K+/day.

### YT-BSwJQsogah0#02 | DEFINITION | topic: unit-economics | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:33
claim: LTV is total revenue per customer over their life, so a low-priced product can have a high LTV by repeat purchase.
detail: Three ways to reach $1,000 LTV: a $1,000 course with nothing else; a $100/month gym membership kept 10 months; skincare at $250 per order for four months. He cites Starbucks as roughly $8,000 LTV per customer over a decade.
numbers: $1,000 LTV examples; Starbucks ~$8,000 LTV (unclear in audio, said as "I think").

### YT-BSwJQsogah0#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: sales-process | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:41
claim: Back-solve for a $1M goal at $1,000 LTV: 21 deals a week means about 5 closed a day, which at a 1-in-3 close rate means 15 calls, and at a 66% show rate 22 booked appointments.
detail: Round up to 5 so you overshoot. At about 50 outreach attempts per appointment that is roughly 1,000 outreach a day. He concludes a $1,000 deal does not justify that hand-to-hand effort and the price should be nearer $10,000.
numbers: 21 deals/week; 5 deals/day; close 1 in 3; show rate 66%; 50 outreach per appointment; ~1,000 outreach/day.

### YT-BSwJQsogah0#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:53
claim: At the same $1M goal, deal size sets the workload: about nine $10K deals, one $100K deal, or roughly 83 $1K deals a month, but around 830 $100 sales.
detail: The lower the price, the harder the goal. He suggests starting with premium services in the $5,000 to $15,000 range and over-delivering, because low-priced volume needs operational skill with large numbers of people, a different skill from marketing and sales.
numbers: $83K/month in $10K, $100K, $1K or $100 units.

### YT-BSwJQsogah0#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:45
claim: Very few people make real money on units below about $1,000 of lifetime value, because acquiring customers is too hard without a high LTV.
detail: The ticket price can stay low if customers are worth more over time, for example through habit-forming, sticky, repeat products.
numbers: ~$1,000 lifetime value as the practical floor.
