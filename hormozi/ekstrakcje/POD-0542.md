### POD-0542#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:51
claim: Do the time math on chores: if the extra cost of buying food instead of cooking is smaller than about 25% of your income, outsourcing makes sense.
detail: Home cooking meant shopping, prep, cooking, boxing, cleaning, and cooking twice a week for one person. He says for most people this is about 10 hours a week, a quarter of a work week, and compared the extra cost of eating out with 25% of income; if the extra cost exceeds that, he says he shouldn't do it. He knew a day of working leads earned an extra grand.
numbers: About 10 hours a week for most people; threshold 25% of income; example wage at $15/hour or more.
### POD-0542#05 | DEFINITION | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:28:49
claim: Leverage is the gap between input and output; keep trading tasks yielding 1:2 for ones yielding 1:20, ideally solving for zero time and energy.
detail: If someone else can do the work for less than all the equity or income, trade up. The result is checks arriving while time stays free for the next business or other interests.
numbers: Low leverage 1:1; higher 1:10; example trade from 1:2 to 1:20.

Advice: either hire and train people or buy a machine that matches his quality and run a leather company, or raise prices because demand exceeds his time, which lifts profit while staying craft-based. The craft path is unlikely to reach a billion but can make a few million dollars a year.
