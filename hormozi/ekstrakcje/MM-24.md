### MM-24#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Gym Launch chose its HR platform because the vendor charged $500 and rebated it if Layla attended the onboarding trainings — and then kept the account for years.
detail: The platforms were roughly equal. The vendor's deal: pay $500, attend two or three training sessions, get it back; skip them and they keep it. Layla attended, learned the software and had no wish to learn another, so the company stayed for years. Hormozi adapted the structure into the trial with penalty.
numbers: $500 charge, rebated after 2–3 training sessions.

### MM-24#02 | DEFINITION | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:45
claim: In a trial with penalty, customers get the product free as long as they meet your terms and pay only if they don't — the mirror image of the win-your-money-back attraction offer.
detail: Win-your-money-back: pay first, earn it back by meeting terms. Trial with penalty: same actions and criteria, but the incentive is avoiding fees. It is not "try it and see if you like it" but "free as long as you do what makes you a perfect fit for my next offer; if not, you pay." Either way you win: non-compliers pay, compliers are likely to buy next. "Trial with penalty" is an internal name only.

### MM-24#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:19
claim: Illustration: closing 3 of 10 on the attraction offer, downselling 4 more to a trial and upselling 3 of those afterwards doubles customers from 3 to 6.
detail: The trial converts people who would otherwise leave with nothing. Hormozi's sales rule: every qualified prospect buys something — only the terms change.
numbers: 3/10 closed → +4 trials → +3 upsold = 6 customers (2x).

### MM-24#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: paid-ads | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:19
claim: Hormozi prefers to present the trial with penalty after a prospect has already refused to pay, rather than advertise it as a front end — though advertising it is a valid choice.
detail: The HR vendor advertised it to get customers; it can work as a front end. Offered last, it stays a conditional concession rather than an expectation. Getting people to put skin in the game is also the way to actually help them.

### MM-24#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:21
claim: Tie trial terms to the actions that produce results, plus showing up to sales consults and advertising you — customers who get results want to buy.
detail: Reuse the win-your-money-back criteria: advertise the business, attend sales consultations, do the result-producing work (Hormozi forgot the fourth). Fees attach to non-consumption and missed consults. People come to solve a problem, not to trial; taking action is what solves it.

### MM-24#06 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: money-models | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:21
claim: When someone won't commit to the trial terms, tie the terms to the outcome they want.
detail: Sequence: ask whether they want the outcome (yes) → doing these things is how the outcome happens, so without them they never get it → this is the only way you can give the whole service free → if they really want it they'll follow through and pay nothing; the fees just add incentive → if this doesn't work for them, nothing else will.

### MM-24#07 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:25
claim: Trial-with-penalty terms for consumer, business and therapy offers.
detail: B2C "28-day kick-the-habit blueprint": attend all consulting calls, post progress in the group weekly, journal daily in the app, attend feedback and transformation sessions (the upsell points). B2B "5-day first five clients challenge": 100 outbound messages a day, report stats, attend daily training, post in the group daily after homework, attend the graduation call (upsell). Therapy "28-day breakthrough": do homework before each session and show up; $50 non-compliance fee per missed homework or session, explained as "without the homework we can't help you in the session."
numbers: 100 outbound messages/day; $50 fee per miss (therapy).

### MM-24#08 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:30
claim: Size fees so they add up to what the win-your-money-back offer would have cost, weighting the most important actions higher.
detail: Ten required actions on a $500 offer = $50 each. Weight by importance to the business and to their results: e.g., sales consultations $100–150, minor items $25.
numbers: $500 / 10 actions = $50; consults $100–150; minor $25.

### MM-24#09 | FRAMEWORK | topic: money-models | also: sales-process, objections-closing | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:55
claim: Hormozi's process for selling a trial with penalty: offer it last, always get the card, sell staying and paying, sell consumption commitments, make check-ins required — then upsell by trial outcome.
detail: 1) Offer the trial only after a clear no to the first offer. 2) Take ID and credit card. 3) Get agreement they will stay long-term if it works. 4) After the card, explain the fees and have them initial each. 5) Explain each mandatory check-in and its purpose. Afterwards, upsell whether they like it, hate it or don't use it. Each step is detailed in later atoms. This beats "leave a card and hope".

### MM-24#10 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:55
claim: Present the trial as a relaxed favour and jump to logistics: request their ID, which makes them take out the wallet, then swap the ID for a card.
detail: Opening: "That sure is a pickle. Tell you what: let's begin with a free start, and you stay on only if you like it. Let me get your ID." With the wallet already out, they can't say they forgot it. Complete the contract yourself: no awkward staring, space for small talk, no form errors. Keep the ID as collateral, point at the wallet: "I'll trade you for whatever card you want to use." Type in the card number yourself to avoid typos — crucial when a separate finance team handles charges later. On Zoom or phone, they show the ID and card to the camera.

### MM-24#11 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: - | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:38
claim: Ask for the card "just to complete your account profile"; if they say it was supposed to be free, agree and say this is how it has always been done.
detail: Early on Hormozi lost many 14-day trials when prospects offered to give their card after the trial. The fix: "What card can I put down so I can complete your account profile?" If they balk: "Oh, a hundred percent, it is free — this is how we've always done it," and shrug. Appealing to an unseen policy-maker almost always gets the card; after adopting it he says he practically never missed a trial again.

### MM-24#12 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: retention-ltv | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:41
claim: Sell staying and paying before starting the trial: ask directly whether they will stay long-term if the programme gets them the result, and don't start them if not.
detail: Leverage has shifted — you're now giving work for free, so it's a takeaway sale. Say you'll only extend this if they do some things for you, then ask the stay question. If no, don't start them (unless you advertised a free trial). Set honest expectations: they won't reach long-term goals in the trial but will build habits; you "can't ethically promise" a quick fix. That gives you the moral high ground. If you can't sell staying when it's free, work on your sales or accept they're a poor prospect.

### MM-24#13 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: money-models | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:16
claim: Explain the fees only after you have the card, framed as a bet on themselves that keeps the trial free, and have them initial each fee.
detail: Moves: "We'll do our part as long as you do yours — fair?" → skipping hurts results, so a small ding keeps them on track → follow through and it's all free → "the best way to get you results and still keep it free — the best of both worlds." Before the card, fees trigger far more questions; after it, with a "how we've always done it" attitude, most agree. Keep fees few: sales consults, the key result action, and advertising for you (posts, a testimonial) — if you give for free, get something back.

### MM-24#14 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:13:46
claim: Present the mandatory check-ins (really the sales consults) by their benefit to the customer and never call them sales meetings.
detail: Walk through each: "The first we do X so that you can…, the second Y…, the third Z…; we charge if you miss them because it's the only way to get your results." Gym example: nutrition session to fix food, progress meeting to adjust and keep results coming, transformation meeting for after-pictures. Little to object to.

### MM-24#15 | FRAMEWORK | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv, objections-closing | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:13:46
claim: Every trial ends one of three ways — they like it, hate it or don't use it — and each has its own upsell.
detail: Like it: they're already on automatic billing, but still meet them mid- or end-trial (transformation meeting) and offer the longer-term or higher-value version; pitch upgrades at the first and second consults too — they lacked trust at first, and successful customers gain more from better offers. Hate it: apologise and sell the better-fit offer (next atom). Didn't use it: reach out several times, require a meeting, offer to waive the fees if they get back on track, or offer something better.

### MM-24#16 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: retention-ltv | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:14:59
claim: When a trial customer hates it, take all the blame, then sell them the higher-level offer — about half buy.
detail: Ask what they would have liked to be different. Tell them they're right and you're angry at yourself; never blame them — only one person can be angry and it must be you. Ask for a chance to make it up because you're outraged at their experience. Since you now understand their needs, offer the higher-level thing: "That's on me, I should have listened and offered you this."
numbers: ~50% of unhappy trial users buy the higher-level offer.

### MM-24#17 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:16:30
claim: Hormozi doesn't bill non-starters — he uses the fees as leverage to bring them back, and bills only after repeated misses if at all.
detail: Charging people who never started is a reliable way to earn one-star reviews; a small fee isn't worth it. Lifetime value comes from getting them back on track. Let people make up for goofs: after a miss, reach out and offer to waive the fee if they return. If they miss again you are justified in billing, though he usually doesn't.

### MM-24#18 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:16:30
claim: Tune the trial by where it breaks: no takers → lower requirements; takers who don't follow through → sell the fees and meetings harder; no back-end ascension → re-emphasise staying and paying.
detail: For follow-through, highlight the meetings as mandatory, walk through each initialled line out loud and get a nod rather than letting them read. Case: when Hormozi saw reps skipping the "if we get you results, will you stay?" question, ascensions fell; adding it back to the script made far more trials ascend.

### MM-24#19 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:17:56
claim: Never say "penalty" to customers — call it a trial done in a special way that gets people results.
detail: Nobody wants to be penalised, and the term confuses people. If they ask why it works this way: "That's how we've always done it."

### MM-24#20 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:17:56
claim: Use pay-less-now-or-pay-more-later as the downsell for physical products and one-time services, and trial with penalty for recurring products and services where the customer must do work to get results.
detail: Hormozi has only made trial with penalty work when results depend on customer effort — common in services and software. He has not seen it work where customers do nothing.

### MM-24#21 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:18:48
claim: If asking for a card on a free trial feels awkward, charge a token amount up front — it justifies the card and confirms it works before recurring billing.
detail: First month for $1 (or $10 down), then the normal monthly rate. Hormozi's view: anyone with sales skill should be able to close a free trial with a card anyway.
numbers: $1 first month or $10 down.

### MM-24#22 | DEFINITION | topic: money-models | also: - | fw: Rebate | novelty: canon | ts: 00:18:48
claim: A rebate is a smaller-scope win-your-money-back: they pay up front and get the money back (or credited to their service) after completing a few simple actions.
detail: Simple trial: card taken, billed later. Trial with penalty: card taken, billed later, plus fees for missed activation steps. Rebate: paid now, returned on completion — and the card is proven because it was charged. Best for clear, small requirements, like the HR vendor's $500 for attending two or three webinars.

### MM-24#23 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:19:54
claim: Charge small fees per missed item rather than the full price for any miss — make money from results and conversions, not by nickel-and-diming.
detail: A lump "miss anything, pay the full boat" rule makes people quit after one miss ("why even try now?"). Per-item dings (e.g., $50 each) keep them engaged.

### MM-24#24 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: canon | ts: 00:20:40
claim: Trials with penalty convert at the same or much higher rates on the back end than plain trials and keep goodwill, because you visibly try to help.
detail: Use mid-trial check-ins to make offers: more of what they love, a swap if they have problems, a make-up chance if they aren't using it. A plain no-strings trial can follow as a further downsell, but by then the person is probably just trying to leave. Everyone buys something.
