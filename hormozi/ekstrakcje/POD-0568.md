### POD-0568#01 | CASE | topic: content-brand | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:46
claim: A podcaster who made one ask, earned well, and then asked constantly saw returns and listenership fall until he stopped.
detail: He drew the wrong lesson (asking pays), so he asked more. Each ask earned less, the audience shrank, and eventually asking no longer felt worth it because the goodwill was gone.

### POD-0568#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: content-brand | also: paid-ads | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:41
claim: Big media sets the ceiling on ask frequency: television runs 13 minutes of ads per 47 of content; Facebook shows one ad per four posts.
detail: He says the biggest media companies want to ask as much as possible without losing their audience, so this ratio has been studied heavily.
numbers: TV 13:47 minutes (about 1 : 3.6); Facebook 1 ad : 4 posts.
### POD-0568#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: other | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:15
claim: Free media lets one person bank goodwill with millions of people and later collect on it, because help creates a felt debt.
detail: Public giving also keeps reinforcing the brand and forces content quality to stay high.

### POD-0568#04 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: outreach | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:00
claim: A newcomer should open with a "declaration of business" post to the existing personal following, offering something free.
detail: Content: you may know me from school, work or socially; this is what I do now; I would like to give you something that helps you; if not, keep following. People who have watched your ordinary posts have already given you some goodwill, and a quick positive response encourages continuing.
applies_to: Beginners with an existing personal social media following.

### POD-0568#05 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:12:36
claim: Making a bonus available only to live event attendees trains the audience to show up next time.
detail: Access to a long-built project is given only to people present live (tracked by scraping attendee lists). He accepts annoying the ~99% who miss it so the next announced giveaway is taken seriously.
numbers: ~99% of audience expected not to attend (his estimate).
