### POD-231215-01#10 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:36:11
claim: Ben (Managing Director of Business Development), in a presentation at the team meetup, lists five behavioural biases: status quo, groupthink, confirmation, peak-end and hindsight, with mitigations for most.
detail: Per Ben: Status quo: ask which option is the status quo and ensure other options exist. Groupthink: reward debate and seek a view from outside the group. Confirmation: force yourself to gather opinions that make you uncomfortable. Peak-end: people judge an experience by its emotional peak and its end. Hindsight: document decisions and write a hypothesis.

### POD-231215-01#13 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:42:18
claim: Respond to people's bids for attention and accept compliments, because poor reactions teach people to stop giving them.
detail: People speak up hoping for a reaction; if none arrives, they stop. Rejecting praise eventually means receiving none, and you may wrongly conclude you are not good. Busy leaders skip responding to messages and miss chances to answer bids.

### POD-231215-01#14 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:43:55
claim: Sheila (managing director of people), in her presentation, says hard conversations are about 70% self-awareness and 30% delivery, and describes types: what happened, intention versus impact, and identity.
detail: Per Sheila: in what-happened conversations each side arrives with own facts. Make the conversation about impact, since focusing on intention leads to distrust. For identity, acknowledge the stakes and say this does not define either person. She says it is a skill you can develop.
numbers: Per Sheila: ~70% of hard conversations is self-awareness; ~30% is delivery.
### POD-231215-01#15 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:45:41
claim: Sheila (managing director of people) and the discussion at the meetup: set the goal of a hard conversation first, and aim at a solution, not at punishing the person; the later the feedback, the more specific it must be.
detail: Vague remarks such as "you've been kind of slacking" do not change behaviour; listing the facts is irrefutable. Latency and specificity are inverted curves: the sooner, the more general it can be; the later, the more specific. In the book-launch vendor example Leila described, the goal was to get the vendor to accept some responsibility, not really to change his behaviour.
