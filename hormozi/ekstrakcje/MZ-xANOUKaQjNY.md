### MZ-xANOUKaQjNY#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:35
claim: Enterprise value rests on three levers: number of customers, lifetime gross profit per customer, and a divisor for risk.
detail: Value to an investor is all future cash flows discounted to the present. The owner can move only three things: more customers, more lifetime gross profit from each, and lower risk (how likely the cash flow continues). Together they make enterprise value (EV). He promises ten items that each unlock a further level of EV.

### MZ-xANOUKaQjNY#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:35
claim: If everything else stays equal, multiplying customers by ten multiplies company value by ten, sometimes more.
detail: Customer count scales value linearly at least; the other two levers (profit per customer, risk) multiply on top of it.
numbers: 10x customers gives 10x value, all else equal.

### MZ-xANOUKaQjNY#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: - | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:33
claim: Lifetime revenue and lifetime gross profit can differ by an order of magnitude, and only the second counts for value.
detail: Meal business selling $10 meals, 10 meals per customer on average, gives $100 lifetime revenue. With only $1 earned per meal, lifetime gross profit is $10. To earn more, get more customers or make each worth more.
numbers: $100 lifetime revenue vs $10 lifetime gross profit per customer.

### MZ-xANOUKaQjNY#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Growing from $0 to $4.4M a month in 20 months was chaotic because he and his wife personally ran every department.
detail: They hired, fired, trained and onboarded while serving more customers than they could handle. Whenever customer numbers dipped he returned to making ads and content himself, feeling like a performer on call. He hit a wall and was the biggest company in every mastermind he visited, so nobody could show him the next step.
numbers: $0 to $4.4M per month in 20 months.
