### POD-0056#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:10
claim: Smaller rivals attack the leader to borrow attention; the leader ignores them.
detail: Pepsi advertises against Coke while Coke never names Pepsi, and Apple mocked PCs until it grew bigger and stopped. Bashing is a normal consequence of being visible. Smart competitors copy the leader and others attack, but attackers align themselves with a lie when results are public.

### POD-0056#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:07
claim: Answer attacks by focusing on customers' problems; if you want, also post social proof that isn't specifically bashing, and let the proof talk.
detail: He posted a video of gyms that grew their monthly recurring (EFT) payments rather than respond to critics. Bashing triggers the ego; looking at customers' problems leaves no attention for it. When doubting a competitor, he hints that three old testimonials may mean they are maybe not the best choice, and that someone selling scale without being scaled is a warning sign.
### POD-0056#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:44
claim: He contrasted a critic's yearly goal with his own weekly sign-ups to show scale difference.
detail: A rival coach would work all year for 15 clients at $1,000 a month, while his company signed 25 in a week. Most critics earned about a quarter million a year, which his company made in two days. He posted a video of every gym that had tripled its EFT.
numbers: 15 clients at $1,000/month; 25 sign-ups in a week; ~$250,000 a year versus two days; EFT tripled (no other figures stated).
### POD-0056#04 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:38
claim: He let a fitness coach's wife into his program out of sympathy, and the couple later publicly attacked him.
detail: She was in tears about her gym closing, so he agreed. After the program saved their gym they turned on him. He says the first fault was his: he had a hunch when he accepted, and should have trusted it.
