### POD-0023#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:48
claim: The first hire is a trainer for afternoon sessions, because sales appointments arrive then and selling is the owner's best use of time.
detail: Math: three afternoon sessions a day for five days is 15 sessions at about $20 each, so about $300 a week. Selling two $500 programmes a week brings $1,000, leaving about $700 profit on the hire.
numbers: 15 sessions x $20 = $300/week cost vs 2 sales x $500 = $1,000.
applies_to: Owner-operated gyms

### POD-0023#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:30
claim: Second, hire for morning sessions (another $300 a week), so you stop training entirely.
detail: Total about $600 a week, $2,400 a month for three morning and three afternoon sessions. It also saves you from 4 a.m. starts. Your time is worth more than $15 to $20 an hour.
numbers: $300 + $300 = $600/week; $2,400/month.

### POD-0023#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:16
claim: Third and fourth hires are administrative assistants, or a sales manager as the flexible fourth, giving two assistants plus one sales manager as the locked-in location.
detail: If campaigns are running actively, assistants help make calls; if not, they handle billing, comments and messages, freeing you to learn lead generation. With a sales manager as the fourth hire, you will probably take up the spillover admin.
numbers: 2 admin assistants + 1 sales manager.
### POD-0023#04 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:11
claim: Hand off selling last, trading money for time, and ideally to a salesperson as good as you.
detail: Such salespeople exist but are uncommon; keep them busy. Then the owner checks daily that sales are made, leads called, service delivered, declines picked up.
