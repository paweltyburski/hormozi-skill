### POD-0069#01 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:32
claim: Answer "that's a lot" by agreeing, asking about the most expensive thing they own, and dividing its price by uses or hours.
detail: Steps: agree it is a lot; ask what the most expensive item they own is (excluding a wedding dress); ask how often worn and for how long owned; compute price per wear; then compare with the program, as the body is worn every day, makes the whole wardrobe look better, and costs less than a meal per day.
numbers: example: $1,000 heels worn about 10 times = $100 per wear; about 27 hours = roughly $40 per hour; program cost $2,500 versus a $100 wear (the per-day figure "eight" is unclear in audio).

### POD-0069#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: Virtuous Cycle of Price | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:54
claim: Do not deny prospects the pleasure of an expensive purchase because of your own insecurity about price.
detail: People enjoy buying costly things they care about, with excitement that rises with price. The seller's doubt about worth should not rob them, and the extra money funds better fulfilment.

### POD-0069#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:50
claim: Ask candidly: what was the last big thing you bought, and how often do you use it?
detail: Works for prospects who struggle with priorities; compare to something they understand, such as shoes, a purse or a dress.
