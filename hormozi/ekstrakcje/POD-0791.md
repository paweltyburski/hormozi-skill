### POD-0791#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: AAA | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: The best closers ask for the sale the most times, so the aim is to ask repeatedly without losing rapport.
detail: Asking often sounds pushy, so something must protect the relationship; AAA is that protection after each obstacle (any reply that is not a yes once price is presented).

### POD-0791#02 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: AAA | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:18
claim: Acknowledging first builds rapport and buys a few seconds to think.
detail: Repeat back what they said without necessarily agreeing. Jumping straight into an overcome sounds like a canned sales response and costs rapport.

### POD-0791#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: AAA | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:04
claim: The associate step has three types: generic positive (lightest), a foil (fictitious look-alike), or appeal to authority.
detail: Lightest: generic praise ("smart question"). Second: a foil, someone just like the prospect who raised the same concern and then bought or got top results; he says this works for any type of overcome. Last: appeal to authority, if the rep has status: "I used to believe that too, a mentor told me something."
### POD-0791#04 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: AAA | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:34
claim: A foil lets you say the hard truth to a made-up person next to the prospect, not to the prospect.
detail: Ask permission to share what you told "Janet". For "it's a lot of money": it is good it hurts, because cheap purchases failed before as losing them cost nothing, so the pain means commitment. Then ask if they doubt they would hit the goal.

### POD-0791#05 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-team | fw: AAA | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:06
claim: A young or low-status rep should borrow authority from the boss instead of their own beliefs.
detail: An 18-year-old selling to 40-year-old owners cannot say "I used to believe". Say "funny you say that, Alex told me something this morning, mind if I share it?" The rep is only the messenger. After the story, ask for the sale again.

### POD-0791#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:06
claim: Never answer a prospect's question unless you know the answer is acceptable; answer with a question.
detail: If you do not know the right answer, it is the wrong one. Answering lets the prospect judge you, and whoever asks the questions controls the sale. Reply "how many were you looking for?", "which ones?" or "why?". If their figure matches what you offer, confirm and ask them to start.
applies_to: Questions such as weekly calls included or number of trainer certifications.

### POD-0791#07 | DEFINITION | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: updates | ts: 00:08:26
claim: Feature objections are preference objections: the buyer wants the outcome their own way.
detail: Hormozi renames what he used to call fit objections. "No calls with you", "no email support", "no PDF" all mean a preconceived route that the product does not match.

### POD-0791#08 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:11
claim: Handle a preference objection by asking the intention behind it, then testing your answer before giving it.
detail: Never say "none". Ask "what specifically would you ask him?" or "why do you want that?". Then: "if we had X, Y and Z already tested, would that solve it?" On a yes, ask them to start. Since the answer is accepted in advance, nobody disagrees.

### POD-0791#09 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:09
claim: Once the intention is known, show how existing features reach it better than their requested way.
detail: Example, accountability: after permission, offer three ways the product already delivers it, noting you first built it their way and it failed to produce the outcome. Never disagree with a prospect; keep asking until they say the right answer themselves.
