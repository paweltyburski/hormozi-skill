### LS-X39rITUMEPA#01 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:23
claim: Question every requirement in your process with three follow-ups: what does that mean, how do you know that, and so what.
detail: Applied to content production, the team asked who decided videos must be colour graded. His best videos were not graded and some graded ones flopped, so colour grading was removed from the process; then the question became what else can be cut. Hormozi says the three questions are his own, not Elon Musk's.

### LS-X39rITUMEPA#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: sales-process | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:10
claim: Stay inside the domain where you have proof of authority, and tell the whole truth including the parts where you look bad.
detail: Asked on a big podcast whether people should consult him on happiness, he declined: his authority is scaling a business and selling. Admitting negatives makes the positives believable.

### LS-X39rITUMEPA#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: content-brand | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:59
claim: Audience versus algorithm is a false choice: the algorithm only quantifies human interest, so make what the people you want as buyers find interesting, among topics you also like.
detail: The business requires content your customers find interesting; the human cost is boredom and burnout, so pick topics that interest you as well. Content for a niche (he wants a few multi-$100M owners) gets few views, but targeting is now so good that niche audiences are easy to reach.

### LS-X39rITUMEPA#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:32
claim: Being your own customer is the best case; as you level up your interests drift from your audience's, so serve the audience's current need and do the job in front of you.
detail: A formerly overweight coach now enjoys advanced programming, but the audience still needs weight-loss basics. His meta frame is to do the job well regardless of fleeting moods, since the eyeballs go to you or to a competitor.

### LS-X39rITUMEPA#06 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:19
claim: Volume only helps with a feedback loop: take the top 10 of 100 posts, make the next 100 resemble them, and repeat.
detail: Two posts teach nothing, a hundred give a top ten to copy. Keep the effort constant but let the output change; repeating the same thing is spinning wheels. He calls the Rule of 100 reinforcement learning done by humans.
numbers: Top 10 of every 100 posts as the sample to imitate.

### LS-X39rITUMEPA#07 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: content-brand | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:42:56
claim: To move a winning single-image ad into video, apply the same Rule of 100: make many videos, then iterate from the top 10%.
detail: A listener found his best-performing ad was a meme image through Rule of 100 testing. Same method for video: volume, find the top tenth, iterate.
numbers: Top 10% of videos as the basis for iteration.

### LS-X39rITUMEPA#08 | DECISION_RULE | topic: paid-ads | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:23:22
claim: Paid ads are close to a one-way door for services, since they need phone teams and other infrastructure; for checkout-page products they are easier to start.
detail: For services sold by phone, he says you will probably need phone teams and other infrastructure. Treat content as top of funnel that warms the audience, and retarget with ads; as content grows, ads work better.
applies_to: Service businesses selling by phone.
### LS-X39rITUMEPA#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:21
claim: Organic leads, paid follows: organic content can seldom carry a direct ask, while ads can target and ask directly, so combine them.
detail: Organic CTAs hurt reach and shares, whereas ads guarantee reach to a chosen audience. A viewer sees ads and organic as one brand. He wants more organic than paid output. Burning through the warm audience with paid forces you to rebuild selling for colder traffic. Organic plus paid layering gives a higher return on ad spend.

### LS-X39rITUMEPA#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:21
claim: Once cold ads convert, they outperform warm traffic in volume because the unknown audience is far bigger.
detail: Pure cold advertising is possible but changes the whole machine; the sweet spot for most people is organic foundation first.

### LS-X39rITUMEPA#13 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:27:59
claim: To communicate clearly, write down the real-world action you want, then say only that.
detail: Reduce what you say to the "TLDR" and ask what someone can do with their hands or body as a result. The Leads and Offers books were compressed to a fraction (four or five times shorter) of their drafts this way. Communication exists to change behaviour; clarity beats cleverness.
numbers: Books were cut to roughly a quarter to a fifth of the first length.

### LS-X39rITUMEPA#14 | SCRIPT | topic: sales-process | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:31:22
claim: Every message tells the reader what you want, what they get, and why it is worth it, then uses an example when it turns abstract.
detail: Readers are distracted, so avoid jargon; if jargon appears, zoom out with "it is kind of like..." and give an example.

### LS-X39rITUMEPA#15 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: sales-process | fw: What Would It Take | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:33:16
claim: Three questions he asks most: what problem are we solving, what do you want to happen, and what would it take.
detail: Use them with teammates in meetings: first the problem (such as a churn issue), then the desired outcome, then "what would it take?" which assumes success and lets people reverse-engineer. Apply it to yourself ("what would it take to make 200 calls a day?") and orient life around it.

### LS-X39rITUMEPA#17 | DECISION_RULE | topic: content-brand | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:34:30
claim: A personal brand needs demonstrated efficacy, so a business brand usually needs a business first, while a brand in a field such as beauty can come before the company.
detail: Followers trust someone who has done what they cannot. For B2B-business content without owning a business you have no proof. Choose by which audience you need trust from.

### LS-X39rITUMEPA#18 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:35:41
claim: Choosing a business to start is hard because there are too many options; apply filters to shrink the pool to a few.
detail: Filters: remote or in person; services, products or software; sales motion (in person, phone, checkout page). Confidence in a decision is typically inversely correlated with the number of options. Pick from four, not a thousand.

### LS-X39rITUMEPA#26 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: objections-closing | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:51:41
