### YT-BYpTRiRqS1Y#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: promotions-launches | fw: Sales to Fulfillment Continuum | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:29
claim: A live coaching event with the 100K turnkey and 15-25K done-with-you ladder: typical close counts per day.
detail: Offers like this normally have a done-with-you tier of $15K-$25K and a turnkey tier of about $100K. The owner said nobody in his market can pay it, but only 55 people attend his quarterly workshop. Hormozi describes the real model: fill rooms of about 100 with ads, run three sessions in a day, pitch each, and close a handful. He also noted about 9% of Americans have a $1M net worth including their home.

### YT-BYpTRiRqS1Y#04 | CASE | topic: product-delivery | also: promotions-launches | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:15
claim: A motocross training business found that its many cheap tour days lost money while five-day camps netted over $100K each.
detail: Single-day dates went from 70 in 2023 to 140 in 2024 and brought huge operational drag. The bottom 20 dates netted negative, the next 20 under $1,000. Three five-day camps in 2024 each netted well over $100K, so the plan is 25 camps. One-day price $300, five-day price $1,200, both too low. The owner fears a single copycat who copies single days.
numbers: One-day $300, five-day $1,200; 70 to 140 tour dates; bottom 40 of 140 dates net below $1,000; camps net >$100K each; plan 25 camps.

### YT-BYpTRiRqS1Y#06 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: Virtuous Cycle of Price | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:30
claim: Hormozi's costliest mistake: cutting price and adding value to counter a competitor, which left churn unchanged and removed 20% of revenue.
detail: A competitor poached semi-employed testimonial customers and sold one-on-one coaching for less. Hormozi relaunched to his existing base with a big value stack and a lower price, cutting recurring revenue. The first community reaction was a complaint that he had not done it sooner. Churn did not move because $3,000 to $2,500 a month was still above the threshold of "a lot of money". The lost profit was multiplied at the sale, and the unprofitable business was later killed by the undercutting competitor.
numbers: Price $3,000/month to $2,500/month; top line cut ~$6M a year (stated also as $500K a month); lost profit ~$6-7M a year; ~$50M at the sale multiple; churn unchanged.

### YT-BYpTRiRqS1Y#07 | CASE | topic: paid-ads | also: content-brand | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:32
claim: A real-estate-agent coaching brand at $2.5M, with easy group delivery, needed to add paid ads next to organic.
detail: Delivery can double without strain, so this is a pure advertising play. Hormozi: paid typically gives a one-time 3-5x lift off the baseline (not a promise). Picture an audience pyramid: organic across more platforms, more often and better raises how many people exist to monetise; ads reach further down. Do both in parallel on a ten-year view.
numbers: Ads: one-time 3-5x off baseline; revenue $2.5M, goal doubled.

### YT-BYpTRiRqS1Y#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-process | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:03
claim: Switching from warm-audience challenge selling to cold ads in all likelihood breaks the sales motion, so ads and sales process need to be built together.

### YT-BYpTRiRqS1Y#10 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: verticals | fw: Big Head Long Tail | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:18:24
claim: A $6.6M sales-coaching and lead-gen firm for financial advisors had a churn problem like most agencies.
detail: Sales come from paid ads to a VSL then a phone team. The firm pivoted from lead gen to sales coaching because advisors cannot close, with lead gen sold on the back (big head long tail). Hormozi's diagnosis: customers who get activated stay, so fix activation and the avatar, and stop turning continuity out of front-end skill.
numbers: Revenue $6.6M, goal $20M+.

### YT-BYpTRiRqS1Y#11 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: retention-ltv | fw: Training | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:13
claim: Gym Launch trained client staff with a daily group role-play ("boiler room") split into five parts.
detail: Every morning trainers and front-desk staff drilled the script in groups, as an add-on, like a sales-manager meeting. Parts: intro, discovery, offer, objections and looping, and a fifth day on the hot topic. Keeping each piece crisp stops errors. Sales training is done team-side, not one-on-one.

### YT-BYpTRiRqS1Y#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: Big Head Long Tail | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:06
claim: Education businesses fail when they build continuity from front-end value; separate one-time skill from consumables.
detail: Once clients have the skill the value drops, so the recurring price should cover only what they still need ongoing (here lead gen). One-time value is worth much more than consumables, though buyers may lack money for it. Charge up front and make the continuity small enough to be a no-brainer.

### YT-BYpTRiRqS1Y#13 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Activation Points | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:34
claim: Fix stick by selling only avatars that activate, defined by demographics, quantifiable size, and the behaviours of those who stuck.
detail: Bucket one: demographics. Two: quantifiables (such as business size). Three: behaviours of people who activated and stayed, versus similar people who did not; reverse-engineer that into the activation process. Gym Launch would not have worked if it sold to every fitness professional, such as trainers with ten clients who cannot pay. Below a payment threshold, serve them DIY instead. Raise price to fit the better avatar.
