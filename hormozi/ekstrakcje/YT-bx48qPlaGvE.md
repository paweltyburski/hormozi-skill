### YT-bx48qPlaGvE#01 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:46
claim: Ghost products: tell the buyer to skip, or buy cheaper elsewhere, one item on your list, to earn trust before selling the rest.
detail: Found by accident when a key product ran out during a launch and he sent customers to Costco for it; they then bought everything else he recommended. He started crossing items off deliberately and eventually stopped stocking them. Giving up part of the sale shows you act in the buyer's interest.
numbers: Near-100% close on supplement recommendations; first-time sellers reached 80-90% in retail.
applies_to: Sellers with several products, retail or services.

### YT-bx48qPlaGvE#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:00
claim: Send away the lowest-margin items and keep the high-margin ones.
detail: The concession should cost little. If you know the buyer needs the rest, you can use a ghost product to gain trust faster; he says the difference between manipulation and help is intention.
### YT-bx48qPlaGvE#03 | SCRIPT | topic: sales-process | also: retention-ltv | fw: Menu Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:45
claim: For consumables, explain how to use each product, anchored to a habit they already have, before asking for the sale.
detail: State the benefit and dose, ask what they do every morning without fail, and tell them to keep the product beside it, with the dose written on it. Then ask to use the card on file, so buying is one yes. Piggybacking avoids building a new habit.

### YT-bx48qPlaGvE#04 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:10
claim: On a budget objection, rank items by necessity and remove them one at a time, restating the problem each solved.
detail: Ask if they are on a budget, then remove the lowest item and name the cost of its absence so the problem returns and they put it back. Do not call removed items unimportant.

### YT-bx48qPlaGvE#05 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:00
claim: Ask what old-identity spending (drinking, smoking, going out) the programme replaces, to show the money exists.
detail: After a budget objection, quantify what they spend on the habit, then point out that it funds the purchase.

### YT-bx48qPlaGvE#06 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:33
claim: Learn selling by joining the sales process of a low-ticket, high-traffic business such as a car wash or salon.
detail: Hundreds of repetitions teach more than courses; he credits low-ticket volume for making high-ticket sales easy.

### YT-bx48qPlaGvE#07 | EXAMPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:00
claim: Service sellers can refer a non-core task to a cheap outside contractor, so the seller seems to sit on the buyer's side of the table.
detail: Example: a web-design firm says a side item would cost more in-house and offers a contact in the Philippines. The aim is to move from across the table to shoulder to shoulder.
