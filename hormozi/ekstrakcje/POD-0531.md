### POD-0531#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: paid-ads | fw: More Better New | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Two owners each spent about $50,000 a month on Facebook and said the market was saturated, while their industries were worth billions.
detail: A fitness app owner (about $3M a year revenue) refused to spend more because he thought weight loss was exhausted on Facebook. A chiropractor-marketing agency spent about $50,000 a month on one platform and thought the same of its niche. Hormozi's point: the market is not capped, the owner's mindset is.
numbers: Weight loss ~$60B industry (US); chiropractic ~$16B; agency's total ad spend ~$600,000.

### POD-0531#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: paid-ads, outreach | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:24
claim: To test "my market is saturated", zoom out one level at a time and ask what methods, platforms and media have not been tried.
detail: His questions to the agency: only Facebook? Hulu, YouTube, Spotify, direct mail, radio? Cold calls, cold emails, voicemail drops? Content for the niche on any platform? Every answer was no. Then ask volume within each: one story a week or several a day, $100 or $1,000 or $100,000 a day.

### POD-0531#03 | TACTIC | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:01
claim: A telltale of an uncapped niche: dozens of competitors target the same audience on the same single platform.
detail: Add up the ad spend of all of them and compare with the market. Four levers for more leads right now: a new method, a new platform, more types of media on the current platform, more volume.

### POD-0531#04 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:05:33
claim: Pyramid of markets: after maxing out your niche you can expand in five directions: up-market, down-market, adjacent, broader or narrower.
detail: Using hair salons as the example. Up-market: bigger operators. Down-market: individual stylists renting chairs. Adjacent: a similar market (lash, nail, med spa; for a chiropractor, physical therapy). Broader: the whole beauty industry, sharing one customer psychology. Narrower: a niche inside the niche (extensions, blowouts), the only direction that shrinks the market, yet it may bring more money.
applies_to: Businesses with a worked niche, mainly past the first plateau.

### POD-0531#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:37
claim: Moving down-market the number of prospects is about 10 times larger, and moving up it is about one tenth, with the factor sometimes being more than 10.
detail: Individual stylists outnumber salon owners roughly tenfold; salon owners outnumber the next level up by a similar factor. Value per customer moves the opposite way.
numbers: Factor of 10, or sometimes more, per level up or down.

### POD-0531#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:39
claim: Think like an investor: once you have talked to roughly 40% of the whole industry, you have his blessing to start the next vertical or move in one of the other directions.
detail: Owners think they hold a 20% share because five competitors exist, yet may not have reached 1% of the market.
numbers: About 40% of the industry contacted as the green light.
### POD-0531#08 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:19
claim: Gym Launch had maxed the micro-gym niche (talked to 20,000 of about 50,000, using every channel), so it expanded up-market to health clubs.
detail: Micro gyms are small-group boutique studios; the segment was crowded, with CrossFit gym counts down about 30%. Options considered: personal trainers (down), health clubs (up), physical therapists or chiropractors (adjacent), any health studio (broader). Already narrow (30 customers, lease, one employee). It also reused existing channels, needed minimal operational change, and ran a beta with 20 health clubs.
numbers: ~50,000 micro gyms in the US; 20,000 already spoken to; beta of 20 clubs.

### POD-0531#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:25
claim: Hormozi describes asking his team which avatar they provide the most value to in absolute dollars, and that is how Gym Launch picked health clubs as its next direction.
detail: Health clubs gained tens of thousands of dollars in about a month and doubled or tripled bottom line by fixing onboarding, sales, retention and upsells. They were business owners first, with about 10 employees and $500,000 invested, so they understand return on capital, whereas micro-gym owners were fitness-first and needed belief work (it is fine to sell, to charge more, to pay staff on time).
numbers: Health club owner profile: ~10 employees, ~$500,000 invested in facility.
### POD-0531#10 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: content-brand, offers | fw: Riches in the Niches | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:30
claim: Narrowing the avatar has doubled, tripled or sometimes 5x revenue even after cutting the audience by about 85%.
detail: Condition: if one specific avatar can make 10x more money than the broad avatar, niche down and tailor messaging, offer and delivery to it.
numbers: Revenue 2x-5x with ~85% fewer people targeted.

### POD-0531#11 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:33
claim: Owners in a room of eight entrepreneurs doing about $500M a year each were no smarter or harder-working than him; they had been at it longer and chased bigger markets.
detail: Big businesses take time, and a niche does eventually saturate; the answer is a bigger market, not a new product for the same people.
