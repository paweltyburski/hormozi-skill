### YT-kzKxrifSMNg#01 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:37
claim: To persuade, attach a positive identity label to the person, get their agreement, then present the purchase as consistent with that label.
detail: Sequence: 1) label them with a flattering trait ("you seem a loving, caring man who wants to support his family"); 2) get a yes; 3) say that people like that invest in the programme. Cognitive dissonance makes them act consistently with the label. It can be woven into narratives and sales scripts, and works in reverse to harm someone.

### YT-kzKxrifSMNg#02 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:40
claim: Use a mental refrain, "and that's okay", after any negative feeling to cut the judgment you add to the emotion.
detail: You cannot control emotions, but you can control the judgment you attach and the response you choose. Hormozi says he once obsessed about happiness, treating every unhappy moment as bad, and made himself more miserable. He frames this as personal opinion, not advice.

### YT-kzKxrifSMNg#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:38
claim: Listen for "should, must, need" language, since these words signal implied threats and broken thought patterns.
detail: Example: "I must have coffee before I start my day". Labeling normal swings (tired, stressed, sad) as bad risks turning a passing state into a lasting problem.

### YT-kzKxrifSMNg#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:56
claim: Define terms before debating: undefined words like healthy, wealthy and success hide different meanings and drive most disagreements.
detail: Quoting Socrates. Contracts from the sale of three of his companies spent the first half of 50+ pages defining terms (such as non-compete scope) before the short agreement itself.
numbers: Contracts over 50 pages; roughly half defined terms.
