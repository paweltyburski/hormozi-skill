### MZ-WJ8VbTiTLO4#01 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: paid-ads, sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A coaching founder at $600K in year one stalled at tiny ad spend and one group call a day; advice was to raise spend and build a cold process.
detail: Mobile charcuterie founder sold a $47 course (6,000+ copies), then a 10-week coaching programme for mothers in 9-to-5 jobs. She runs Meta ads with 5 to 7 creatives and one closer taking one group call a day. Goal: $5M a year. Hormozi saw spend and capacity as unused, not an offer problem.
numbers: Course $47; coaching $2,500 paid in full; 260+ clients in a year; $600K first-year revenue; $200/day ad spend; ROAS about 4x; close rate about 40% (her data is weak).

### MZ-WJ8VbTiTLO4#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: scaling-constraints | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:32
claim: To a stalled owner he asks what stops them doing more, then checks spend, call volume and close rate.
detail: Sequence: what is stopping more, how much is spent daily, how many calls are taken, what is the close rate, and does the owner feel good about spending more. Group calls mean one call can hold several buyers.

### MZ-WJ8VbTiTLO4#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:06
claim: A sales motion built for warm people does not scale; selling to cold can serve warm, but not the reverse.
detail: Scaling spend brings progressively colder audiences and more inefficiency. Add a step that heats cold prospects (his microwave image): warm people go through it and get hotter, cold ones become warm enough to buy.
numbers: Expected spend to reach the goal: about $5,000/day instead of $200.

### MZ-WJ8VbTiTLO4#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: paid-ads | fw: Track Lose Print | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:25
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: he said that if it were him, he would build the cold acquisition process first and then add more ad spend.
detail: He told her to keep putting money in and run it hard until the wheels fall off, noting that scaling will bring colder traffic and her warm-oriented sales process will not scale, so she needs a cold process.
### MZ-WJ8VbTiTLO4#05 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:42
claim: A fourth-generation meat processor capped by distributor consolidation weighed a strategic buyout against growing his retail side.
detail: Wholesale portion-cutting for food service shrank from 8 to 9 distributor customers to about 3 plus small ones, as big distributors buy suppliers. A venture-backed buyer offered a strategic price; he has more passion for retail. Hormozi called it a risk question with no right answer.
numbers: Wholesale sales $20M, EBITDA about $2.7M; retail about $10M with profit unclear (shared expenses); about 60% of business in one account (side unclear in audio).

### MZ-WJ8VbTiTLO4#06 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:49
claim: A strategic buyer may pay about 7x EBITDA versus an average near 5x, and a sale is taxed as capital gains.
detail: Hormozi's rough math gave about $17M (he used a slightly lower profit figure than stated). Selling at capital gains instead of income tax adds about 20% in effect, so the payout equals roughly 9 to 10 years of current income.
numbers: Multiple 7x offered vs 5x average; payout about $17M; effective uplift about 20%.

### MZ-WJ8VbTiTLO4#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:33
claim: With no right answer, ask which choice you would regret more in ten years and which path makes you the person you want to be.
detail: Used after the owner said greed kept him from selling; Hormozi offered this as the right question to ask.
