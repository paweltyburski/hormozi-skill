### POD-0861#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Very small business owners cannot sustain a stable vendor business because they themselves are unstable.
detail: Retention problems with this segment are not a flaw in your product. In the Shopify comparison he gives, best-in-class retention for such customers is about 50% a year. The only model he has seen work for them is ultra-low price with high gross margin.

### POD-0861#02 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: verticals | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:16
claim: A seasonal holiday-lighting firm should either build a seasonal-workforce machine or work the other nine months, not start a second business.
detail: Revenue about 310,000 with about 142,000 EBITDA, nearly all from 90 days of holiday lights; the owner wants permanent lighting for year-round work. Hormozi: the issue is delivering only 90 days a year. Option one is the Halloween-store model: use the off months to build a pipeline and rapid training (for example 100 verbal commitments yields about 20 proficient workers). Option two is permanent lighting. Pick one.
numbers: Revenue about 310,000, EBITDA about 142,000; 100 commitments to about 20 proficient workers.

### POD-0861#05 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: pricing, hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:16
claim: An estate-planning firm growing 20% a year with zero EBITDA should fix pricing and compensation before buying leads.
detail: Revenue 2M, growth 20% a year for five years, goal 10M. Cost is people, not fixed machinery or rent, so the leak is underpricing, overpaying, or both. Raise cash flow first, then go aggressive on the front end. Owner thinks the team is at capacity, but Hormozi suspects spare room.

### POD-0861#06 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Point of Greatest Deprivation | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:11
claim: A trivia-night business (1.1M revenue, LTV:CAC about 12:1) has a failing upsell because it is sold at peak satisfaction, and it may not need an upsell at all.
detail: Bars hire weekly trivia on slow nights; margin about 25%; the music-mashup upsell churns after about three months; about 20% of clients come by referral. Core is already sticky, so revenue retention is solved. Growth lever: more outbound; one rep makes 100 calls a day but should make 300, and three more at 300 a day.
numbers: Revenue 1.1M, goal 10M; LTV:CAC about 12:1; margin about 25%; 100 vs 300 calls a day.

### POD-0861#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Point of Greatest Deprivation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:17
claim: Time the upsell to the new need that the first win creates, not to the moment the customer is full.
detail: After a steak you feel full on steak but empty on dessert. In B2B, solving lead generation creates the new problem of working the leads, so lead nurture sells next. The point of greatest value on one vector can be greatest deprivation on another.

### POD-0861#08 | CASE | topic: management-ops | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:14:23
claim: In a family business where one arm earns and the other loses, decide by signing a buy-out with a payment clock.
detail: Kitchens and bathrooms at 320,000 revenue (goal 1M) share a family with a commercial real-estate sister company draining construction cash. Father is about 56 and keeps postponing retirement. Hormozi: wait, leave, or force terms; the best is to buy the business now, price agreed in writing and paid over three to five years, with an estate attorney to handle tax. Stay with the high-margin business.
numbers: Revenue 320,000, goal 1M; payments over 3-5 years.

### POD-0861#10 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:23:14

### POD-0861#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: paid-ads | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:31:16

### POD-0861#13 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery, scaling-constraints | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:34:28
claim: A website-as-a-service firm with 85% annual retention should have scaled the cheap model, not jumped to high-ticket.
detail: 700 clients at 200 a month, 40-50 new a month, 2.1M sold last year, 950,000 profit, in Quebec. Four months ago it moved to high ticket, but ads run only about four months because of snow and clients stop ads when full, so LTV:CAC fell; clients leave after four months. Hormozi: they were selling from their own wallet. Go to 200 new customers a month, which quadruples the business to about 500,000 a month.
numbers: 85% annual revenue retention; 200 a month price; 700 clients; goal 200 new per month.
