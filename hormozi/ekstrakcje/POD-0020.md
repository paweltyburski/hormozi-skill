### POD-0020#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: Delivery Cube | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:54
claim: Sell the vacation, not the plane flight: every tier promises the same destination and differs only in speed, comfort and support.
detail: Options run from walking to buses, regular flights and a private jet, all reaching Maui. Pitch the destination, keep the pitch nearly identical across tiers, and let the customer pick the vehicle. Start with the top, one-on-one tier to anchor the price of lower ones.

### POD-0020#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:46
claim: A sale rests on two questions: if it works as promised, is it worth the price, and do I believe it will work?
detail: Competitors in a market make nearly the same promise, and prospects almost always say yes to question one. The winner addresses question two. Most sellers keep inflating the promise instead of building belief.

### POD-0020#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:29
claim: When a prospect agrees it is worth the price but will not buy, ask what they would need to see to believe you can deliver it.
detail: Example: losing 60 pounds is worth $5,000 to them (plastic surgery costs about $10,000). Confirm the value first, then ask what proof they need.
numbers: example: $5,000 for 60 pounds lost.

### POD-0020#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: offers | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:29
claim: Promise only what your repeatable process reliably delivers, not an outrageous multiple.
detail: He says he can reliably take a gym from about $30,000 to $100,000 a month, but not to $300,000 from one gym; he first says he doesn't think they 10x'd anyone's business, then says they did for Monica. He notes that market claims keep getting more outrageous.
numbers: $30,000 to $100,000 a month; six figures a month is the ceiling he promises.
applies_to: Gym owners
