### POD-0832#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: pricing | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:38
claim: The money ladder has six rungs ranked by how early and how safely you are paid relative to the work.
detail: From bottom to top: employee (works first, paid later); independent contractor (often half upfront, half after); in-demand professional (paid before or well ahead of the service); banks (paid first, collateral); insurance (paid in advance, may never pay out); royalties such as government, religion and franchisors (paid off the top, work questionable). He sees it as a continuum, not a rule.

### POD-0832#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:31
claim: The aim is not to switch category but to change how you ask for money so cash arrives earlier and less risk is carried.
detail: Work is the vehicle of risk. A surgeon booked three months ahead is paid today, which gives the practice a cash advantage over firms that deliver first and invoice later. This shaped how he set payment terms when selling Gym Launch.
numbers: Surgery paid three months ahead (example).

### POD-0832#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:34
claim: Banks protect themselves by being able to recover money in at least three ways.
detail: For example a personal guarantee, the house itself and a third source. They get paid first and keep earning monthly, taking on little work.
numbers: At least three recovery paths per loan.

### POD-0832#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:27
claim: Insurance is paid upfront for a promise that may never be paid, and its float compounds.
detail: His example is life insurance: pay for 20 years and stop, and the insurer keeps everything; he calls it a box of air at 100% margin. He claims the float is tax-free (unverified). Very old institutions like banks and insurers must have durable payment structures.
numbers: 20 years of premiums in the example.

### POD-0832#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:41
claim: Model businesses that last for centuries; endurance matters more than fast growth.
detail: He thinks only two S&P 500 companies are 100 years old (GE and Ford, from memory, unverified). Japanese firms prize longevity and avoid anything that could take them to zero, unlike American emphasis on rapid growth.
numbers: Roughly two 100-year-old S&P 500 firms (his recollection).

### POD-0832#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:39
claim: Hormozi personally wants to position like a royalty whenever he can: minimal risk, paid upfront, off the top, as many times as possible, with collateral as a bonus if a guarantee is possible.
detail: Collateral examples: governments can jail you, banks take your assets, insurers hold the money you gave them and the risk they calculate. He thinks even a small nudge toward the royalty end could dramatically shift how durable the business is. He thinks the point of business is to stay alive.
