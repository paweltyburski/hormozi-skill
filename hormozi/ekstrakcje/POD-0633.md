### POD-0633#05 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: sales-process | fw: Warm Outreach Steps | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:40
claim: If you need money tomorrow, export every contact from email, phone and social profiles into one sheet and open each message with something personal that has nothing to do with your product.
detail: Spend about 30 seconds per person finding what is new in their life (baby, move, race), open on that, then use acknowledge-compliment-ask to steer toward your topic (fitness: "how do you find time to cook?"). Use the compliment to label them with a trait you can reuse later in the sale. End by booking a roughly 10-minute qualification call, then a real conversation.
numbers: Example list: 700 Instagram followers plus 400 Facebook friends plus contacts.

### POD-0633#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:29
claim: A setter call runs only clarify, label and overview past experiences, then books the longer call; low-ticket sales can go cradle to grave in one call.
detail: Stopping in the middle of the pain cycle leaves the prospect aware of the problem and wanting help; the setter says he or she needs time to put something together and asks for a small commitment (the integrity tie down: if I can solve it, are you open to moving forward). On the second call he runs the first three steps again and goes deeper before selling.

### POD-0633#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:30:21
claim: Obstacles surface before the ask and objections after it; clear obstacles (for example "I just want information", other decision makers) up front.
detail: Check for other decision makers during nurture and again at call start; if one is needed, reschedule rather than proceed. The explain-concerns step is only used after a no. Each time an objection is resolved, confirm readiness with a loop-back such as "fair enough, ready to move forward?" and stop selling the moment they say yes, then take the card.

### POD-0633#09 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: sales-process | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:30:21
claim: Train new reps on the script from back to front, because someone who only knows the opening cannot close, while someone who knows only the close sometimes can.
detail: Most trainers start with rapport and the opening. The first half of CLOSER is only questions; the single statement a rep makes is when selling the outcome, then the ask follows. This also answers the intuition question: follow the script, and avoid piling extra information on a buyer who is ready.

### POD-0633#10 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: sales-process | fw: GROWTH Onboarding | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:26:13
claim: During a service onboarding period, send a short no-reply progress email every day instead of one weekly meeting, so the client receives repeated set-and-meet-expectation cycles.
detail: Example: a 14-day SEO onboarding where you actually do about 25 things in week one. Daily updates plus a warm handoff mean the first week contains many touchpoints while competitors have only one. Result described: higher trust, fewer back-outs, more ascension into the next product, more referrals and testimonials. The 24 hours after a sale should be choreographed: text from onboarding at signature, handoff by name.
numbers: Day 7 would be roughly the 8th touchpoint; ~24 hours post-close planned.
