### POD-0518#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: retention-ltv | fw: Seven Ways to Ask for Referrals | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:23
claim: Word of mouth has a linear part (ask at every close for referrals) and a quadratic part (engineered customer experience).
detail: Linear tactics give a steady uplift each time (a rep asking "who else needs this?"), but almost nobody does it. The larger return comes from designing the experience so that customers get results in a choreographed way; exceptional product is what drives word of mouth.

### POD-0518#04 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: sales-process | fw: Hinge Method | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:10
claim: In person, make the introduction on the spot: ask for a friend, take the customer's phone, photograph the two of you and text it to a thread with all three.
detail: The message then comes from the friend with you already shown in person, so the prospect cannot easily ghost or be rude, and a layer of like and trust exists already.

### POD-0518#05 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:58
claim: Shorten time to first win: if the first real result takes 90 days, design a material win in seven days and string smaller emotional victories after it.
detail: Example is a portfolio business where users take about 90 days to earn their first dollar (real-estate type deals). Then test which of the early wins are activation points, and reorient all customers toward them.
numbers: 90 days to first dollar versus a seven-day win target.

### POD-0518#06 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: Activation Points | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:45
claim: Find activation points by comparing your favourite customers' paths with others, then interviewing them for "magical moments", keeping the ones you can engineer.
detail: Pick the customers you love working with, list the experiences and steps they had that others did not, and ask them for meaningful moments. Split moments into engineerable and not, and build the engineerable ones into onboarding. B2C versions need not involve money (weight-loss chunk outcome).

### POD-0518#11 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: retention-ltv | fw: Conditional Guarantee | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:57
claim: Slice your client data by activities (logged in, attended four coaching calls, sent the emails) to substantiate claims, then use those activities as the guarantee conditions.
detail: Many businesses dislike what they find (for example only 20% log in), but you can still state an average for those who did the five things. Customers then see what to do, outcomes improve, and marketing matches the experience.
numbers: example: only 20% of clients log in.

### POD-0518#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:14:16
claim: The higher the ticket, the more logic the buyer needs: they already want the promise emotionally but do not believe it, so supply logical reasons.
detail: A $10M deal is not closed with an origin story. Gym Launch ads lean on averages (leads, sales, cash added, churn cut), and state that gyms already above these benchmarks do not need the service.
numbers: claim in ads: triple a brick-and-mortar gym's profit within 12 months on average (as stated).

### POD-0518#13 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:57
claim: Making what you sell match what you deliver is one of the easiest ways to raise a business's value.
detail: Teams change hooks, offers and funnels while the product stays the same, so buyers get something different from what they bought. Keep the sold promise and the onboarding aligned ("this is what you saw, this is what you get").

### POD-0518#14 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:12
claim: In his examples, a fee of $250,000 for $1M of value created, or $10M for $50M of net worth, is reasonable; selling at a price beyond your skill destroys your reputation.
detail: Raising prices is the outcome of providing more value. If you can make someone $1M you can charge $250,000; if you create $50M of net worth for an entrepreneur, asking for $10M is reasonable. People who want to sell at $10M with a $100,000 skillset get eaten by word of mouth.
numbers: $250,000 fee for $1M created; $10M for $50M of net worth.
### POD-0518#15 | FRAMEWORK | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:23:44
claim: Hormozi and Leila screen people with three values: unimpeachable character, sincere candor, competitive greatness; the red flag is ego.
detail: Character means pride in the association on and off the field. Candor means having hard conversations and taking hard feedback with empathy, not just bluntness. Competitive greatness means having some drive; it can come from fear of failure or insecurity, and the ideal is a pull rather than a push, though he accepts different forms of fuel. Three values rather than five or ten, because you can triangulate decisions against three. Taking hard feedback is an element of humility.
numbers: portfolio companies accept about 0.2% of applicants (as stated).
