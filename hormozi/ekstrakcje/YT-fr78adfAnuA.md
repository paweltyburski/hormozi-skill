### YT-fr78adfAnuA#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: other | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A business does not have to become an AI business; it should use AI as one more tool, the way it uses the internet.
detail: Two mistakes he sees: believing you must turn into a tech company, and assuming the tech person will do it for you. Early adopters of a tool get disproportionate returns, as with the first companies to have a web page.

### YT-fr78adfAnuA#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:49
claim: The owner needs "cloud to dirt" knowledge, from strategy and people down to connecting APIs, because only that person sees what combinations are possible.
detail: A technical employee answers within a narrow view of the business and defaults to common, commoditised automations. The large gains come from the owner's business judgment laid over technical ability.

### YT-fr78adfAnuA#03 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:26
claim: Learn an automation by feeding a tutorial video's transcript to an AI and following its steps, sending a screenshot each time you get stuck.
detail: Plenty of videos explain specific automations. Take the link or transcript, ask the AI to help you build that, and repeat with screenshots and "what do I do now" until it works.

### YT-fr78adfAnuA#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:50
claim: Compare AI with human processes fairly: an AI half-built in weeks will lose to a process tuned for years, so give it the same training and SOP effort.
detail: Owners install a half-finished AI function, compare it to a long-optimised human one and declare it worse. Ask how long the human version was trained before judging. His marketing team already has an AI SDR matching the human team.

### YT-fr78adfAnuA#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:31
claim: AI sales tools are not drag and drop; they speed up a sales process that already works and do not replace having one.
detail: Moving his AI setter into another business is like putting one of his reps into it: the first attempt goes badly, and worse if the process is weak. Ask whether you would have a human phone every opt-in; cheaper is not the same as effective. Agencies that know only AI and not sales will struggle.

### YT-fr78adfAnuA#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: sales-process | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:33
claim: Do not advertise that you use AI; customers care only that they get it faster, cheaper, better and with less risk.
detail: Human psychology and persuasion do not change. He does not talk about his CRM or call software either; the outcome is what sells and whether people buy again depends on skill.

### YT-fr78adfAnuA#07 | EXAMPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:57
claim: AI cuts risk and labour: examples from large firms, and five support agents at his own book launch.
detail: Cited (figures as stated, not checked): PayPal cut fraud losses by about $700M in a year; a JPMorgan contract tool saved about 350,000 lawyer hours (12,000 credit agreements); a 700-agent customer service switch saved about $40M. For his book launch, five agents handled about 120,000 tickets, mostly asking where to get the free items, and resolved 90% without a person.
numbers: PayPal $700M fraud losses avoided per year; JPMorgan 350,000 hours; about 120,000 tickets, 90% resolved without a human.

### YT-fr78adfAnuA#08 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:01
claim: Pick one slice of your day, set aside a block, and automate it fully from start to finish; treat "intuition" as pattern recognition you can describe as observable steps.
detail: People resist because they fear AI; he says you are the best person to automate your own workflow, which frees time for higher-value work. Whenever you say "gut feeling", ask which behaviours produce the result.

### YT-fr78adfAnuA#09 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:35
claim: Generate content ideas from your own week: connect a bot to your calendar and transcribed calls and ask for the five most interesting situations or decisions.
detail: The result is true stories from real events, which solves the proof problem in AI-generated content.

### YT-fr78adfAnuA#10 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:00

detail: AI can also run automatic thumbnail tests and build a skill from what wins over time. Include trend research on visual as well as verbal hooks.

### YT-fr78adfAnuA#11 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: content-brand | fw: User-Generated Content Flywheel | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:18

detail: Also generate static ads from data sets: for a community, every new member win listed in a tab is run through six proven visual templates and turned into ads automatically.
numbers: six high-converting templates per win.

### YT-fr78adfAnuA#12 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:08
claim: Use AI to enrich leads, answer fast with personalised voice notes, images or Instagram DMs, and hand off between AI agents for scheduling.
detail: Dynamic scheduling hands the lead from one AI to another, making availability less of a constraint and pushing speed to contact up sharply.

### YT-fr78adfAnuA#13 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: other | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:39
