### YT-Luvfw14pVx4#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:19
claim: Proving that a bias against you exists leaves you no better off, so treat it as fixed and win anyway.
detail: Bias exists because people learn from patterns, so it will never disappear. Winning an argument that you are disadvantaged only confirms the disadvantage. Treat life as a single-player game where nothing will change, and spend effort on being better than the stereotype instead.

### YT-Luvfw14pVx4#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:21
claim: Blaming bias gives an ego-saving excuse for not winning; the useful stance is "I am not undeniable yet".
detail: The excuse feels comfortable because the alternative, "I am not good enough yet", hurts. Accepting the excuse costs more than the bias does. Reframe the gap as the only thing you control and aim to be bulletproof, with nothing anyone can hold against you.

### YT-Luvfw14pVx4#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:40
claim: A stereotype works in your favour once you break it, because people notice the person who defies expectations.
detail: Someone who looks like the stereotype but behaves better becomes a separate category in the other person's head ("this one is different"). Overcompensate: be kinder, more articulate, work harder than the norm. Hormozi's example is a college friend who always over-tipped to counter a stereotype about his group.

### YT-Luvfw14pVx4#06 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:57
claim: To catch up with a better rep, find the best person on the team and do twice their activity.
detail: The reasoning: the best rep keeps pulling ahead if you only match them, so you must copy what they do plus a second full day of the same work. Hormozi's phrasing is that volume negates luck. He arranged only an interview; the job was a chance to work leads, not a guarantee.
numbers: Target activity level is 2x the top rep.

### YT-Luvfw14pVx4#07 | CASE | topic: sales-team | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:12:46
claim: An 18-year-old moved from outbound to qualification to the closing team and became top closer, after clearing extra hurdles no older rep faced.
detail: He became best on the outbound team, then best in qualification (second tier of sales). The closer team was all older family men, and the sales manager doubted him because of his age and his link to the founder. He required the teen to self-generate a customer end to end, click to close, and then repeat it a second time to rule out a fluke. He entered at the bottom of the closing team and still became its top performer.
numbers: 2 self-generated end-to-end closes required before joining the closing team; age 18.

### YT-Luvfw14pVx4#08 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: operator-mindset | also: hiring-talent | fw: What Would It Take | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:09
claim: When you feel a bias against you, he suggests asking the decision maker "what would it take" for the specific thing you want, then doing it.
detail: Use it for a place on a team, a manager role, a raise, a promotion, or a deal with a customer. Acknowledge their hesitation openly, then ask what you would have to do objectively to change their mind, and overcompensate beyond what a normal person would offer.
