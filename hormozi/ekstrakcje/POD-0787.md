### POD-0787#01 | DEFINITION | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Focus is the quality and quantity of what you turn down; it is built by removing distractions, as value is built by removing friction.
detail: Top performers do not have willpower you lack, they have removed what you keep. An Olympic coach framed the gold-medal gap as what rivals have that the champion lacks (an off switch) and as more time put in, not higher return on time.

### POD-0787#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:07
claim: Unmade decisions are the heaviest load; decisions are instant given information, so make them with imperfect information and course correct.
detail: Few business decisions are truly irreversible, especially early when stakes are low. Perfect information usually means the opportunity has expired. Image: walking up a mountain in mist, the next steps become visible only as you move, so you end up ahead of anyone trying to plot a perfect path.

### POD-0787#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:07
claim: When a prospect says they need to think about it, ask what they need to think about and what information they would need to decide, then define what would make it a yes or a no.
detail: If they cannot say what makes it a yes, ask what would make it a no, and reverse-engineer the yes from that.

### POD-0787#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:42
claim: Switching between multiple tasks cuts output per unit of time by roughly 75% (Hormozi's recollection of research).
detail: He argues skill is leverage on work, so doing fewer things is itself a skill, and focus is an action that strengthens with repetition rather than a trait.
numbers: About 75% efficiency loss from multitasking.

### POD-0787#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:05
claim: Work capacity is trainable like muscle: recovery capacity grows, so you can add volume over time; the fix for low capacity is first removing what drains focus.
detail: A 21-year-old Hormozi was told by a law-firm partner that it takes about ten years to learn how to work. Only after a bare calendar still leaves you unfocused should you work on capacity itself.
numbers: About 10 years to learn how to work (attorney's remark).

### POD-0787#06 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:01
claim: Calendar audit in order: unmade decisions, then non-work (including elaborate morning routines), then relationships, then hobbies; keep rest, but earn it after work.
detail: Work as much as possible and rest as efficiently as possible, since over a week output drops if you never rest. A hobby that functions as rest can be the reward at the end of the workday. Do not expect the season to be fun: most daily tasks are brick-laying.

### POD-0787#07 | CASE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:11:50
claim: Hormozi's big growth periods followed "seasons of no": GMAT study for four hours a day, then living at his first gym, then flying out for launches, and now no travel or speaking.
detail: During the gym years he read no general business books and watched no motivational videos, only material on getting members, selling and pricing. He cut off friends and family at each stage.
numbers: 4 hours per day of GMAT practice after work; 100% of discretionary time.

### POD-0787#08 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:50
claim: In a small onboarding experiment ("The 100"), 86% of participants made a dollar: all who had earned before, and 62% of those who had never earned anywhere.
detail: Three cohorts: people who had earned on the Skool platform, owners who had earned elsewhere, and people who never made a dollar. The second cohort shows existing skills transfer, so the question becomes which extra skills beginners need that can be removed.
numbers: 86% overall; 100% for the first two cohorts; 62% for never-earned beginners.

### POD-0787#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: outreach | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:30
claim: What separates those who make money is the expected effort, which can be off by 100x: beginners expect 5 outreaches per customer when it takes about 200, and the first customer pays a tenth of what they imagined.
detail: These are expectation problems, not knowledge gaps: declarative knowledge (what) versus procedural knowledge (how). You only learn to sell by getting on the phone and to run ads by running ads.
numbers: Expected 5 reach-outs per customer vs actual about 200; first sale about 1/10 of imagined price.

### POD-0787#10 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: Four R's | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:16:51
claim: Test whether a calendar item is real work: it must help track (get attention and contact info), convert (raise the chance they buy) or deliver (retain, resell, refer, review); otherwise it is not work.
detail: Money is made by converting attention into goods and services exchanged for money. Filling time with things that are not work is, in his words, most common at the beginner level; at the advanced level the problem is filling time with work that is not the constraint.
### POD-0787#11 | EXAMPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:18:26
claim: Doing work you enjoy instead of work that is the constraint adds potential but no throughput: a marketer generating more leads while the sales team is the bottleneck.
detail: The same logic repeats down the chain if you then add sales but delivery, customer success or inventory is the limit. He says this error appears at every size from $1M a year to $1M a week.

### POD-0787#12 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:29:21
claim: Ask what would need to be true to 10x the business in a year; there is often only one such condition, then check whether it is feasible with current resources in 12 months.
detail: If yes, the best use of time is an order-of-magnitude move rather than optimisation, since a CRO split test gives a 5% lift while conversion is capped at 100% but lead flow can be 100x.

### POD-0787#13 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:49
claim: Almost every billionaire Hormozi knows judges who they let into their circle by one razor: does this person, habit, call or trip raise or lower the likelihood of reaching their goals; the bar rises as goals grow.
detail: Cutting is not permanent: a better version of you will find more doors than you lose, whereas staying in the old circle can cost you both. Hormozi says he still cuts people out regularly.
### POD-0787#14 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:28:39
claim: Take the whole to-do list, pick one task, erase the rest, work it with a timer, and note your time estimate versus actual.
detail: Tasks believed to take two hours often take 20 minutes once understood (ten minutes to figure out, two to do). Understanding the problem is part of solving it, so 45 minutes of understanding plus five of fixing is not unproductive. With portfolio founders most of his talk is about what they will not do.
numbers: Example: 45 minutes to understand a problem and 5 minutes to solve it.

### POD-0787#15 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:30:39
claim: A focused fool beats a distracted genius, and five tasks done one after another finish faster than five started together.
detail: Focus is like a magnifying glass on paper: spread energy only warms it. Parallel starts make each take longer because, unable to rank, you fear delay and start everything. The same applies inside one business: one business focused outgrows five, and one task outgrows five.
