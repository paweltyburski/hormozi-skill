### POD-0636#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: sales-process | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:15
claim: Ed Mylett says strong persuaders frame in three moves: pre-frame what is about to happen, stay in the frame while delivering, then post-frame what just happened.
detail: Ed Mylett's observation: weak framing hurts closes, ads and affiliate offers alike. Hormozi agrees and describes framing as contextualising, a teaching skill: translate jargon into what the product means for the buyer ("your house will be protected, you sleep well").
### POD-0636#02 | CASE | topic: promotions-launches | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:01
claim: At his book launch he built a large value frame first, then stepped out of it and gave everything free, which switched the frame to generosity.
detail: The presentation explained what each part would cost, how it was made and what it does, with the price dropping as the pseudo-pitch went on. People said they were ready to buy at the lower price points. Only about a tenth of the book's work went into the presentation. Hormozi stresses that giving a thing without first showing its worth is not processed as valuable.
numbers: Price walked down through $12,000, $5,000, $3,000 before going free; about 200 hours on the presentation versus about 2,000 on the book.

### POD-0636#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:04
claim: Ed Mylett says that before a product is reduced to its access point (or given away), you should create a frame of its value.
detail: Mylett's point: most sellers who give product away never build a value frame first. Hormozi's aim was to justify a $12,000 gift and why everyone present would have paid that.
### POD-0636#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:17
claim: Whoever you blame is the person you give power over your life to.
detail: At 19 he blamed his mother for his behaviour and realised he was handing control to the person he liked least. His start of the way out of poverty is saying "my fault": fault is beside the point, since it is your problem and no one else will fix it.

### POD-0636#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: outreach | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:49
claim: Think in actions, not feelings: 100 cold calls produce business whether you love the work or feel you do not deserve it.
detail: Like weight on a bar, the work does not care who you are. He found it freeing that results follow the activity even when self-worth is low.

### POD-0636#06 | CASE | topic: lead-getters | also: outreach | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:40
claim: A new auto-detailing owner with a small referral programme got too few leads, and was told to knock on doors with flyers every weekend.
detail: Told by Ed Mylett about his nephew: 18 clients, a $25 referral reward, only three clients had referred anyone. Advice: print flyers and knock on 100 doors each Saturday, show the Instagram work. Doing the "ugly" activity with a number attached lowers fear because losing becomes mathematically unlikely.
numbers: 18 clients, 3 had referred; $25 referral reward; 100 doors per Saturday.

### POD-0636#08 | DEFINITION | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:13:12
claim: Sadness is a perceived lack of options, solved by knowledge; anxiety is too many options, solved by making a decision.
detail: Hormozi calls this a work in progress. For sadness, learning what action to take removes the hopelessness. For anxiety, list the decisions you have not made and confront them.

### POD-0636#09 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:52
claim: His calendar is built from the back of the day forward, so meetings sit late and the morning stays free for the work that moves the business.

### POD-0636#10 | EXAMPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: hiring-talent | fw: Internal Core Four (Recruiting Mirror) | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:16:51
claim: Making yourself known works the same for customers, affiliates, employees and agencies, and a launch was staffed through a chain of directors.
detail: He hired a director of people, who hired a director of brand, who ran a media team posting content for the launch. A director of marketing contacted agencies and picked one to run the ads. The point is that lacking leads means not doing enough of the four methods, and leverage means moving one level down only if you have not hired.
numbers: 300 pieces of content a week in the run-up; 6 agencies contacted; audience goal of about 500,000 registrations (unclear in audio).
