### POD-0854#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:51
claim: Five-point "star" for missed tasks: walk through why they did not do it, as a shared script that depersonalises the conversation.
detail: Show the framework to staff in advance so it becomes a language. Ask in order whether they knew you wanted it, what you wanted, how to do it, and when it was due. The fifth point is not in the retained audio (unclear in audio). Hormozi contrasts this with opening by saying they are under expectations, which makes people defensive; this framing shifts the vibe and has both sides tackle the problem together.
### POD-0854#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:13
claim: First question: did you know I wanted you to do this? The fix is to write it down.
detail: If you rarely follow up, staff learn that mentions are not real asks, especially from idea-rich founders. If they say they didn't know, record the task and confirm by email for a timestamp.

### POD-0854#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:51
claim: Second question: did you know exactly what I wanted, defined as behaviour or outcome and format?
detail: A "report" could be printed, emailed, spoken or a presentation. Two or three extra minutes of clarity probably saves roughly two or three hours. Feedback like "be less creepy" fails; describe the specific behaviour. He ties poor prompting of AI to the same communication problem.
### POD-0854#04 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: Document Demonstrate Duplicate | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:31
claim: Third question: did they know how? Match instruction detail to skill: the less skilled, the more specific.
detail: A senior marketer can be told "run a campaign"; a junior needs it chunked into tasks like writing emails. Train via document, demonstrate, duplicate: write the steps while doing the task, do it in front of them, then they do it in front of you, and repeat for next week's task.

### POD-0854#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:19
claim: Fourth question: did they know when? Hormozi personally wants end of day to be his default deadline.
detail: He claims an end-of-month organisation works at one thirtieth the pace of an end-of-day one, and end-of-day versus end-of-week is roughly seven times. Deadlines are only part of the answer.
numbers: end of day vs end of week = 7x speed; vs end of month = 30x (his claim).
### POD-0854#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning, pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:29:05
claim: Statements you treat as facts ("no one can sell like me", "we cannot charge more", "not industry standard") are self-made rules; change them.
detail: Reality follows the rules you accept. "No one can" is not solvable, "I do not yet know how to find someone" is. Build in a world with fewest rules; owners often cap price and pay by industry norm.
