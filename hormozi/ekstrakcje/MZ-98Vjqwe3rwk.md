### MZ-98Vjqwe3rwk#01 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: verticals | fw: Crazy Eight | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: An indoor-air-quality company ($1.3M, Google Ads only) sells a one-off fix and wants more LTV.
detail: Only about a quarter of front-end buyers buy the annual paid checkup plan. Hormozi says it is a sales problem with two parts: take rate on the plan and upsell scripting during the visit. He also expects room to raise prices.
numbers: $1.3M revenue; ~25% plan take rate.

### MZ-98Vjqwe3rwk#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: sales-process | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:20
claim: A front-end close rate near 35% is normal for properly priced services; a cheaper bolt-on should close 70-80%.
detail: Position the add-on as something everybody does, an assumed close, not a new sale.
numbers: 35% front-end; 70-80% bolt-on take rate.

### MZ-98Vjqwe3rwk#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: More Better New | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:07
claim: Spend more on the one working channel before adding a second.
detail: He thinks they'd probably reach about $3M just on Google Ads by spending more; at that point costs start rising and the maintenance plan needs to start paying dividends.
numbers: Probably ~$3M reachable on Google Ads alone.
