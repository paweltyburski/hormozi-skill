### POD-0230#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:47
claim: The single habit that most affects an entrepreneur's output is sleep; everyone he knows who earns a lot has it dialed in.
detail: He says he has no daily routine; what matters is energy. Consistent sleep forces choices about food, alcohol, travel and social life.

### POD-0230#02 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:04
claim: Anchor on a fixed bedtime and let the wake time follow, rather than forcing an early alarm on short sleep.
detail: Cutting sleep to five hours to rise at four is unsustainable and makes you less sharp. He claims short sleep raises hunger, slows processing, hurts short-term memory and increases emotional reactivity.
numbers: Bed around 8:30 p.m.; wakes around 4 a.m. naturally.

### POD-0230#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:23
claim: What he personally does: he keeps the same sleep schedule on weekends and tries to keep it when changing time zones, and he skips events that disrupt it. He says he is not claiming it is the best way.
detail: The body does not know about weekends. When in Southern California he kept Austin hours, waking about 2 to 2:30 a.m. and sleeping about 7 to 7:30 p.m. He skips late dinners, drinking and high-adrenaline events.
numbers: Two-hour time difference; bed at about 7:00 to 7:30 p.m. local.
### POD-0230#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:21
claim: Successful entrepreneurs have dedicated uninterrupted time each day, which means staying up late or rising early.
detail: He thinks late nights allow a better social life but cannot do it himself; even in college he stopped working at 9 p.m. and restarted at 3 or 4 a.m.

### POD-0230#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:23
claim: A low barrier to making exceptions turns them into the habit, so draw a clear line.
detail: "Taco Tuesday" drinking and "donut Wednesday" mirror the weight-loss client who treats every day as a special occasion. Those without discipline need all-or-nothing; he abstained from alcohol for two and a half years.
numbers: 2.5 years without drinking.

### POD-0230#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:08
claim: Being at 100% rather than 80% each day compounds into a gap nobody can close in about five years.
detail: He compares it to Kobe Bryant taking the extra 20% repeatedly, and says most people will not accept the social trade-off.
numbers: 100% versus 80% daily output; gap becomes unbridgeable within about 5 years.
