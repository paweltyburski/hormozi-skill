### YT-TFxT3G5jwtU#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:26
claim: People default to B-plus problems because they know how to solve them, while the A-plus problem is messier and needs analysis and iteration.
detail: Quick wins give a dopamine hit and fill the day, so the number one item sits untouched. Growth is measured by gain per task, not tasks finished; side work drains resources from the trunk.

### YT-TFxT3G5jwtU#02 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:47
claim: Open a prioritisation meeting with an explicit amnesia rule: nothing before today is anyone's fault, and "who did this?" is asked only to understand.
detail: Staff fear blame for small fires. After the clean slate, give each person specific cover: if another department complains about slow turnaround, they say the boss approved it. The owner signs the paychecks, so his permission to let minor fires burn carries weight.

### YT-TFxT3G5jwtU#03 | CASE | topic: paid-ads | also: management-ops | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:08
claim: A paid-ads portfolio team lost three weeks to distraction while the tracking pixel was broken; Hormozi had them fix only the pixel across seven funnels.
detail: Bad pixel data feeds the platform poor signals, so no conversion work can matter. He admitted the pages looked ugly and still refused to touch them. After the fix, reassess: some conversion problems may vanish because better traffic now arrives.
numbers: Issue lasted 3 weeks; 7 funnels.

### YT-TFxT3G5jwtU#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:19
claim: Do not rank the whole list; pick only the top item, finish it, then re-assess in the new context.
detail: Debating whether item eight beats seven is wasted effort. After the top item is done, items two to five often become irrelevant or another one jumps ahead, so earlier work on them would have been wasted.

### YT-TFxT3G5jwtU#05 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:56
claim: Shorten the meeting cycle on a priority from weekly to daily or twice daily, and check in an hour after assigning work.
detail: A weekly cadence signals low importance. Each meeting carries a mini deadline; between meetings an early "where are you with that?" shows urgency. Also tell people their regular work is now secondary, since an urgent task stacked on normal duties stalls.

### YT-TFxT3G5jwtU#06 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:15
claim: Make progress visible to all (dashboard, thermometer) and attach a shared reward to hitting the target.
detail: A public marker reinforces the team each time it is seen. Add a "pot of gold", such as a team dinner or the rest of the day off, so people see what is in it for them.

### YT-TFxT3G5jwtU#07 | DEFINITION | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:13
claim: Commitment is the elimination of alternatives; saying it is easy, removing the alternatives is the proof.
detail: Examples: marriage, or throwing out food and alcohol when dieting. In business the boss must remove everything that is not the priority, because raising priority alone does not raise commitment.

### YT-TFxT3G5jwtU#08 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:14:37
claim: A four-step routine for any team priority: prioritise, create urgency, eliminate everything else, then reassess and reprioritise.
detail: Name the single most important thing; set meeting times and deadlines; clear the rest from plates; when done, review the list and repeat. He says that if you do the first three, it gets solved significantly faster than you thought because everyone focuses and clears the way.
### YT-TFxT3G5jwtU#09 | SCRIPT | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:52
claim: Kick-off sequence: ignore all problems but one, meet the same day, split the work into line items, collect estimates in minutes or hours, and have each person repeat the task back.
detail: Every line item was assigned and sized in hours, not days. The read-back exposed that people often heard something different, so he restated until each got it right, then set a second meeting hours later to show work.
numbers: Estimated total for the pixel fix: about 6 hours; follow-up meeting same afternoon.

### YT-TFxT3G5jwtU#10 | CASE | topic: sales-process | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:17:25
claim: A portfolio company running a regular sales event had a low show rate; making that the company-wide goal grew revenue over 35%.
detail: Staff blamed lead quality. The team computed that reaching the industry-standard show rate would add 35% revenue and, at a 10% margin, more than triple profit. Minor service and sales issues burned without hurting the result.
numbers: Industry-standard show rate = +35% revenue; margin about 10%, so profit above 3x; actual growth above 35%.

### YT-TFxT3G5jwtU#11 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:36
claim: To confirm the one priority, ask whether any other use of resources would give a comparable return with no risk in the next 30 days.
detail: In his portfolio-company example the answer was no, so they made that metric the only priority for everyone in the company. The test combines size of upside, risk and time window.
### YT-TFxT3G5jwtU#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:13
claim: The A-plus problem is soaked in the unknown, so understanding it counts as progress: pull the thread and the next step appears.
detail: Items two to five are familiar in shape; for the top problem you do not know where to start or how it ends, and the next step only shows after the first.

### YT-TFxT3G5jwtU#13 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:44
claim: Raise information transparency so everyone knows financials, conversion rates, margins and how money is made.
detail: Hormozi notes public companies show employees everything and still function; the fear is irrational. More people knowing means more people solving problems.

### YT-TFxT3G5jwtU#14 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:54
claim: Before announcing a new priority, have each person show everything they are working on and clear stale requests.
detail: Asks from two months ago may still drain attention or be already solved elsewhere. Frequent list reviews ("drop this, table that") reduce anxiety and let the manager praise wins promptly.
