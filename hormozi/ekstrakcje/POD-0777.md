### POD-0777#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:05:23
claim: Find new audience for your one product instead of new products for your existing audience.
detail: An audience only gives an artificial boost at launch. Creators who respond to a fading launch with another product shrink returns each time.

### POD-0777#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:48
claim: Each follow-on product is the next-best idea, sold to buyers already asked once, so sales decline.
detail: Illustration: widget one sells 100, widget two 50, widget three 30. Problems: later products were passed over first, and the best buyers are already used up.
numbers: Illustrative units: 100, then 50, then 30.

### POD-0777#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: content-brand | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:49
claim: A second product feels like another give, not an ask, if the first was exceptional and enough time passed.
detail: He gives a book follow-up after three to six months as the case. Most products are mediocre, which is why audiences treat launches as asks.
numbers: Gap illustrated: 3-6 months between launches.

### POD-0777#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:23
claim: The maintenance level of audience selling is new followers times conversion.
detail: About 100 new followers a day at 1-2% conversion gives 1-2 sales a day, depending on price. After the launch spike this feels small and prompts a pointless second launch.
numbers: 100 followers per day; 1-2% conversion; 1-2 sales per day.

### POD-0777#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:00
claim: Launch-and-crash cycles lead to overhiring, layoffs and a damaged reputation.
detail: Falling sales after a launch leave payroll too large; he sees influencers with many businesses and few employees.

### POD-0777#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:00
claim: A good product spreads beyond the audience through word of mouth and colder ads, and itself creates warm audience.
detail: Much of his own audience entered through his books, recommended by others. Only knowing one way to get customers, plus impatience, is the skill deficiency.

### POD-0777#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:34
claim: Improve the one product and widen reach through better content, ads, outbound or affiliates.
detail: If the product is good, it will ideally reinforce your position positively in prospects' and future customers' minds.
### POD-0777#08 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:29
claim: Ramp slowly: sell to 1-5% of the audience, gather feedback and testimonials, improve, then offer to the next slice.
detail: This builds sustainable revenue above the launch-and-crash model. His own book launch was an exception because he validated it with trusted readers first; most teams lack the operational skill for a big one-shot launch.
numbers: Slice: 1-5% of the audience per round.

### POD-0777#09 | EVOLUTION | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:15
claim: He now spends a lot more time on product than before, because once you know how to sell, selling feels empty when you must keep doing it again and again unless the product is good.
detail: Over the last 4-5 years he reinvested in product; he dislikes thinking of sales from 10 years ago that pay nothing today (loosely speaking). The secret of very big businesses is not losing customers: even a small base held for 20 years is large.
numbers: Roughly 4-5 years of added product focus.
### POD-0777#10 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:15
claim: Ask: if I could keep only one of my businesses, which, and how much more would I earn putting all time into it?
detail: Being CEO of four ventures plus media means none is run well. Sunk cost is no reason to keep one; act the day you see the better version. Skills to build: focus, patience, product iteration.
