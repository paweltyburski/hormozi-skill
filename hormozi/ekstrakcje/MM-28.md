### MM-28#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: strategy-positioning, finance-exit | fw: Continuity Discount Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Hormozi's Austin neighbour built and sold a waste-management company by offering apartment complexes a free year of service for signing five-year agreements.
detail: With no family funding, he maxed out his credit cards for a year and pitched big apartment buildings: switch to me, sign five years, get the first year free (effectively a six-year deal). He had to deliver a year before being paid — a brutal cash cycle — but captured large market share and sold to a national conglomerate.
numbers: 1 free year + 5 paid years.

### MM-28#02 | DEFINITION | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: Continuity Discount Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:30
claim: A continuity discount gives products or services free in exchange for a commitment to keep buying over time — an easy sale that anyone can close.
detail: Common in internet, pool cleaning, gyms, landscaping, storage, leases ("first two months free when you sign for a year"). Works in any business if you decide two things up front: how you apply the discount (four ways) and your cancellation policy, because not everyone keeps commitments.

### MM-28#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics, retention-ltv | fw: Continuity Discount Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:19
claim: A continuity discount can be applied four ways: up front, at the end, spread over the term, or after a couple of paid months.
detail: 1) Up front: free time first, paid term starts after — converts more customers but has higher churn, and delays cash. 2) At the end: free time earned after paying the whole term on time — converts fewer customers but has lower churn. 3) Spread: discount divided across every payment — keeps cash flow while giving the full discount. 4) Hybrid: collect first and last month (or a fee) up front, then the free time, then the term. Summary of trade-offs at 00:19:04.

### MM-28#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Continuity Discount Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:19
claim: Putting the free time up front works best in industries with a history of enforcing contracts or holding collateral; skip it if your churn is historically high.
detail: Examples he gives: cell carriers (cut service), storage (they hold your stuff), real estate (eviction), cars (repossession). It acquires customers but not profitably — cash is delayed, as the waste company lost money in year one. To protect cash, give the free time as a discount spread over the term instead, like a rollover credit used as a front-end offer (00:03:07).

### MM-28#05 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Continuity Discount Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:07
claim: Never let free months eat into the committed term: "three months free with a year" means 3 free plus 12 paid, not 3 plus 9.
detail: A common mistake: you overcome the hard one-year sale but don't collect a full paid year. Structure it as a 15-month agreement — 33% more money for the same pitch (00:07:12).
numbers: 12 vs 9 paid months = +33%.

### MM-28#06 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Continuity Discount Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:52
claim: Back-load the discount: customers get the free months at the end only if they pay every month of the term on time — Hormozi's favourite.
detail: You can still advertise "three free months when you sign up for a year", then explain the terms when they come in. They earn the free time, which encourages on-time payments and good behaviour.

### MM-28#07 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Continuity Discount Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:39
claim: Spread the discount across the term — advertise the total as free months or a credit, then deduct it evenly from each payment.
detail: Three free months on a $200/month year = $600 discount = $150/month for 12 months. Advertise "$600 credit" or "three months free", then explain it is spread over the term; afterwards the price returns to full. The same discount can be displayed four ways — e.g., $1,000/month with three months free: "3 free months", "$3,000 off", "25% off", or a comparison (00:07:12).
numbers: $600 over 12 months → $150 instead of $200 per month.

### MM-28#08 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Continuity Discount Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:35
claim: Collect one or two payments before the free time starts — first and last month, last month, or a setup/onboarding fee — to liquidate CAC.
detail: Hormozi's preferred middle ground: "You get your three months free, but you pay first and last." Those two months offset acquisition cost and commissions, give the customer skin in the game, and prove the payment method works before the free period. Variants: last month plus a fee, first and last plus a fee. You can still advertise big chunks of free time. Those who accept tend to be great customers.

### MM-28#09 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Continuity Discount Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:12
claim: Common continuity discounts seen across industries.
detail: Real estate: two free months on a 12-month lease. Storage: a "free" first month for a 12-month commitment. Gym: two months free when signing for 12. Cleaners: three free cleanings on a six-month contract. Massage: free sessions on a twice-monthly plan. Spread the free value across the term so customers still pay up front — a rollover upsell using cash.

### MM-28#10 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:13
claim: Bill every four weeks instead of monthly — 13 cycles a year adds 8.3% revenue for no extra work.
detail: Present the price weekly if you like ($50/week) but charge $200 every four weeks; don't actually bill weekly (card changes and one-week pause requests become a headache). Hormozi never saw take rates differ between four-weekly and monthly. Called the highest value-per-word point of the course.
numbers: +8.3% revenue; at 20% profit margin, profit goes to ~28% (~41% more profit).

### MM-28#11 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:57
claim: Quote prices as "X plus a 3% processing fee" — Hormozi never lost a buyer over it, and it drops straight to profit.
detail: The extra 3% of top line needs no extra work. In a 10% net margin business it adds 30% to profit. It also becomes a bargaining chip for the next tactic.
numbers: +3% revenue; 10% margin → 13% (+30% profit).

### MM-28#12 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:53
claim: Drop the processing fee if the customer gives a second payment method to keep on file — the reduction in involuntary churn exceeds the 3% you forgo.
detail: Subscription businesses suffer heavily from failed payments: cards get replaced or expire (new numbers, fraud, moving house), hit their limit, or lack funds. Script: "Want the 3% fee gone? Simply add a backup payment method, in case the first one ever fails." If they ask why: the fee covers the cost of chasing updated payment details, so you pass the saving along. If involuntary churn falls ~20%, that is worth more than 3%.
numbers: ~20% churn reduction vs 3% fee.

### MM-28#13 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:53
claim: Make the second payment method ACH (direct bank debit) where possible — bank accounts change far less often than cards and rarely decline.
detail: ACH (automated clearing house) is how banks pay each other and is offered by most processors. People change banks much less often than cards; it either has the money or not. Ideal pairing: one card, one ACH, obtained by waiving the processing fee. ACH processing is also cheaper.
numbers: ACH ~1–2 percentage points cheaper to process.

### MM-28#14 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: lead-getters | fw: Continuity Discount Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:39
claim: Deliver the free time as a physical gift card redeemable after the first few payments — every outcome is good for the business.
detail: Mail it to remote customers and frame it as giftable to a friend. Outcomes: they redeem it later (you already have payments and a verified card), they give it to a friend (a free new customer — apply their discount another way, e.g., spread), or they forget it (a full-price signup). Functionally like a rebate.
numbers: Redeemable after the first 3 payments (example).

### MM-28#15 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: pricing | fw: Lifetime Discount | novelty: canon | ts: 00:13:28
claim: Offer a lifetime discount that kicks in at your most common churn month, advertise it up front, and count down to it.
detail: Amazon rice subscription: 5% off for subscribing, 15% off after five months to the same address — likely set just past where people typically drop off. Advertising it lets you charge more up front (buyers know it will fall) and keeps them longer. At months two and three remind them: "two months left", "one month left."
numbers: Amazon 5% → 15% after 5 months.

### MM-28#16 | FRAMEWORK | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: Continuity Discount Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:15:14
claim: Four cancellation approaches trade signups against stick rate: cancel during trial, zero cancellations, prorated savings fee, or a one-time breakup fee.
detail: Cancel-in-trial allowed: more sales, lower stick. Zero cancellations: fewer sales, higher stick — but only with good customers; with unenforceable customers (Netflix doesn't try) you get the cost without the benefit. Prorated fee: cancel at month six after two free months → pay back what was saved. Breakup fee: a flat amount (e.g., two months) whenever they leave — raises both sales and stick, earns money on exit and is a negotiating chip. Lighter terms → more signups but more leave; stricter → fewer signups, more stay.

### MM-28#17 | DECISION_RULE | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: Continuity Discount Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:16:12
claim: Decide the cancellation policy in advance; Hormozi's favourite is a cancellation fee equal to the discount they received.
detail: Common options: 30- or 60-day notice, cancellation fees, cancel anytime. Since everyone enters continuity on some discount, "cancel any time by paying back the discount" is simple: $600 discount → $600 to leave early; three free months → pay for three months. Have them initial it at signup.
numbers: Fee = discount received (e.g., $600).

### MM-28#18 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:17:22
claim: Make it obvious how to cancel and how to contact you — customers with nowhere to complain inside will complain outside.
detail: A clear process prevents one-star reviews and lets you talk to and possibly save the customer; hidden cancellation makes people vanish and complain publicly. Small businesses don't get rich by making things hard for customers — it loses more money.

### MM-28#19 | SCRIPT | topic: retention-ltv | also: objections-closing, money-models | fw: Fee or Feedback | novelty: canon | ts: 00:18:24
claim: Fee or feedback: offer to waive the cancellation fee if they sit down for an exit interview, then let them vent, out-anger them, own the mistake and offer a do-over.
detail: Say: "I can charge the cancellation fee, or if you'll tell me what we could do better, I'll waive it." Not a mandatory in-person cancellation. In the conversation: no immediate pitch; let them vent and feel validated; be more upset than they are (the angry-boat idea) — once they calm you down, you've won and they often save themselves. Say it was your mistake and unacceptable, ask to make it up, fix solvable problems. If they wanted more, rollover upsell to a higher tier — many who complain about a cheap offer want pricier features. Feedback improves the product either way (00:19:04).
