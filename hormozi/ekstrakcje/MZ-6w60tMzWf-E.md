### MZ-6w60tMzWf-E#01 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: verticals, unit-economics | fw: Cost to Acquire Talent | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:46
claim: A trade show booth builder can't hire welders at $50/hour while each welder yields about $200,000 gross profit, so pay $150,000-$180,000.
detail: Company: guaranteed-deadline trade show booths, $10M revenue, 30% margin, aiming at $20-25M. A new welder sells about $500k a year, materials about 30%, leaving about $350k before labor. Market for certified welders reaches $85/hour. Offer the higher rate in ads.

### MZ-6w60tMzWf-E#02 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: verticals, finance-exit | fw: Cost to Acquire Talent | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:30
claim: A dentist earning $700k gross profit per dentist should pay a $150k headhunter fee instead of $2,500 a month in ads.
detail: Practice: $20M revenue, $4.6M take-home. Hiring four dentists at $150k each drops profit to about $4M in year one, then adds about $2.8M profit a year (four times $700k, inferred). He compares it to an investment returning $2.8M a year for $600k.
numbers: Gross profit per dentist $700k a year; fee $150k per hire; extra profit $2.8M a year.

### MZ-6w60tMzWf-E#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: hiring-talent | also: unit-economics | fw: Cost to Acquire Talent | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:06:38
claim: Lifetime gross profit per employee to cost of acquiring talent (LTGPE:CAT) mirrors LTGP:CAC for staff.
detail: Compute what a hire earns the firm per year times expected tenure, against recruiter fee plus total pay. Example: six-year tenure, $20k recruiter, $150k pay a year, about $1M cost (his arithmetic is rough) against $200k gross profit a year, so it pays back.
numbers: Example: tenure 6 years; cost about $0.9M-$1M; gross profit about $1.2M.
