### MZ-0QoFlch7N1k#01 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: verticals | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A Dallas-area foundation contractor (helical piers, drilling, foam jacking) with $5M revenue and $1.2M profit, tracking to $7.2M and $2.1-2.3M, wants $10M profit on roughly $30M revenue.
detail: It manufactures the piers to engineer standards, installs them, sells them to other installers and does foundation repair.
numbers: Revenue $5M, profit $1.2M; this year $7.2M revenue, $2.1M-$2.3M profit; goal $10M profit, $25M-$35M revenue.

### MZ-0QoFlch7N1k#02 | CASE | topic: product-delivery | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:31
claim: The drilling line is capital intensive, hard to train, high in insurance risk and takes most management time, yet it brings only about 5% of profit.
detail: Owners feared cutting it because it once provided most leads (20-30%, now about 5%) and "gateway" work. Hormozi's figures: about 20% of revenue, 5% of profit, 95-96% of headaches. Advice: crank up the good lines to create a supply constraint, backfill with the drilling staff, and sunset drilling.
numbers: Drilling about 20% of revenue, 5% of profit, about 95% of headaches; lead share fell from 20-30% to 5%.
