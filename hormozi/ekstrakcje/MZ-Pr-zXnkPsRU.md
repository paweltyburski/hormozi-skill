### MZ-Pr-zXnkPsRU#01 | CASE | topic: operator-mindset | also: verticals, strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A firefighter captain who runs a San Diego design-build ADU company asks when to leave his $168k job.
detail: Design margin about 80%, construction about 30%, supply-constrained by tradespeople, never marketed, partnered with his father at 70-30 (could be 50-50). He works about ten days a month at the fire station.
numbers: Revenue $5M, goal $10M; net about $1.2M; fire department pay $168,000 a year; business share about $600k.

### MZ-Pr-zXnkPsRU#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:33
claim: Quit when the added top line needed to replace the salary is within reach: salary doubled for the split, divided by margin.
detail: Replacing $168k at a 50-50 split needs about $340k of profit; at roughly 25% margin that is about four times, or $1.2-1.5M extra revenue. The caller said he could do that.
numbers: Break-even about $340k extra profit; margin about 25%; extra revenue $1.2-1.5M.
### MZ-Pr-zXnkPsRU#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:35
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: Hormozi suggested that ten more full days a month would probably let him figure out some of the constraints, and that becoming less productive after leaving the job would be a double net negative.
detail: For this caller, who said he wants to quit, thinks he can grow the business and is afraid, Hormozi's read was that the other reasons he gave were rationalisation. He said leaving the job would be a net negative if the caller became less productive.
