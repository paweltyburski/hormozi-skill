### YT-ZTSI3DDP_4A#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:48
claim: The Algorithm (a book by Tesla's former president on Elon's approach to manufacturing) has five steps: question requirements, delete, simplify, accelerate, automate.
detail: He made it mandatory for everyone reporting to him. Order matters: automation comes last, once the process has been stripped and sped up.
### YT-ZTSI3DDP_4A#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:48
claim: To find waste, ask how many hours of real work a task contains and compare that with how long it takes to deliver.
detail: If the work is 10 hours but delivery takes a week, everything beyond the 10 hours is uncaptured value. Then ask why the steps must happen in that order and why each requirement exists.
numbers: Example: 10 hours of work versus a one-week delivery.

### YT-ZTSI3DDP_4A#04 | SCRIPT | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:27
claim: Challenge any requirement with three questions: what does that mean, how do you know that, and so what.
detail: Meaning forces the team to spell out the step. Evidence checks whether there is any proof it changes results. "So what" asks why it matters. His colour-grading example failed the evidence question.

### YT-ZTSI3DDP_4A#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: content-brand | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:27
claim: Colour grading was dropped from video production because his best videos were not graded and some graded ones flopped.
detail: A raw, phone-shot plane video, made in anger at the team, became a big hit, which showed that substance beats production. He applies the same to backgrounds and makeup: do not major in the minors.

### YT-ZTSI3DDP_4A#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:27
claim: Delete so aggressively that you have to add some things back.
detail: It is very unlikely that a step in a process is an irreversible door, so you can add things back; everyone is afraid of deleting. Rule of thumb borrowed from Musk.
### YT-ZTSI3DDP_4A#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:14
claim: Compare a step's share of total cost with its share of output quality, and drop it if the cost share is far larger, since a team with other work has an opportunity cost.
detail: Example: if colour grading takes 4 of 10 hours (40% of cost) yet adds perhaps 5% to quality, it is not worth it. Producing 1.5 videos at 95% quality beats one at 100%.
numbers: 4 of 10 hours (40% of cost) for roughly 5% of quality (illustrative).

### YT-ZTSI3DDP_4A#08 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:06
claim: End each meeting with who does what by when, then test the deadline against the time on task.
detail: Ask how much real time it takes and what they are doing in the meantime that matters more. If nothing, shorten the deadline. Shorter communication cycles (daily rather than weekly) are claimed to multiply team speed by about 7x. Vendors can be pulled in by checking progress hourly.
numbers: Weekly to daily update cycle: roughly 7x faster (his claim).

### YT-ZTSI3DDP_4A#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:29
claim: Eliminating a task is more efficient than automating it, so automate only after steps one to four.
detail: AI access tempts people to automate things that should not exist. Litmus test: if AI usage costs rise month after month while views, leads or revenue do not, the automation is wasted spend.

### YT-ZTSI3DDP_4A#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:38
claim: In a trust economy, content that sells is education that demonstrates expertise on many typical customers and conditions, which makes instructions easy to follow and credible.
detail: Entertainment-only creators become media businesses that sell attention to advertisers. For people selling their own product or service, education is the door. People follow instructions only if they understand them (plain words) and believe the payoff is worth the risk.
