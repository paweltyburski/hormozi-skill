### YT-BMF2fWHyDrg#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: unit-economics | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:32
claim: Use the returning-customer slice as a profitability litmus test: if free repeat sales alone would not make money, the cause is either high fixed costs or a pricing problem.
detail: Hormozi imagines removing all new customers and keeping only the repeat revenue, which cost nothing to acquire. That slice should be the most profitable part of the business. If it still would not clearly turn a profit, the cause is either high fixed costs (he asked about them at the start; the owner said they are not too bad) or a possible pricing problem, which he says he is zeroing in on.

### YT-BMF2fWHyDrg#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: product-delivery | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:47
claim: With a large SKU count, ask whether products are made to order: inventory can be a huge cash cost, while made-to-order SKUs are merely options.
detail: He worries about many SKUs because stocked inventory ties up cash (his supplement company orders 8-16 weeks ahead). When goods are made to order from raw materials bought as needed, the many SKUs are then just optionality, and what remains to judge is operational efficiency, meaning how hard it is to make all the different items.

### YT-BMF2fWHyDrg#04 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:09
claim: Screen a small acquisition for key-man risk, customer concentration and a clear reason to buy; a retiring owner plus 70% of revenue from one client is a pass.
detail: Hormozi's voice-over cites Acquisition.com's value-acceleration scorecard (raise value, cut risk). Red flags: a retiring owner who quietly holds undocumented know-how, and one customer giving most revenue who may leave with him. Third test: why bother, which problem the purchase solves. A better raw-material supplier is not reason enough to buy a company; instead try to learn who the seller's overseas vendors are.

### YT-BMF2fWHyDrg#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:25:11
claim: Price on willingness to pay, not cost-plus; vendor cost increases should not set your price.
detail: Hormozi says every cost-plus business he knows earns little. Under value-based pricing the selling price is separate from what goods cost, so supplier increases need not trigger price changes. Two ways in: a steady small increase every month (5-6%), which takes no thought; or split tests, roughly 80/20, on the fastest-selling items, which yield profit faster.

### YT-BMF2fWHyDrg#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:26:06
claim: Keep raising price while units sold times gross profit per unit keeps rising; a drop in sales velocity alone is not a reason to stop.
detail: Owners panic when fewer people buy, but profit usually rises before it falls. His example: doubling a price that triples or quadruples margin is worth it even if a quarter fewer customers buy. If a test goes too far, lower the price again. For this owner only, he says he would start the price test on the gag gifts, which he judged much less price-sensitive than the recurring and car-show buyers.

### YT-BMF2fWHyDrg#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:50:26
claim: At a ~6% net margin a 6% price rise roughly doubles profit; owners who have not raised prices 50%+ over the recent 3-4 years of inflation are earning much less.
detail: Hormozi notes that low-margin firms gain most from small price moves: 5-10% improvements double or triple profit, and a 25% rise could multiply profit 4-5x. Older businesses tend to change prices too rarely. Pricing is the change with the least operational drag: you edit a number or say a bigger figure.

### YT-BMF2fWHyDrg#08 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:32:55
claim: Buy search ads on your own brand terms and their variants, then on related long-tail terms.
detail: Hormozi says he has rarely seen anyone fail to profit from owning their own branded keywords; the objection of paying for a click you would have got anyway fails because you do not always get it. Intent-based search suits niche gift queries that nobody looks for while scrolling social feeds. He rates it a one-time setup worth perhaps a 10-20% boost.

### YT-BMF2fWHyDrg#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:30:35
claim: There is no posting-frequency ceiling; the limit is how many good posts you can make.
detail: Platforms want to spread content to hold attention, and each post reaches only 1-2% of an audience, so more posts reach other people rather than repeating to the same ones. His example is a Bollywood page posting about 100 times a day. For trend-driven brands he suggests a young hire who knows current memes.

### YT-BMF2fWHyDrg#10 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: paid-ads, lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:37:36
claim: Run a TikTok loop: post organic content, put ad money behind the winners, send winners to affiliate creators as models, and boost and copy what they make.
detail: About 10% of posts go viral and 90% flop. Winning pieces get ad spend and are sent to influencers with "make things like this"; their versions inform your own next iterations. Keep it hook-centric and reuse a winning hook many times. Repurpose the same clips on Instagram. Low-price impulse products ($20-$50) suit TikTok Shop.

### YT-BMF2fWHyDrg#11 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: Buy X Get Y Free | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:41:39

### YT-BMF2fWHyDrg#12 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:43:15

### YT-BMF2fWHyDrg#13 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:45:08
claim: Build two email streams, behaviour-triggered flows and long-term nurture, starting weekly and adding frequency while deliverability and engagement stay healthy.
