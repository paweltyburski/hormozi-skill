### POD-0443#01 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: finance-exit | fw: Virtuous Cycle of Price | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A portfolio company went from about $250,000 to over $500,000 monthly profit in under eight weeks by adding 50% to its price and changing nothing in the product.
detail: Research on similar services suggested the market could bear it. Sales rose, not fell, because the higher price signalled value and fitted the founder's brand. A further bump was planned. A later passage mentions a plan to double the price after bigger rivals with similar value were seen charging far more (unclear how the two relate).
numbers: ~$250K to $500K+/month profit; +50% price; under 8 weeks.

### POD-0443#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:32
claim: With 100 customers at $100/month, $8,000 costs and 33% churn, doubling the price lifts profit from $2,000 to $12,000 (6x), doubling customers to $4,000, and doubling retention gives a little more than $4,000.
detail: Doubling retention beats doubling customers because the same acquisition spend now buys customers worth twice as much. Doubling price adds $10,000 of pure profit because fulfilment cost is unchanged.
numbers: 100 customers; $100/month; $8,000 cost; $2,000 profit; doubling price = $12,000 profit.

### POD-0443#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:22
claim: A customer's last paying month is usually more profitable than the first because acquisition and onboarding costs fall on the start.
detail: So doubling lifetime gross profit per customer lets you afford a higher acquisition cost than adding customers does.

### POD-0443#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:40
claim: Let the math decide: even if doubling the price cuts unit sales by a third, profit still rises because margin per sale is much higher.
detail: With profit per customer up 6x and two thirds of the volume, profit roughly triples. Raising price is often how the maximum profit point is found, though not always, and demand usually does not fall in proportion.
numbers: 2x price, one third fewer sales, ~3x profit.

### POD-0443#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:43
claim: People split-test pages, scripts and offers but forget to test price, the strongest of the three growth levers (customers, frequency, price).
detail: Positioning how you charge influences what price works. A Veblen good is one where a higher price raises demand because price itself confers value.

### POD-0443#07 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:46
claim: Raise price for new customers only, grandfather existing ones, announce the coming increase to clear the pipeline, and allow at least two months for sales to settle.
detail: The pre-increase rush pulls sales forward and leaves a dip next month, so judge only after the dip unless you are bombing on the phones.
numbers: At least 2 months of adjustment.

### POD-0443#08 | SCRIPT | topic: pricing | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:10:38
claim: Run a four-question price survey on customers: price too high to consider, price so low you doubt it works, price that is a stretch but you would buy, price that is a bargain.
detail: The four answers give a willingness-to-pay band, and you graph them. Add questions on revenue, employees and industry to segment, since the same result is worth more to some customers (5% more return could be worth $5M to a wealthy investor and $100K to another), then build tiers per avatar.

### POD-0443#09 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: Value vs Willingness-to-Pay Matrix | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:55
claim: Extend the survey with two questions over a list of 20 or so deliverables: which one would you keep if only one remained, and which one could go without changing your decision.
detail: With 100 to 200 responses you see value clusters and can plot a demand curve. Multiply customers who would buy at a price by gross margin to find the profit-maximising price, and do the same at the bargain price for market share.

### POD-0443#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: pricing, finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:28
claim: Choose the price point to match strategy: a platform may price low as a loss leader for share, while an owner building a cash-flowing asset aims at the profit point and grows slowly to enterprise value.
detail: With a survey you stop guessing at price.

### POD-0443#16 | SCRIPT | topic: pricing | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:28

detail: Show that the money builds infrastructure and not just your pocket; mention what you "would like" to do, not promises.

### POD-0443#17 | SCRIPT | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:16
claim: To raise prices on existing customers, send a personal hand-signed letter listing improvements and plans, with a postscript offering help if the new price truly strains their basic needs.
detail: Most readers conclude it will not apply to them. If someone objects, ask whether they cannot afford groceries or rent; if not, point out that paying less means you deliver less and invest less, and most accept.

### POD-0443#18 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:23:20
claim: Hold the moral high ground: if someone says you should not raise your price, ask whether they want you to stop fulfilling your promises.
detail: The argument is that better service needs more money, and a promise made publicly is kept. If they cannot afford it, that is acceptable.
