### YT-iFhiK5zORGk#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: promotions-launches | fw: Scarcity | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:53
claim: Chanel creates absolute scarcity by giving each store one or two bags, hiding what will arrive, and not selling online.
detail: Stores do not know the delivery or when production stops, so every bag is effectively limited edition, which supports higher prices.

### YT-iFhiK5zORGk#02 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: promotions-launches | fw: Scarcity | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:29
claim: At a charity event, tickets rose from $10,000 to $25,000 and more were sold, which he attributes to scarcity shifting demand.
detail: He donated $1M that night after the event's staging. The lesson: constraining supply shifted the demand curve, so people who might wait felt pressed to buy.
numbers: $10,000 to $25,000 per ticket, with higher unit sales.

### YT-iFhiK5zORGk#03 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:25
claim: Selling three seats at $100,000 a day beats five at $20,000 and is easier to deliver.
detail: At $20,000 for five people, the top buyers might have paid $200,000 each. Fewer clients means lower operational drag and more over-delivery. Most people cut supply but keep the old price.
numbers: 3 x $100,000 = $300,000 vs 5 x $20,000 = $100,000; top buyers maybe $200,000.

### YT-iFhiK5zORGk#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:13
claim: Marketing shifts the demand curve, and textbook supply and demand ignores this, so scarcity plus demand creation lets you raise price and still sell more.
detail: Most entrepreneurs work on supply rather than demand. Also, relief at hearing the price means you could have charged more.

### YT-iFhiK5zORGk#05 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: promotions-launches | fw: Whisper Tease Shout | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:30
claim: Declare yourself full, keep a waitlist, and release capacity periodically so pressure, which is pricing power, keeps building.
detail: Capacity is your choice, even zero. Tease and hint at what is coming, delaying satisfaction; the waitlist signals quality. Releasing steam constantly stops pressure building.
