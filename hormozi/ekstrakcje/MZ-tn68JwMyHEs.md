### MZ-tn68JwMyHEs#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Anyone can learn anything, but whether to train a person is a return-on-resources question: hire the person with the smallest net skill deficiency.
detail: Even a weak candidate could become a surgeon, yet it is not worth his time. When a hire lacks a skill, choose between training them and buying someone who has it.

### MZ-tn68JwMyHEs#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:41
claim: He rejects "hire for attitude, train for skill" because attitude is a set of trainable behaviours.
detail: "Be charismatic" gives no instruction; convert it into specific behaviours (speak louder, greet people by name, handshake, eye contact, repeat their last words, nod) and people then read as warm.

### MZ-tn68JwMyHEs#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:56
claim: Training cost decides what to hire for: a frozen-yogurt cashier can be taught in an hour or two, so hire for social behaviour; an AI researcher cannot, so tolerate a charisma gap.
detail: Past register experience matters less than the social skill bucket.
numbers: Register about 5 minutes, mopping about 5 minutes, whole role about 1-2 hours.

### MZ-tn68JwMyHEs#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Pushing an unqualified person forward is unkind; real care means early feedback that they do not yet meet the requirement.
detail: When a hire cannot do the job, ask whether the posting was vague, the candidate overstated, or both, then show the exact behaviours.
