### YT-mOKl6dodMTg#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:51
claim: When a rule caps session size, reprice for the smaller group instead of reopening at the old price.
detail: A cap of 10 people per session includes the coach and often a helper, so about 8 clients; delivering one-to-eight at a $100-per-month price does not pay. With roughly 50 clients servable a day, a normal $129-$150 unlimited plan should become a higher-priced plan.
numbers: Cap 10 people per session, ~8 clients; reprice to about $300-$400 per month for one-to-eight service.
applies_to: Group-training gyms under capacity limits

### YT-mOKl6dodMTg#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:06
claim: After a closure, the clients who return are eager, so supply and demand favour a price increase.
detail: Not everyone comes back, but those who do want it badly, and there are more people wanting sessions than there are session slots. That gives room to raise prices to match the new level of service.

### YT-mOKl6dodMTg#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:51
claim: Each point of gross margin moves a gym's bottom line sharply, because the average gym nets only about 12.5%.
detail: Moving from 75% to 83% gross margin adds about 8 points to profit, taking a 12% net margin to about 20%, almost double.
numbers: Average gym net margin 12.5% per year; 75% to 83% gross margin adds ~8 points of profit.
applies_to: Gyms

### YT-mOKl6dodMTg#04 | TACTIC | topic: unit-economics | also: product-delivery | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:44
claim: Work out how many sessions you can run from recurring revenue, a payroll budget and the cost of a session.
detail: Take confirmed monthly recurring revenue, allow 20% of it for delivery (at 80% gross margin), divide by the cost per session for sessions per month, then divide by 4.3 weeks for sessions per week. Rent and admin are not part of this delivery cost.
numbers: 4.3 weeks per month; delivery cost at most 20% of revenue.

### YT-mOKl6dodMTg#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:44
claim: A gym with 100 returning members at $100 a month can afford about 23 sessions a week.
detail: Of 150 prior members, 100 agree to return, giving $10,000 recurring. At 80% margin $2,000 funds fulfilment; at $20 a session that is 100 sessions a month, so 23 a week. Schedule four sessions a day Monday to Friday (two morning, two afternoon) and three on Saturday morning.
numbers: 150 prior, 100 returning, $10,000 per month, $2,000 payroll, $20 per session, 23 sessions per week.

### YT-mOKl6dodMTg#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:37
claim: Plan only on billing that actually goes through, not on members' promises to return.
detail: Some cards may not go through and some people may wait or not come back. Start billing, see what clears, then set the schedule if you can. If you cannot, take previous revenue, subtract the cancellations and then another 10% as a safety margin.
numbers: Extra 10% safety cut.
### YT-mOKl6dodMTg#07 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:06
claim: Reopen with the fewest sessions you can, not as many as there are clients.
detail: Adding overhead to give every client a slot (10 sessions a day) destroys margin and is painful to reverse. Start at two sessions a day with about 90% margin and add. Cut sessions that never had good attendance.
numbers: Start at 2 sessions per day, ~90% margin.

### YT-mOKl6dodMTg#08 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:55
claim: Switch the main offer from unlimited to two or three sessions a week to double or triple capacity.
detail: Fewer sessions per client means smaller groups, so he frames the cut as added value through more personal, controlled sessions, which supports a higher price. Keeping the old price while giving more individual service does not pay by his math.
