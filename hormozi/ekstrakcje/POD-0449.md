### POD-0449#01 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:51
claim: An owner with $5M revenue and $2M profit planned to pay tax and invest the rest in property, stocks and crypto to reach a net worth goal.
detail: She expected about $1.2M left after tax. Hormozi saw this as missing where wealth actually forms: the value of the business she owns.
numbers: $5M revenue, $2M profit, about $1.2M after tax (her estimate).

### POD-0449#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:49
claim: You do not make a billion, you own something worth a billion; you are paid for what you do and earn returns on what you own.
detail: Billionaire wealth is equity value, not cumulative take-home pay.

### POD-0449#03 | EVOLUTION | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:00
claim: His model of billionaire wealth went through three stages.
detail: First, earn a billion every year; second, take home $2B pre-tax over a lifetime; now, own an asset valued at a multiple of earnings.

### POD-0449#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:41
claim: One extra million of profit adds ten times more owner wealth through valuation than through take-home pay.
detail: $5M profit at 10x is $50M; at $6M it is $60M, a $10M gain with no tax until sale. Take-home after 40% tax rises only from about $3M to $3.5M.
numbers: 10x multiple; value $50M to $60M; after-tax take-home $3M to $3.5M.

### POD-0449#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:57
claim: Put focus into growing the main business, not into side investments of its cash.
detail: Matching a $10M valuation gain by investing the $3M take-home would need extraordinary returns and full-time effort; the business already has full-time attention.

### POD-0449#06 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:34

detail: He says companies of that size tend to trade around 15x. At a 30% margin he says about $6M revenue a week (arithmetic at $1.5M profit gives nearer $5M).
numbers: $70M x 15 = about $1B; 30% margin.
### POD-0449#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:08

detail: He says he would never pursue an opportunity capped at a hundred million dollars a year, because a $10 million per year business is easier, in the context of thinking in bigger increments than a six-figure goal.

### POD-0449#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:58
claim: A bigger goal changes the opportunities you pick: not limited by your time, large market, high gross margin, long horizon.
detail: Small goals cap effort at 100% and are measured in tiny units; an oversized goal means 20% of full effort already beats a modest target.

### POD-0449#09 | TACTIC | topic: unit-economics | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:58
claim: Convert an income goal to daily activity by working backward from price and close rate.
detail: $10M at a $10,000 price needs 1,000 sales a year (about three a day); at one close per three calls that is nine calls a day; then find the cost per call.
numbers: $10M, $10,000 price, 3 sales and 9 calls per day, 1-in-3 close (illustrative).

### POD-0449#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:01
claim: To sell something for $10,000, deliver about ten times that in value to the buyer.
detail: If nothing sells at $10,000, find what makes someone else $100,000. Low earnings trace to too little value delivered.
numbers: Fee about 10% of value created.
