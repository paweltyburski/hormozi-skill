### POD-0448#01 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: offers, sales-process | fw: Bonus Checklist | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: In one-to-one selling, hold bonuses back and reveal the one that answers an objection when the prospect raises it.
detail: Bonuses also let the buyer receive more than promised, which makes happy customers.

### POD-0448#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:00
claim: When a prospect says "I need to think about it," ask the simplest question in sales: what is your main concern, what are you afraid of, what would a bad outcome look like?
detail: This pulls their fears out into the open.

### POD-0448#03 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:00
claim: Concern loop: normalise the fear, show how it is prevented, check "fair enough?", close; if refused, ask "what else concerns you?" and repeat.
detail: Steps: (1) hear the fear; (2) say every customer has the same one; (3) give a short anecdote of how you solved it; (4) re-present the offer. Keep cycling through concerns until none remain.

### POD-0448#05 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: content-brand | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:18
claim: You are giving away the right amount when it makes you a little anxious about how much value you hand out.
detail: Holding back the ask as long as possible lets goodwill compound faster than revenue; he uses free large-platform products as analogues.

### POD-0448#08 | TACTIC | topic: verticals | also: strategy-positioning, pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:10:03
claim: Skill-training models: train a media buyer or similar role and sell them for a one-time fee, or sell a staffing arrangement with a monthly fee plus a low wage.
detail: Example prices: $10,000 once for a trained media buyer, then roughly $200 a month for the assistant; or $1,000 a month for an assigned full-time rep while you also pay the person about $200 a month. The second is a staffing play with good enterprise value and sticky clients if the people are good. The margin is the gap between what the assistant earns and what the owner would normally pay.
numbers: $10,000 one-time; $200/month assistant pay; $1,000/month staffing fee.

### POD-0448#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: leads-fundamentals, hiring-talent | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:12
claim: From zero to $1M pick one lead channel and master it; at maybe around $3M a year, you can hire someone who has already run another channel for a similar business, rather than paying someone to learn it.
detail: At the start you have no leverage; later you can fund a specialist to build the second channel and a team around it.
numbers: Thresholds ~$1M and maybe ~$3M/year.
