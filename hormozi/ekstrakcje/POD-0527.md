claim: Acquisition.com's portfolio is internet-based businesses, about 80% e-learning and training and 20% generic services, with a $3M minimum size.

### POD-0527#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: hiring-talent | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:54
claim: Going from $3M to $30M typically needs two layers of leadership: usually one high-level executive plus a manager-level team to reach roughly $10-15M, and then both layers to reach $30M.
detail: Companies at $3M are typically missing even the completed first layer. This is the people constraint; the others are offer structure, data and culture.
numbers: First leadership layer usually needed by ~$10-15M; second layer needed for $30M.
### POD-0527#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:47
claim: In $3M-plus internet businesses the offer, product, packaging and pricing are usually unoptimised: they undercharge or charge for the wrong things.
detail: Ask what is actually valuable to the customer and who is actually buying, because the people in the Facebook group or CRM may differ from the people who buy. Often the whole offer structure has to be rebuilt.

### POD-0527#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:43
claim: Where to hire depends on the founder type: a product-driven founder usually lacks sales, marketing and tracking, while a promotion-driven founder needs the back of the house cleaned up.
detail: Product-driven founders often have no clear customer journey, do not measure time to value or NPS, and lack customer health scores (predictive metrics for lifetime value). Find the constraint, then attack it ruthlessly end to end.

### POD-0527#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: outreach | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:12
claim: Inquiring internet companies typically run paid ads but lack organic reach because the product is not yet good enough, and few have real outbound.
detail: Running ads that lead to DMs is not outbound. Outbound takes about eight months to work and needs operational discipline. Their paid ads are also often off-brand and not calling out the right person.
numbers: Outbound ramp ~8 months.

### POD-0527#06 | BENCHMARK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:58
claim: In the first year they mostly fill holes, and that alone tends to triple a company's profit before the big initiatives start.
detail: They mostly fill holes first (e.g. paid marketing that is off-brand or not targeting the right person); the profit gain is almost by accident.
numbers: Profit about 3x in year one from filling holes.
### POD-0527#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:43
claim: Fixing a stuck $20-30M company starts from negative, because it carries cultural, management and technical debt, whereas a $3-10M company starts at zero.
detail: Paying down that debt means restructuring teams, moving people out and fixing compensation before growth resumes. This work builds the enterprise value that makes a business sellable.
numbers: One company stayed roughly flat for about 9 months, then tripled in the next 6 months.

### POD-0527#08 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:29
claim: Minority deals are structured as a share of cash flow plus profit interests, aligned to the founder's own exit wish, with a goal of tripling the company within five years.
detail: Unlike traditional PE, no drag-along clause forces a sale; incentive is balanced between holding and selling. The founder's desired path is clarified in the first two weeks. Most targets are profitable, but the founder is "a thousand hands" so valuation would be low; they need expertise more than cash.
numbers: 3x in 5 years; alignment settled in first 2 weeks.

### POD-0527#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:11
claim: The four assumptions behind the venture: grow your own reach, invest in markets growing faster than the S&P 500, be able to add real value, and partner only with exemplary people.
detail: Plan: a first pod of 15-25 companies, capped, then possibly pods for software or e-commerce. They would sell the "eggs" (spun-off companies), not the "goose" (the holding firm). Later idea: after five years of returns on a pod, raise outside or audience money to buy more of those companies.
numbers: Pod size 15-25 businesses; five years of returns before raising.

### POD-0527#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:21
claim: He chose a purely inbound, organic model for the firm because a minority stake rests on trust, and inbound customers are easier-going, give more influence and attract better talent than volatile paid acquisition.
detail: Without majority control he cannot force decisions, so founders must trust his advice; a stable acquisition method also allows hiring better talent. It takes longer to build but supports the business underneath.

### POD-0527#11 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:05
claim: His content routine: batch long-form videos, test ideas on Twitter, repurpose to short video and Instagram, and increasingly film the business itself rather than create separate content.
detail: Long-form videos 2-3 a week come from one recording day every two weeks. Twitter stream-of-consciousness shows what interests people and seeds the short-form scripts. An operator partner should have content captured by a team, not create direct-to-camera, which does not scale.
numbers: 2-3 videos/week from one day every two weeks (4-6 videos per day).

### POD-0527#12 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: retention-ltv | fw: Ascension Path | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:25:46
claim: Hormozi's sequence, which he says is usual: roughly $0-3M is product-market fit, $3-10M is cleaning up the back end, and then attack new acquisition channels, since the extra margin lets you be inefficient at scale.
detail: Mistake he names as common: thinking marketing got you here so more marketing is the answer. Without referrals, ascension and LTV work, a marketing-only company has to sell twice as many each month just to stay flat, and it becomes very difficult to scale past maybe 10-ish million. Cleaning up the back end, raising what each customer is worth and how many customers they bring, can still triple the business without more units sold per month.
numbers: Can triple the business from back-end fixes without more monthly units; scaling gets very difficult past maybe ~$10M.
### POD-0527#13 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:28:11
claim: For education products, information loses value quickly after learning, so continuity is easier with consumables (accountability, tech support, community), and it is not uncommon for a year-two price to be lower than year one.
detail: The ongoing value he names is new people entering the network and new material added; he says to price based on the consumable, not the one-time content.
