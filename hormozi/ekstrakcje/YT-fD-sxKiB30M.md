### YT-fD-sxKiB30M#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:13
claim: Business value rises when you flip the risks that depress the multiple into reasons that raise it.
detail: Acquisition.com lists a company's negatives and risks one by one and fixes each, so that what was a discount becomes a pillar of value and counts twice. Examples: finding a reliable customer channel, or hiring a key person for a new territory. He compares it with "forced appreciation" in real estate (renovating) as opposed to waiting for the market.

### YT-fD-sxKiB30M#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:42
claim: The multiple on profit is the market's estimate of risk, meaning how long profit would continue if the owner left.
detail: If profit seems nearly certain to continue after the owner leaves, the multiple is high; if it could vanish in months, it approaches zero, in which case the buyer simply takes it over. Two levers move value: EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation, in practice profit before accounting effects) and the multiple.

### YT-fD-sxKiB30M#03 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Prep for Sale | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:59
claim: A $3M-revenue, $1M-profit business can be bought almost for nothing and become a $40M company in 12-24 months.
detail: Buy it with seller financing and a small deposit; it is small, owner-dependent and volatile, so unattractive to institutions (a local doctor might overpay, which is not real value). Hire a CEO and COO, add a marketing head, update sales ops and raise prices, which funds more marketing. Result in his sketch: $12M revenue, about $5M profit, an 8x multiple.
numbers: Start $3M revenue, $1M profit, down payment around $500K; target $12M revenue, ~$5M profit; 8x gives $40M; 12-24 months.

### YT-fD-sxKiB30M#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:59
claim: Two businesses with equal revenue and profit can differ in value from near zero to tens of millions depending on reliability and growth.
detail: Business one: $10M revenue and $3M profit, down from $20M and $6M, transactional, one acquisition channel, founder runs delivery, marketing and operations: unsellable to outsiders. Business two: revenue $5M, then $10M, pacing $20M, all recurring, expanding margins: about a 12x multiple, roughly $36M. Build a business valuable to anyone, not just to you.
numbers: Business two: $3M profit this year, $6M-$7M next, 12x multiple, $36M value.

### YT-fD-sxKiB30M#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:13:36
claim: Test every use of money or time against three value drivers: more customers, more value per customer, or lower risk.
detail: If you cannot say precisely which of the three an activity improves and how you know, ask whether it is the best use of effort. Most people spend on things that create no value because they do not know where value is made.

### YT-fD-sxKiB30M#06 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:15:09
claim: Five levers raise the multiple: debt capacity, organic growth, categorization, size premium and age.
detail: Debt capacity depends on cash flow, not a fixed down payment, so cash-rich firms can carry much more. Growth: a business that is believed to grow 20% a year doubles in five years, and buyers using leverage multiply their return. Categorization: traditional service, tech-enabled service and SaaS carry rising multiples. Size and age follow in the next atoms.
numbers: With 20% down and a doubling business, buyer return 4x-5x on equity.

### YT-fD-sxKiB30M#07 | EXAMPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:17:34
claim: Investing about $100,000 to move a service firm into tech-enabled delivery can add three turns of multiple.
detail: If streamlined processes and software let staff deliver 5-10 times more, the business may be valued like tech-enabled service. For a business making $1M a year, going from a 5x to an 8x multiple adds $3M of value for $100K invested.
numbers: $100K cost, +3 turns (5x to 8x) on $1M profit = +$3M enterprise value, a 30x return.

### YT-fD-sxKiB30M#08 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:21
claim: A big part of why larger profit earns a higher multiple is that huge funds have minimum cheque sizes; institutions begin to take notice at about $5M of profit and he says crossing $10M gives a bit more on the multiple.
detail: Unlike wholesale goods, businesses get a size premium. A bank contact said their minimum cheque is $150M, so they pay operators to assemble assets. Key-man risk is normal below the institutional level. In his example, waiting a year could take a $25M sale to $75M.
numbers: Institutional interest from ~$5M profit per year; at $5M profit value roughly 25-40 ($M, as stated); $5M to $8M profit lifted a $25M sale to $75M in the example; minimum cheque $150M.
### YT-fD-sxKiB30M#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:08
claim: A business gains value each year it survives and grows, until a decline makes it nearly worthless.
detail: A private-equity managing partner told him a business becomes more valuable every year until it does not. A 10-year-old firm looks safer than a 1-year-old. So keep growing or at least hold.

### YT-fD-sxKiB30M#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:58
claim: A mediocre opportunity pursued for years beats hopping to a new one every six months, because the new one has to catch up and you give up the compounding.
detail: Wealth built this way takes about six years rather than six weeks. Someone four years into a business who jumps loses year five of the old business and must match its year one. Value-adding moves like recategorisation take time.
numbers: Time to generational wealth: about 6 years, by his estimate.
