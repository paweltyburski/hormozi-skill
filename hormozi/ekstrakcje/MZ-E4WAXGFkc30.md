### MZ-E4WAXGFkc30#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A 22-year-old owner of one pizza-and-ice-cream store ($2.4M, 30% profit margin, 500-700 pizzas a day) wants $10M in eight years and must choose between maxing the store or opening a second.
detail: He owns 33% of the first store (with his father), 50% of the second, 100% of later ones, and has hired a GM to shadow him for six months. Hormozi: second location is fine, but nail it then scale it; he lacks data on why customers come.
numbers: $2.4M revenue; 30% margin; 33% ownership of store one; $10M target in 8 years.

### MZ-E4WAXGFkc30#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:08
claim: Hormozi prefers giving the GM three to six months running the flagship while you only do back office; if the flagship dips, he'd know not to open the next store.
detail: Owners who "leave the day to day" often still work 16 hours; the test is that the manager does the whole job and the business holds or grows. Overexpanding leaves problems at both locations, which is harder to undo; he says an extra year now shaves about four years off the back.
numbers: 3-6 months test period.
### MZ-E4WAXGFkc30#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: hiring-talent | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:10
claim: A privately held (not franchised) brick-and-mortar manager typically has a profit share and about $100K on-target pay.
detail: Range: about $75K if weak, $120-130K if they excel. Owner's plan paid 5% for hitting KPIs plus 1% profit share per year, capped at 10% (unclear in audio); Hormozi called it workable but not his choice.
numbers: $75K / ~$100K / $120-130K on-target earnings.

### MZ-E4WAXGFkc30#04 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:10
claim: Set up customer tracking before expanding, since decisions without data carry bigger stakes later.
detail: Use the owner's spare bandwidth during GM training; the owner did not know why customers were coming.
