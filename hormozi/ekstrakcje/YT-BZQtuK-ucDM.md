### YT-BZQtuK-ucDM#01 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:47
claim: Cutting the price of a coaching program by 25% to reduce churn left churn unchanged and cost about $5M a year in profit.
detail: The program sold at about $42,000 a year. His team pushed for a lower price; he cut it and added services, pitched as a big stack. Customers cheered, churn did not move, costs rose and the top line fell.
numbers: Price from ~$800 to $600 a week (-25%); overhead up roughly $100K a month and top line down about $400K a month (figures partly unclear in audio); margin was 70%; income fell by about a third; profit loss about $5M a year.

### YT-BZQtuK-ucDM#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:53
claim: Do not cut prices in hopes of reducing churn; he says most times lowering prices makes you a lot less money.
detail: He reasoned that halving churn would double LTV and justify the cut; churn did not respond. He says that most times lowering prices means making a lot less money.
numbers: Assumed churn 10% to 5% = 2x LTV; actual churn unchanged.
### YT-BZQtuK-ucDM#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:42
claim: A price cut on a 40% margin product needs double the unit sales to hold profit, while a price rise may need far fewer than expected.
detail: On a $100 item with $40 profit, dropping to $80 halves the profit per sale, so volume must double. Raising to $150 lifts profit per sale to $90, so you probably won't lose half your customers; you might lose only a quarter and still make about double the money.
numbers: $100 price, $40 profit; at $80 profit is $20; at $150 profit is $90, with 75% of the volume it is roughly double.
### YT-BZQtuK-ucDM#04 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:49
claim: Moving bootcamp clients from $99 to $299 a month by switching them to a semi-private program lost a third of clients but doubled revenue.
detail:  Clients got two free weeks of the new service, then auto-enrolled, with a note to contact him if it caused real hardship. Fulfilment cost fell because only two thirds of customers had to be served, so profit more than doubled.
numbers: $99 to $299 a month; one third left; revenue ~2x; profit more than 2x.

### YT-BZQtuK-ucDM#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:30
claim: In a service business, add value and charge more to serve fewer people; more value for less price and more people usually loses money.
detail: Fewer customers at higher prices cuts delivery load; adding services while cutting price raises cost and cuts income at once.
