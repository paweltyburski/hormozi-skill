### MZ-FuAg47f5Af0#01 | CASE | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals, money-models | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:13
claim: A coaching business marketed a partner's degree programme on a master services agreement and saw LTV:CAC fall from about 10:1 to 2 to 4:1.
detail: The two entities stay separate: the firm markets and sells, the partner delivers, with a revenue split. The same funnel was sold as a repackaged programme to the same people.
numbers: CAC about $3,000 to $4,000; LTV $10,000 to $12,000; split about 80% to them; earlier CAC $800 to $900 (2020 to 2022).

### MZ-FuAg47f5Af0#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: strategy-positioning | fw: CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:10
claim: Very low CAC is usually a temporary arbitrage that the market catches up with; the lasting arms race is LTV.
detail: CAC across an industry is fairly comparable; $3,000 to $4,000 was benchmark. LTV had to be nearer $20,000 to $30,000.
numbers: Target LTV $20,000 to $30,000 versus $12,000.

### MZ-FuAg47f5Af0#03 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:56
claim: Fix the business back to front: sell a roughly $30,000 next offer to about half the buyers of the $12,000 one.
detail: Half buying a $30,000 thing adds about $15,000 average, making LTV about $27,000 and the funnel work.
numbers: 50% take $30,000; average +$15,000; LTV about $27,000.
