### MZ-qdyqRspscKs#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Coaching is not capped; a business that churns customers every six months must sell more each year just to double.
detail: Doubling means selling twice, then four times, then eight times as many customers, which is unsustainable.

### MZ-qdyqRspscKs#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:40
claim: Blending one-time value and consumable value in one monthly price makes customers feel it is not worth it by month three.
detail: Example: a $30,000 package billed at $2,500 monthly; month one feels worth it, month two brings guilt about leaving, month three feels overpriced.
numbers: $30,000 total value billed at $2,500 per month (example).

### MZ-qdyqRspscKs#03 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals, retention-ltv | fw: Big Head Long Tail | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:56
claim: A petite-women fitness brand sells a 90-day programme for $3,000 then $49 a month; 55% convert, with 10% monthly churn.
detail: She feared reliance on her celebrity, about 200,000 followers. Hormozi liked the model but said you still must keep marketing.
numbers: $3,000 up front; $49/month; 55% conversion to membership; 10% monthly churn; about 80 new clients a month (uncertain).

### MZ-qdyqRspscKs#04 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:39
claim: Present the up-front fee as onboarding and the monthly plan as a second purchase after a gap, so members compare it with the big first price.
detail: The second buying loop means cancelling $50 a month feels foolish after paying $3,000; a gap lowers the drop-off in conversion.
