### POD-251114-05#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: promotions-launches | fw: Penny Gap | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:49
claim: A newspaper ad promising $1,000 back per $100 never drew a response: too good to believe.
detail: A marketer ran it as a believability test. A crazy offer needs a true reason; a going-out-of-business sale makes 90% off in 30 days credible, where bare 90% off would not.
numbers: $1,000 back per $100; 90% off in 30 days.

### POD-251114-05#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: promotions-launches | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:34
claim: Free gives the most leads at the lowest cost per lead from the same audience, which matters most in small local markets.
detail: With limited eyeballs, free gets the largest share raising a hand, then you sell as many as you can.

### POD-251114-05#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: promotions-launches | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:38
claim: Many large consumer companies use free front ends and rely on quality to retain.
detail: Facebook, YouTube, Dropbox storage, Uber first ride, Netflix 30 days, Claire's ear piercing, European Wax Center wax, Public Storage free month.

### POD-251114-05#04 | FRAMEWORK | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: promotions-launches | fw: Create Flow Monetize Add Friction | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:23
claim: When free brings too many leads, add friction five ways: qualification, information asked, steps, forced consumption, ad length.
detail: Friction raises quality and cuts volume, so find the sweet spot. Also add it when the team cannot handle the volume, such as manual daily meetings.

### POD-251114-05#05 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:09
claim: State the qualifications everywhere the prospect looks.
detail: Example: must be over 25, employed and a homeowner. Repeat it in copy, creative and landing pages.

### POD-251114-05#06 | TACTIC | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:09
claim: Information friction depends on the number, type and format of questions.
detail: A 20-question application adds friction; a first name is lighter than a cell number or income; multiple choice is lighter than open-ended answers.
numbers: Example: 20-question application.

### POD-251114-05#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:55
claim: Choose enough steps to remove time-wasters without losing lazy high-value buyers.
detail: A one-step email opt-in beats a five-step form on volume; age check, form, video and self-booking compound the loss.

### POD-251114-05#08 | TACTIC | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:40
claim: Forced consumption raises quality most but pays only when eyeballs are cheap; otherwise place it between steps.
detail: Example: hide the call to action until a 40-minute video plays. Longer ads work similarly: a two-hour video draws fewer but more valuable clicks than a 30-second one.
numbers: 40-minute video; 2-hour versus 30-second ad.

### POD-251114-05#09 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:38
claim: Judge a campaign by qualified leads per dollar: a messier one can win.
detail: $1,000 for 500 leads, half qualified, gives 250 qualified; $1,000 for 200 leads at 80% gives 160. The first is better in dollars.
numbers: 250 qualified versus 160 qualified per $1,000.

### POD-251114-05#10 | BENCHMARK | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: sales-process | fw: Penny Gap | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:36
claim: In four gym split tests of 10 markets each, free and non-free leads closed at the same rate, while going from non-free to free most times cut lead cost by 5x or more.
detail: Close rate and ticket size showed no advantage for non-free offers.
numbers: 4 tests x 10 markets; lead cost down 5x or more (most times).
applies_to: Gyms (local markets).
### POD-251114-05#11 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:21
claim: If premium leads cost 5 to 10 times more, prices should be at least 5 to 10 times higher.
detail: Common mistake: premium offer at free-offer prices. Price, prospect, process, promotion and product all reflect one structure or the other, so comparisons between free and premium are not fair. At equal price, free beats premium unless high-ticket selling is mastered.
numbers: Lead cost 5-10x higher means prices should be at least 5-10x higher.
