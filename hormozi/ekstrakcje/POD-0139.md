### POD-0139#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: Crazy Eight | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:41
claim: Per 1% improvement, price moves profit about four times, churn about twice, and acquisition cost once.
detail: The data comes from a SaaS pricing researcher's analysis of about 11,000 software companies, cited by Hormozi as evidence that price is the strongest profit lever. Upsells also cost about a quarter of acquiring a new customer, making that revenue about four times as valuable.
numbers: 1% price = 4x profit effect; 1% churn = 2x; 1% acquisition cost = 1x; upsell cost about 1/4 of acquisition.

### POD-0139#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:57
claim: Genie comparison: doubling customers doubles profit, halving churn doubles lifetime value, but doubling price at the same cost raises profit sixfold.
detail: Base case $10,000 a month revenue with $2,000 profit (20% margin, $8,000 fulfilment cost). Doubling customers gives about $4,000 profit (costs may rise). Halving churn lets you spend twice on acquisition. Doubling price to $20,000 with the same $8,000 cost gives $12,000 profit.
numbers: $2,000 profit becomes $12,000 (6x) by doubling price at constant cost.

### POD-0139#03 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: Ascension Path | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:32
claim: Bolting remote one-on-one coaching with check-ins on to a gym membership at $200 a month, taken by a third of members, adds about $100K profit a year in a 155-member gym.
detail: The add-on is daily check-ins, food help and twice-monthly in-person visits that also sell supplements. About 51 members take it: roughly $10,200 extra revenue, about $2,550 fulfilment cost (at $50 each), so about $8,200 a month net. It takes about a month to implement, lowers churn through the one-to-one relationship, and funds higher pay for top trainers. No extra square footage.
numbers: 155 members x 1/3 = 51 at $200; cost $50 each; net about $8,200/month or about $100,000/year; implementation about 1 month.
applies_to: Group-training gyms.

### POD-0139#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:41
claim: The average gym earns about $35 profit per member per month, and for a larger training centre roughly $167 a month typically tends to be the point where it makes real profit.
detail: Compute your own: take-home divided by members, e.g. $6,000 over 155 members is about $39. In his worked example profit per member moves from $35 to $54 after the add-on, which he calls 2.5x (the arithmetic is unclear in audio, since $35 plus $54 is about $89).
numbers: About $35 profit/member/month; $167/month ($39/week) typical point of real profit; $6,000 / 155 = about $39.
