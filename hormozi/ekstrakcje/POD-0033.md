### POD-0033#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: "I don't have the money" usually means the pain was not established before the price, so the offer is judged not worth it.
detail: The prospect is saying the solution is not worth their money, not that none exists. The fix starts earlier, in the pre-sale conversation. The script was refined with Nick Sinecki, head of sales at Lady Boss Weight Loss and formerly the top rep at Six Pack Shortcuts (75 reps, over $1M in monthly phone sales).
numbers: 75-person sales force, $1M+ per month (Six Pack Shortcuts).

### POD-0033#02 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: Epiphany Bridge | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:46
claim: Answer the money objection by agreeing, telling a story about someone just like them, and asking permission to share it.
detail: Sequence: agree and say you understand; move the talk away from their circumstance to a person in the identical situation yesterday; say she reached the same conclusion; ask "mind if I share a story?"; tell the "we vote with our dollars" story; show the cost breaks down per day and that the new identity saves money elsewhere (less drinking, junk food, doctor visits); conclude it is a priorities issue, not budget.
numbers: about $10 per day framing.

### POD-0033#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:28
claim: A 14-year-old buying her first makeup kit was told to budget monthly; the purchase marked a new identity and new priorities.
detail: Hormozi saw it at a cosmetics store. The same logic applies to buying coaching: a fit identity carries a new spending priority. Money goes to what we care about, so changing priorities means changing where it goes.

### POD-0033#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:28
claim: Use the identity reframe mainly on recurring offers, where a permanent fix means a permanent cost.
detail: Front-end offers sell on emotion even at high ticket; the resistance is to ongoing fees. A lot of people don't like confronting a permanent cost (he gives himself as an example, saying he would rather pay a big sum for a short period than a smaller monthly amount for a year), so introduce the recurring cost with an identity shift.
numbers: example: $2,000 for 12 weeks preferred over $150 per month for a year.
