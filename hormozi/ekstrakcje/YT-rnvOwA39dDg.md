### YT-rnvOwA39dDg#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:17
claim: US wealth tiers by assets: $1-5M is about 1 in 69 people, $5-30M about 1 in 338, $30M+ about 1 in 1,383, billionaires about 1 in 468,000.
detail: He presents these as four layers above "the top 1%". Top 1% by income starts around $400,000-$440,000 a year, whereas top 1% by assets is only about $1-1.5M, which shows high earners spend what they make.
numbers: high net worth $1-5M (1 in 69, about 1.5%); very high $5-30M investable (1 in 338, 0.3%); ultra high $30M-$1B (1 in 1,383, 0.07%); billionaires 1 in 468,000; top 1% income about $400-440k a year.

### YT-rnvOwA39dDg#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:12:40
claim: How soon you can stop working depends on the share of income you spend, not on the amount you earn.
detail: Each working year buys (savings divided by spending) years of living. Example: $100,000 income, $20,000 spent, $80,000 saved gives four years of living per working year, so about ten years of this builds forty years of expenses (before any investment return). A $400,000 earner saving 10% buys only a tenth of a year per year, because living costs about $360,000-$400,000. He also states that living on 10% of income with 10% growth could make you free in roughly 12-15 months.
numbers: 80% savings rate = 4 years of living per year worked; 10% savings rate = 0.1 year per year worked.

claim: He claims about $15,000 a year (around $1,200 a month) invested in an index fund for 30 years at 9% gets you above $5M.
detail: Offered as the realistic route into the top 0.3%. He adds that someone earning $400,000 a year (about $35,000 a month) who lives on about $5,000 a month could put in about $10,000 a month.
numbers: $15,000/yr (about $1,200/month) for 30 years at 9% (his stated result: over $5M); $400k earner: about $35k/month, lives on about $5k, invests about $10k.
### YT-rnvOwA39dDg#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:22
claim: He judges expenses by their cost relative to his income, and personally has lived on less than 5% of what he made.
detail: He drove a roughly $6,000 car while holding $1M of after-tax cash, lived on $30,000 a year until his income passed $1M a year, and says a Bentley costs him under one week of income. He wants large reserves and says that for him, spending less lowers his anxiety. He thinks the extra enjoyment of a Bentley over a normal car isn't worth the money. Early on, after their first $100,000 they had three years of living at $1,500 a month.
numbers: He lived on less than 5% of his income; $30,000/yr living cost; $1,500 per month household budget at start.
