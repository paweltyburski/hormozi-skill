### MZ-viM1QcKAK3w#01 | CASE | topic: lead-getters | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:31
claim: A watch dealer must choose between recruiting deal partners and training virtual assistants to find stock.
detail: Partners work, but they tend to under-declare price or over-declare cost, so he gets a fair cut but not everything. Hormozi leans to partners if the owner has a reliable way to bring them in, yet sees VAs as the longer-term solution.
numbers: Watches cost about $80,000 to $240,000 each; VA pay $5-$10 per hour.

### MZ-viM1QcKAK3w#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:31
claim: Never count the other party's money; judge a deal only on whether it works for you.
detail: If the partner earns more, they will bring more deals. A partner's cut being bigger than expected is a feature of the arrangement, not a bug.

### MZ-viM1QcKAK3w#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: lead-getters | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:22
claim: In this caller's deal-partner setup, Hormozi says the downside of partners is that if they make enough money doing deals with you, they eventually stop, so it is a short-term relationship.
detail: He illustrates with something like eight deals before a partner can buy for themselves. He asked the caller which option scales faster given their skill set, said he personally likes partnerships if there is a reliable way to bring partners in, and favored VAs for the long term.
numbers: About 8 deals before a partner can fund their own purchases (his illustration).
### MZ-viM1QcKAK3w#04 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: sales-team | fw: Document Demonstrate Duplicate | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:51
claim: Turn buying judgment into a written rubric or decision tree so a VA can screen deals.
detail: The VA calls other wholesalers and sends deals that match; the owner still approves. The tricky part is that prices move daily, so someone must know a reasonable buy price.
