### MZ-cixnFmFGz_M#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:17
claim: Charging gyms 100% of upfront cash for turnaround launches worked financially but failed because he did not control fulfilment.
detail: After 32 in-person gym turnarounds over two years, he flew in, ran sales and marketing for 21 days and took all upfront cash collected. The gym then delivered to the new members with a weak product and no retraining. Neighbouring gym owners offered the same service for less, so refunds hit about $150,000 across three facilities within months. He had to keep selling more each month to cover prior refunds.
numbers: About $100,000 cash collected per gym in 21 days; roughly $150,000 refunds in a few months; eight gyms would have meant $800,000 a month.

### MZ-cixnFmFGz_M#02 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:04
claim: He found the licence price by naming the highest number he could think of, hoping for a no; the owner said yes.
detail: A desperate gym owner begged for help after the pay-for-performance model was dropped. Hormozi offered only the playbook (ads, pages, targeting, five text messages, reminder sequence, lobby layout, where to sit) and quoted $6,000 to get a refusal. The owner agreed at once, and the next quoted $8,000 also said yes. The product was the front-end marketing machine, not a course or back-office system.
numbers: $6,000 then $8,000 first quotes, both accepted.

### MZ-cixnFmFGz_M#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: verticals | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:21
claim: Licensed gyms averaged $30,000 extra cash in their first 30 days, which justified the $16,000 system and later the licence.
detail: The in-house team did about $100,000 in the same period, but weaker licensees still reached 30% of that. After that result, three-year licence agreements were signed. As gyms grew from 100 to 250 members in two months, new problems appeared (hiring trainers, scaling a sales team, class layout, ascending to semi-private), and each became a follow-on product from his own six-gym experience.
numbers: $30,000 additional cash in first 30 days per licensee; $16,000 system price; $42,000 a year licence on three-year terms; 100 to 250 members in two months.

### MZ-cixnFmFGz_M#06 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:14:23
claim: An opportunity vehicle is judged by three multiplied factors: potential units, gross margin per unit, and supply-demand dynamics for a new entrant.
detail: Number of potential units to be sold times the possible gross margin per unit gives the size. The third multiplier is whether the supply-demand ratio favours entering. Example: telecom has a huge market and margin per unit but a terrible ratio for a new entrant. His mentor's verdict on him: running gyms was a level two opportunity for a level ten skill set.

### MZ-cixnFmFGz_M#14 | TACTIC | topic: verticals | also: lead-getters | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:27:01
claim: The supplement drop-ship kiosk made gyms sell once and earn monthly with no operational work.

### MZ-cixnFmFGz_M#15 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:30:17
claim: Cutting licence price by 25% to lower churn changed churn not at all and simply lost 25% of revenue.
detail: Wanting lower churn to look good to a buyer, he dropped the weekly licence from $800 to $600. Starting a second company in parallel was his other error. In year three licensing fell to roughly $20M while supplements made about $17M.
numbers: $800 to $600 per week; about $42,000 a year at the old price.

### MZ-cixnFmFGz_M#16 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Downsell Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:31:35
claim: Both layers used the same logic: a high-priced short front end, then a lower rate for the longer continuity.
detail: Launch was $16,000 for 16 weeks (about $1,000 a week); the continuity, Legacy, was $800 a week for more content. For gym members, a $500 challenge credited back as $50 a month dropped their $250 monthly fee to about $200. The front end paid for acquisition, so no outside capital was needed.
numbers: $1,000/week front end; $800/week continuity; $250 to about $199/month for members.

### MZ-cixnFmFGz_M#17 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: Point of Greatest Deprivation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:32:34
claim: Charge most at the point of peak excitement and lower the price over time, because information loses value the longer someone holds it.
detail: Unlike tech start-ups with free trials, he charged a large amount up front, since he had no funding and his bank account was the burn rate. He sold on day one, before the first workout makes the effort real. Lowering cost over time suited his near-100% gross margins.
