### YT-aOq1Hdz0w3k#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:07
claim: Opportunity screen with three lenses: how many people could buy, how much profit per unit (value delivered minus delivery cost), and the competitive dynamics.
detail: He and his wife talk to 10-20 service, information, e-learning or coaching businesses a week after screening, so they need quick snap judgments. For the first lens, check which assumptions keep demand true over time (weight loss, for instance, will not stop being wanted). Owners can use the same lenses to ask how to widen the audience, raise value or cut cost, and move into a newer space.
numbers: 10-20 businesses per week reviewed after screening.

### YT-aOq1Hdz0w3k#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:26
claim: A business can pass two lenses and fail the third: mobile phone service has huge demand and pennies of marginal cost per user, but incumbents make entry hard.
detail: The opposite extreme he uses: a hypothetical crypto-mining device earning buyers $50,000 a year for about $500 cost, in a brand-new space with few competitors, would pass all three.
numbers: Hypothetical: $50,000 per year return on a $500 device.

### YT-aOq1Hdz0w3k#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: unit-economics | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:37
claim: Very valuable but very costly products offer little profit opportunity; tiny value at near-zero cost can still be interesting if enough people buy.
detail: This is why he prefers course, e-learning and coaching businesses: they can be moved from a low-value model into one with enterprise value.

### YT-aOq1Hdz0w3k#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:04
claim: The largest companies sell something very valuable at almost zero marginal cost (ads, software, phone networks); information and education come closest at about 100% gross margin without development cost, but churn higher.
detail: Information value declines once the skill is learned. Small owners wrongly think charging more is bad; gross profit is what lets you reach more people.
numbers: Info and education gross margin close to 100%.

### YT-aOq1Hdz0w3k#05 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: Problem-Solution Cycle | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:10
claim: To fix churn in a skill-teaching business, peel out the consumable parts that people want again each month, such as community and accountability.
detail: The skill itself is consumed once; community and accountability get re-consumed to reach the next stage. Also, solving a problem reveals new ones: see problems as blank cheques, each with a dollar amount attached.

### YT-aOq1Hdz0w3k#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:35
claim: Total addressable market is a weak yardstick, because founders expand their vision and adjacent markets open over time.
detail: Facebook began as a college yearbook network. A business dies or is sold when the founder runs out of vision. Yet do not switch avatars at small scale: one avatar can reach roughly $100M a year. Use a three to five year view with the current avatar, then ask whether over twenty years the business can eat more of the market, including through acquisitions.
numbers: Single avatar can scale to about $100M per year; horizons: 3-5 years versus 20 years.
