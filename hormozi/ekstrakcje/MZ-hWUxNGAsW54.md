### MZ-hWUxNGAsW54#01 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: The test for any AI push is the plain question of whether you are making more money, even after paying for tokens.
detail: He sees founders using AI to do low-value tasks fast and building products the base models will soon swallow. Adopting new technology is good, but it must show up in revenue.

### MZ-hWUxNGAsW54#02 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:09
claim: A firm spent far more building an AI system to replace cheap virtual assistants than the assistants cost, and the task was not even its constraint.
detail: Eleven overseas VAs did data-cleaning work reliably as part of a quoting process. The company paid for a replacement AI system that cost several years of VA wages. Growth was limited by lack of demand, so automating this process did not matter.
numbers: 11 VAs at about $11,000 per month in total; AI build about $350,000 (roughly three-plus years of VA cost, as stated).

### MZ-hWUxNGAsW54#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:55
claim: Use AI inside your business instead of starting an "AI business", and advertise the customer's outcome, not the technology.
detail: Customers care about the solution to their problem; many people are also wary of AI, so an AI label can hurt rather than help.

### MZ-hWUxNGAsW54#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:20
claim: Value stays with whoever owns stakes: decisions, liability and risk, because someone must be responsible.
detail: AI can recommend, but a person or company must own the decision, pay taxes and bear the downside and upside. Audiences want real stakes (a spectacle with real money, chess or F1 with human players), so real-world content keeps its value; fiction is more exposed.

### MZ-hWUxNGAsW54#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:51
claim: Do not hand your hardest thinking to AI: it agrees with almost anything and delegating makes you weaker.
detail: He asks the same decision question to several models and gets scattered answers, so judgment must stay with the human. He keeps the hardest thinking for himself to keep his own mind sharp.
