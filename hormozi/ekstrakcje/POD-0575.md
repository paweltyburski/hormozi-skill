### POD-0575#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-team | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:19:46
claim: Conviction in the product is not yes or no but a degree, and it can be scored per rep.
detail: Hormozi would rate a rep two or four out of ten on how much they believe in the product, and says that score maps to closing percentage more than any other factor. Because it is measurable and changeable, it can be worked on. First person you must sell is yourself.
numbers: Conviction scored 0-10 per rep.

### POD-0575#02 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:32
claim: Repair a weak sales team by asking what happened in the last 7, 14 or 30 days that made them believe in the product less, then fixing that.
detail: Hormozi prefers this to drilling objection scripts. If the cause is a real product or service issue, fix it and send the team daily updates on what is being done. Beginners cannot fake tone; belief fixes tone and word choice.
numbers: Look-back windows: 7, 14 and 30 days.

### POD-0575#03 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:41
claim: Build conviction by doing the detailed work on the product until you can prove it is excellent; doubt fades when you hold the evidence.
detail: His bar for excellence rose: a book took about 2,000 hours of his writing plus 1,500 of an editor's. For a sandwich shop he would spend a year tasting the best shops and tuning details such as toasting time. Someone who has tested every variable can talk about the product with conviction, and meets criticism with a list of what was done.
numbers: Book: ~2,000 hours writing + ~1,500 hours editing; example toast time 17 seconds.

### POD-0575#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:14:54
claim: When owners say they need more customers, often the product is simply not good enough; better marketing on a leaky product fails.
detail: Sandwich shop example: if past customers were served well, there would already be a line at lunch. Marketing a weak product is like pouring spaghetti into a colander. Fix the product, and if you do not love it, fix it until you do.

### POD-0575#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:36
claim: Companies that launch a new program every twelve weeks to follow trends (crypto, then AI) tend to bottle-rocket up and fizzle instead of going deep.
detail: Depth means falling in love with one product and delivering transformation. A million-dollar business wanting ten million can often be heard in how it sells: rehearsed lines from someone who does not believe in it stalls.
numbers: New product about every 12 weeks (the pattern criticised).

### POD-0575#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:33
claim: The goal of the conversation is a decision, not a purchase; a clear no is better than a miserable maybe.
detail: Train reps to expect no. If every prospect said yes, a page on a shopping site would do the job; people who come to a seller have demons in the closet to be worked through. A true no frees the prospect and the rep.

### POD-0575#07 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: What Would It Take | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:10
claim: To a stall, ask what main things they use to decide, what they fear happening, and at what point it is unreasonable not to believe it works.
detail: Hormozi (from selling gym memberships) says that when someone says they need to think, they think they need time but what they really want is information, and he is their only source of it. Ask what the thing is they do not want to happen if they buy. A lot of times that gets to the real fear, such as a spouse's reaction.
### POD-0575#08 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: - | fw: Three Core Beliefs | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:15
claim: Spouse objection: play it out three years ahead and reframe permission as support.
detail: Steps: 1. Name the pattern, that every time they try to help themselves the partner says no. 2. Ask how they will feel in three years. 3. Point out the resentment forming and that they are handing the partner responsibility for their success. 4. Reframe: they need support, not permission. Layers of blame: circumstances, other people, then avoidance of the decision.

### POD-0575#09 | CASE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:11:11
claim: The top salesman at Hormozi's old company stayed persistent without pushing by linking the sixth no to the prospect's own stated problem.
detail: He would say he was not giving up on them, repeat what they had told him (for example trouble paying the mortgage), and suggest something emotional was blocking the decision, then invite them to work it out together. Selling is "a dance, not a fight".

detail: It signals you are working with them on a good decision.

### POD-0575#11 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-process | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:14
claim: Qualify out poor fits and recommend something else; he says this gives you more conviction in the people you do sell.
detail: If the call shows the product will not fit, let them go and give a recommendation. He says that letting a couple of poor fits go means you know why you sell and are not just selling every person with a heartbeat. "Conviction over commission".
