### POD-0818#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A Seattle-area chiropractic chain of six clinics, about 75% insurance-billed, wants $5M EBITDA in three years and an institutional sale, but cites uneven lead flow as the main constraint.
detail: Patients are 35 to 65 with pain or movement loss. Owner's constraints in order: inconsistent lead flow once past one clinic, sales infrastructure (insurance verification slows same-day plans and doctors struggle to do it), scaling (small hiring pool because buying a clinic means placing a doctor), and holding standards across sites. Owner bought all leads so far. Hormozi's diagnosis: weak front-end offer and sales flow, ad spend too low, and operations.
numbers: Trailing-12-month revenue $5.2M; profit ~$1.2M (~23% net); goal $5M EBITDA in 3 years.
applies_to: Multi-location healthcare services.

### POD-0818#14 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: finance-exit | fw: Prep for Sale | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:18:03
claim: The clinic owner pays doctors a base plus 10-20% of net profit; Hormozi says that is usually what he likes for high-skill roles, and suggests a profits interest as an option if an equity-like stake is wanted.
detail: Equity buys cash flow, sale value, risk and control; doctors want only the first two and are not given control. A profits interest sets a baseline value (for example $500K) and gives them a percentage, say 15%, of growth above it, with no tax on grant, and it returns to the pool if they leave. But he doubts equity changes behaviour in this role; profit share gives faster feedback. Buyers recalculate financials with their own add-backs.
numbers: The clinic owner's doctors get an $80K base, and the top one over $150K; profit share of 10-20% of net profit (owner's figures); Hormozi's profits interest example: baseline $500K, 15% of growth.
