### POD-0474#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: pricing | fw: Delivery Cube | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Test prep tutors usually sell time one-to-one at $40-$80 an hour; he would sell a score outcome in groups of eight.
detail: Sell the outcome (better exam, school or job), preferably standardized tests, since a higher score leads to better schools and pay. Run semi-private tables of eight at $35 a session, four hours a day, with about 50 students attending three times a week, grouped into class A and class B, with a seventh day for make-ups. Needs no inventory or location.
numbers: $35 per session x 8 students = $280/hour; ~$1,100 per day for 4 hours; 50 students.
applies_to: Solo service providers, students

### POD-0474#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: Delivery Cube | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:29
claim: Semi-private groups charge about two-thirds of the one-to-one price with eight times as many students.
detail: Students work problems in turn, so no one waits through another's work and everyone still gets personal attention. Time is used more efficiently than in one-to-one.
numbers: ~2/3 of one-to-one price; 8x people.

### POD-0474#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:00
claim: Four hours at $60 one-to-one earns $240; four hours one-to-eight at $35 earns about $1,100.
detail: That is roughly 4.5 times the income per hour, and one-to-one cannot scale because there is no margin to pay anyone else.
numbers: $240 vs $1,100 for 4 hours; ~4.5x.

### POD-0474#04 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:20
claim: Price against the outcome by working out how many problems the student must solve.
detail: Score tracks the number of problems solved. Example: every 1,000 problems adds about 20 points (he admits the figure is illustrative), so an 80-point goal needs 4,000 problems, roughly 1,000 per month, so four months. Package price is 35 x 4 sessions a week x 17 weeks; prepaying takes $5 off each session.
numbers: 1,000 problems ~20 points; 4,000 problems for +80; 1,000 per month; prepay discount $5 per session.

### POD-0474#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:10
claim: His own GMAT preparation: eight books of problems, four hours a day for four months, a weekly Saturday test.
detail: He scored above Harvard's mid-range. Pattern recognition grew from repetition; the same ~100 problem types recur with new names and numbers, so volume predicts score.
numbers: 8 books; 4 hours/day; 4 months.

### POD-0474#06 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: unit-economics | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:48
claim: To scale, hire teachers at about $50 an hour, which keeps gross margin above 80%.
detail: Keep service gross margin at 80% or higher, so cost per class hour must stay below about $56 of the $280. Teachers can earn more than in their day job. A one-to-one model at $60 would only allow about $12 an hour for staff. Templatize the problem practice and the signup process.
numbers: Teacher pay $50/hour; cost ceiling ~$56 per $280 hour; $12 at one-to-one pricing.

### POD-0474#07 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: lead-getters | fw: Warm Outreach Steps | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:11:54
claim: Message your network with your own before-and-after score and ask whom they know who needs test prep.
detail: Structure: where I started, the method, the result, then the question. Asking about others feels less pushy, and nine in ten answer "me". Use DMs, including parents and adults.
numbers: ~9 in 10 reply that they want it themselves.

### POD-0474#09 | TACTIC | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: retention-ltv | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:46
claim: Take the first 5-10 students free, bill a fee for each day homework is skipped, and ask for feedback, a testimonial and a referral.
detail: The penalty gives them skin in the game and improves results. Telling prospects you need great results for your first clients adds incentive. With more than 10 interested, start charging.
numbers: First 5-10 students free.
