### POD-0607#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: Track Lose Print | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:56
claim: Ten ads at $100 each: nine lose, one returns $200, and scaling that one is what makes the campaign pay.
detail: The campaign looks like a $1,000 spend returning $200, but the single winner (spend 100, get 200) can be scaled to about $10,000 returning about $20,000. The net cost to find the machine was about $800. Expect nine losers in ten, and at larger scale about 99 to find one mega winner.
numbers: $1,000 on 10 ads at $100 each; loss on 9 ($900); winner $100 to $200; scaled $10,000 to about $20,000; nine of ten ads lose.

### POD-0607#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:10
claim: Advanced advertisers always have the next generation of ads in production so several winners peak at different times.
detail: Mid-tier advertisers scale a winner until fatigue (colder audiences, falling ROI), then panic and scramble for the next one. Staggering two to five winners smooths the whole business. Run a testing and creation cadence daily or weekly.

### POD-0607#06 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:34
claim: Newsletter walk-through: $10 gross profit a month, 20-month life, $50 CAC gives $200 lifetime profit and 4:1.
detail: He spends $50 and recovers $10 a month, breaking even in month five and profiting from month six. Still a good ad deal, but a cash-flow constraint, not a model constraint; fix it with upfront or prepaid payments. Above 3:1 lifetime gross profit to CAC counts as scalable.
numbers: $10 gross profit/month; 20 months average stay; $200 LTGP; $50 CAC; 4:1; break-even month 5; 3:1 minimum.

### POD-0607#08 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:12
claim: Gym Launch beat free-trial gyms by charging $500-$600 upfront, so each sale funded the next customers.
detail: Rivals ran 7-, 14- or 30-day free trials, paid for delivery and sales while about a third converted to a $99 membership, taking about three months to recoup. Gym Launch charged for a defined-end programme, with CAC of $100-$200, leaving about $400 per sale to buy more customers. Gyms filled in a month. The remaining bottleneck was gym capacity.
numbers: Competitor trial recoup about 3 months, one third converting at $99/month; Gym Launch front end $500-$600; CAC $100-$200; about $400 left per sale.
applies_to: Local service businesses with little cash.
