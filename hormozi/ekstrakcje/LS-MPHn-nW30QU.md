### LS-MPHn-nW30QU#01 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:10
claim: To get a yes on a borderline request, phrase it as a no-based question, because saying no feels safe to the other person.
detail: Examples: "would it be crazy if I asked for X" or "would you be totally against Y". It works only for requests near the edge of what they might accept, not for impossible asks. The idea is credited to Chris Voss.

### LS-MPHn-nW30QU#02 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:47
claim: To get people to complete a task, announce that most have already done it and single out the handful who have not.
detail: People want to stay with the herd, so framing non-completion as the odd-one-out position pushes action. Saying nobody has done it instead tells them that not doing it is normal. Example: a team survey, with a deadline for the few remaining.

### LS-MPHn-nW30QU#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:38
claim: A message is an inbound bid for your time, so at scale you must budget attention and feel no guilt for unanswered DMs, texts and emails.
detail: Requests are unlimited while your time is fixed. A related quiet tactic: to end a friendship without confrontation, slow your reply times until the other person looks elsewhere.

### LS-MPHn-nW30QU#05 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:07:55

### LS-MPHn-nW30QU#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-team | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:31
claim: When choosing a sales leader, pick the best human rather than the best salesperson.
detail: If you take the person best at the job, sometimes you'll lose their production. Someone who is a great human will give you a good leader. If one person is both, you have probably already promoted them.
### LS-MPHn-nW30QU#08 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:18
claim: Before delivering bad news such as a firing, tell the person it will not be a pleasant conversation so they can brace.
detail: Doctors are taught to prepare families this way. It reduces pain and wild reactions and also calms the deliverer's nerves. Opening line idea: this isn't fun and I'm not looking forward to it, but we have to do it.

### LS-MPHn-nW30QU#09 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:09
claim: For big decisions such as which business to start, list what you do not want first and filter, rather than pick the perfect option.
detail: Filters for a business: in person or remote, selling or running checkout, physical products or services. Choosing among hundreds of options is paralysing; progressive filtering narrows the field like shopping filters on a web page.

### LS-MPHn-nW30QU#10 | SCRIPT | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:20:25
claim: To get depth from a prospect without an interrogation feel, alternate "can you give me an example" and "tell me more" until you reach the pain that links to your product.
detail: Surface answers do not lead to buying. Sample: "why isn't the business growing faster" gives "not enough leads"; example request gives "10 last week"; follow-up shows normal is 15; then "tell me more". People like talking about themselves, and "tell me more" is phrased as a command, adding assumed authority.
numbers: Illustrative: 10 leads last week against a normal 15.

### LS-MPHn-nW30QU#12 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Win-Back Campaign | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:19
claim: A business owner needing fast cash has three sources: prepayment from existing customers, a credit offer to past customers, and a limited prepaid top-tier offer to the whole list.
detail: 1) Ask monthly customers to prepay two or three months for a small discount. 2) Email past buyers with a subject like "I owe you money", crediting their previous spend (for example $500) toward a larger offer such as $3,000. 3) Run a giveaway or buy-X-get-Y-free (buy six months, get six) or an ultra-premium one-to-one slot limited to five customers who prepay.
numbers: Examples: $500 past spend credited toward a $3,000 package; top-tier capped at 5 buyers.

### LS-MPHn-nW30QU#13 | DEFINITION | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:25:21
claim: Cash flow is cash in minus cash out over a period, and in cash-basis accounting a prepaid six-month service counts fully as revenue when paid.
detail: Example: a bank balance moving from $100 to $300 is $200 of cash flow. Prepayment therefore pulls cash forward.
numbers: $100 to $300 = $200 cash flow.

### LS-MPHn-nW30QU#14 | DECISION_RULE | topic: verticals | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:26:34
claim: Business type decides early cash flow: construction, inventory-based e-commerce and software are cash-constrained early, whereas a service where you sell your own time can cash-flow from day one.
detail: In e-commerce, profit goes back into inventory when growing; in software, development cost comes before revenue; for a solo service the cost is only food and rent. All can generate high cash flow at scale, but selecting the business is a key cash decision.

### LS-MPHn-nW30QU#15 | CASE | topic: unit-economics | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:28:30
claim: For a solo cold-email agency, cash flow starts the moment a stranger pays; the skill is allocating money to the highest return (ROIC), and direct revenue actions come before indirect ones.
detail: Example of choice: spend $5,000 on a website build or run $5,000 in ads to a simple funnel built in minutes; ads can return $10,000 to $100,000 while the website works only if something else happens. Early on, focus on direct ways: let people know, get paid, make them happy so they refer.
numbers: $5,000 budget; potential ad return $10,000-$100,000 (illustrative).

### LS-MPHn-nW30QU#16 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: Five Upsell Moments | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:30:11
claim: For a five-week high-ticket hypnotherapy programme, make the continuation offer at weeks two or three, showing progress without resolution and opening the next problem.
detail: Do not wait until the end. Once the first issue is addressed, people keep finding another problem, so identify the next one first and have the product ready (as a weight-loss client moves from 10 pounds to toning or competing). Example: trauma work from one cause, plus other habits like smoking or nail-biting.
numbers: Programme length 5 weeks; offer at weeks 2-3.

### LS-MPHn-nW30QU#17 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: product-delivery | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:31:50
claim: Hold standards above your customers' expectations, because expectations only rise; bet on what never changes: people want it fast, easy and guaranteed.
detail: If you deliver in an hour, customers soon see any longer wait as bad and want 45 minutes. Bezos-style bets on constants map to the value equation ingredients. Making mediocrity unacceptable is the stated route to being undeniable.
