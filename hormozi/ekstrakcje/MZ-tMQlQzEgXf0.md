### MZ-tMQlQzEgXf0#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit, verticals | fw: Buy vs Build | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A dentist with three offices wants to invest in and flip other practices, but Hormozi says to open two more offices himself.
detail: He avoids full purchases because of state licensing, and wants equity stakes with warm leads via social media. The minority model is unfun: you want control, they won't execute. Those who do it buy majority control.
numbers: Revenue about $20M, three offices at about $6-7M each, goal $35M.

### MZ-tMQlQzEgXf0#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:21
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: since his business had just reached the easy point with a team in place, he should go hard at the same thing rather than do something different.
detail: Hormozi told him opening two more locations would be a significantly more valuable enterprise than selling one-offs. He said one very clean exit is owning the whole local market, and alternatively going to an adjacent market.
### MZ-tMQlQzEgXf0#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: verticals, finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:31
claim: Having several dentists in a location makes it harder for any one to have you by the balls; his own advice was to probably keep expanding locally first.
detail: It is easy to have a mutiny when there is one dentist, hard when there are four. Buying retiring dentists' practices was floated as an option.
