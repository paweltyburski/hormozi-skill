### MZ-sobMAG-5yFs#01 | CASE | topic: sales-process | also: unit-economics | fw: Basic Sales KPIs | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Testing every variable across about 4,000 appointments a day showed availability was the number one lever on throughput.
detail: His scheduling-software company let two data scientists test variables across industries. The lever is the count of bookable slots per day, seven days a week. In his view fixing show-up often earns more than raising close rate.
numbers: About 4,000 appointments a day across industries; show-up example of 25%.

### MZ-sobMAG-5yFs#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: sales-process | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:50
claim: A gym owner who only took sales appointments on two mornings a week had a convenience problem, not a lead problem.
detail: The slots were 7-9 a.m. on Tuesdays and Thursdays. He compares it to a spa that can see you now beating one that has a slot next week.
numbers: Two 2-hour windows per week.

### MZ-sobMAG-5yFs#03 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:35
claim: Offer booking slots in short increments and at off-round times, because a 3:05 start signals detail and gets people there on time.
detail: Appointments can still last 45 or 60 minutes; only the start grid is fine. Online booking showed higher schedule rates with 15-minute starts than with hour blocks.
numbers: 15-minute versus 60-minute increments.

### MZ-sobMAG-5yFs#04 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:35
claim: Pulling an existing appointment earlier boosts show-up rates sharply.
detail: When qualifying a lead on the phone, say a slot just opened (for example four o'clock today) and offer to move them up from their later booking. It can be done by a setter or by the closer.
