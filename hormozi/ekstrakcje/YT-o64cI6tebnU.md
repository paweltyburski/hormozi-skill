### YT-o64cI6tebnU#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Keys Roofing, a two-month-old premium roofing and gutter contractor in St. Louis, was moved from chasing full replacements to selling repairs at volume.
detail: Revenue about $41,000 in two months with $24,248 profit (~59%), roughly $20,000 a month. Leads came from door knocking and referrals. The owner's stated limit was salespeople. Hormozi saw the real asset in the owner: he reliably turns doors into money. Advice: orient the business around the jobs competitors ignore and systemise them.
numbers: Replacement average ticket $18,000-$25,000; about 1 sale per 20 doors knocked.

detail: Being resource-constrained in both time and money is simply a fact at this size. Knowing you can knock doors and close is what keeps a young company alive.

### YT-o64cI6tebnU#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:22
claim: Repairs run about 64% gross margin on a roughly $1,500 ticket, against about $7,000 gross profit on a replacement, so the plan was to lift the repair ticket.
detail: Replacement gross profit about $7,000. Repair ticket about $1,500 at about 64% gross margin. ### YT-o64cI6tebnU#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:22
claim: Pick work other firms consider beneath them, then systemise it until it earns good gross profit.
detail: Repairs are work other people have cast aside. Getting the first sale is the hard part, and the next (proximate) sale is significantly easier, for example by getting a foot in the door with a repair and then offering to fix everything while the crew is on site.
### YT-o64cI6tebnU#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: verticals | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:32
claim: A high-volume, small-ticket model is stabler than a few big jobs.
detail: Sales reps get far more repetitions because the cycle is short, there is little insurance or delayed-payment admin, and cash flow is rarely a constraint. Add more repair crews and the model becomes the machine.

### YT-o64cI6tebnU#06 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: Sales Aids | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:53
claim: Use two different videos: one watched after the on-site visit, one before the onboarding call.
detail: The first video sells the company and long-term roofing as an investment. The second covers things homeowners do not know about their own roof, framing the add-on conversation the closer will have.

### YT-o64cI6tebnU#07 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: Three Core Beliefs | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:37
claim: Structure for the sales video, about seven minutes.
detail: Intro with promise and the pain avoided; proof from similar neighbours with equal or bigger houses; the plan; old way versus the company's named process; then each common objection answered by stating the belief, why it is wrong, what is right, and a matching customer story.
numbers: Length about 7 minutes.

### YT-o64cI6tebnU#08 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:14
claim: Reframe roof work as a cost paid either way: a maintained roof is enjoyed during ownership and shows up in resale value.
detail: Fixing everything on sale day only improves the house for the next owner. Put this in the video rather than arguing it live.

### YT-o64cI6tebnU#09 | SCRIPT | topic: sales-process | also: money-models | fw: Menu Upsell | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:14:41
claim: The onboarding-call closer runs the Menu Upsell on repairs as a mini close.
detail: Step 1, unsell what they do not need, to earn trust. Step 2, prescribe what they do need. Step 3, offer a harmless A-or-B choice (install day, crew, colour). Step 4, take payment on the card already on file. Justification: the crew is already on site with the roof open, so it is cheaper to fix everything now.

### YT-o64cI6tebnU#10 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:00
claim: Express door-to-door commission as dollars per door.
detail: Pay 10% on repairs. A repair ticket of $4,000 pays $400, which divided by 50 doors is about $10 per door, so each knock has a visible price.
numbers: 10% commission; $4,000 ticket; $400 per sale; 1 sale per 20 doors for the owner, 40-50 doors assumed for hires.

### YT-o64cI6tebnU#11 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:12:27
claim: Sell an annual maintenance membership whose real purpose is one yearly roof visit that leads to more repairs.
detail: Price $15-$20 a month, billed annually ($180-$240). The fee is credited toward future repairs. Hormozi said the money hardly matters; the booked appointment does. He said a good pitch could get about 80% to buy; the owner had launched it but residential offers were not landing yet (timing or script suspected).
numbers: $180-$240 per year; target take rate about 80%.

### YT-o64cI6tebnU#12 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: lead-getters | fw: Four R's | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:22
claim: Tie a review to a lower membership price.
detail: List the membership at $500 a year, and drop it to $200 if the customer leaves a review. Order after a job: tidy better than found, ask for the Google review first, then offer membership. The owner also phones past clients every 6-12 months with notes on their family.
numbers: $500 list, $200 with review.
