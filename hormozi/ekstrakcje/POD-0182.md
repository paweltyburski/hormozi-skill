### POD-0182#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Four Types of Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: A service business needs four sales per client: the service sale, a bolt-on sale, a continuity sale and a prepay for the duration.
detail: The service sale anchors high and steps down. The bolt-on sells ancillary items the client would buy on recommendation (for a gym: heart rate monitors, supplements, meals). Continuity is upsold once they are in. Most businesses sell one thing, badly, such as handing over a price clipboard or offering a free week.

### POD-0182#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:32
claim: Close the most expensive option on the first visit, since the prospect's pain peaks the day they walk in.
detail: They made time and drove over, so that moment will not return. A larger payment also makes them financially and emotionally invested, which Hormozi says is a coach's real job: programmes can be looked up online, but getting people invested cannot.

### POD-0182#03 | SCRIPT | topic: sales-process | also: pricing | fw: Anchor Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:35
claim: Replace the advertised short fix with a goal-based plan by getting the prospect to agree the short fix cannot reach their goal.
detail: Sequence: confirm their real goal (lose 40 pounds), get agreement that the 21-day front-end offer will not achieve it, state the time needed, and quote the per-week price. Their agreement during the exchange is what makes the long plan feel like the solution rather than a pitch.
numbers: Example: 24 weeks to reach the goal plus 10 weeks to maintain, 34 weeks at $99 a week, anchored as roughly a $3,000 ticket.

### POD-0182#04 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: objections-closing | fw: Downsell Flow | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:14
claim: Step down in order: pay in full on a card, smaller down payment with weekly payments, a short fixed term, then a free trial with commitments.
detail: If she cannot afford it, offer to put the full price on a card and pay on her own terms. Next cut the down payment and keep a prepay discount. Then shorten the duration, so results come first. The $3,000 anchor makes the smaller asks feel small.
numbers: Example ladder: $3,000 prepay; $1,000 down with 10% off and $66 a week instead of $99; 6 or 12 weeks for $600 with no further payments.

### POD-0182#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:59
claim: In the free-trial step the client initials a short list of commitments, with a small fee per miss.
detail: Free 21-day training in exchange for eight workouts and three appointments: a food review within 48 hours, a feedback appointment, and a final one for progress pictures. A card goes down as contingency and each missed commitment costs a small "motivation tax". The rep points to each line on the contract and has them initial it, so showing up becomes their own promise.
numbers: 8 workouts and 3 appointments in 21 days; $25 fee per miss.

### POD-0182#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-process | also: money-models | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:11
claim: Nobody should walk out without signing something; everyone can sign the free trial with commitments.
detail: Prospects agree that results need showing up and changing eating, so the commitments are easy to accept. This keeps control of the process, so the rep can move into sale number two within 24 to 48 hours even for the trial group.
### POD-0182#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:00
claim: The typical gym closes about one in three walk-ins on a roughly $100 membership, which is about $30 per show.
detail: The comparison case is a gym that gets about $300 from nine shows, which Hormozi says is too little to fund marketing, staff or commissions.
numbers: 3 of 9 shows close at about $100 each, $300 per nine shows, about $33 per show.

### POD-0182#08 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: sales-process | fw: Downsell Flow | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:46
claim: Nine walk-ins sold with the step-down ladder produce about $7,200 against $300 for the plain membership pitch.
detail: Outcome of the nine: two prepay at $2,500, one pays $1,000 with a contract, two pay $600, and four give a card for the trial. That is about 23 to 24 times the other gym's revenue per nine shows, with every person still entering continuity.
numbers: 2 x $2,500 + 1 x $1,000 + 2 x $600 = $7,200 versus $300.

### POD-0182#09 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:11:45
claim: Higher commitment predicts who finishes, so the ladder also gives clients more value.
detail: Hormozi's rough guess at nine months: both prepaid clients and probably the $1,000-down client finish, the $600 clients have a coin flip, and maybe one of four trial clients finishes. That gives four or five finishers against about one from a membership-only sale, where two of three quit in month one from poor onboarding.
numbers: Estimated finishers per nine shows: 4 to 5 against 1 for memberships (rough estimate).
