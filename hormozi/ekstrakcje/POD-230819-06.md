### POD-230819-06#01 | DEFINITION | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: outreach | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:28
claim: Warm versus cold is decided by permission to contact, and private versus public by whether one person or many receive the message at once.
detail: Warm audiences gave permission (friends, followers, past and current customers); cold ones did not (strangers, bought or built lists, platform access). Automation does not change the category: a 10,000-person email is one-to-one done quickly by a machine. If you lack leads, the cause is too little skill or volume in one of the four methods.

### POD-230819-06#02 | CASE | topic: outreach | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Warm Outreach Steps | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:49
claim: His first clients came from a 12-week free training offer where clients donated to a charity instead of paying, which made asking feel safe.
detail: In 2013, with about $51,000 saved, he skipped an MBA ($150,000 of loans estimated) and messaged contacts. Six said yes (two school friends, a college friend and three of their referrals). After 12 weeks he asked them to pay him instead; none minded, and their referrals added five or six more paying clients. The business made about $4,000 a month and replaced his job income.
numbers: ~$4,000 per month; 6 initial clients plus 5-6 referrals; ~$51,128 savings at start.

### POD-230819-06#03 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: operator-mindset | fw: Warm Outreach Steps | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:45
claim: Stack easy yeses early: a charity-donation or free offer lets a nervous seller practise asking without risking a rejection of a price.
detail: He asked for a feel-good donation because asking for money felt too frightening, and the early yeses built confidence for the first business. Free terms he suggests: the person promises to use it, give feedback and write a review if it deserves one, followed by a plain "does that sound fair?".

### POD-230819-06#05 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: sales-process | fw: Warm Outreach Steps | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:23
claim: Follow up up to three times: once a day for three days or until they reply, and weekly for physical mail.
detail: The first reach-out is the hardest and slowest; the second takes minutes and the third seconds. A personal opener should relate to something you know about them, and you should not ask for anything in it.
numbers: 100 contacts per day; max 3 attempts over 3 days; mail weekly.

### POD-230819-06#06 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Warm Outreach Steps | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:04
claim: Email and voicemail combine the personal opener and the offer in one message; conversations give the offer after only a few exchanges.
detail: For email, use two to three sentences showing you researched them, then go straight to the lead magnet or offer. In a conversation, make the offer after three to four exchanges (messaging or phone) or three to four minutes in person.
numbers: 3-4 exchanges or 3-4 minutes before the offer; 2-3 sentence opener in email.

### POD-230819-06#07 | SCRIPT | topic: outreach | also: lead-getters | fw: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:40
claim: Instead of pitching, describe a past client's struggle and result and ask whether anyone they know has that struggle.
detail: Structure: say you are taking a few free case studies; describe the problem in the contact's own terms; name the dream outcome and the guarantee; cite one client who had the same struggle and got it, plus a different kind of client; ask who comes to mind. If they say nobody, ask in jest who they dislike. Matching the testimonial to the contact implies the likelihood of success without saying it. Three outcomes each win: interest, a referral, or both.

### POD-230819-06#08 | EXAMPLE | topic: sales-process | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:17:44
claim: An advertising agency can show a recording of a client's call to a lead and ask the owner if they could handle such calls, which demonstrates the outcome live.
detail: Demonstrating the result is the eleventh proof lever; the point is that people do not want leads, they want customers, and this shows what customers look like.

### POD-230819-06#09 | EXAMPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process, strategy-positioning | fw: Perceived Hidden Costs | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:21:29
claim: Warren Buffett offered to work for Benjamin Graham for free and was told he was overpriced, because training him would cost Graham time.
detail: His lesson: the real cost of anything you sell is often the effort of the buyer, and early customers train you for free, so cut their cost. He notes Buffett still got Graham to accept the free offer.

### POD-230819-06#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: outreach | fw: Penny Gap | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:23:23
claim: Begin charging only once referrals start arriving; then step the price up every five clients until a sweet spot is found.
detail: Referrals are his litmus test that you are good enough. The script's free line becomes 80% off for the next five, then 60% off, then 40%, and so on, which also adds urgency because the price visibly rises. Raising 20% per five can continue past the full price.
numbers: 80% off, 60% off, 40% off in blocks of five clients; 20% raises per five.

### POD-230819-06#11 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: Pre-Paid Post Guarantee | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:24:34
claim: Offered "pay less today or get all your money back", most buyers choose full prepayment with the guarantee over a payment plan without it.
detail: Example: $2,000 a month for three months with no guarantee against $6,000 upfront with the guarantee. He says the majority take upfront, so a guarantee can be used to pull in more cash. The wording came from a peer (Dr. Cashy).
numbers: $2,000 x 3 months vs $6,000 upfront; majority choose prepay plus guarantee.

### POD-230819-06#12 | BENCHMARK | topic: outreach | also: unit-economics | fw: Warm Outreach Steps | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:27:06
claim: Warm outreach chain: 100 contacts give about 20 replies, about 4 take the free offer, and about 1 converts to paid.
detail: The first two steps are roughly one in five each; the last step is about one of the four free takers converting to a paid offer. The numbers vary by offer value and trust, but the chain lets you forecast customers per 100 reach-outs, and results improve with practice.
numbers: 100 reach-outs -> ~20 replies (1 in 5) -> ~4 free takers (1 in 5-ish) -> ~1 paying customer (1 of the 4).
