### YT-yEKu6q0W3gs#01 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: Anchor Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Create a version of your offer at 10x, 50x or 100x your average price and put it first on the menu.
detail: It anchors every cheaper option and a few buyers take it. Expect about one in twenty to say yes. The top price should provoke a gasp; if it doesn't, it is not high enough. Then offer roughly 80% of it for a tenth, and everything looks smaller.
numbers: Multiples 10x, 50x, 100x; one in 20 buys; example silver $10k, bronze $8k, gold $100k.

### YT-yEKu6q0W3gs#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Competing on price only works if the whole business is built on operational efficiency; otherwise being cheap leads to break-even.
detail: There is no strategic benefit to being the second cheapest; there is one for the most expensive.

### YT-yEKu6q0W3gs#03 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:54
claim: Place the middle price close to the large price so it looks like a deal and sells the most.
detail: A share of buyers always takes the cheapest. Of the rest, those influenced by comparison pick the middle if it is just a little below the top, because buyers want deals rather than cheap items.
numbers: Example: small $5, large $10; middle at $9 outsells middle at $6.

### YT-yEKu6q0W3gs#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: Price to Value Discrepancy | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:42
claim: If someone doesn't buy an expensive offer, they don't see the value; people find money for something clearly worth far more than its price.
detail: Illustration: a new Ferrari offered for $10,000 would see buyers borrow and call family. So design the offer so value dwarfs price, and "they can't afford it" disappears.
numbers: Ferrari for $10,000.

### YT-yEKu6q0W3gs#05 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:32
claim: Build price tiers for different buyer types, such as $100, $200 and $1,000 a month, not three prices close together.
detail: Price by the value to a specific avatar: a service worth 10% of a store's sales is worth $100,000 to a $1M store and $10M to a $100M store. Value comes from the buyer's size, not yours.
numbers: Bad: $100, $110, $120 a month; 10% lift on $1M = $100,000; on $100M = $10M.
