### YT-rMJIOK_FgJk#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: There are six ways to structure a trade of work for money, ranked by payment timing and risk: work then pay, pay as we go, pay then work, when X happens you pay me, buying and selling risk, and no matter what you pay me.
detail: The levels: 1) classic employment (paid after work, risk traded for reliability); 2) contractor milestones or half now, half later; 3) full payment first, for owners with leverage such as surgeons; 4) outcome-triggered pay (rev share, profit share, bonuses, equity, percent of spend); 5) insurers who take on risk and get paid while nothing happens; 6) the party who controls money flow, with the state's taxes as the extreme. The last two work only in specific circumstances.
### YT-rMJIOK_FgJk#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:04
claim: You are paid in proportion to the risk you take, especially risk others perceive as high but you can actually control.
detail: The better the structure, the more the other party carries the risk and the more you earn; market overpays those who look risky yet are not (mispriced bets, as in distressed-debt investing). Shifting risk onto yourself in a deal is possible only when you are good.

### YT-rMJIOK_FgJk#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:22
claim: Owning a business is not automatically less risky than employment: roughly half of owners earn nothing in a year and the median owner earns about California minimum wage.
detail: Employee average tenure is 3.9 years (cited as US Bureau of Labor Statistics), versus 3 to 12 months for an independent contractor engagement and 1 to 3 months for temp work, so vendors face roughly five times the annual turnover. Hence vendors are fired much faster than employees even though they get some money up front.
numbers: Employee tenure 3.9 years; contractor engagement 3-12 months; temp 1-3 months; about 5x annual turnover for vendors; almost half of owners make no money (he cites it as a statistic).
### YT-rMJIOK_FgJk#04 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:43
claim: Use layaway so the client sets their own payment plan and work starts only once it is paid.
detail: Ask how much they can afford per month (for example $50 on a $600 job, taking a year), then state that work begins when paid up. Often they then offer to split larger early payments to start sooner, so people pay for speed. If they stop paying you lose nothing; the only risk is an unfinished payment. Third-party financing and attorney-style retainers that are drawn down and topped up work on the same principle.
numbers: Example $600 job at $50/month = 12 months; reported to work "nine times out of ten".

### YT-rMJIOK_FgJk#05 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: Implied Guarantee | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:18
claim: Outcome-triggered pay detaches income from hours: structure the fee as "when X happens you pay me".
detail: Forms include revenue share, profit share, bonuses, accelerators, equity, a percentage of ad spend or consulting fees as a percentage of profit or revenue. Example: no fee until the client reaches the top three in Google Maps. Works well when you have the skill to produce the outcome fast. Consumer versions: a bonus for losing 20 pounds or finishing a project by a date. Shifting terms in an employment offer (lower base for large upside) is the same move.
numbers: Example job posted at $100,000: offer to work for $60,000 base with upside to $180,000.

### YT-rMJIOK_FgJk#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:26
claim: When taking a share of results, prefer revenue share or royalties over profit share because profit can be manipulated by spending, and get paid first.
detail: Top line is top line, so royalties and licensing sit higher on the ladder. To move up as an owner, also sell guarantees and warranties (like device protection plans), taking on risks such as delays or difficulty for a premium. Control of the money flow (franchisor, payment processor remitting the remainder) is the highest level.

### YT-rMJIOK_FgJk#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:44
claim: Take on risks that are mispriced, meaning the market perceives them as high but you can control or limit them, because people overpay to avoid risk.
detail: People count the number of failures rather than the size of the upside; a stock can only fall to zero but rise without limit. Longest-lived companies (100+ years) are good at carrying risk and getting paid for it, as insurers show. Best reward comes where the world sees more risk than you actually take.
