### YT-kloJJeiysxg#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Every business has four growth levers (traffic, conversion, price, churn), and each task must tie to one of them or it should not be done.
detail: Scale until something breaks, fix it, then scale again. Traffic is one lever; conversion covers page tweaks, a better VSL, testimonials, bullets; price is tested; churn has its own list of tactics. Pick the lever that is the biggest constraint.
### YT-kloJJeiysxg#02 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:34
claim: Reactivating a dormant free group counts as a traffic project, and it only wins if it beats simply buying more or better ads.
detail: Hormozi's test: how much work is it, and would more ad spend or better ads produce more traffic for less effort? If yes, drop the group idea. Likewise, if landing-page conversion is clearly low and untouched, a day or two cleaning copy, adding fresh testimonials and writing a three-minute VSL may double growth.
numbers: Page rework estimated at 1-2 days; doubling page conversion roughly doubles growth.

### YT-kloJJeiysxg#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: More Better New | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:56
claim: Make teams argue for any new initiative against simply doing more or better of what already works.
detail: Applied example: if posting is capped at twice a day on Instagram because three performs worse, improve quality instead. Tighter hooks that fit the target audience raise view quality and count. The burden of proof sits on the new idea.
numbers: Posting limit example: 2 posts a day was the sweet spot, 3 did not perform as well.

### YT-kloJJeiysxg#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:58
claim: Small owners get stuck by dropping something that works to chase six new things, and they have the least leverage to afford it.
detail: At small scale your own time contributes directly to income, so giving a third of it to a new project can cut the business by about a third. Once a team exists, assign the improvement to a named person and measure the result.

### YT-kloJJeiysxg#10 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:08
claim: To find what is wrong with a funnel, test it on the least informed person and iterate wording through many versions before launch.
detail: Case (told by the co-presenter): a School Games ad confused a friend who did not know the platform; that exposed the real problem: nobody understood what the event was. ### YT-kloJJeiysxg#11 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:06
claim: Improve a product with three questions: what do people value (spread it), what do they not value (cut it), what do they misunderstand (reword it).
detail: Examples: winner interviews drew little attendance, so they were cut for a weekly Q&A, and attendance and views rose. One-day recordings used to unlock after a hurdle; he changed it to three free members. There is no silver bullet; the problem is usually disguised.
