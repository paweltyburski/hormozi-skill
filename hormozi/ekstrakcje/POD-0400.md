### POD-0400#01 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:35
claim: To learn a skill fast, pay an expert hourly to work on your own business while explaining his thinking, rather than buying a course.
detail: In about 2013 Hormozi wanted to learn Facebook ads. The course author did not sell his time, so Hormozi asked him to name a price and agreed. Each week for eight weeks the expert ran ads for his business while he asked him not to click, but to explain why he would turn something off or change it. He considers this a better return than any other spend because the learning is personalised.
numbers: $750 per hour for 8 hours (transcript says ~5,600 total; 8 x 750 is $6,000, unclear in audio); worth 10x to 100x the cost of a cheap online course.

### POD-0400#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:49

detail: Layla: expecting one coach to be a silver bullet puts unrealistic pressure on a fallible person; expect to need dozens of mentors or programs. Alex adds that the test is directional: does this move me closer to my goal than what I do now? The ability to judge improves as you go.

### POD-0400#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:41
claim: Being CEO of several businesses at once is very hard, so Alex would first ask why you need to be.

### POD-0400#05 | DEFINITION | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:15:31
claim: A good business framework must be both valid (correct in many circumstances) and useful (leads to a result).
detail: "Some things work and some do not" is valid but useless. A made-up step sequence that assumes what is in a prospect's mind is untestable, so it is false as a model. A one-click purchase breaks a "seven stages of awareness" sequence. He builds a model by defining terms, then trying to break it in as many ways as possible.
numbers: The leads book used 58 draft versions of one central model; the leads framework took about five days on an iPad.

### POD-0400#06 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:21
claim: Define each basic term before trying to improve it: ask what exactly a "lead" or "advertising" is.
detail: Quoting the idea that wisdom starts with defining terms, he asks whether a YouTube subscriber or an Instagram follower is a lead, and what separates advertising from marketing or promotion. Without definitions, a goal cannot be measured. The leads work started from these questions.

### POD-0400#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:21

### POD-0400#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:44
claim: Most advice sounds right but is wrong because it is stated as certain and given without the receiver's context.
detail: His example: a friend said "because you can, you should" with great certainty; testing it with a counter-example shows it fails. Shortcut: take advice from someone who has maxed out a skill and thought hard about the narrow subject. Advice depends on the person's goals and preferences.

### POD-0400#09 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: sales-team, management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:24:03
claim: A portfolio company wanted more sales volume; the Gym Launch fix (long hours) did not fit its work-life-balance value, so they used split shifts.
detail: The diagnosis was limited sales availability. The Gym Launch answer was 10 to 12 hour days, but the company valued work-life balance, so they proposed stacking two people in six-hour shifts each. The company said it would work perfectly. Lesson: check the client's values before applying your own playbook.
numbers: 10 to 12 hour days rejected; two six-hour shifts adopted.
applies_to: Teams scaling sales availability.

### POD-0400#10 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:25:46
claim: When teaching to many, state who the advice applies to and name the exceptions; otherwise it sounds arrogant and people misapply it.
detail: In a talk on meetings he said it applies to about 95% of people and that 5% will do fine without any meetings. Unqualified advice at Gym Launch led owners to make wrong decisions. Another tactic: answer "what about my situation?" comments and turn the answer into new content.
numbers: Meetings advice: applies to ~95%, exception ~5%.

### POD-0400#11 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:28:41

### POD-0400#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:31:26
claim: Patience is a strategic advantage because most people zigzag, repeatedly selling cheap things and spending audience goodwill for short-term gains.
detail: To change a behaviour change the perspective or the procedure. He treats impatience as a weakness that delays long-term success, and finds that seeing others hit "zero to three" repeatedly without going to ten makes waiting easier. Activity is not progress: a few high-leverage bets matter most, so replace activity with thought about where to place them.

### POD-0400#13 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:34:32
claim: Write down the few assumptions your long-term plan rests on and reject any new opportunity that cannot coexist with them.
detail: Acquisition.com rests on four assumptions: the founders get more popular, they can grow businesses at the target range, their target markets keep growing, and a personal brand attracts the right companies. If true, reaching a billion is a matter of time. A friend proposed turning the site into a forum for SEO traffic; after two weeks of thought he declined, since it conflicted with the plan.
numbers: Four assumptions; two weeks of deliberation; target a billion.

### POD-0400#14 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:37:13
claim: As you grow, distracting opportunities grow in the same proportion, so the ability to say no must scale as well.
detail: A $100,000-a-year owner easily declines a $10,000 side deal; a $10 million owner gets $10 million offers and a $100 million owner gets $100 million offers. He calls this the red-dress woman in the Matrix wall that entrepreneurs hit each time they level up.
numbers: Opportunity size scales with your size, roughly 1:1 with revenue level.

### POD-0400#15 | EVOLUTION | topic: content-brand | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:39:09
claim: After wanting to stay anonymous, he chose a personal brand because owned media delivers advertising value for free.
detail: Triggers: celebrity founders who built billion-dollar companies on a media channel; a conversation in which another entrepreneur said he would accept fame as the price of impact; and learning that B2B brands such as Nomad Capitalist and Neil Patel's worked. ### POD-0400#16 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:39:26
claim: Test commitment to a long-run bet with the question: if five years of one activity almost certainly paid off, would you really do only that?
detail: Most people say yes but their actions do not match, because it is easy to do something else. He suggests asking whether it would be unreasonable for a defined volume and skill level of activity for five years not to deliver, given the evidence. He notes that those switching back and forth may have short-term pops ahead of you but are slow long-term.
numbers: Horizon: five years of consistent personal-brand work.
