### POD-0447#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: verticals | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Hormozi says that if he built a physical product brand he would probably pick something like a nose strip: huge markup, recurring use, no willpower required.
detail: A nose strip costs pennies and sells for many times cost; people will rebuy every day if breathing improves; use needs no memory or healthy self-image, unlike supplements. He would rather tie it to something people do anyway, such as sleeping, which is why beauty products interest him more than fitness products.
numbers: Example cost 2 cents, price $1.
applies_to: Physical product launches

detail: Fitness apparel can be worn by someone who does not train and still signals a person who works out.

### POD-0447#04 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:15
claim: Print a word on the strip, mirrored so it reads in the mirror, as a daily reminder.
detail: Flavours become words people are working on (patience, kindness, persist). He cites a study in sales rooms where one word on the wall affected team performance. A clear strip showing only the word is the ideal.

### POD-0447#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:07
claim: The product must work first: disposable beats reusable, and the strip must stick to oily and non-oily skin.
detail: He dislikes the reusable kind because he loses the piece and has to leave bed to find it. The biggest failure would be a strip that does not stay on.

### POD-0447#06 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: pricing | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:59
claim: He would sell $19.99-$24.99 packs with five term options and cost of goods driven by shipping.
detail: Strips currently retail for about 30 cents each and cost a penny or less at scale. Options are 1, 3, 6 and 12 months of packs, with up to 15% off for 12 months. Gross profit about $16 on $20 after shipping of $2.50-$4.
numbers: Retail $19.99-$24.99; $0.30 per strip; cost under $0.01; shipping $2.50-$4; ~$16 gross profit per $20.

### POD-0447#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: unit-economics | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:59
claim: Steady-state subscribers equal monthly signups divided by monthly churn.
detail: 10,000 page visitors at 5% gives 500 signups; with 10% monthly churn the base plateaus at 5,000. At $20 that is $100,000 per month revenue and about $80,000 gross profit, roughly $1M a year.
numbers: 10,000 x 5% = 500; 500 / 10% = 5,000; $100,000 and $80,000 per month.

### POD-0447#08 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:30
claim: Build the capacity to print custom words on demand to charge 2-3x.
detail: A custom word costs $30 against a standard pack. White-labelled glue strips are copyable; a printing operation fed by a monthly text asking "what word this month" is a moat with high enterprise value.
numbers: $30 custom word; price 2x-3x.

### POD-0447#09 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: finance-exit | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:50
claim: Send influencers strips with their community's word and offer them 40% of revenue.
detail: Because margin is high, the partner earns a good return without building a business. A hundred influencers at a tenth of his volume equal ten times it, about $10M of volume, leaving roughly 20% margin after costs. That points to $3-4M EBITDA that does not depend on one face.
numbers: 40% share; 100 influencers; ~$10M volume; EBITDA $3-4M.

### POD-0447#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:43
claim: A wedge product pulls people in, and the brand promise keeps them: they buy for the breathing and stay for the betterment.
detail: Make the brand about the prospect and who they want to become, not the founder. Other cases he gives: fitness (come for the bikini, stay for community) and dating (come to meet people, stay for confidence).
applies_to: Physical and service brands

### POD-0447#11 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:12:43
claim: The garlic-and-ham story: give people what they want to deliver what they need.
detail: A boy cannot get the dog to eat garlic for ticks until his grandmother wraps it in ham. The thing they come for is not always the thing that keeps them.

### POD-0447#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:19
claim: Retention comes from a journey of becoming, since each solved problem ends the reason to stay.
detail: A customer goes through several transformations; after the acute one, a broader never-ending journey keeps them. People buy brands that reflect who they want to be, so they keep paying to reinforce that identity.
