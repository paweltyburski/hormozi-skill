### MZ-yiNHqY8opxA#01 | CASE | topic: sales-process | also: verticals, strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A wildfire-sprinkler company ($2.2M in year one, aiming at $200M in five years) faces an insurer deal mandating its product to 16,000 clients but closes only 2% of paid leads.
detail: Upmarket focus (high net worth and commercial) lifted close rate about 400% in three months. Paid leads close 2%, referrals 45-60%. Hormozi: they lack a sales motion; at 1% of 16,000 that is 160 deals, at 2% 320 deals; at about $50K a sale that is roughly $15M. Installing a sales process could multiply the business 7x, maybe 10-20x.
numbers: 16,000 clients; close 2% paid, 45-60% referral; $40-60K per sale; 320 deals x $50K = ~$15M (his figure).

### MZ-yiNHqY8opxA#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:46
claim: When choosing between customer types, Hormozi asks what the choice changes in his day-to-day activities; if little changes he is much more likely to do it, and if he must start another business it becomes a cost-benefit of the opportunity size.
detail: Question was whether to treat the insurer's 16,000 homes as one commercial client or as many B2C clients. He noted installs are done per home anyway; the caller said the product is the same and delivery won't be a problem. He sized revenue by probability of closing times number of customers.
