### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#01 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: GROWTH Onboarding | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: In six months a software portfolio company saved about $2M of bottom-line profit by fixing onboarding, expectations and support incentives.
detail: Ninety days after launching new software, churn, satisfaction and upsell take-up were bad. The old onboarding put 25 new customers on one call (company mission, a third of the time spent on password resets, hard pitch at the end) for a 55+ low-tech audience. Hormozi notes that a big inflow after launch breaks infrastructure and does not mean the sales method is wrong.
numbers: 25 customers per onboarding call; one person ran 6-8 such calls a day; about a third of call time on password resets; $2M saved in six months.

### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: Ascension Path | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:28:48
claim: Results of the whole programme: cancellations down 61%, 25% of escalation callers bought a higher tier, ascensions up 2.4x.
detail: Ascension to the higher-priced offer rose from about 20% to 48% of customers company-wide. Upgrades cost almost nothing to deliver in software, so they drop to profit and are not counted in the $2M.
numbers: -61% cancellations; 25% of escalation calls ascended; ascension 20% to 48% (2.4x).

### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:47
claim: The first 24 to 48 hours after purchase frame the whole relationship; adding proper onboarding to a recurring service can add two to three months of LTV.
detail: A bare email with links is not onboarding. Nailing this window raises upsell take-up and retention.
numbers: +2 to 3 months LTV for a recurring service.

### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#04 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: sales-process | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:44
claim: Run onboarding one-to-one as the "reinforce" step of CLOSER: restate why they bought, their problem and past attempts, and tie next steps to their stated personal goal.
detail: Ask why the stated goal (an extra $10,000 a month) matters to them, then say that the next steps serve that goal. Follow-through on the next steps doubled or tripled (exact figure not remembered). Give a quick win live (logins set up), connect support during the call, and leave every customer set up and logged in.

### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:44
claim: Pilot a costly change on a small slice and prove it out before scaling it.
detail: Only 15% of new customers got one-to-one onboarding because the team could not handle 25 per hour otherwise. Investor logic: a $70,000-$100,000 rep who saves $500,000-$2M is 5:1 or better; take any 5x-10x internal bet.
numbers: pilot size 15%; rep cost $70K-$100K vs savings $500K-$2M, about 5:1.
### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#06 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: BAMFAM | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:50
claim: End onboarding with call notes, tutorials tailored to what the customer said they care about and a named point of contact introduced live.
detail: Telling customers up front that you will take the notes frees them to listen. Link only the tutorials for the features they chose. Then a baton pass: salesperson to onboarding person to account rep, each introduced and a meeting booked, so the customer never has two contacts at once and never falls into no man's land.

### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Five Upsell Moments | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:03
claim: Two upsells work almost anywhere: more of the thing just bought, or more help with it; and delivering well first raises the odds of the next yes.
detail: Quality of onboarding raises perceived likelihood of achievement before the next offer, so the team needed less hard selling. Owners should not fear making offers right after a sale: buyers act in a burst (the marathon runner buying shoes, shorts, headphones), and a purchase often creates a new identity ("I'm a runner now") with matching products. Selling the full set immediately is more congruent with the dream you sold.

claim: Common complaints map to the value equation: "too techie" means effort too high, "not enough support" means likelihood fell, "takes too long" means delay.
detail: Three buyer complaints in this case: too hard to use, too little support (expected hand-holding on a DIY product), slow results.
### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:15:36
claim: Re-scripting the sales call to set honest expectations solved two of the three complaints without lowering close rate.
detail: Listening to calls showed how phrasing implied ease and speed. Lower, believable claims can raise perceived likelihood because prospects believe them; the team sold the same amount. The alternative is to add to the back-end delivery, which costs money.

### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#10 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:08
claim: Put support chat inside the product and log which page opened it, to learn where customers get stuck and to feed the product roadmap.
detail: A support team existed but was hard to reach, so the complaint was perception. After the chat bubble, support requests showed that 82% of complaints came from one point, which the product team then simplified. Customers have both an assigned account rep and general support.

### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-team | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:52
claim: Never let the sales team handle dissatisfied customers: it kills their conviction and their calendar; create a dedicated escalations team that triages.
detail: Conviction (tone, pace, emphasis) is a strong predictor of close rate and falls when reps field complaints about last week's sales. The triage team becomes the catch-all, which raised the sales team's close rate and morale.

### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#13 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:05
claim: Pay the escalations team a 10-15% commission on saved customers.
detail: Without incentive, customer success did not chase customers. A saved sale means losing a commission instead of a refund, a chargeback, and the bigger cost of lost reputation. Paying about $200 to save a $2,000 sale beats $0.
numbers: commission 10-15%; $200 to save $2,000.

### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#14 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:51
claim: Script and rehearse customer-success conversations as you do sales: roughly 90% of problems fall into a few repeated cases.
detail: Customer support asks "what can I help you with?" without structure. The back-end questions (cannot log in, step one, something broke) are as predictable as sales objections (spouse, no card). List them, script the answers and practice.
numbers: ~90% of customers present the same few problems.

### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#15 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:27:57
claim: Reach out to an unresponsive escalated customer with rising frequency, the inverse of lead nurture.
detail: Leads get contacted less over time as interest fades. A customer with a problem gets one attempt (email, SMS, call) on day one, two on day two, three on day three, because urgency grows. With the incentive in place, reps made three to five attempts.
numbers: 1, 2, 3 attempts on days 1, 2, 3.

### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#16 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:58
claim: As a litmus test, your CAC should only rise with CPMs; if it doubles or triples in a short period, the likely reason is negative word of mouth.
detail: Example: ads that once got customers at 10:1 and now at 5:1 or 3:1. If CPMs rose 15% a year, CAC should rise about 15%, and LTV growth can offset it. A bigger jump means that people who would have bought were told not to by unhappy customers. Unserved customers cost more than a refund: three to five lost customers.
numbers: CAC growth roughly equal to CPM growth (example 15% a year).
### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#17 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: management-ops | fw: NPS | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:31:25
claim: A low refund rate hides the many who dislike you but do not bother to refund; track CSAT and NPS, because measuring is itself an intervention.
detail: When he first asked, the company had almost no such metrics. Tracking alone moves behaviour (like weighing yourself). You are never too big for good customer service (Chick-fil-A, Amazon).

### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#18 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: Ascension Path | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:33:42
claim: Call the unhappy customers: they pick up quickly and one in four buying an offer four times as large recovers all refund losses.
detail: Let them vent and ask why they did not buy. With a $1,000 product and a $4,000 upsell, one of four angry callers buying the upsell covers the other three. He cites Paul Graham: talking to customers solves most business problems.
numbers: 1 in 4 buys a 4x offer = 100% of refund loss recovered ($1,000 vs $4,000).

### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#19 | DECISION_RULE | topic: retention-ltv | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:34:31

detail: Counterintuitive: oftentimes the wealthier the customer, the less they want outreach. Hormozi himself does not want to talk to vendors; some customers who spend a lot relative to their means will want to talk all the time.
### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#20 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:35:22
claim: Two companies each adding 100 customers a year are identical on day one but not at year three if one loses everyone.
detail: A business losing 100% yearly and selling 100, 200, 300 is still marketing-capped, like direct-response companies with a $300M marketing team earning $30M. As an investor, his first question is how many of last year's customers still pay, because that is the base to build on.

### YT-afbP6sB_Atc#21 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:39:08
claim: In another company the second-highest-LTV customers were users of only three of ten services who were proactively moved to a half-price plan.
detail: They were reached before cancelling, kept the same value in their eyes and "never cancel"; a cancellation after two months is worth far less than ten months plus referrals. Treat customers as seriously as leads.
numbers: using 3 of 10 services; plan at half the current price.
