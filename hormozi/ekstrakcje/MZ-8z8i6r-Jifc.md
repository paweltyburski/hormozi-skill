### MZ-8z8i6r-Jifc#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Hard wins are what change you, and easy ones are forgettable; as competence grows the stakes you need to play for must grow too.
detail: A win that mattered at five is not a win when competent. Illustration: a billionaire playing ten-dollar poker hands wastes time, so you must put more on the line to win bigger.

### MZ-8z8i6r-Jifc#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:17
claim: Your standards always outrun your capacity, so you will always feel short of where you want to be, which is not the same as being bad.
detail: Quoting a drummer: it is fine to feel you suck, because as capacity rises standards rise too. The gap is felt lack, not actual lack.

### MZ-8z8i6r-Jifc#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:00
claim: Shortcutting inputs with AI can deliver outputs but misses that people tell the story of their life through the process.
detail: Using his $100M Leads book: even a verbatim AI-generated copy from a brief would show AI knew about leads, not him. Focusing only on outcomes leads to shortcuts that fail, and only on inputs means never checking whether the suffering produced anything. Both views (purpose is who you become versus what exists because of you) have merit; in hard times he leans to the first.
