### YT-1IhvZC-Wpqs#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:50
claim: Give people what they want to get the chance to give them what they need: wrap the "garlic" in "ham".
detail: From a story of a child who could only get a dog to take garlic (the cure for ticks) once it was wrapped in ham. Prospects lack your context, so go back to who you were before you knew what you know, meet them where they are, then lead them across the bridge. You cannot begin with the end.

### YT-1IhvZC-Wpqs#02 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: offers, promotions-launches | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:14
claim: Honest fitness messaging (count macros, lift) drew no buyers, so he sold short challenges and detoxes first and introduced the real method afterwards.
detail: People in a quick-fix mindset responded to short-duration offers. Once trust was earned, he reframed: nobody wants to lose it and regain it, so a long-term approach (calories, macros, proper training) was the "garlic". That conversation was now honest and had context.

### YT-1IhvZC-Wpqs#03 | CASE | topic: content-brand | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:50
claim: A dentist client hated the ad and landing pages that were working, and was told he is not the customer because he already has nice teeth.
detail: Owners judge marketing from the position of someone who already knows the answer; the test is how the real prospect responds.

### YT-1IhvZC-Wpqs#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: content-brand | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:02
claim: When competitors market a quick-fix version that works, treat it as the prospect's current truth and relate to their problems and fears before converting them.
detail: Use this wherever the buyer must take steps or break a belief to get the result, such as coaching or any transformation.
