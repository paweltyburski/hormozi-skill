### POD-0546#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:30
claim: The "buy box" splits investment criteria into hard metrics a business must meet and soft factors about the team, and Hormozi says he might even argue the soft ones are more important.
detail: Hormozi says business owners can read the criteria as a way to raise their own company's value, and founders can use them to start a business correctly, since fixing a broken one is harder. He joined a group of gym owners before he had a gym to avoid mistakes.
### POD-0546#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:14
claim: Target companies are cash-flow positive, profitable, growing and founder-led, with at least $1M in cash profit.
detail: The first rule was more than $3M of top-line revenue; it changed to at least $1M of cash profit, not paper profit, since they invest for cash flow. Portfolio at that time ranged from about $1M to $20M profit, with a median near $5M; a few outliers carry a portfolio.
numbers: Minimum $1M cash profit; portfolio range ~$1M to $20M profit; median ~$5M.
### POD-0546#03 | DEFINITION | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:13
claim: Cash-flow positive and profitable are different: cash accounting records money in and out, while accrual spreads lumpy items over the year.
detail: Example: an annual event that costs $1M in one month looks like a large loss on a cash basis even though its revenue arrives around then. Bigger businesses use accrual for a balanced picture; most small businesses use cash.

### POD-0546#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:14
claim: Hormozi prefers simple, boring businesses with few moving parts and asks how soon and how deeply AI could disrupt them.
detail: He feels more confident about in-person experiences and physical services (a lawn business, a photography chain) and thinks there is more time before disruption than for something like a design agency. A photography chain they backed went from 1 to about 40 locations in roughly 30 months.
numbers: Photography chain: 1 location to ~40 in ~30 months.
### POD-0546#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:49
claim: A target needs either exceptional marketing or an exceptional product; his investing mistakes were companies mediocre at both.
detail: With a strong product, proven growth systems can be wrapped around it. Without either, growth is hard.

### POD-0546#06 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:34
claim: Assess product quality from public reviews: customer review sites, an employee review site, and a business-complaint bureau.
detail: Some one-star reviews are normal; the majority should be very happy with the outcome. Profit grows from how valuable the core outcome is to a specific customer.

### POD-0546#07 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:14
claim: Estimate the value the product gives the average customer and how much of it the firm captures; under-monetisation is an opening for pricing or repackaging.
detail: Quantify the customer value first, then compare with price.

### POD-0546#08 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:08:15
claim: Hormozi likes to build at least one compounding vehicle into a business, because otherwise scaling is only by force (ads, affiliates), which is hard to sustain at big numbers.
detail: In his experience there are two ways (maybe three): customers who keep buying and buy more each year, or a selling machine that compounds itself, most often word of mouth, including salespeople who recruit salespeople. Forced growth is easy at $200K-$1M a month but hard toward $10M a month; otherwise you flatline.
numbers: Forced growth works around $200K-$1M/month; difficult toward $10M/month. Example: sales staff growing 10% a month.
### POD-0546#09 | DEFINITION | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:07
claim: Recurring revenue is a fixed subscription; reoccurring revenue is repeat buying without a contract, and either lets you predict that today's customers still buy in five years.
detail: Being credited for today's sales years later avoids restarting from zero every period. Examples: a streaming subscription (recurring) versus a soft drink or ad platform (reoccurring).
numbers: Test horizon: predictable share of customers still buying in 5 years.

### POD-0546#10 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:12:25
claim: When customers never leave, the same ad spend builds a customer base that profits compound on.
detail: Spending $10,000 a month to get 10 customers a month, and losing none, gives 1,200 customers after 10 years on the same $10,000 monthly spend. Profit then drives enterprise value as the sum of all future cash flow.
numbers: $10,000/month ad spend; 10 customers/month; 1,200 customers after 10 years.

### POD-0546#11 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:15
claim: Choose a niche that is easy to win, where rivals are fewer and less capitalised.
detail: Tech start-ups face very smart, well-funded competitors; a lawn-care company does not. Paraphrased advice from an investor friend: fish where the fish are, in an open pond, not where everyone is already fishing.

### POD-0546#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:14
claim: Founder fit weighs most: choose partners you want to be locked in with for years and want to see rich, who live by three values.
detail: The values are sincere candor, unimpeachable character and competitive greatness. Poor founders also repel good staff, which would harm the talent in the holding company.

### POD-0546#13 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:28
claim: The larger the investment, the more the existing leadership team matters; the smaller, the more the founder matters, because the team can be added around them.
detail: Check whether a founder can attract the people to go from, say, $1M to $10M a month. Hormozi says that he wants at least 20% of the leadership team to have already been there and done that.
numbers: Hormozi wants at least 20% of the leadership team to have been to $100M once or twice (example: a company at $20M revenue and $7M EBITDA).
### POD-0546#14 | BENCHMARK | topic: lead-getters | also: unit-economics | fw: Referral Rate minus Churn | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:01
claim: When 25-30% of customers come by word of mouth, acquisition spend is effectively discounted by about a third.
detail: Referred revenue drops largely to the bottom line. A business with no word of mouth is not neutral: negative word of mouth is quietly eroding its profit.
numbers: 25-30% of customers from word of mouth is a strong sign; about one third discount on acquisition cost.
