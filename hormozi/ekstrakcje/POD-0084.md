### POD-0084#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: management-ops | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:58
claim: An internal sales contest lifts results sharply, but the lift disappears within days once it ends.
detail: In a beta, Hormozi ran an internal competition and everyone beat their sales numbers. Three to four days after it ended, daily sales for the group had fallen to less than half. His lesson: focus alone can double sales, and contests are only a temporary source of focus. Contests also wear out because people get used to them.
numbers: Daily group sales dropped by more than half within 3-4 days after the contest ended.

### POD-0084#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:50
claim: A lasting substitute for contests is a team that sincerely believes it is rescuing customers from a market that fails them.
detail: Instead of competing against each other or against rivals, the team competes against the customer's problem, which is bigger than any company and never gets stale. It only works if leaders really mean it and their actions match their words, because staff will detect a lie. He states that culture beats strategy every time.

### POD-0084#03 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:44
claim: Restate the vision and the reason for the work often, leading with why before how and what.
detail: Gym Launch does a full-team monthly meeting that restates the vision, because new hires keep arriving. The same message is repeated in snippets daily and weekly. Team meetings ask how the company is solving the problem better and how much market penetration it has reached, rather than how it is doing against competitors.
applies_to: Growing teams with steady new hires.

### POD-0084#04 | TACTIC | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Every new play is beta-tested on a small set of representative markets before rollout, with several betas running at once.
detail: Gym Launch tests each new execution (marketing, sales or otherwise) with about 15 gyms in 15 representative markets. At any time four or five betas run, each targeting one area such as show-up rate, page conversion or gym close rate, usually by trying different scripts.
numbers: About 15 gyms in 15 markets per beta; 4-5 betas in parallel.

### POD-0084#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: verticals | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:38
claim: In one beta, the average gym added $12,500 of recurring monthly revenue in 30 days from a single play.
detail: Hormozi saw the daily sales of the beta gyms during the contest. Separately he states the company went from zero to over $30 million in roughly two years (the exact span is unclear in audio).
numbers: Average +$12,500 recurring revenue per gym in 30 days; company at $30M+ in about 24 months (unclear in audio).
applies_to: Gyms (Gym Launch clients).

### POD-0084#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Many-Sided Die | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:33
claim: A test whose result you already know is not an experiment; accept that most tests against conventional wisdom fail.
detail: Roughly nine in ten experiments against conventional wisdom will not work, because conventional wisdom is usually correct. The one in ten that hits is worth a thousand runs, so Gym Launch deliberately fails often and fast in order to find rare big wins on large problems.
numbers: About 1 in 10 tests is a home run; 9 in 10 are not.
