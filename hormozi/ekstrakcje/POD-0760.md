### POD-0760#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:44
claim: SCALE is a repeating cycle for growing any activity: Start, Compound, Augment, Leverage, Expand.
detail: Start the thing; compound by doing more volume; augment by doing it better; leverage by making it consistent and reproducible by someone else (checklist, system); expand into a new channel, territory or process, which restarts the cycle at Start. Example: Instagram posts, then better posts, then reliable output, then TikTok. In customer service: owner does it, does more, gets better, systematises, hires the next person. Ask where you are on the cycle in each function.

### POD-0760#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:51
claim: Volume comes first because more begets better; reliability matters more than occasional peaks.
detail: Better is only valuable if consistent (home runs every time beat alternating home runs and misses). You cannot learn what is good without doing a lot.

### POD-0760#03 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:43
claim: Improve by doing a batch, studying the top performers against the rest, and repeating more of what differed.
detail: Fire ten arrows, find the closest, review the "film" for what differed, do more of that and less of the rest, repeat. Costs time, not money, and relies on data rather than claims. Skill grows with attention to detail: when you cannot tell your top 10% from bottom 10%, your job is to find unobserved variables. For ads, study the best ads you ran and ask why, which beats finding the newest hack; same for 10/10 customer reviews and for referrers.
numbers: Example batch of 10 attempts per round.

### POD-0760#04 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:36
claim: Skill test for hires: how many and how good are the metrics they track, and what action each metric triggers.
detail: A candidate who says "I will sell hard" shows no skill. Level one: which variables exist. Level two: if a metric moves, what behaviour changes it. A master sees many small markers (e.g. a 92 out of 100 aiming for 93), a novice judges only by the outcome. The same frame is used to train staff: tell them the variables and how to move them.

### POD-0760#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:36
claim: Scaling stages are better defined by headcount than revenue, since revenue-based frameworks only describe the author's business.
detail: Organisation structure and communication needs depend on number of people more than on business type; e.g. benefits unlikely at one employee, payroll needed at five. Exceptions exist (Instagram sold for $1B with 13 employees). Departments can sit at different stages.
numbers: Instagram: ~$1B sale with 13 employees.
