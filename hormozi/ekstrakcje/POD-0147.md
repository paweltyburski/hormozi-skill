### POD-0147#01 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: sales-team | fw: Anchor Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:48
claim: Train reps to pitch the biggest package first and aim for a gasp; no gasp means they did not go high enough.
detail: The idea is from Nick Elliott (who ran a 35-person phone sales team selling $2,000 packages to cold opt-ins, earlier at Six Pack Shortcuts). Make the long, paid-in-full package the first price; because the shock is the goal, new reps lose their fear of it. A stretched price expectation lifts later purchases.
numbers: 35-person phone team; $2,000 packages.

### POD-0147#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Anchor Upsell | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:22
claim: At a high-end tailor, a $12,000 first jacket made a $3,800 suit feel like a deal and later spending feel like a rounding error.
detail: He ended up spending about $10,000 that day with his wife on clothing and accessories.
numbers: $12,000 jacket; suit about $3,800; about $10,000 total spend.

### POD-0147#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-team | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Reps sell out of their own wallet: if a price feels high to them, they assume it does to the buyer.
detail: Remedy: explain that experts underestimate the value of what they know (a mechanic selling a radiator, a layperson who simply wants it handled). Do not let their experience devalue the offer.

### POD-0147#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:41
claim: The more someone pays, the more they pay attention; sell the highest personal investment, not a close.
detail: Someone who pays $2,100 cares more than one who pays $21. Frame the goal as how much the person can invest in themselves; selling to close makes tone combative in objections.
numbers: $21 vs $2,100 example.

### POD-0147#05 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: money-models | fw: Sell to Goal | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:45
claim: Whatever promotion a person walks in for, sell their long-term goal with a guarantee and a same-day discount for paying in full.
detail: A short programme probably cannot solve their problem. One or two of every five can be upsold this way.
numbers: front end about $600 to $2,000-$3,000 tickets; 1 to 2 out of 5 take it.

### POD-0147#06 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:45
claim: In general, a $600 price is typically the impulse-buy price almost everyone can be sold at, yet he says 20% to 30% could pay two to three times that.
detail: Underselling that group costs revenue; match the investment to what each person can afford.
numbers: $600 impulse price (typically); 20-30% could pay 2x to 3x.
### POD-0147#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:52
claim: If they decline the big package, downsell to their maximum, shortening duration as needed while aiming to solve the original problem.
detail: He says that selling this way means you can 2x or 3x the average front-end ticket.
numbers: Can 2x-3x average front-end ticket.
### POD-0147#08 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: Sell to Goal | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:39
claim: A 33-week plan at $99 a week means asking for $3,300 upfront, justified because 6 to 8 weeks cannot change a life.
detail: Losing 50 pounds will not happen in six weeks; 33 to 36 weeks genuinely can change a life.
numbers: $99 x 33 weeks = $3,300.
