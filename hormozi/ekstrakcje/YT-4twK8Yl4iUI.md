### YT-4twK8Yl4iUI#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: lead-getters | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Growth should be a loop (effort and money buy customers, customers get results, results produce reviews and word of mouth, which bring more customers) rather than a line needing fresh input per customer.
detail: Most firms run only the first half. The loop is built by adding a deliberate process at the results-to-reviews step and the reviews-to-customers step, so the business eventually runs without constant founder input.

### YT-4twK8Yl4iUI#02 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: retention-ltv | fw: Seven Ways to Ask for Referrals | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:46
claim: Tactic one for reviews: give a surprise gift after delivery, then let a different person (a manager) offer to pay the staff member who served them if the customer leaves a review.
detail: The gift scales with spend and banks reciprocity. The incentive is aimed at the server or plumber, not the customer, so the customer repays a person who helped them. The farther the asker is from the owner, the more compelling. Check local advertising law. Works for physical products with a QR code in the box; gift before or after both worked.
numbers: Gift roughly proportional to spend: $20 spend, mints; $100 spend, $5 dessert; $1,000 spend, ~$50 gift.

### YT-4twK8Yl4iUI#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: lead-getters | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:15
claim: Size the gift and staff bonus to what one extra review is worth, which is highest when the business has few reviews.
detail: In one story (Leila choosing a hair person on Yelp), a 5.0 listing with about 20 reviews lost to a 4.6 listing with about 1,000. He asks what each extra review is worth if the most-reviewed listing ends up with 90% of search volume, and answers "a hell of a lot". Keep the system running even at high volume.
numbers: 5.0 stars with ~20 reviews lost to 4.6 stars with ~1,000 in one anecdote; 90% of search volume for the top listing was posed as a hypothetical ("if"), not a measured figure.
### YT-4twK8Yl4iUI#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:01
claim: A restaurant manager spending about five minutes an hour on a review pitch could collect nearly 4,000 reviews a year.
detail: 10 tables turned per hour for 10 open hours is 100 tables a day; if one in ten reviews, that is 10 a day. The pitch takes about 20 seconds a table.
numbers: 100 tables/day x 10% = ~10 reviews/day, almost 4,000/year.

### YT-4twK8Yl4iUI#05 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:10
claim: Track the share of each server's tables or technician's jobs that leave a five-star review, as a revenue-linked satisfaction metric, and copy the top performer.
detail: If one person gets far more reviews, ask what they do differently and teach everyone.

### YT-4twK8Yl4iUI#06 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:00
claim: Make reviewing frictionless with a QR code by the check, and rotate the target platform as each fills up.
detail: Move the code from Yelp to Google, then Meta, then eventually to an Instagram follow or a phone-number sign-up for the text list. Customers find the business through different platforms, so spread coverage.

### YT-4twK8Yl4iUI#07 | CASE | topic: lead-getters | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:51
claim: To open a second gym with no reviews, he swapped overstocked $5 T-shirts for reviews on three platforms plus a check-in.
detail: Members at the first gym got a free shirt for trying the new location and posting on Yelp, Google and Facebook and checking in. Overnight he had roughly 155 five-star reviews (number unclear in audio) and members wearing the merch, creating offline word of mouth. Suits consumer businesses with regular visits; B2B and one-off services should use tactics one or four.
numbers: Shirt cost $5; four actions required (3 reviews + check-in).
applies_to: B2C businesses with a recurring member base.

### YT-4twK8Yl4iUI#08 | CASE | topic: lead-getters | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:06
claim: A high-volume budget restaurant opening a new site offered a dollar off the meal for a review, about $1 per review.
detail: The dollar was about 5% of the average ticket. A QR code sat on the check; the server brought it back with the discount applied after seeing the review. The owner agreed 5,000 reviews for $5,000 was a good deal.
numbers: $1 off, ~5% of average ticket, so about a $20 ticket.

### YT-4twK8Yl4iUI#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:52
claim: Latency weakens an incentive: a discount available right now at the check beats a bigger one on a future purchase.
detail: Risk and delay behave alike in the buyer's mind; most people prefer $2 off today to $100 in a year. Sequence: present price, get acceptance, offer the discount, verify the review, recompute, hand over the final check.

### YT-4twK8Yl4iUI#10 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:14:55
claim: For online or B2B services, unlock a trial of a higher tier, a training, or an event ticket in exchange for a review.
detail: Marginal cost of a higher-tier trial is low, and the customer gets a taste of the upgrade, possibly becoming a more valuable customer. Example: an SEO agency adds a domain or backlinks, or gives a 30-day upper-level trial.
applies_to: B2B, national or digital businesses with several service levels.
