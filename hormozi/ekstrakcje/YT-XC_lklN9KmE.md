### YT-XC_lklN9KmE#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Owners set member or customer goals but skip the arithmetic that connects the goal to weekly inflow, so inflow is usually the cap.
detail: Gym owners name targets of 200, 300 or 500 members without working backwards to the number of new sign-ups per week the goal needs. Retention matters, but at some point acceptable metrics set the required inflow.

### YT-XC_lklN9KmE#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv, verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:46
claim: A service business should not expect churn below about 1% a month, and services are "easy in, easy out", so a large one must accept back-end churn and use fast inflow as its advantage.
detail: Near-1% churn usually only happens when growth is purely organic referral. Software is stickier because switching is a pain, while switching a service is easy. A service owner who does not maximise how fast people can be signed up wastes the main advantage of the model. Grow from organic, outbound and paid together.
numbers: Service churn floor about 1% per month.

### YT-XC_lklN9KmE#03 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:34
claim: About ten gyms on the done-for-you service paused in one week because they could not handle the lead flow and booked appointments.
detail: A software company's CEO relayed the top complaint about Hormozi's programme: owners felt expected to sell all day. Hormozi's reply: that is what they said they wanted. Most owners want a full gym until they see what filling it takes. Earlier, gyms flown into by his team went from about 80 to 280 clients in 21 days and owners panicked.
numbers: ~10 gyms paused; 80 to 280 clients in 21 days.

### YT-XC_lklN9KmE#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:07
claim: A trial or front-end sign-up is only the "try" phase; even recurring sign-ups must be resold into a longer commitment.
detail: Stickiness appears after the second sale. Hormozi saw this across his own gyms, so the inflow math should count both the trial sale and the later conversion sale.

### YT-XC_lklN9KmE#06 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:42
claim: At 200 members and 10% monthly churn (20 lost), a gym converting half its trials must sell 40 trials a month; at the 35% industry average it must sell 60.
detail: To grow rather than merely replace losses, a lot of times you need more than double the inflow, and sometimes even more, since you also lose some efficiency on the back. Without this volume you will never grow.
numbers: 20 lost per month at 200 members and 10% churn; trial-to-member 50% (own) vs 35% (industry average); 40 versus 60 front-end sales per month.
### YT-XC_lklN9KmE#07 | EXAMPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: sales-process, paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:33
claim: Worked back-solve for a 300-member gym at 10% churn: 30 sign-ups a month just to hold, which requires roughly 60 appointments a week and about 15 leads a day.
detail: Hold 300 at 10% churn means 30 replacements. Converting half of trials means 60 trials, or 15 a week. Closing one of two conversations means 120 conversations a month, about 30 a week, so about 5 a day over six days, roughly 2.5-3 hours of 30-minute sessions. Allow for no-shows and the figure doubles to about 60 appointments a week. To grow another 30 on top, double it again.
numbers: 30 replacements; 60 trials a month; 120 conversations a month; ~60 appointments a week with no-shows; 30-minute sessions.

### YT-XC_lklN9KmE#08 | BENCHMARK | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:40
claim: Working from the appointment target back to leads gave about 15 leads a day, which at $10 a lead is $150 a day, or about $4,500 a month in ad spend.
detail: Hormozi says he thinks a lot of people set goals and balk once the required effort reaches even a third of what it would take. The ad spend figure is the cost of the leads feeding the funnel above, for a gym.
numbers: 15 leads/day; $10 per lead; $150/day; ~$4,500/month.
### YT-XC_lklN9KmE#09 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:46
claim: Run selling and fulfilment concurrently by blocking calendar time: two hours daily for outreach to existing customers, two hours for onboarding, plus orientations and sales slots.
detail: Do not run ads for a couple of weeks, stop, and then service them. Inflow must continue every week. If a team cannot manage its schedule to do both, that is the actual constraint.

### YT-XC_lklN9KmE#10 | EXAMPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:14:17
claim: Staffing math for 30 new customers a week: one employee handles onboarding consults and conversion meetings, a second full-time salesperson takes the first sales calls.
detail: 30 onboarding consults of 30 minutes is 15 hours a week, 3 hours a day over five days. 30 conversion meetings from trial to membership add about 3 more, making 6 hours a day, plus 2 hours of outreach to current customers. The separate salesperson meets about 60 prospects a week, 10 a day at 30 minutes, 5 hours daily.
numbers: 15 hours/week onboarding; 6 hours/day onboarding plus conversion; 60 sales meetings/week.

### YT-XC_lklN9KmE#11 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:55
claim: Meeting 200 prospects means about 100 will say no, and an owner who cannot accept that emotionally will never reach the volume.
detail: Experienced salespeople know the objections come from the prospect's baggage, not from them. A person who says no for lack of a card is just the next one to move past.
numbers: 200 meetings to 100 no's at a 50% close rate.

### YT-XC_lklN9KmE#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:06
claim: Do not expect order-of-magnitude gains from conversion or retention; work from realistic metrics and, if needed, work double time to pay for the hire that removes the bottleneck.
detail: You will never close all shows or keep everyone month to month. Waiting for perfect improvements means never starting.
