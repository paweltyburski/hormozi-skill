### POD-0515#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: outreach | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:15
claim: People underestimate required volume by 10x to 50x: someone sent 100 DMs over eight weeks when the rule is 100 a day.
detail: That is roughly one fifty-sixth of the effort. Typical thinking is "what if I double", but the real gap is often far larger.
numbers: 100 DMs in eight weeks versus 100 a day (about 1/56 of the effort); call team minimum 200 calls a day versus 10 a day.

### POD-0515#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: management-ops | fw: Ad Assembly Process | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:15
claim: A portfolio company was told to hold a full creative day every 14 days and recorded four ads, where Hormozi's standard was at least about 40.
detail: When ads dominated his business he ran 30 to 40 new ads every three days. He asked what the person did all day; four ads every two weeks felt like a lot to him. Cold calling is now about 75% of the gym business.
numbers: 4 ads versus about 40 minimum per creative day; 30-40 new ads every three days; cold calls about 75% of gym business now.

### POD-0515#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: operator-mindset | fw: Leverage | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:49
claim: Leverage means finding the one variable with the most influence and focusing ruthlessly on it; people spend 80% on polish and 20% on the content that drives 80% of results.
detail: Litmus test: if you already work most of your hours and get little, the issue is not hours but that what you do is irrelevant ("cutting grass with scissors").
numbers: Split of 80/20 between content and polish.

### POD-0515#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: management-ops | fw: CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:29
claim: Every business objective reduces to getting more customers or making them worth more; he writes both on the board at quarterly reviews.
detail: For each quarterly objective, ask which of the two numbers it moves. Which one is the bottleneck depends on the stage.

### POD-0515#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: retention-ltv | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:38
claim: At $1-3M prove fit with one product, one avatar and one channel; from about $3M pause acquisition and fix retention and LTV before pushing marketing again.
detail: Doubling a business by selling alone requires doubling sales velocity each time (50 to 100 to 200 to 400 customers a month), which becomes untenable and plateaus. He says that usually, if retention and LTV are fixed properly, the same acquisition can grow the company from $3M to $10M with much less effort, and then added marketing gets it to $30M and $50M.
numbers: 50, 100, 200, 400 customers a month; usually $3M to $10M with fixed acquisition, then $30M and $50M.
### POD-0515#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:05
claim: Growing by marketing alone can make you rich but not wealthy, since a business built on front end without word of mouth and high LTV is hard to sell.
detail: The media arbitrage eventually shuts down, margins compress and the business can vanish overnight. A stable customer base paying for a product they like gives gross profit to weather that and build other channels.

### POD-0515#09 | BENCHMARK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:46
claim: Acquisition.com has eight portfolio companies (eleven counting three it mostly sold but still owns 25-40% of), and he says maybe about 25% of the entrepreneurs who come to him are product-driven and usually the other 75% promotion-driven.
detail: Product-driven founders usually need acquisition work; promotion-driven ones usually need back-end work. He suspects his brand attracts promotion people.
numbers: 8 companies (11 including three at 25-40% ownership); roughly 25% product-driven, usually 75% promotion-driven (his estimate).
### POD-0515#10 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:19:19
claim: In portfolio companies at about this size he repeatedly fixes three things: data tracking, talent, and monetisation.
detail: Analogy: a Mona Lisa full of bullet holes, filled in systematically. Data: most lack consolidated metrics for customer lifetime value and click-to-close, so decisions are by feel. Talent: "genius of a thousand hands", so first real directors and managers are needed, some growing from 10 to 30, some replaced. Monetisation: reprice, restructure the offer, add ascension or continuity.

### POD-0515#13 | PRINCIPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:30:30
claim: Word of mouth is the only quadratic acquisition channel: every other channel is dollar in, fixed amount out, while word of mouth multiplies.
detail: Every business already has word of mouth, whether neutral, positive or negative. Negative talk silently prevents sales, which is how a company can be shrunk by it without noticing.

### POD-0515#14 | DECISION_RULE | topic: paid-ads | also: lead-getters | fw: CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:31:24
claim: If cost per acquisition rises far faster than ad costs (CPM), Hormozi suggests hidden negative word of mouth is likely working against you, rather than only platform changes.
detail: Paid-ad owners blame platform changes when CPMs rise 10-15% but acquisition cost quadruples. Positive word of mouth lowers acquisition cost even when impression cost holds or rises.
numbers: CPM up 10-15% with CAC up 4x; test case: CAC up 3x with CPM up 30%.
