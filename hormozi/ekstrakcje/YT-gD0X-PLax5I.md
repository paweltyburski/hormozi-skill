### YT-gD0X-PLax5I#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:33
claim: Work means outputs, and outputs equal volume (how many times) multiplied by leverage (what you get each time); speed is output per unit of time.
detail: He rejects physics, hours on the clock and effort as definitions. Hours are a weak separator, since you can work 16-18 hours a day and if everyone did, no one could work harder than another. To outwork others you raise volume, leverage, or both. He usually teaches leverage but found that many owners do far less volume than he assumed.
### YT-gD0X-PLax5I#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: leads-fundamentals, retention-ltv | fw: Crazy Eight | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:50
claim: Growth has four levers, each tied to concrete activities: more traffic, higher conversion, higher prices, and more repeat purchases.
detail: Traffic: advertise more or better. Conversion: practise sales or improve the offer. Price: simply decide to raise it. Repeat buying: reach-outs, exit interviews, prepayments, better onboarding, faster results, client appreciation events. Time spent outside these has the same effect as doing nothing. Small businesses should put most effort into traffic and conversion, since without customers there is nobody to reprice or resell.
applies_to: Smaller businesses weighted to traffic and conversion

### YT-gD0X-PLax5I#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: paid-ads, outreach, content-brand | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:20
claim: Spend about four hours a day on your chosen advertising method, because excellence in advertising comes from repetition.
detail: The four hours mean: scripting, analysing, writing copy and recording ads if you run ads; reaching out across platforms if outreach; making content on every platform if content; and outreach, ads or content that feed affiliates if you use partners. Skill is measured by volume (he points to having taken 4,000 sales consults).
numbers: 4 hours per day in the active acquisition method.

### YT-gD0X-PLax5I#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:56
claim: Let non-constraint fires burn for a season, and when a new constraint appears, hand off the mastered job and give the new one four hours a day.
detail: Strategy is prioritising. If traffic is mastered and leads pile up but the owner cannot close, conversion becomes the limiter: train someone else to run advertising and put the four hours into closing. If your business is stalled and you keep doing the same things, those things are the wrong ones.

### YT-gD0X-PLax5I#05 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: sales-team | fw: Game Tape Review (Five A's) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:00
claim: Conversion practice list for an owner who must close: track the close rate and rehearse the script daily with feedback.
detail: Record all sales conversations (brick and mortar: record from behind the person with a visible sign that you record); watch recordings of winning sales each morning; say the script aloud daily; hire someone for daily feedback; take two minutes of mental prep before each consult; send a pre-sale questionnaire to everyone; qualify by phone to pre-sell them. Once the skill is built, maintaining it takes less time.
numbers: 2 minutes of prep before each consult.

### YT-gD0X-PLax5I#06 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:12:16
claim: Split time into maker blocks (long, uninterrupted, low urgency, high importance) and manager blocks (short, many), and avoid mixing them, since the two meeting is when disaster happens.
detail: Maker time is blocks of four to six hours, 14 per week; an interruption destroys the whole block, so a productive maker calendar is empty. Manager time is blocks of 5-15 minutes, 100+ per week, filled with no gaps for training, coordination and firefighting. Inserting small management slices into maker time kills half a day. Explain the difference to the team.
numbers: Maker: 4-6 hour blocks, 14 per week. Manager: 5-15 minute blocks, 100+ per week.
### YT-gD0X-PLax5I#07 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:43
claim: Protect maker time with fixed habits: one project, early start, no notifications, no windows, and treat weekends as work days.
detail: His routine: decide the single most important project (it can stay the same for a long time), wake at 4 am and work until 9 am, notifications off, dark windowless office, earplugs. There are no emergencies except a real 911. Same bedtime every night including weekends; drinking costs two days (the day and the recovery). Weekend work adds 104 uninterrupted days a year. The time slot can be late evening instead.
numbers: 4 am to 9 am (5 hours); 104 weekend days a year; bed 9 pm, asleep by 10 pm, up 4-5 am.

### YT-gD0X-PLax5I#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:17
claim: You become more productive by eliminating drains than by adding optimisations, and real maker hours decide who moves 30 times faster.
detail: Eliminations are permanent and free energy; time hacks are not kept. He compares an owner doing about 3 productive hours a week with someone doing 16 hours a day six days a week, which is 96 productive hours. Many owners, honestly counted, have about four real maker hours a week.

### YT-gD0X-PLax5I#09 | BENCHMARK | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:18:08
claim: Outsourcing food, cleaning and laundry costs about $1,500 per month and returns 96 hours, worth it if your hour earns more than $15.
detail: Time per month and replacement cost for an average American: food 50 hours, $800; cleaning 25 hours, $500; laundry 16 hours, $200 (these add to 91 hours, he states 96). claim: His split with his wife and partner: he does four maker days and one manager day, and she does the reverse.

### YT-gD0X-PLax5I#11 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: unit-economics | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:30:12
claim: Once you get it right, the business constraint doesn't change that often; sub-constraints chunk down, but fundamentally they all chunk up to getting more customers or making them worth more, and those oscillate.
detail: Once you know you need more leads you keep doing that, sometimes for a very long time; a second channel is a narrower version of the same constraint. He sees many owners obsessing over lowering lead cost, and finds it easier to fix the sales process and add a zero to the price, after which lead problems go away.
