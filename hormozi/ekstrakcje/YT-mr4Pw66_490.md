### YT-mr4Pw66_490#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Prep for Sale | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:45
claim: Two companies with the same $10M revenue and $2M profit differ greatly in owner wealth: one that needs the owner is worth far less than one that runs without them.
detail: The owner working 80 hours a week keeps about $1M after 50% tax and saves roughly $500K a year. The hands-off business sells at 6x profit, so it is worth about $12M. Adding $500K of profit gives the first owner $250K after tax but adds about $3M of value to the second. He calls the gap roughly 20x between a sellable company and one that is not.
numbers: 6x profit multiple; $12M value; +$500K profit = +$3M value vs +$250K after tax; owner-run version nets about $500K a year saved.

### YT-mr4Pw66_490#02 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:05
claim: Run a time study: set a timer, and every 15 minutes write one word on what you did, for a week.
detail: Do it in a plain spreadsheet. It gives the list of everything you do that must be handed off, and can also be run on a key person to reduce dependence on them. He finds it makes the week unusually productive.
numbers: one entry per 15 minutes, one week.

### YT-mr4Pw66_490#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:05:39
claim: List every task you do and assign each a project, a process or a person, then colour it green, yellow or red.
detail: Green: someone on the team can take it now once taught. Yellow: a one-time project or process you know how to install. Red: you do not know how, or you need a person you lack. Work from green to red. A project is one-off; a person does it on a continuing basis.

### YT-mr4Pw66_490#04 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: product-delivery | fw: Employee Empowerment Wheel | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:06
claim: Write decision trees ("if this, then that") for common scenarios, which makes roles easy to train.
detail: Example from the supplement company's support team: change address, card, flavour or billing cadence, cancel, refund. More people in a function means more standardised questions. Complex or one-off roles resist this. He admits snapping at the support manager that it could not be that hard and being rebuked for it.

### YT-mr4Pw66_490#05 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:43
claim: Ask each employee "how do you and your role make the company money?"; many will not know.
detail: For non-revenue roles, reverse the costs of doing it badly into metrics. For a camera operator: wasted team time, weaker media, lost footage, ill-fitted equipment become the percentage of shoots with files saved correctly, named, on the right drive, timestamped, lit and started on time. He thinks that knowing the link gives people more meaning and impact.
### YT-mr4Pw66_490#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: Document Demonstrate Duplicate | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:26
claim: The graduation test for a handoff is whether someone at about 80% of your skill delivers 100% of the result.
detail: Initially he accepts 80%, sometimes 60%, if there is a clear path to improving. Two belief changes: if you can do it right with part of your time, someone else can do it better with all of theirs; and you can outwork anyone in the business but not everyone.
numbers: 80% skill target, sometimes 60%.

### YT-mr4Pw66_490#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:20
claim: Trading 200 hours a month of doing for 20 hours of managing is a 10x gain in leverage, and repeating it at the next layer gives another jump.

### YT-mr4Pw66_490#08 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:09
claim: Test whether the business works without you: it survives a month away, ideally grows, and at the extreme grows over a three-month absence.
detail: Surviving is the baseline; improving while away is what buyers want. For brick-and-mortar owners wanting a second or third location, he calls three months off with growth the key test, called the phone test (not explained here). If flat, it may decline when your attention moves, leaving double the liability, debt and overhead with half the talent. Overexpansion is a leading cause of failure.
numbers: leave one month; stress test three months.

### YT-mr4Pw66_490#12 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: Cost to Acquire Talent | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:46
claim: Spot strong people by how precisely they describe their role in metrics, such as cost per hire, time to fill and two-way fit.
detail: His first good HR hire asked about cost of acquiring talent, time to fill, and two-way fit (the share of hires rated 10 out of 10 by both employee and manager at 90 days). He had tracked none; he compares "no lawsuits lately" to judging a product by low refunds.

### YT-mr4Pw66_490#13 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: sales-team | fw: Interview Scorecard | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:34
claim: Interview more people than seems sensible, use group interviews for frontline to manager roles, and the volume tunes your pattern recognition.
detail: A co-founder interviewed 600 developers over about a year to find the CTO. After ten conversations you can judge a role far better than after one; by the fiftieth you spot who has game. He had to hire five sales managers over six months to a year each before finding one who fit.
numbers: 600 developer interviews of 30-60 minutes over about a year.
