### YT-n6SHLmKcY0E#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Payback Period | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:32:31
claim: Use a cheaper front-end offer before an expensive one when buyers need much information to decide and when you must pull cash forward; with inbound, brand-fed traffic he says a business could probably get away with going straight to the expensive offer.
detail: Whether a business needs front and back end "depends" on how much information the buyer needs and on cash flow: a $100,000 decision takes time while ad and labour costs run with no return. A front end mainly pulls cash forward (shorter payback), and everything after that is gravy. For this owner, whose traffic is almost entirely cold ads, he says the bar must be lowered so he can prove himself before the big step.

### YT-n6SHLmKcY0E#05 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Rollover Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:34:25
claim: When crediting a front-end purchase toward a recurring retainer, spread the credit across the year rather than taking it all off the first payment.
detail: Two options offered: subtract the credit from the first quarter, or raise the list price slightly and knock a fixed amount off every quarter if they commit now. Hormozi prefers spreading because a big first-payment cut invites an easy yes followed by cancellation when the price doubles; the credit must be used within the 2-4 week window after purchase.

### YT-n6SHLmKcY0E#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:14
claim: Aim for the sweet spot of friction: remove bad friction that stops everyone, add good friction that qualifies; the pricier the product, the more friction you will generally need.
detail: Bad friction: entering the same details twice, slow pages, video hosted where it suggests other content. Good friction: qualifying questions that keep the wrong people out. His phrase is that a bigger plane needs a longer runway. He found the duplicate form by filling in the owner's funnel himself and advises walking through your own funnel on a regular cadence, since, he says, a lot of owners set up their funnel years ago and never go through it again.

### YT-n6SHLmKcY0E#08 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: paid-ads | fw: What-Who-When | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:41:51
claim: Write benefits as the moments the buyer will experience, with the benefit as the main line and the metric as the subtext.
detail: Feature lists ("data-driven menus", "functional processes") make the reader do translation work, and each bit of effort loses some readers. Ask when the owner would feel the change (no more messy back bar, no thrown-out produce, no queue) and write that; put "+50% speed of service" underneath. Describe pain more precisely than the prospect can, since that builds belief better than promises. Add result disclosures.

### YT-n6SHLmKcY0E#10 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:38:20
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: because his retainer clients churn after about two quarters, fix retention first, since improving the front end would only pull people into a leaky bucket. General statement: in consulting or education, once the thing is implemented there is little reason to keep paying, so make the back end stickier or accept it is one-time work and charge accordingly.
detail: He says consulting and education tend to graduate clients. For a bar owner he suggests something that keeps changing and requires the consultant (new staff are always arriving and need training; a monthly meeting showing the client their own numbers pulled from their system, perhaps two hours per account, which he thinks is worth it). Otherwise accept one-time revenue, charge for a good margin, and know there is less enterprise value than in businesses where clients never want to leave.

### YT-n6SHLmKcY0E#11 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Five Upsell Moments | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:46:24
claim: Sell a quick-ROI front end, aim for at least half to upgrade, and ask at the first milestone, again a week later, and a last chance before day 30.
detail: Hormozi's stance is never to ask for money he has not already made the client; the next offer should cost a fraction of the gain already delivered. Customers are far likelier to buy again than prospects (he says about 10x). The founder should deliver the front end at first to learn it.

### YT-n6SHLmKcY0E#12 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-process | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:50:38
claim: Hormozi is a big believer in founders selling the product in the beginning; for this owner he said to keep taking the calls basically until he cannot anymore.
detail: Reasons given: closeness to customers and fast feedback on marketing, avatar, offer and price; removes the doubt whether a salesperson or the leads are the problem; founders, being the most convinced, typically outsell any salesperson; no commission frees cash.
