### POD-0015#01 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Ask what service you would have to deliver to justify 4x your price, then implement the cheap items from that list.
detail: Taken from a book on 10x thinking; he suggests 4x is more realistic. Example items: personalised grocery lists, weekly check-ins, a monthly grocery-store trip with the client, someone answering questions online at any hour, and a 15-minute weekly in-person goal talk. Many are inexpensive and simply never done.
numbers: Price multiple used for the exercise: 4x (10x is too much).

### POD-0015#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:17
claim: Saying "we are a family" does not differentiate a gym, since every gym says it; consistently excellent service does.
detail: Holes in the bucket come from service quality, not from being bad people. Communicate the standard to the team and customers, who then police the team. That culture supports higher prices, retention and referrals.

### POD-0015#03 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:05
claim: Send handwritten cards to members; 200 a month takes about 20 minutes each morning.
detail: A monthly personal note, for example about a good leg workout, is a service people tell friends about.
numbers: 200 cards a month = 50 a week = about 10 a day = about 20 minutes a day.

### POD-0015#04 | SCRIPT | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: GROWTH Onboarding | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:01
claim: Early onboarding sequence: swag bag, handwritten card, email and voicemail from the owner within four days, then a second card.
detail: The second card says the owner wants exceptional service and asks to be told if it is anything less.
numbers: Contacts within first 4 days: card, email, voicemail.

### POD-0015#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: retention-ltv | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:47
claim: Call members who have been absent for a week, because clear behaviour patterns precede cancellation, and ask cancellers for honest reasons.
detail: Track those signals. Ask those who leave to be frank about what could have been better; people who say they "got busy" are rarely too busy for an experience they enjoy.
numbers: Trigger: a week without attending.
