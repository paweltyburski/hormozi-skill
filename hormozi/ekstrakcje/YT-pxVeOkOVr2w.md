### YT-pxVeOkOVr2w#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:50
claim: Domino's "30 minutes or free" showed an offer can carry a mediocre product.
detail: The delivery promise, with a free-pizza guarantee, needed no sales pitch and made pizza its own listing category in the phone directory when other restaurants shared one. The offer drove growth more than the food did.

### YT-pxVeOkOVr2w#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:38
claim: "10 CDs for a penny" was an early free-trial style front end backed by continuity, and FedEx's next-morning promise won on value with no price element.
detail: The CD club offer pulled people in at nearly free and recovered money through continuity. FedEx's "there by tomorrow morning" shows an offer can be all value, and short enough to say in a few words.

### YT-pxVeOkOVr2w#03 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: promotions-launches | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:38
claim: Make an aggressive offer believable by giving a credible reason why you are doing it.
detail: A marketer who promised $1,000 back for every $1 got no replies because the deal was too good to believe. Reasons like overstock or inventory about to spoil make a big discount plausible.
numbers: The $1-for-$1,000 ad drew zero responses.

### YT-pxVeOkOVr2w#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: offers | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:57
claim: An offer is clear enough only if you can explain it in one or two sentences.
detail: If you cannot explain the offer to someone else that briefly, he says it is not clear enough to a prospect.
### YT-pxVeOkOVr2w#05 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: promotions-launches | fw: Free Giveaways | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:39
claim: A friend ran a $25,000 transformation challenge and sold sign-ups on the page without phone calls.
detail: Participants paid about $300 online to enter a contest for the large prize, while the giveaway stayed related to the core offer. Entrants raise their hand as needing the service, so leads arrive cheap and qualified. Winners are chosen once; the others are offered the programme at a discount. He notes the need to avoid anything that counts as gambling.
numbers: $25,000 prize; roughly $300 to enter.

### YT-pxVeOkOVr2w#06 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Continuity Discount Offer | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:25
claim: Offering the first month free in return for a long contract commitment (not a contingency) lifts conversion and contract value.
detail: Example: first 28 days free if they commit to a nine-month contract. Because the price is not faced at the front, a high share convert and contract values tend to be higher, but the load shifts onto fulfilment and compliant advertising.

### YT-pxVeOkOVr2w#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:10
claim: About 30% of trial takers call right away to say they did not mean to be billed, so sell the continuity, not the trial.
detail: Selling a trial only sells trying it. Make sure people know they will be billed and, he suggests, bill only those who at least verbally agreed to continue, to avoid chargebacks.
numbers: ~30% of people who go through a trial or free front end call to dispute billing (his estimate).
