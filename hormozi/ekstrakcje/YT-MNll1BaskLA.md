### YT-MNll1BaskLA#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:37
claim: Inversion: list the fewest things that would guarantee you lose, flip each into a positive action, and let the shortest list guide you.
detail: Brains find problems faster than solutions, so use that. Imagine the least successful version of you and write what they do or skip: late, unprepared, no follow-up, no advertising, no referral asks. Smaller lists are more potent, and a single item that guarantees failure often becomes the North Star. Then invert each into its opposite (always follow up, advertise constantly, ask for referrals). He attributes the idea to Einstein and Munger.

### YT-MNll1BaskLA#02 | CASE | topic: lead-getters | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:01
claim: A salesperson at a company he works with outsold the old top rep 2x just by asking every buyer to bring someone else.
detail: The referral ask was already in the script but not enforced. When he asked the sales director why, the answer was that it was now being enforced. Lesson: the winning behaviour is often simple and already known.
numbers: Rep outperformed previous top salesperson by 2x.

### YT-MNll1BaskLA#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:38
claim: The less skilled someone is, the more finely instructions must be broken down; one unclear step in the chain stops the task.
detail: A skilled person acts on "go advertise"; an unskilled one asks "but how?". Two people attending the same training get different results because of the skills each brought and the skills the task requires. Breaking tasks to a level nobody can fail makes it easier for the skilled and possible for the unskilled; if someone stalls, suspect a single step they did not understand.

### YT-MNll1BaskLA#04 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:25
claim: Operationalize every trait into behaviours you can see: no feelings, thoughts or intentions in instructions.
detail: "Be charismatic" becomes a list: smile when people enter, vary tone, remember names, ask about them, nod and respond, keep eye contact, greet everyone entering and leaving. Only observable actions go into instructions, because you cannot know what others think; you only need to know that following the process produces more buyers. He argues traits are bundles of smaller skills, so anything you can learn is teachable, and rejects the idea of being born a certain way.
numbers: Example charisma list of 6-7 actions.

### YT-MNll1BaskLA#05 | DEFINITION | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:46
claim: Patience means deciding what to do in the meantime, and traits are continuums, not on/off.
detail: Being patient is doing anything except the thing you are avoiding, including doing nothing; people holding investments are already patient. Ask "how patient am I?" not "am I patient?", and move along the scale by action until you describe yourself that way.

### YT-MNll1BaskLA#06 | DEFINITION | topic: operator-mindset | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:02
claim: Learning is a new behaviour under the same condition; intelligence is how fast you change it; confidence comes from proof gathered in the past.
detail: Example: a phone rings and with no script you improvise; if the next time you read the script, you learned. Someone who never changes is slow regardless of IQ, and you can beat the "smartest" by changing faster. Confidence is domain-specific (a confident realtor, a nervous parent): practise in the exact situation until it bores you, as a speech rehearsed in a mirror can fail on stage. A plan that needs luck is a bad plan; dig the well before you are thirsty.

### YT-MNll1BaskLA#07 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:21:19
claim: Five stages of opportunity hopping: uninformed optimism, informed pessimism, valley of despair, informed optimism, then the win.
detail: Most people quit at the valley and restart with the next idea, repeating the cycle (some ten times). Stage four is when you understand the process, have broken it into actions you can perform regularly, have a feedback cycle, and measure yourself on completing committed actions. Staying longer gives compounding returns.

### YT-MNll1BaskLA#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:23:51
claim: Hard markets are when you build proof: wealth is made in downturns because the market share shifts to those still standing.
detail: Prospects not buying now are deciding who they will pick later, and they favour the person who survived several downturns. His line is that you do about three times the work for the same result in a down market with roughly ten times the eyes on you, so winning then shows you can win anytime. A smooth sea never made a skilled sailor.
numbers: Rhetorical: 3x work for same outcome, 10x attention in a downturn.

### YT-MNll1BaskLA#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:28:36
claim: Motivation scales with the perceived gap between where you are and where you want to be, so it depends on who you compare yourself with.
detail: Hunger works the same way. For money the deprivation is psychological: someone at $5,000 a month aiming for $10,000 is half way; someone at $1M a month aiming for $10M is a tenth of the way, so the second is the more deprived. Mixing with people with ten times your net worth makes you feel poorer than when you were poor. Environment sets standards, and changing it changes behaviour (his room-temperature illustration).
numbers: $5K to $10K per month (50% of goal) vs $1M to $10M per month (10% of goal).

### YT-MNll1BaskLA#11 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:31:36

detail: Surrounding yourself with higher-level peers widens the gap and raises motivation, but a cleaner method is comparing to your future self instead of other people. He credits networks such as the room he was speaking in as valuable because they reset the standard.

### YT-MNll1BaskLA#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:32:02
claim: Replace "be, do, have" with just "do": identity is a description of what you repeatedly do, so act like your target self.
detail: Honest means states facts; hardworking means finishes tasks as fast or faster than agreed; a realtor represents buyers and sellers. Doing the things that person does gives the results and, in time, the label. New identity also brings new spending priorities, as shown in a cosmetics store where girls were told they must now budget for makeup.

### YT-MNll1BaskLA#13 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:36:13
claim: Start a new habit when you are busiest, because a change that waits for free time disappears when you are busy again.
detail: Learn to make it work in worst conditions and it will hold in the best. If you are busy but not progressing, you are doing the wrong things, so subtract before adding. His wife found four hours a day of non-work phone use on his screen-time report.
numbers: 4 hours per day of phone use that was not work (his own case).

### YT-MNll1BaskLA#14 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:39:24
claim: The "when-then" fallacy is reversing the order: you act now to get the result later, not wait for the result before acting.
detail: Examples: saving when you have more money, getting healthy before the hospital, going to the gym once you have a six-pack. Also: people who move faster do not move faster, they get more from each step (leverage).

### YT-MNll1BaskLA#15 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:42:39
claim: Reset expectations at the predictable quit point: say "this is what hard feels like", because people expected hard and are now in it.
detail: Every year the fraternity pledge class revolted between day 10 and day 16. As president he asked 20 freshmen who thought it would be easy (none) and who thought hard (all), told them this was hard, and they returned. The same applies to income plateaus: uncertainty about results is the hard part, and you can feel terrible and still win.
numbers: Revolt window: day 10 to day 16; 20 pledges.

### YT-MNll1BaskLA#16 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: Many-Sided Die | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:40:33
claim: Whenever stuck, break the vague term into smaller chunks, then measure yourself yes or no on whether you did the actions.
detail: "Advertise more" becomes "do 100 DMs"; once the skills exist, chunk back up into the general command. You succeed by default if you do actions that make failure unreasonable. Marriage, health and business cannot be won, only outlasted, which makes the result a decision you fully control.
numbers: Example action: 100 DMs.
