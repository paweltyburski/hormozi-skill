### MZ-lYe2eEK76Go#01 | CASE | topic: content-brand | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:37
claim: A life-coach certification seller with 250K Instagram followers got relationship content comments but no buyers.
detail: Business sells certification to high earners wanting meaning; revenue $3M, goal $10M. Relationship red-flag content drew men and women arguing; trauma content drew victims. Hormozi: wrong bait, easy to fix because feeds are interest-based.
numbers: Revenue $3M; 250K followers; goals $10M, $50M.

### MZ-lYe2eEK76Go#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:02
claim: Change what you say and you change who you attract, since audiences follow the narrative.
detail: A pro-victim narrative attracts victims, while a business topic reaches business-minded viewers.

### MZ-lYe2eEK76Go#03 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:17
claim: Make the frameworks you teach inside the program your content; fear of giving away secrets is misplaced.
detail: Hardly anyone acts on free content, and those who do tend to want more.

### MZ-lYe2eEK76Go#04 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:24
claim: Keep the client stories but reframe them from the viewpoint of the coach who would buy your training.
detail: Open with a hook story (a client who slapped her husband) and show how a coach should handle such a client. Existing content marketed to end customers rather than to business owners.
