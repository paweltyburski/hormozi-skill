### POD-0161#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:18
claim: Marketing the hybrid offer externally for about 90 days (June to September 2019) produced record inbound sales but poor-quality prospects, because the online element attracted side-hustle seekers.
detail: Hybrid meant one-on-one nutrition and accountability added to in-gym training, as weight-loss centres have long done. Mentioning online brought entrepreneurs wanting a laptop lifestyle, non-starters and no-shows, which hurt team morale. Internally some gym owners wrongly concluded he no longer wanted to serve gyms. After 90 days, those still around and successful were almost all gym owners.
numbers: 90-day test, June to end of September (2019).

### POD-0161#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:04
claim: In his 90-day hybrid run, the clients who stayed and succeeded were mostly full-time gym owners with over 25 clients, real leases and employees, meaning real business owners.
detail: Those clients succeeded, whereas people who "could have a gym" mostly did not. After seeing this, he changed the external messaging, dropping the word online and calling it one-on-one nutrition and accountability. Real owners also hold realistic expectations about the work time involved.
numbers: Success concentrated among gym owners with 25+ clients.
### POD-0161#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:21
claim: People read one hook, not the whole story, and no one likes change, so frame a new idea as a small add-on to what the customer already does.
detail: He cites David Ogilvy: no ad is consumed in series, so each must carry the full concept. If he did it again he would not say hybrid or online, but "take your large group, add this on top, make more money, don't sell workouts". The internal and external name became one-on-one nutrition and accountability.

### POD-0161#04 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:18
claim: Bolt one-on-one nutrition and accountability onto an existing group membership base for added recurring revenue at nearly full margin.
detail: Customers value daily follow-up on food more than workouts, which chains now sell as unlimited group classes at low prices. Gyms with a lease and enough members need not change the group offer. Sold with a diagnostic sale to the person's goal, framed as solving the problem in a set period so they eventually no longer need the coach.
numbers: $10,000-$15,000 a month recurring added to an existing base; group front-end packages of $3,000-$5,000; large group at $400+ a month with the add-on; chains sell group classes at $59 a month unlimited.

### POD-0161#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:22
claim: At about $425 a month per client, only 180 members are needed for a $1M run rate, reached in about 12 months from zero with 8% attrition and 100 leads a month.
detail: With lower attrition (5%) he thinks it takes around seven months (not sure of the math).
numbers: 180 members x ~$425/month; 8% attrition; 100 leads a month; about 12 months from zero (about 7 months at 5%).
