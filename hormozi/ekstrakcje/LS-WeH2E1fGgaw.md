### LS-WeH2E1fGgaw#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:22
claim: If a bigger, successful business of the same kind exists, push on, because you lack skill rather than a viable model; pivot only when doing something never done before, where demand itself is unproven.
detail: His first step is to find someone doing a version of the business well, see what they do differently and close the gap: replicate before you iterate. Examples: dry cleaning, lawn care, HVAC, garage doors, weight loss by any method. For about 99% of people the answer is to push, meaning a thousand improving iterations with feedback, not the same weak attempt a thousand times.

### LS-WeH2E1fGgaw#30 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 01:35:40
claim: It is rarely necessary to bet the whole company: keep the proven, lower-return line and fund the new one from its profits; if the new one needs all resources, judge it on a risk-adjusted return.
detail: Answer to an apparently joking question about pivoting an inherited arms maker against the board. If one line returns 4:1 and a new one 10:1, run both; an inferior line can still be productive. An unproven 10:1 must be discounted by its odds (20%? 50%?). Kodak and Blockbuster stories are rare; far more often owners kill good businesses from boredom with a shiny idea. Show the board evidence and make an appropriately sized bet.
