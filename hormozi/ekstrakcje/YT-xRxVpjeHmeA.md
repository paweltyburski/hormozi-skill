### YT-xRxVpjeHmeA#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: finance-exit | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:45
claim: An indoor family adventure park (Texas) faced bankruptcy within weeks: demand constrained and cash constrained at once, with heavy bank debt.
detail: Total build cost $22M, of which $8M was raised and the rest bank debt. Unprofitable. Peak days run at about half of capacity, so capacity is not the issue; admissions, marketing and conversion are. Owner tracked outputs rather than the input numbers that create them, and put in his 401k to survive. Plan: one big bet to generate cash fast, then check back after 90 days.
numbers: Revenue about 4.8 (likely $M a year, unclear in audio). General admission $32.46 per person plus ~$11 spent in the park; this stream ~70% of revenue. Goal $10M revenue.
applies_to: Large fixed-cost leisure or location business.

### YT-xRxVpjeHmeA#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: promotions-launches, money-models | fw: Money Model | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:13
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: with cash short, Hormozi suggested selling the strongest possible season-pass offer to the list, and the owner proposed summer camps because moms book summers right now.
detail: Hormozi first said to sell a season pass to the list. The owner suggested summer camps, since parents book summers in the coming months before the slow season. numbers: Existing summer packages: $300 for 3 days a week, $500 for 5 days a week, $2,000 for any day all summer. Average ticket guessed at $1,200-$1,300; at 15 closes a day (about $16,000 a day) that adds roughly $450,000, which Hormozi said is not enough. Owner's hope was $200,000.
### YT-xRxVpjeHmeA#04 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: sales-process | fw: Sales Aids | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:50
claim: Fix a campaign landing page by cutting repeated banners, putting trust beside the button, adding a safety stat and guarantee, and leading with a video.
detail: Drop the header, which is prime space with no value, and reduce four banners to one carrying the times. Keep one set of reviews relevant to the offer, put the parent rating next to the button, show an injury stat for a parent's main fear (zero hospital visits last year), add a day-one money-back guarantee, shorten the text and place a video letter at the top. Later report: the video was the best element and as an ad was the best converter.

### YT-xRxVpjeHmeA#05 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: promotions-launches | fw: Buy X Get Y Free | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:36
claim: Run one offer through four sources (email list, general admission attach, paid ads, phone follow-up) and test several phrasings of the same deal.
detail: Ad creative: video clips as primary creative (the owner had a creator's permission to use his videos). Offer wordings to test: buy four weeks, get six free; buy one month, get the whole summer; buy one month, get two free; as little as $200 a week; early bird. Always state the reason for the offer (early bird). The wordings mean the same, only which one clicks differs.

### YT-xRxVpjeHmeA#06 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Free Giveaways | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:11
claim: Camp giveaway structure: first prize is the full-summer pass, second prize a large discount that equals the real offer, many second-place winners, then a claim deadline.
detail: First prize valued at $5,000 (ten weeks at $500). Second prize $3,000 off, leaving the $2,000 ticket; more than one person can win second. Campaign runs 7-10 days with a countdown on the landing page and daily emails and texts counting down. After the draw, second-place winners get 72 hours to claim, with another reason to hurry because camp fills. Then fire the buy-four-get-six variants and follow up by phone. Check local lottery and giveaway law first.
numbers: 7-10 day giveaway; 72-hour claim window; prize $5,000 value, second prize $3,000 off.

### YT-xRxVpjeHmeA#08 | EXAMPLE | topic: promotions-launches | also: offers | fw: Free Giveaways | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:57
claim: Critique of the park's giveaway ad: say "free", state first and second prizes plainly, and do not tell everyone they win a scholarship.
detail: Good: strong visuals and energy, and it opens with the expensive thing, which anchors price. Mistake: promising every entrant a prize drains the excitement of winning. Say instead that more than one person can win, announce prizes by place, and draw at the end of the seven-day countdown. Small wording change, big conversion effect.

### YT-xRxVpjeHmeA#10 | BENCHMARK | topic: promotions-launches | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Urgency | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:03
claim: Usually, roughly a third to half of a campaign's leads arrive in its last 48 hours, even for a six-week campaign, so model for it and keep pushing through the dead middle.
detail: In the middle, lead costs rise and it feels like nothing works. Do not stop. Here the cost per lead was the lowest the company ever had.
numbers: Usually a third to half of leads in final 48 hours. Giveaway produced 3,700 leads (~120 a day in a local market). Summer camp sales rose 325% against prior year. Park admissions doubled over 4-6 weeks after ads using the creator videos began.
