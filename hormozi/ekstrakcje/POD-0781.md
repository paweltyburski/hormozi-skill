### POD-0781#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: sales-process | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Hormozi's levels of selling: 101 is the one-on-one flow (clarify, label the problem, sell the vacation, three-pillar pitch, metaphors, explain away concerns, reinforce the decision), 201 is the AAA framework used between prospect statements, 301 is a more advanced approach built around a frame.
detail: The 301 idea: pick a frame, ask what else would have to be true for it to hold, and let that frame carry through the conversation. He explained it to his sales director, and the director's team then had a meaningful increase.
### POD-0781#02 | CASE | topic: sales-process | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:05
claim: In the first Gym Launch ad he had to sell memberships in two bare apartments above a convenience store, so he chose a frame that made the place a feature.
detail: Frame: a private, discreet gym for people who do not want to be watched. Individual rooms were privacy by design; a famous client might appear, so do not approach them. He told it to women losing weight, who related to not wanting stares. Everything said was true; imagination filled the rest.
applies_to: Local gym with no equipment

### POD-0781#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:05
claim: Every detail must be congruent with the frame; one false edge pops it.
detail: A premium frame fails if other details are cheap. Example from a book on a high-end establishment: a British voice on the phone and gold-and-black envelopes kept a premium feel. Check each element against the frame and cut what does not match.

### POD-0781#04 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:55
claim: Mentally cut your price by 80% (or to a tenth), pitch with the tone, body language and scarcity that fits, then state the real price.
detail: For a B2B service with market value near $50,000, he asked the director how he would sound at $10,000 if sure to sell out; the answer was very different. The price only exists in the seller's head, so adopt the state of a deal that sells itself ("we will hit capacity, move fast").
numbers: Example market value $50,000 vs imagined $10,000; cut price by 80%.

### POD-0781#05 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: sales-team | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:54
claim: Ask a rep: if every other prospect today had bought immediately, how differently would you speak?
detail: Then talk that way. All of that is you, not them, and they will take your lead and you set the frame.
### POD-0781#06 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:14
claim: Engineer overheard persuasion: let prospects hear what seems like private praise.
detail: Greeting a customer who did not buy as if they bought, when the next prospect is walking in. Asking a passing member "should she do this?" so they say "do it now, it changes your life", and the prospect pays. Overheard remarks feel more credible than addressed ones.

### POD-0781#07 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: product-delivery | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:57
claim: Shape the physical room to support the frame, for example floor-to-ceiling testimonials.
detail: A room covered in results makes doubting efficacy unreasonable. He also sold in empty rented shells with a card table and two chairs, and took about $100,000 for a free weight-loss challenge.
numbers: ~$100,000 sold from an empty space (free challenge with no gym).

### POD-0781#08 | BENCHMARK | topic: sales-process | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:08
claim: Typical close rates for a fairly priced phone sale of a B2B service are about 30-35%; Hormozi tries to get above 85%, which he says is possible only when the buyer doesn't feel sold.
detail: He had an 83% close rate on people walking in the door and over 90% on supplements, because it didn't feel like a sale. He suggests adopting the frame that obviously they will sign up.
numbers: Typical 30-35%; walk-in close 83%; supplements 90%+; target above 85%.
### POD-0781#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:41
claim: Making sales easier can beat getting better at sales.
detail: Hormozi spent his career on making the sale easy (price point, scarcity, frame), while his director focused on skill. Both change how likely people are to exchange money.
