### YT-4GQLJjH9-oA#01 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: sales-process | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A webinar-funnel portfolio company stuck at about $570K a month for six months was lifted to a $10M annual run rate in four months by fixing show rate, webinar bookings and close rate.
detail: Ads send people to a video where the founder pitches a call. Starting point: show rate 18%, 58 calls booked per webinar, close rate 14%. Results: show rate 24%, 102 booked calls per webinar (+77%, same ad spend), close rate 35%, CAC from $5,763 to $2,868, ROAS from 1.62 to 3.96, revenue from $569K to $860K a month, 30.7% profit margin or roughly $250K to $300K profit a month. The constraint was acquisition, broken into funnel steps (click, opt-in, schedule, show, close) and each inspected.
numbers: Revenue $569K to $860K/month; CAC $5,763 to $2,868; ROAS 1.62 to 3.96; close 14% to 35% (about 2.4x); margin 30.7%.

### YT-4GQLJjH9-oA#02 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:06
claim: To lift webinar show rate, add a multi-channel reminder sequence: a nine-email series, SMS, behaviour-triggered extra content and voicemail drops.
detail: The root problem was a weekly live pitch, so people who booked early had days to forget. Emails were simplified and benefit-led, hitting different angles (pain, speed, ease, being stuck), and alternating proof with pain; they included calls to action so opens and clicks protected deliverability. Anyone who clicked or opened got hidden behind-the-scenes content, which also showed sophistication. Voicemail and video covered other media preferences. Hormozi thinks SMS was the biggest single lever.
numbers: Nine emails; show rate 18% to 24% (a 33% lift); SMS open rate 95-99% (his figure).
applies_to: Webinar or event-based funnels.

### YT-4GQLJjH9-oA#05 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: sales-process | fw: Hook-Retain-Reward | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:16
claim: Study your webinar or sales video for drop-off points and spikes in engagement, then spend the effort on the first five minutes and first 60 seconds.
detail: Drop-offs show what to fix; spikes show what to expand. Here many left at the start. Beginner presentations meander for one or two minutes and lose around 40% of the audience before the point is reached. In another portfolio company, re-scripting only the first five minutes of a three-hour presentation lifted close rate 20%.
numbers: About 40% lost in the first two minutes of weak intros; +20% close rate from a five-minute rewrite of a three-hour presentation.

### YT-4GQLJjH9-oA#08 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: promotions-launches | fw: Scarcity | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:30
claim: Create a takeaway by stating who the offer is for and who it is not, then state the real capacity limit, with first-come-first-served as the urgency.
detail: Name the qualifying criteria (above, below or adjacent is not a fit) to repel the wrong people and attract the right ones. Scarcity comes from actual constraints such as only two or four closers, an agency that can take five clients, or 20 calls. Use "typically" for slots that usually fill. He calls this among the most underused ethical scarcity methods.

### YT-4GQLJjH9-oA#09 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: objections-closing | fw: Scarcity | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:46
claim: During the webinar, make bookings visible: ask people to say in chat when they book, call out names as they book, and announce slots as sold out.
detail: The chat creates social proof and an open loop; reading out "Rosie booked" builds a rush. Calling "Wednesday is sold out" pre-frames the sale, and chunking down (Wednesday morning sold out, then afternoon) gives mini urgency even with few slots. It is compliant because it only states facts.

### YT-4GQLJjH9-oA#10 | SCRIPT | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:50
claim: Replace rapport chat on a sales call with a short credential intro, ask what resonated on the webinar, then say the call is to check whether they qualify for the guarantee or bonus.
detail: Old opening: small talk, apologising for limited time, asking for pen and paper. New sequence: name, relevant background, link to the person they saw on the webinar, ask what resonated (returning them to the state in which they took action), then frame the call as qualification (budget, authority, need, timing, or size, industry and owner status). The takeaway puts the closer in the driver's seat. Say strict criteria, like admissions, and what you will offer if they do not qualify.

### YT-4GQLJjH9-oA#12 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: sales-team | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:50

### YT-4GQLJjH9-oA#15 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-team | also: lead-getters | fw: Lead Scoring | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:52
claim: Send the best leads to the best closers, and for closers below KPI halve their calendar, then give a week or two to improve or exit.
detail: Not everyone is worth training to sell. If best closers get best leads you sell more, and if the worst get the worst you lose least. Treat it as an optimisation for any sales process.
numbers: Calendar cut by half when below KPI; 1-2 weeks to prove improvement.

### YT-4GQLJjH9-oA#16 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:23:30
claim: Pay closers on cash collected rather than on closed deals, so payment plans pay them less than paid-in-full.
detail: In this company closers were paid the same whether the customer paid or not. Example: a $10,000 product sold as five payments of $2,000 earns a 10% commission on only the first $2,000, i.e. $200, against $1,000 for paid in full, five times more. This pushes real closes and sold customers who stay longer.
numbers: 10% commission on cash collected; $200 on a $2,000 first payment vs $1,000 on a $10,000 full payment.
