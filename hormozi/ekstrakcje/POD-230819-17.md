### POD-230819-17#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: lead-getters | also: pricing | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:36
claim: Charge for affiliate onboarding at 10-20% of what the average affiliate earns in their first 12 months.
detail: It covers the cost of advertising and lets him afford proper onboarding and training of every affiliate. Too low leaves them uninvested, too high deters sign-ups. Raise the minimum every five signups until you find the point; too few starters means lower the commitment, too few following through means raise it.
numbers: 10-20% of average year-one earnings; example: affiliate earning $40,000 a year pays $4,000-$8,000.

### POD-230819-17#14 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: lead-getters | fw: Whisper Tease Shout | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:25

### POD-230819-17#15 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: content-brand | fw: Whisper Tease Shout | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:33
claim: Tease reveals the product, the launch date and the value elements; shout adds bonuses, scarcity, urgency and a specific action, with a rising cadence.
detail: Use the what-who-when angles during tease. Shout names exact calls to action and exclusive launch bonuses. Movie trailers are the model: short teasers a year out, longer ones nearer release.

### POD-230819-17#17 | FRAMEWORK | topic: lead-getters | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:15
claim: Three integration levels, easiest to hardest: affiliates give your lead magnet away, sell it separately, or sell your core offer.
detail: Level one makes their offer more valuable so they can charge more (trial/sample, problem reveal, or first step of a multi-step plan). Level two lets them keep the cash from selling your lead magnet while you profit on the upsell. Level three splits the money, ideally paying forever. They run level one twice a year as an event and level three continually; other portfolio firms use level two.
