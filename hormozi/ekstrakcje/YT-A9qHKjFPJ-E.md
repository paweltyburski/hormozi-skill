### YT-A9qHKjFPJ-E#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Business risk splits into idea risk (does the idea work at all) and execution risk (can I carry it out), and he deliberately takes only execution risk.
detail: He chooses ideas where demand is virtually certain, leaving himself as the only variable. Regulatory or environmental risk is a possible third kind. A new entrepreneur who picks a fundamentally unproven idea cannot tell whether failure is the founder or the idea.
applies_to: Newer entrepreneurs choosing a first business.

### YT-A9qHKjFPJ-E#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:41
claim: In his opinion ("Alex's two cents"), most newer entrepreneurs would be better served going after boring businesses with established needs.
detail: Examples of constant needs: businesses wanting customers, people wanting more income, better-paid jobs, valuable skills, weight loss and entertainment. Since these needs won't change, it lets you hone your business skills, because if it doesn't work, it's only your fault.
### YT-A9qHKjFPJ-E#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Newer entrepreneurs overestimate the payoff and probability of risky bets and underestimate the cost, especially the time cost.
detail: Portfolio logic suits venture capital, which expects one in many to return the fund, but not a founder who puts net worth and years into one company. A 10% chance at a 100x payoff is a good bet only with an unlimited number of tries.
numbers: VC example: ~50 companies, one expected to return 100x; baseball caps runs at 4 per swing while business is long-tailed.

### YT-A9qHKjFPJ-E#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:20
claim: Counting years of life as the money to bet shows why a 1-in-10, 100x gamble is unaffordable for one person.
detail: Imagine 35 working years as 35 dollars and a minimum bet of 5 years (a realistic time to find out an idea fails; building something epic takes about 15). You get roughly three bets. Losing all three leaves you 15 years in with no money, damaged reputation and doubt about skill.
numbers: 35 years of career; bet size 5 years; success odds 1 in 10 at 100x.

### YT-A9qHKjFPJ-E#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:26
claim: Between two bets with similar risk-adjusted return (payoff times probability), he tends to take the one with the higher likelihood; this is his personal approach.
detail: His example: a 6x payoff with a 4-in-5 chance beats a 10x payoff with a 1-in-2 chance. He pays down execution risk by other means, strings together near-certain gains and lets compounding do the work. Wealthy people he knows control risk by reducing it, for example a celebrity with a huge audience writing a book that will almost surely sell.
numbers: 6x at 80% (EV ~4.8) versus 10x at 50% (EV 5), preferred the first.
### YT-A9qHKjFPJ-E#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:11:33
claim: Assume the good things you expect do not happen and check whether you still win; if so, in Hormozi's opinion it is an opportunity worth pursuing.
detail: List the five things you believe will go right, assume none do, and judge the deal on what remains. This leaves room to be pleasantly surprised. Trade up step by step: a high-paying job first, then, as he would do it, quit only after making sure the side hustle probably brings more income, which he believed reasonable to reach in a short time.
### YT-A9qHKjFPJ-E#07 | EXAMPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:22
claim: Repeated near-certain multiples beat one long shot: a 3x payoff at 95% certainty, compounded, reaches 100x quickly.
detail: Starting with 35 units, bet 5 and get 15, then bet 15, and so on; by compounding the portfolio passes 100x of the stake without relying on a 1-in-10 outcome. It is unglamorous but limits downside.
numbers: 3x per bet at ~95% likelihood (his arithmetic in the video is rough).
