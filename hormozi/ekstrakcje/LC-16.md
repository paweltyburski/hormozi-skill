### LC-16#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Seven Levels of Advertising | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Advertising grows through seven levels, each with one primary action.
detail: (1) Friends know: one offer, one avatar, one platform; warm outreach. (2) Everyone you know, consistently; you know the exact inputs per engaged lead; maximize warm outreach and post content (he ran paid ads with a case-study lead magnet but wishes he had started content). (3) Employees advertise: you have maxed your personal inputs, not the platform; hire people to advertise profitably (he hired a videographer and a media buyer). (4) Referrals: fix the product until referrals are consistent, then scale advertising with a bigger team. (5) More places, ways and people: advertise profitably with at least two methods on multiple platforms. (6) Hire killers: veteran executives build departments per method or platform without you. (7) Unknown — he will report back after crossing $1B.
numbers: Level 4 target: 25%+ of customers from referrals.

### LC-16#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery, scaling-constraints | fw: Seven Levels of Advertising | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:50
claim: Before scaling advertising further, go back and upgrade the product — most people skip this and never recover.
detail: Your early product was an MVP; your standards should now be higher. Run the referral process's continuous-improvement cycle so the product does not decay while more people hear about it; use referrals as proof it still works. Otherwise churn rises, you land on the "hamster wheel of death", and a damaged brand takes far longer to fix. A mentor (ex-COO of Broadcom) told him: go slow to go fast.

### LC-16#03 | TACTIC | topic: scaling-constraints | also: paid-ads, lead-getters | fw: Seven Levels of Advertising | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:17
claim: Expand advertising in a set order: new audiences on your best platform, then every placement and media type it supports, then a new platform, lead getter or Core Four activity.
detail: Add the next channel only after the team gets consistent results on the current one.

### LC-16#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: paid-ads | fw: Seven Levels of Advertising | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:17
claim: For acquisition leadership hire proven experience, not potential — and weigh years of hands-on experience over age.
detail: Look for people who have already won the gold several times; give them stronger, outcome-tied incentives. He says he would often prefer a 25-year-old who has advertised for 10 years over someone with 40 years who only reached a certain level (probably lacks the chops, less in touch). Executives from huge corporations who are now just people managers and no longer know what is new scare him and often do not work out. He wants the chops first and then teaches the leadership skills.

### LC-16#05 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: outreach | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:07
claim: Cold outreach failed twice for Hormozi before working on the third try; the difference was who ran it.
detail: Attempt 1: external consultant with experience — failed. Attempt 2: internal hire without experience — failed. Attempt 3: internal hire with experience — worked. Cold outreach is a people-heavy, operationally complex machine, so the manager matters most.

### LC-16#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:14
claim: Real growth is messy: you only learn which audiences, methods and platforms work by trying many for long enough.
detail: Nobody can know the best move in advance. What is certain is that the more you advertise, the more people learn about what you sell and the more of them buy.

### LC-16#08 | FRAMEWORK | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: lead-getters, scaling-constraints | fw: $100M Leads Machine | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:01
claim: The finished $100M Leads machine runs every Core Four method and lead getter through teams, with the owner out of the loop.
detail: A media team scales free content in all media types on many platforms; regular offers go to the warm audience for customers or affiliates; the audience is ravenous enough that any launch is immediately profitable; teams scale profitable paid ads across platforms; a cold outreach team brings more customers than you can handle; an affiliate manager launches and integrates new affiliates daily; recruiters and agencies bring in more lead getters; the product is good enough that a third of customers refer others; executives drive all of it without you.
numbers: ~1/3 of customers bring referrals.
