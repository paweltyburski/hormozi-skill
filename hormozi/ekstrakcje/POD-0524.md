### POD-0524#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:50
claim: Money is condensed time, and the main difference between wealth levels is the time horizon someone thinks in.
detail: Mastering time means mastering money. You can judge how wealthy someone is by the horizon they talk in: today's cash, this week, month, quarter, year or decade. Building because a problem deserves solving, not because rent is due this week, leads to a different business.

### POD-0524#06 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: sales-process | also: - | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:31:10
claim: Start every sale by asking what problem they are solving; "just finding out more" is not a real reason to be on the call.
detail: Sometimes you have to ask the question several ways until they name the problem. Assume the person wants what you have; once they admit a problem, you can proceed.
### POD-0524#07 | SCRIPT | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:32:03
claim: Label step: repeat the problem back and ask if it is right; then run a pain cycle through past attempts.
detail: Sequence: "you are here because X and struggling with Y, correct?" Then past attempts: what did you do, how did it work, what was good, what was bad, what else. After each answer, affirm and relate ("that must have been hard", a similar story of your own). Then ask permission to describe what you offer.

### POD-0524#09 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:34:21
claim: Brace the buyer by calling the price "extremely expensive" before stating it, rather than minimising it with "only".
detail: If the price truly is high for them, they were braced and are likelier to close; if it is lower than they feared, it lands as relief. Words like "only" raise expectations of a low price, which you then break. He had not tested it on webinars, but infomercials anchor high.

### POD-0524#11 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:37:36
claim: Use neutral or negative closing questions, such as "fair enough?" or "would it be totally crazy if we started Monday?".
detail: A weak agreement is easier to get than a strong one, so make the thing they agree to as small as possible. Negative "no-based" questions follow the negotiation approach of Chris Voss; "not crazy" counts as a yes.

### POD-0524#12 | DEFINITION | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:38:21
claim: Obstacles arise before the price (you disagree with the prospect); objections come after the price (they disagree with you).
detail: Selling is everything before price; closing is after. Resolve obstacles early, because they become bigger objections once the price is stated. Even "I just want more information" is an obstacle. He frames "no" as the job; a yes would not need a salesperson.

### POD-0524#13 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: Three Core Beliefs | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:39:59
claim: He derived his objection model from Albert Ellis's three ways people upset themselves, instead of picking one sales author's list.
detail: Other lists he compared: decision maker / stall / money; time, money, spouse, fear, shame; and three tens. Ellis boiled many reasons down to blaming circumstances, other people and self. The pre-condition is that the person already wants the result and your product would solve it.

### POD-0524#14 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: Three Core Beliefs | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:41:17
claim: Under "circumstances" there are three buckets, time, money and fit, each with its own three answers.
detail: Money: resources are not the point, resourcefulness is; ask how they would find the money if they had to. Fit means a clash with identity (for example "no cardio"): you cannot change the result without changing the method, and weak pain may mean the moment is not yet. Time uses macro, micro and when-then answers.

### POD-0524#15 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: - | fw: Three Core Beliefs | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:43:45
claim: Time answers: macro (everyone has 24 hours, it is priorities), when-then (busy now and later, so start now with support), micro (their current routine is not working).
detail: Macro: if you are busy now and will be later, begin now when you have the most support, and learn it in a busy period. Micro: if they work all day yet lack results, cut what is not working to free time for the programme.

### POD-0524#16 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: - | fw: Three Core Beliefs | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:44:31
claim: For "I need to ask my spouse", ask about awareness, approval, role reversal, then reframe to asking for support, not permission.
detail: Steps: does the spouse know of the struggle; do they approve of it; if roles were reversed, would you support them. Play out 12 months of staying the same and ask who they would blame. Hand them a short script to say to the spouse, and add a no-risk guarantee (for example three days) so the spouse's veto costs nothing.
numbers: Example guarantee: 3 days.

### POD-0524#17 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:43:45

detail: Getting a decision gives them power, removes ego about being right, and in his view produces more purchases. Salespeople should see themselves as coaches serving the customer.

### POD-0524#18 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: Three Core Beliefs | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:47:23
claim: "I need to think about it" is the best objection: you are speaking to the decision maker, so find the variables they will use.
detail: People need information, not time, and you are the source. Ask what their main concern is and what factors they weigh. Then use the "rocking chair" close: they will not sit and contemplate; days later some moment will force the decision, so decide now while informed.
numbers: Example: decision forced about 5 days later.

### POD-0524#19 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:48:31
claim: Use the "burn you twice" reframe when a past failure is the objection.
detail: Compare it to swearing off dating after one school-age breakup: a bad prior experience is allowed to hurt you once when it happens and again when it blocks the thing you need now. They have already acted by booking and showing up, so they are close to the decision.

### POD-0524#20 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:50:50

### POD-0524#23 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:54:00
claim: Hypothetical close: get a yes to the imagined situation, then work backwards.
detail: "If the programme were perfect, would you do it?" then "what is the difference between perfect and this?"; they usually cannot name much, which reveals the real fear. Or "if you had time, would you?" then "how much time do you think you need?" and show it is a fraction. The prior yes ties them to consistency.
