claim: Mastering three things beats most people: shame when rejected, the weight of repetition, and the sting of feedback.
detail: Your ceiling depends on the amount of uncertainty you can stand and the duration; larger ambitions demand far longer endurance than most people give.

### MZ-NnKFn4r3te0#02 | DEFINITION | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:15
claim: Courage is acting when the cost is large and immediate while the benefit is uncertain and delayed.
detail: If the payoff were guaranteed, little courage would be needed. Not playing is the signal of permanent loss; losing is the first signal on the path to winning.

### MZ-NnKFn4r3te0#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:39
claim: Treat feedback as fuel and feeling bad about a loss as a signal to change, not something to avoid.
detail: Without discomfort you repeat the strategy that lost. Reality comes due eventually, with interest; he wants his son to feel loss in order to learn.

### MZ-NnKFn4r3te0#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:02
claim: Pivot when a fundamental starting assumption is disproved; push when the assumption is unproven but execution is the problem.
detail: Example: asking 100 dog owners about a dog skateboard and none want it means pivot. Hearing "maybe, but what is it?" means improve execution and keep pushing. Judgment here is hard.
numbers: 100 interviews, zero interest, in his example.
