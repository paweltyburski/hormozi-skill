### POD-0270#01 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: Virtuous Cycle of Price | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:44
claim: Hormozi cut prices by 25% permanently and added services, which cost about $5M a year in profit and did not reduce churn.
detail: He believed the company had a churn problem, but churn was at or below market; the team's targets were tied to churn and they pushed him. Customers even complained about being charged the earlier price after the change.
numbers: 25% permanent price cut; about $5M a year profit lost; no churn improvement.

### POD-0270#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:44
claim: Check benchmarks in your own industry before treating a metric as a problem; he had extrapolated churn data from another industry.
detail: The mistake produced weeks of effort to fix a non-problem.

### POD-0270#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:44
claim: Churn and lifetime value are different measures; lifetime value is moved by price, churn is only the share who leave.
detail: Staff measured and bonused on churn pressed for a price cut, which would have hurt lifetime value as well.

### POD-0270#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: Virtuous Cycle of Price | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:43
claim: Lowering your price is pretty much never the solution to churn; understand benchmarks and expectations before making such a decision.
detail: Cutting 25% off the top line, which for most people is their profit, cost him $5 million a year of profit, and churn did not fall.
numbers: 25% of top line, which "for most people" equals their profit.
### POD-0270#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:33
claim: Classify decisions as reversible or irreversible and take more time only on irreversible ones; in Hormozi's own case, the price cut was irreversible.
detail: Rule credited to Jeff Bezos. Hormozi couldn't lower his price and then go back to a higher one, and some customers complained when charged the old price again.
### POD-0270#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:19
claim: People stay for fulfilment, growth, contribution, impact and colleagues, not price or pay.
detail: Pay rises give a short bump, then people return to baseline; he applies the same logic to customers and churn.

### POD-0270#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:59
claim: Mistakes break you more than wins make you, so reduce downside risk before maximising upside.
detail: Belief inherited from wealthier people; he says he has made more mistakes than correct choices.
