### POD-0058#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:58
claim: A gym should ideally grow by word of mouth, but if it stalls, learn to market and sell to create cash flow that pays for fixing delivery.
detail: Organic growth usually stalls somewhere around 50 to 80 members because the owner can no longer be there for everyone, so service falls to about what is charged or below and people are not good enough to refer. Marketing then provides payroll, hires and money to learn and scale delivery. Chipotle is his example of a company good enough not to advertise much.
numbers: Organic growth usually stalls at about 50-80 members.
applies_to: Single-gym owners with stalled growth.
### POD-0058#02 | EVOLUTION | topic: product-delivery | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:07
claim: He started as a marketing-first operator and later saw that an excellent service multiplies marketing results; looking back, he says he probably would have run his gyms a lot better.
detail: Having been good at marketing and sales, he found that a really good Gym Launch product compounded results. He says that if he had known this, he would have looked at each member as a source of more customers and tried to blow them away.
### POD-0058#03 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:15
claim: Members pay for fitness but stay for the extras they did not ask for, so add unpromised extras and do not promise them in advance.
detail: Examples: impromptu massages, childcare, events, social gatherings and community. Over-delivering only works if the extras were not promised; under-promise and over-deliver.
