### LC-17#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: lead-getters | fw: Core Four | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: The $100M Leads system runs in sequence: engaged leads, a lead magnet, the Core Four, More-Better-New, the four lead getters, daily execution, and the seven-level roadmap.
detail: (1) A lead is a person you can contact; the target is an engaged lead — one who has shown interest in what you sell, the output of advertising. (2) Engage them with an offer or lead magnet. (3) Advertise it with the Core Four: warm outreach (ask people you know whether they know anyone who wants the free thing — no pitching), posting content (hook-retain-reward, give until they ask), cold outreach (lists, personalization, big fast value, volume through automation), paid ads (targeting, callouts, what-who-when, CTAs, client-financed acquisition). (4) Maximize with More-Better-New. (5) Add lead getters: customers (referrals), employees, agencies (as teachers), affiliates (compounding). (6) Execute daily via Rule of 100 or Open to Goal and the five-step plan. (7) Follow the seven levels toward the full machine.

### LC-17#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: More Better New | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:42
claim: More-Better-New in one line each: remove whatever stops you doing 10× the volume; find and test the advertising constraint until it frees, then do more until it is constrained again; "new" comes third (order may be better-more-new, depending on business size).
detail: "More" asks what keeps you from running the current activity at ten times the volume, then solves it. "Better" finds the constraint in your advertising, tests until it is freed, then you do more until it constrains again. Depending on business size the order may be better-more-new.

### LC-17#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Many-Sided Die | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:12
claim: The many-sided die parable: every player has a die with an unknown number of sides and one green side, and only those who keep rolling get their green streak.
detail: Two players get dice of 20 and 200 sides, each with one green side; neither can see how many sides they have. Rolling green turns another side green; rolling red changes nothing; the game ends only when you stop, and stopping loses. One player keeps rolling regardless of results; the other rolls a few reds, blames the other's die, complains about the wait after his first green, and quits. Lessons: given enough rolls, the die you were dealt does not matter; you never know whether others are on roll 100 or 100,000; the "lucky" are luckier because they play. Hormozi wrote it four years earlier and edited it about 30 times.

### LC-17#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: paid-ads | fw: Many-Sided Die | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:48
claim: Advertising success compounds: early attempts will probably fail, but each success makes the next more likely, and only two things are guaranteed.
detail: The guarantees: the more you roll, the better you get; if you quit, you lose. As red sides turn green, hits shift from exception to rule.

### LC-17#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: paid-ads | fw: Many-Sided Die | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:34
claim: "You cannot lose if you do not quit" — expect to fail more often than you win in advertising; Hormozi says he still does.
detail: Advertising has more failure than almost any other business skill, but wins can be big and you improve with each. Hormozi still expects to lose more often than he wins. He says that expecting a first-roll hit sets you up for failure; even after a hit you must roll again and may get red. He repeated this phrase to himself during his hardest days.
quote: You cannot lose if you do not quit.
