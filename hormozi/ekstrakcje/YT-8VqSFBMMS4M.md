claim: Founder of a high-margin parent-coaching programme who sells through 3-4 hour live marathons and her own DMs is told to replace them with a 45-minute structured webinar, bill every six months, upsell during the intensive and turn delivery Q&A into content.

Result: DM time fell from ~30 to 3-5 hours a week.

### YT-8VqSFBMMS4M#02 | TACTIC | topic: scaling-constraints | also: offers, sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:10
claim: Hormozi's standard fix order for a business is back to front: offer first, then the conversion process, then traffic.
detail: He calls it his three-step approach and explains "back to front" as starting with the thing that is delivered, then how it is sold, and only then how more people are brought in. In this episode he closes the conversion and delivery fixes ("now we have an offer that already renews... now we just need more traffic") before turning to content and ads.

### YT-8VqSFBMMS4M#03 | SCRIPT | topic: sales-process | also: promotions-launches | fw: Three Core Beliefs | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:59
claim: A webinar needs no special language pattern, only clear communication in a classic order: promise, origin story, old way versus new way, three biggest concerns each broken with proof, offer, FAQ.
detail: Open with a big promise. Origin story as the reason to listen. Core transformation as old way / new way: what I used to do, why it failed, the discovery, the result, and that it also worked for other avatars. Then the three biggest concerns, each handled as what they believe, why that is wrong, what is right, then proof (stats, stories; the more forms the better). Visual slides, as many listen in the background. For this caller: then offer, then FAQs ranked by how often each is asked.

### YT-8VqSFBMMS4M#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:49
claim: To scale you have to sell to a cold audience, so pitch as if nobody knows you: a cold-style pitch converts warm and cold, a warm-style pitch converts only warm.
detail: Said after noting that her ads mostly reached people who already knew her. His contrast: selling as if to strangers converts everyone, while selling as if to people who know you leaves strangers unconverted.

### YT-8VqSFBMMS4M#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Five Upsell Moments | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:38
claim: An upsell actually pitched only at the end of a programme usually gets about a third; pitched at the halfway point, the two-thirds point and a last chance, half to two thirds or more.
detail: Reason he gives: people cool off over time, so sell while pain is still high and while they are mid-decision ("still in flight"), because continuing is a lighter sale than starting a new decision. Analogy: a wristband offered while buying marathon shoes gets a yes; offered after the race, a no. He adds that owners fear selling before the customer is done, and says this fear is misplaced. Her figure: 10% now, mostly sold near month 12.

### YT-8VqSFBMMS4M#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:35
claim: Questions customers ask repeatedly in delivery are ready-made content, because the audience does not perceive an answer as repeated even when the creator has given it many times.
detail: His example: Dave Ramsey has answered the same spend-less, save-more question for decades. For this caller: cut each live Q&A answer into a short post (AI or offshore editor, as the cut is repeatable), so 30-50 questions a week become 30-50 posts; answer high-engagement community questions as green-screen reaction videos. Delivery becomes content, pitches stay pitches.

claim: In a green-screen answer video, read the question and answer it; do not open with "a lot of people have been asking me this".

In the same passage, for this caller: point every other platform's bio and descriptions to the one channel where she converts (Instagram DMs), which then feeds the webinar.
