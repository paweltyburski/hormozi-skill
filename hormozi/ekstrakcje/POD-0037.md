### POD-0037#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:19
claim: If you no longer love your customer, the options he lists are to close, to find an avatar you love, or, as his tentative solution, to be passionate about running an exceptional business.
detail: He accepts that many owners who fell into a field once cared (perhaps after a personal transformation) and no longer do. He says he probably would have started his gym around turning skinny guys into jacked ones, the avatar he is passionate about.
applies_to: Gym and service owners who feel flat about their customers.
### POD-0037#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:18
claim: Pretending to be passionate about customers makes you feel like a fraud and staff will not believe you; being honest that your drive is excellence is credible.
detail: Obsess over service, product, repeatable processes and a consistent deliverable. Say plainly to the team that the aim is a world-class business with unbelievable customer service, and staff will take it as real. He also notes that lacking personal experience of the customer's struggle (he was never overweight) is why caring may not feel real.

### POD-0037#03 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:32
claim: A fitness-trainer friend opened a cookie and cake bakery for the margins, aiming at a franchise, and a quickly built ad campaign brought people through the door cheaply.
detail: He was not passionate about baked goods; he cared about improving the business and making a product so good it needs little marketing. Hormozi set up the first campaign in about 90 minutes. He notes that drawing people in to eat is much easier than drawing them in to lose weight.
numbers: $20 a day in ads; about $3 per person who showed up.
