### MZ-4C1s-ZAjrjw#02 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: retention-ltv, verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:29
claim: Show workers a career ladder of about eight levels, alternating title and pay raises, each level unlocking after a new skill.
detail: Example: a nail-salon chain took school dropouts to about $70,000 a year on this ladder. Micro-wins and rising pay make leaving unattractive. With percentage pay, unlock a rising share (e.g. an extra 5% of time) if they keep customers, with quotas.
numbers: 8 levels; up to ~$70,000/year; titles and pay raise alternate.

### MZ-4C1s-ZAjrjw#03 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: management-ops, verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:40
claim: Centralise sales and pay crews a flat amount per clean, with a free redo if the customer calls back about quality.
detail: Output tripled because crews paid per job work faster, as with parcel carriers. Charging about $200 while paying $50 per clean still yielded a better margin for the owner.
numbers: charge ~$200 per clean, pay ~$50; output tripled.

### MZ-4C1s-ZAjrjw#04 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:49
claim: Publish the maximum a worker can earn in a tiered rev share, because people assume they will reach the top tier.
detail: Survivorship bias (as in network marketing, banking and insurance) keeps people working for years; the blended rate paid is then much lower than the top tier shown.
numbers: blended rate about 28% (percentage of revenue, unclear in audio).
