### POD-0280#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:38
claim: Hormozi spent roughly $1.1-1.2M and nine months building a gym CRM he never launched.
detail: Gym owners tracked churn, revenue and customers across spreadsheets and documents, and existing CRMs were partial. He decided to build the best CRM for gyms, using an outside hourly developer. A third company was too much on top of the supplement and service companies, so it stayed unlaunched, about nine weeks from finished. It also distracted his team, and his wife had advised against it.
numbers: About $1.1-1.2M development spend; nine months; about 9 weeks of development left.
applies_to: Founders tempted to build software outside their core.

### POD-0280#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:26
claim: Do not enter a space you do not understand; if building software, have a CTO with equity rather than a developer billing by hours.
detail: You need someone who owns it and knows software deeply. Do not start several businesses at once.

### POD-0280#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:59
claim: Reinvest in more of what already works and put surplus money in something non-business, rather than adding another business that adds complexity.
detail: Have a game beyond the game: the second game uses money to make money. Simple scales. Staying simple is mostly saying no, and it gets harder as money grows because options get cooler.

### POD-0280#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:48
claim: At every income level a new, bigger shiny opportunity appears; the skill is declining one the size of your current business.
detail: At $100K a year a $10K side-opportunity is easy to refuse, but another $100K one is tempting; at $10M a year the same applies to another $10M opportunity. Aim to be energised, not excited, since excitement signals emotion and a bad decision.
numbers: Tempting opportunity is about the size of the current business (e.g. $100K at $100K a year, $10M at $10M).
