### LC-15#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: operator-mindset | fw: Learning Budget | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:39
claim: Set aside a learning budget — a fixed share of ad spend for crazy ideas you give yourself full permission to lose.
detail: Source: at a $10K private event for owners doing $10M+ (June 2017, months after Hormozi had lost everything again), a marketer doing $30M/year was asked where he learns. His answer: at his level courses teach little, so he allocates a percentage of monthly ad spend to wild experiments. Most do nothing; occasionally one takes off and he innovates the whole market again. Being around people at that level stretched Hormozi's sense of what was possible.
numbers: Learning budget suggested at 5–10% of monthly advertising spend.

### LC-15#02 | CASE | topic: paid-ads | also: operator-mindset, scaling-constraints | fw: Learning Budget | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:29
claim: Hormozi turned the idea into a fixed $10K/month budget for experiments expected to lose money, cranked his ads on return, and kept scaling.
detail: Gym Launch had just moved to licensing; it was him, Layla and an assistant doing $300–400K/month, and he was afraid to spend because his habits still matched a poor budget, while others warned that someone would copy them if they did not scale. Spending real money on advertising teaches more than anything else, because you care, pay attention and innovate faster. He attributes later growth to reinvesting a percentage into crazy ideas with permission to fail.
numbers: Learning budget $10K/month; growth path stated as $1M → $1.2M → $1.5M → $1.8M → $2.5M → $4M → $16M/month (now, Acquisition.com).

### LC-15#08 | DEFINITION | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: sales-team, management-ops | fw: Open to Goal | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:22
claim: Open to Goal means working each day until you hit a specific number of outcomes, however long it takes — the Rule of 100 "on steroids".
detail: The Rule of 100 (100 primary advertising actions per day) is the baseline duty. Open to Goal came from a privately held 15-location gym chain run by an ex-football coach: sales managers had to hit a daily sales number and could leave the moment they did — after five hours or after eighteen. You commit to outcomes, not activity, no matter what.

### LC-15#09 | EXAMPLE | topic: sales-process | also: outreach | fw: Open to Goal | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:28
claim: One of a company's top door-knockers of all time refused to stop until he had set 20 appointments, counting each one aloud.
detail: He repeated his current count between houses and quit only at 20 — sometimes by lunch, sometimes after dark. The same pattern recurs across industries.
numbers: Daily goal 20 appointments set.

### LC-15#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Open to Goal | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:26
claim: Open to Goal builds the muscle of doing what it takes, because daily averages fluctuate and a fixed activity count can leave you short.
detail: A 1% reply rate is an average; some days the needed replies take 150 or 200 reach-outs. Committing to the outcome forces you to cover the gap, which he considers more valuable than anything else. His phrase: what is required is greater than your best — your best has to get better.
numbers: Example: 150–200 reach-outs on a bad day instead of 100.

### LC-15#11 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:26
claim: Hormozi structures his day in four ways to protect long uninterrupted blocks for the highest-value work.
detail: (1) Get four to six hours to yourself daily — he wakes very early; others use late nights; the time, not the hour, matters. (2) Start without rituals: coffee, then work; a two- or three-hour morning routine must beat three hours of actual work, and 15–30 minutes of warm-up is plenty. (3) No meetings before noon: starting at 5 a.m., that is seven focused hours a day — roughly a full work week before the workday begins. (4) Newer addition, scheduling back to front: put meetings at the end of the day so the longest block stays unbroken; 5 a.m. to 3 p.m. gives ten hours. Start immediately on the heaviest, most important tasks.
numbers: 4–6 h protected time; 7 h (5 a.m.–noon) daily; 10 h (5 a.m.–3 p.m.) with back-to-front scheduling.

### LC-15#13 | FRAMEWORK | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: hiring-talent, lead-getters | fw: Advertising Checklist | novelty: canon | ts: 00:14:45
claim: The one-page execution plan has five steps: pick the lead type, commit to daily volume, fill out the advertising checklist, work it until you can pay someone, then recruit help and repeat.
detail: (1) Pick the engagement you want: customers, or lead getters (affiliates, employees, agencies). (2) Choose Rule of 100 or Open to Goal and commit to daily actions — 100 reach-outs, 100 minutes of content, or a set amount or percentage of ad spend (the learning budget); bonus: an extra 100 a day of another Core Four method. (3) Fill out the advertising checklist for those actions. (4) Do it daily until you can afford to pay someone else to do it. (5) Then make employees your target lead type and repeat steps one to four until you have the help you need.

### LC-15#14 | TACTIC | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: - | fw: Advertising Checklist | novelty: canon | ts: 00:15:34
claim: The advertising checklist answers who, what, where, to whom, when, why, how, how much, how many and how long for your daily actions.
detail: Who: you. What: the offer or lead magnet. Where: the platform. To whom: the audience or list. When: the first six to eight hours of your day. Why: to get X leads or Y lead getters. How: which Core Four method. How much: 100, or until you hit the goal. How many: number of follow-ups or retargeting touches. How long: 100 days (unclear in audio) or until you hit the goal.
