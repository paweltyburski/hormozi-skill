### POD-0388#01 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: Five concern types | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:06
claim: The Unicorn close turns "I need to think about it" into a hypothetical that exposes the real gap.
detail: Steps: 1) ask whether they would start if this were a perfect, unicorn-grade programme with everything they want; 2) they say yes to the hypothetical (a no means the earlier sale failed); 3) ask what separates the programme as they understand it from that perfect one; 4) their answer names the real obstacle, which you then resolve. A joke inside the line keeps the mood light.

### POD-0388#02 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:01
claim: After a 1-to-10 rating, ask what would make it a 10 and why it is not a 2.
detail: Whatever number they give (usually a 6 or 7, sometimes 8), the figure itself matters less than the follow-ups. "What would make you a 10" names the missing piece. "Why aren't you a 2" flips the question so they defend the programme's merits and talk themselves toward yes. If their reasons are negative, fix those issues, then ask again.

### POD-0388#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:01
claim: An objection step has one aim: isolate the issue, resolve it, then ask again, and repeated asks are normal.
detail: Sales effectively begin at the first no; an instant yes is just a transaction. A no usually means "not yet, I lack understanding", so stay comfortable asking more than once after resolving each issue.

### POD-0388#04 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:33
claim: Slow your cadence and stress the words that matter to the prospect, because delivery changes meaning more than content.
detail: Two levers: speed plus pitch direction, and which word carries emphasis. In a close, slow down so they do not feel rushed and so each word lands, and emphasise their words and goals. He holds that no objection response works if the tone is wrong.

### POD-0388#05 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Build light humour into closes to de-escalate a prospect who is in fight-or-flight.
detail: Prospects have far fewer of these conversations than the seller and feel the tension more. Embedded jokes (the unicorn, an over-the-top low rating on the scale) lower it and keep rapport high; the seller should be the one steadily calming the situation.

### POD-0388#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:05
claim: Make the conclusion the prospect's own idea by questioning with curiosity, never by proving them wrong.
detail: Proving a stalling prospect wrong means both sides lose. Keep rapport through the close, ask questions that lead them to the conclusion, and keep the talk on their goals rather than on how good you are. Many sellers lose rapport at this stage by taking the stall personally.

### POD-0388#07 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:05
claim: Frame the choice as certain failure to reach the goal by not signing versus a real chance by signing.
detail: A prospect who has not hit their goal despite the time already spent is on a path that is unlikely to change without help. Presenting the two options plainly makes buying the obvious way to raise the odds.
