### POD-0191#01 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Answer "I can't spend money, things are uncertain or I lost my job" by narrowing to the few things still within control, such as health.
detail: Steps: acknowledge that much is outside anyone's control, then point to the small set of things they can control and argue their health is one. Expect many no's anyway; the answer helps a minority and the rest require thick skin.
applies_to: Phone sales during a downturn.

### POD-0191#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:34
claim: In a crisis, lead flow is high but conversion is low, so treat selling as a volume game and stack appointments.
detail: He saw the highest lead volume and lowest lead costs in years for remote offers. Closing 6 of 20 still produces six more sales than someone who did not try. The period builds sales skill.
numbers: Example result: 6 sales from 20 appointments.

### POD-0191#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: product-delivery | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:19
claim: Moving from in-person to remote delivery can raise profit even if revenue falls sharply, because rent and most payroll disappear.
detail: Remote one-on-one is also more valuable than group. Gyms that had moved to hybrid eight months earlier were better placed. Hormozi had discussed the hybrid gym with them eight months earlier.
numbers: Top line $30,000/month falling to $12,000/month still earns more once rent and most payroll are removed.
applies_to: Gyms and similar local services.
### POD-0191#04 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:34
claim: A physical-therapy chain of 30 locations shut everything and laid off all staff; Hormozi would have moved sessions to remote with bands and balls.
detail: His point: when the usual way of solving the customer's problem is blocked, find a new way to deliver the same value, fast. He contrasts businesses with demand that vanished (events, cinemas, restaurants) with fitness, which can still serve customers.
numbers: 30 locations closed, 100% of staff laid off.

### POD-0191#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:57
claim: Do not sell long prepaid terms for cash flow during a crisis if you are not sure you can deliver.
detail: He would like to promote pre-sales but feels uneasy: if the business does not survive, taking prepaid money is effectively taking money you cannot return. Pivot the model instead.

### POD-0191#06 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:52
claim: In a crisis, tell the team plainly that fast, large changes are coming and that leadership is not ready either.
detail: Gym Launch built a remote lead-handling tool in a seven-day development cycle with the team working in shifts around the clock, a project previously planned for mid-Q3. Decisions are faster and pivots larger than normal.
numbers: Seven-day dev cycle for a project planned for end of Q2 / mid Q3.

### POD-0191#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:41
claim: Do not give everything away free in a crisis; build a better paid solution, because revenue funds reach.
detail: Competitors were distributing free checklists. Hormozi argues a paid product, taken seriously, lets you advertise and reach more people. Cutting price is often an excuse by someone not proud enough to charge full price.
