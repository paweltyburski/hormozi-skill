### MZ-7cEDLVP9T08#01 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: verticals, unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A sales-team agency stuck at $1.3M a month sees margins compress because founders stopped working for free.
detail: Last year's profit looked fat because founders did the work unpaid; hiring a leadership team, account managers and engineers compressed margins. Fulfilment pods manage fewer clients each because churn was high. Pricing is about 25% of client top line.
numbers: Revenue just under $14M; about $1.3M a month flat; profit about $3M, nearly the same as last year on half the revenue; goal $100M in three years; fee 25% of client top line.

### MZ-7cEDLVP9T08#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: unit-economics | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:36
claim: Ask whether margins compress and then stop, or keep compressing, and what makes up the added cost base.
detail: Continuing compression points to a structural model problem rather than a one-off step up in hiring.

### MZ-7cEDLVP9T08#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: 80/20 Customer Analysis | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:15
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: with a 25% of top line fee, he probably has a bottom-up issue, so raising prices looks more like getting bigger, more valuable clients than going from 25 to 30.
detail: Compute profitability per customer, derive the ideal customer profile, retarget messaging. A single narrow avatar allows templated service levels and automation, so margin should expand over time or at least stay the same as it scales.
numbers: He contrasts getting bigger clients with moving the fee from 25% to 30% or so.
### MZ-7cEDLVP9T08#04 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: management-ops | fw: Value vs Willingness-to-Pay Matrix | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:30
claim: List every rep activity and prune those that don't correlate with retention or even hurt it.
detail: Some activities drive retention, some do nothing, some reduce it. Pruning lets each rep carry more accounts and reverses margin compression.

### MZ-7cEDLVP9T08#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: hiring-talent | fw: Virtuous Cycle of Price | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:44
claim: Good services always face supply constraint, so price rises and pays ever more expensive talent.
detail: Larger customers, premium prices, better reputation, more demand, so talent rises from $100,000 to $250,000 to $1M and multi-million pay.
numbers: Talent pay path: $100,000, then $250,000, then $1M+ a year.
