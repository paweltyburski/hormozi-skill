### YT-Gy-RmpRif-I#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: At a 20% net margin, a price rise multiplies profit far more than its size suggests.
detail: With cost unchanged, a 20% net margin on a $100 monthly price means a 20% increase doubles profit, a 50% increase gives about 3.5x, and doubling the price gives about 6x.
numbers: 20% net margin; +20% price = 2x profit; +50% = 3.5x; +100% = 6x.
### YT-Gy-RmpRif-I#02 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Hormozi tripled the price at his own facility, lost a third of customers, and still doubled revenue.
detail: Two thirds stayed at three times the price. Staff called the cull of complainers "the culling". Profit rose even more because a third fewer customers had to be served, and the remaining ones were those who liked the business.
numbers: Price x3; one third of customers cancelled; revenue about 2x.

### YT-Gy-RmpRif-I#03 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:30
claim: A price-increase letter to existing customers follows a fixed order that centres on the customer, not on the seller.
detail: 1. Say why you are writing (growth, good things happening). 2. List what you already do for them, including costs they never see, to resell the value and show where the money goes. 3. Say what you plan to add (programs, service levels, events). 4. Tie the increase to a promise or value nobody can dispute, so it is framed as keeping a promise. 5. Close with a perspective PS.

### YT-Gy-RmpRif-I#04 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:40
claim: Quote the increase in small units (per week or per day) rather than as a yearly sum.
detail: Break the extra cost down to the smallest unit the customer will accept, as insurers do with a daily figure, so the perceived cost shrinks.
numbers: Example: $5,000 extra a year is framed as about $100 a week or about $15 a day.

### YT-Gy-RmpRif-I#05 | SCRIPT | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:37
claim: End the letter with a PS that offers help to anyone for whom the rise is a real hardship.
detail: The PS says that if the increase would affect their mortgage or groceries they should contact you privately. It gives everyone perspective that nobody loses a home over it, and it opens a one-to-one discussion only for those who truly need it.

### YT-Gy-RmpRif-I#06 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:37
claim: Announce by video and direct mail, switch comments off, and hold your ground once decided.
detail: Turn off comments on the post so it does not become a gossip forum, and ask people to bring concerns to you directly. Expect squeaky wheels who want the rise cancelled; do not back down. Those who leave are usually the worst customers, and replacing a customer is hard for competitors too.
