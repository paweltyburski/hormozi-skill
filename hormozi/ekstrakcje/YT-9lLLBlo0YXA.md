### YT-9lLLBlo0YXA#01 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:22
claim: The comparison close: ask which provider they would choose if both cost the same, let them list reasons, and keep asking for more.
detail: Steps: 1) pretend price is not a factor; 2) they pick you and explain why; 3) keep prompting ("anything else? ROI, risk, track record?"); 4) after minutes of them selling you, say they can now see why you are not $200 more.

### YT-9lLLBlo0YXA#02 | CASE | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: Price to Value Discrepancy | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A coaching agency lost deals to $200-a-month competitors; Hormozi reframes price as return, like a stock, not as cost.
detail: Analogy: personal training at $80 an hour versus a $10 kid down the street. $200 for nothing is a worse deal than $1,000 that returns 10x. Cheap rivals often cannot afford acquisition, and the lost prospect often returns. A $200 client kept a year is only about $2,400 lifetime value.
numbers: $200/month retainer; $2,400 lifetime if kept 12 months; $80 vs $10 per hour illustration.

### YT-9lLLBlo0YXA#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: objections-closing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:53
claim: Ask if the best in the industry would really charge the same as a bootcamp client, and whether they want to buy nice or buy twice.
detail: The price discussion is moved to how likely they are to reach the goal and what another three months of failure costs.
