### MZ-DVtK_djRjjE#01 | CASE | topic: paid-ads | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A Pilates education owner (550-hour school, $13,000 ticket, 2 studios, $1.5M revenue, wants $1B via 500 studios) fired her marketing team after finding that $14,000 a month in ads changed nothing when switched off.
detail: Everything arrived by word of mouth. Ads had once broken even, producing about $14,000 of intro offers a month with an 86% conversion rate. She co-authored the course with a physical therapist, trains teachers, and the studios act as practice space.
numbers: Ad spend $14K/month; intro offers about $14K/month; conversion 86%; revenue $1.5M; school ticket $13,000.

### MZ-DVtK_djRjjE#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: verticals | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:48
claim: In boutique fitness you train low-skilled labor into high-skilled workers who can earn more elsewhere, so the physical venue must be a moat against staff leaving.
detail: An Orangetheory trainer finds it hard to leave because a parking lot lacks the lights, vibe and equipment. A teacher-trainer in a studio can copy the model, creating her own competition.

### MZ-DVtK_djRjjE#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: verticals | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:31
claim: Brick-and-mortar fitness rarely has strong unit economics; the franchisor usually earns the most.
detail: Sandwich chains scale because the product is standardised, while a coach has good and bad days and customers follow the person. Owner-operators with razzle-dazzle cannot be matched by hires you can afford. He knows only one semi-private model (Alloy) that cracked it and guesses Solidcore's economics are poor (unverified).

### MZ-DVtK_djRjjE#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:42
claim: The typical franchise cycle: a strong marketer crushes ads at one site, sells many franchises, private equity buys in, units underperform, the disclosed per-unit earnings (Item 19) drops, and franchise sales die.
detail: He says this describes 90% of franchises, and later says in 99% of franchises the franchisor leaves with the money (figures differ, unclear in audio). He calls it the biggest doom and gloom version.
numbers: Cycle fits about 90% (later 99%) of franchises, his estimate; Item 19 discloses per-location earnings.
