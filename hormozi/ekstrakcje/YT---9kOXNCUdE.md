### YT---9kOXNCUdE#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: An out-of-network pain-management clinic chain with $4.2M revenue and roughly zero profit faced falling insurer reimbursement and rising costs, and had to choose between going in-network and going cash.
detail: Three NYC offices, mixed avatars, mostly insurance. In-network means guaranteed patients but no pricing power, so the game is cost efficiency. Cash means learning to market and sell. The team chose cash. First step was the premium packaging plus advertising, and in the meantime free cash by dropping the lowest-reimbursing patients (for example Medicare) to cut cost.
numbers: Revenue $4.2M; profit ~0; goal $20M; three offices.
applies_to: Healthcare practices dependent on insurance reimbursement.

### YT---9kOXNCUdE#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:19
claim: Choose between the in-network path (win on operational efficiency and return on capital) and the cash path (win on acquiring and selling customers) by asking which problem the owner wants to solve.
detail: In-network, with no pricing power, the business is about operational efficiency, returns on capital and keeping costs low. Cash pay means learning to market and sell to customers.
### YT---9kOXNCUdE#03 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: paid-ads | fw: Menu Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:31
claim: Expect one to three offers to convert profitably on the front end, with the rest of the services sold by cross-sell and upsell after they walk in.
detail: Do not advertise 15 services. Record an ad for one offer, run it on Meta with a pin and about a 10-mile radius, send leads to a CRM or Google Sheet, and have the front desk call within 60 seconds and again if there is no answer. Start upselling existing patients cash packages so the team gets used to people paying.
numbers: 10-mile radius; call within 60 seconds; 1 to 3 front-end offers.

### YT---9kOXNCUdE#06 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: money-models | fw: Downsell Flow | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:23

### YT---9kOXNCUdE#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: lead-getters | also: pricing | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:08
claim: When an affiliate must send a whole project, split a fixed margin pool (for example 20%) between discount to the client and kickback to the referrer, and say it is your bare-bottom price.
detail: Generic 20% kickbacks are everywhere and change nothing. An offer must be good enough to change what the referrer does, otherwise it is too weak or too hard. Do not count the other person's money; if they earn a lot from your lead magnet, your upsell earns more. Raise direct-to-consumer prices to create room.
numbers: Example pool: 20% split as 20/0, 10/10 and so on.

### YT---9kOXNCUdE#10 | EXAMPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: unit-economics | fw: Three-Tier Affiliate Commissions | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:21:25
claim: With a low-cost affiliate-sold item, the cost per customer is the item cost divided by the share of buyers who buy more.
detail: A marker costing $0.50 sold by the partner at $5 gives you contacts; if half buy three more colours, CAC is $0.50 times two. For the railing maker, if one in three introductions closes, CAC for a $50,000 customer is three times the lead-magnet cost.
numbers: $0.50 marker sold at $5; 1-in-2 add-on rate gives $1 CAC; 1-in-3 close gives 3x lead-magnet cost.

### YT---9kOXNCUdE#13 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Conditional Guarantee | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:29:59
claim: For a high-hard-cost home-service product, guarantee on time and on budget or give back your profit, rather than the whole price.
detail: People want the roof, not their money back. List customers' biggest fears (time, budget, disruption) and guarantee those. Returning your gross profit (around 20% in his example) leaves you with a margin and shows skin in the game. Other options: add free time or items, or give back a slice.
numbers: Example refund: ~20% (profit), gross margin ~40%.

### YT---9kOXNCUdE#14 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: objections-closing | fw: Conditional Guarantee | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:31:39
claim: The aim of a guarantee is to maximise net conversions, not to offer the craziest terms; if 10% back sells as well as 100%, give 10%.
detail: Test where the sweet spot is by moving what is guaranteed, and compare sales gain against refund increase. Extra time as a guarantee works but is weak because an unhappy customer does not want more of you.
numbers: Example: 10% or 25% back may equal 100% back in effect.
