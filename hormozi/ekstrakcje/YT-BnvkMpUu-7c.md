### YT-BnvkMpUu-7c#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:50
claim: Judge any investment on four scaled lenses: yield, capital preservation, tax treatment and equity growth.
detail: Yield is monthly cash flow. Capital preservation is the chance the principal comes back. Tax advantage compares capital-gains treatment with ordinary income. Equity growth asks whether the asset gains in value. Each is a matter of degree, not yes or no. A lender gets guaranteed cash but no upside, so the downside must be safe.

### YT-BnvkMpUu-7c#02 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:27
claim: A $450,000 startup note paying 10% at the end of 12 months fails all four lenses.
detail: Principal unlikely to return from a startup; yield is a lump at the end, not monthly cash; no share in upside; at a 45% tax rate the 10% shrinks to about 5.5% net, barely above inflation. He compares it with a roughly 20% index-fund year and calls the deal borderline unethical. A portfolio CEO had received it from a wealth advisor.
numbers: $450,000 principal, $45,000 interest after 12 months, about 5.5% after tax at 45%.

### YT-BnvkMpUu-7c#03 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:10
claim: He lent $2.5M as a short-term bridge loan to buy a foreclosed building, earning 4% a month, about $100,000.
detail: The building was worth roughly twice the price, with four offers between $5.1M and $5.6M and the top buyer having put $300,000 earnest money down. Because the borrowers need liquid cash fast, they accept a high rate, which he states as 48-96% a year. He took personal guarantees from both buyers. He expects deals to find him once he is known to have cash ready.
numbers: loan $2.5M; offers $5.1M-$5.6M; earnest money $300,000; 4% per month = $100,000/month; 48-96% annualised.

### YT-BnvkMpUu-7c#04 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:27
claim: Structure short loans with a minimum carry so a fast repayment still pays a floor.
detail: Planned hold time was 60 days; he gave a 90-day term with a minimum of two months of interest, so even a one-day repayment pays $200,000. A repayment at day 65 rolls into a third month, paying $300,000.
numbers: minimum carry 2 months ($200,000); maximum 3 months ($300,000) on a $2.5M loan at 4% a month.
