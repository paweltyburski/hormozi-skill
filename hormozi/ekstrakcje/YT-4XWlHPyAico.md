### YT-4XWlHPyAico#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: offers | fw: Three Levers of Success | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:06
claim: Rate market, offer and persuasion top down as plus, neutral or negative; he says to move from market to offer when the market is not negative, and to move from offer to persuasion when the offer is not bad or normal.
detail: A great market carries weak offers and weak persuasion, and a bad market defeats strong ones. Most people sit in normal markets (dentists, chiropractors, people in pain), where a unique offer and a good product still earn well.
### YT-4XWlHPyAico#02 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: offers | fw: Four Market Variables | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:45
claim: A founder selling ad software to newspapers on a zero-risk revenue-share offer could not grow because the market shrank about 25% a year for eight years.
detail: He had a strong offer and sales ability and examined every part of the business except the market itself. Size of lever: market beats offer beats persuasion.
numbers: Market shrinking about 25% per year over 8 years.

### YT-4XWlHPyAico#03 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:26
claim: Turning a normal offer into a strong one can take half a day to a day by recombining, repackaging, repositioning, renaming and changing terms.
detail: The offer is the easiest lever to change because you cannot move from a normal market to a great one without changing the business. Terms, price and how the customer redeems are all in scope.
numbers: Time needed: about half a day to one day.

### YT-4XWlHPyAico#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: sales-process | fw: Three Levers of Success | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:26
claim: His recommendation for most people is to fix the offer first, then build persuasion skill, and then look for a market to apply it to.
detail: In his opinion, persuasion (marketing, selling, getting customers through multiple channels) is usually the most difficult lever to acquire and takes a lot of volume, practice and failure. With a great offer, even someone with weak or normal persuasion still makes money.
