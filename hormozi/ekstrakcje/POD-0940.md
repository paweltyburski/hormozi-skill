### POD-0940#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:05:45
claim: When someone is not doing a task, walk a ladder of causes before blaming motivation.
detail: Check in order: 1) did I actually communicate that I wanted it (my fault if not); 2) do they know how (training gap); 3) do they know when (missing deadline); 4) is something blocking them; 5) only last, motivation. He thinks that, by and large, most people prefer to stay employed and do a good job, so if you take that as true, ask what is getting in the way; he adds there are definitely times motivation is the issue. Called the diamond/management document.
### POD-0940#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:07:00
claim: Hire for the smallest skill deficiency, so attitude wins for low-skill roles and aptitude for rare specialist roles.
detail: A cashier or cleaner can be taught the tools in about two hours, while teaching friendliness and reliability takes far longer, so pick attitude. For a top-tier specialist (his AI researcher example), tolerate poor communication that can be coached, since the reverse cannot be trained. He holds that every skill is trainable; the owner's question is whether training this person is the best use of resources.
numbers: Cash-register training about 2 hours.

detail: A faster learner may start behind but may pass an experienced candidate in about six months; the choice depends on how long you can wait. Soft skills are just skills that are harder to define and measure.

### POD-0940#04 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:27
claim: Gauge intelligence in an interview by the quality of the candidate's questions.
detail: A good question shows research and links two parts of the business (for example asking about revenue retention across the media brand and the advisory practice, or about a vehicle not in the pipeline). A generic question such as a five-year vision is weak when the answer is already public. Also watch how they break down a problem.

### POD-0940#05 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:32
claim: For senior roles, give candidates a real current company problem instead of a hypothetical case.
detail: Consulting firms use cases to see how people think; here the real case adds engagement. Worst case you get free consulting, best case you find someone who can implement the answer. Candidates may bring frameworks or experience the owner lacks. Send the problem with company context in advance and have them prepare thoughts.

### POD-0940#06 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:13
claim: Screen salespeople by sending a script in advance and having candidates perform a one to two minute piece in a group call.
detail: With about ten candidates on one call, those who did not rehearse sound poor and are cut within minutes, signalling lack of skill or work ethic. Then correct a mistake and ask them to retry, to observe coachability, ego and learning speed. Repeated roles (editor, salesperson) get structured processes; this process matched the one in a sales-hiring book he later read.
numbers: About 10 candidates per call; 1-2 minutes each, against 30-minute calls.

### POD-0940#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:15:09
claim: Second-time founders grow faster mainly because they learned to recognise competent people at each role level.
detail: At roughly $1-3M the owner must hand off marketing, sales, delivery and low-level management; each department stalls until one competent person is found, with 6-12 month cycles of interviewing and onboarding. ### POD-0940#11 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: offers | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:59
claim: Attract talent with a value proposition (growth, merit-based advancement, guaranteed demand) so you are not competing on pay alone.
detail: Employer offer: fast career advancement irrespective of age or tenure (one hire got a large promotion after 90 days), pay above market, and guaranteed demand, which lowers risk because the question is only the best use of existing demand. ### POD-0940#14 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:25:38
claim: Fire fast when the person is the company's constraint; otherwise it is a trash-can fire, not a kitchen fire.
detail: A mediocre person in a role that is not limiting growth is probably not the first priority, but once that role becomes the constraint the issue surfaces and is handled. They will not sacrifice growth and the other employees' careers to avoid an uncomfortable talk. Reputation as a brutal employer matters, and sometimes people turn around with feedback and coaching, which he says probably happens half the time.

### POD-0940#16 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:28:06

### POD-0940#17 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:29:34
claim: Pull deadlines forward by questioning them: a level two manager asks what is blocking, reprioritises, then asks for hours of work.
detail: Level 0 and 1 managers accept the date given. Level 2 asks what else is blocking, resets priorities, gets a new date, asks how many hours the task really takes, then challenges the gap (an example: four hours of work at noon, so why not by four?). These can feel confrontational but move a whole organisation fast.
numbers: Example: 4 hours of work, started at noon, due by 4 pm.
