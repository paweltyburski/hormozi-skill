### MZ-bfd01ESMz6w#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: More Better New | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: An optical frames wholesaler ($4.5M, flat) sees independent opticians fall from 13,000 to 12 as private equity consolidates.
detail: Retention with surviving accounts is strong; after acquisition accounts move to larger suppliers. Owner considers a consumer brand. Hormozi: that is a new, riskier business; treat it as a demand problem.
numbers: 13,000 independents to 12 in ~24 months (as stated); revenue about $4.5M.

### MZ-bfd01ESMz6w#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:37
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: because his frames customers were consolidating, Hormozi would approach the private equity groups and offer to supply their whole portfolio at a super wholesale price, or alternatively run ads to optometrists.
detail: For this caller there is a smaller skill gap than building a consumer brand; he said closing a handful of such deals could take the business from $4M to $20M, and the buyers might also want an option to buy it later. For the supply issue he said he thinks a phone sale could work.
numbers: $4M to $20M from closing a handful of private equity deals (said to this caller).
### MZ-bfd01ESMz6w#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:50
claim: B2B with high revenue retention is worth more than a one-time consumer brand unless the brand is huge.
detail: Do not kill a flat business that is not dead; the same objections remain in the alternative, with more risk.
