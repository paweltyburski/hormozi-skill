### YT-YhxiVIQp_ws#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:00
claim: The two ways he cites to get around federal tax are living in Puerto Rico and exporting services, or expatriating; separately, moving to a zero-income-tax state cuts state tax. He stayed in Texas rather than live on an island.
detail: A zero-tax state versus about 15-16% in California (his figure). US citizens are taxed globally, so the options are Puerto Rico (reside at least about six months a year and export services, roughly 4% federal and 0% state) or giving up citizenship. He declined Puerto Rico because a difference between $100M and $180M of net worth changes nothing and the residency terms cost freedom.
numbers: Puerto Rico about 4% federal; California about 15-16% state (as stated); at least six months' residence.
applies_to: US taxpayers.

### YT-YhxiVIQp_ws#04 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:30
claim: Buy undervalued assets and hold them: unrealised gains are untaxed, and loans against the assets are not income.
detail: Paying $30 for something worth $100 triples the money with no tax until you sell. Borrow against the assets to live, provided the loans stay below the assets' growth, and let the rest compound untaxed. He cites an investor who has held the same holdings for decades. The goal is assets that will still exist in 30 years, not quick flips.
numbers: example $30 purchase of a $100 asset = 3x untaxed.

claim: He skipped a captive-insurance scheme that would have saved about $250,000 a year, and the tax authority later reversed its ruling and charged back interest and fees.
detail: Lesson: rules can change retroactively, and small savings for aggressive structures are not worth hassle and risk. He treats rule changes like platform rule changes: stay inside the rules.
