### POD-0373#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:32
claim: Treat business as an infinite game: choose strategies where waiting makes you win, since the longest time horizon wins.
detail: Finite games have known players, rules and a winner; infinite games (health, wealth, marriage, long-lived companies) aim to keep playing. Playing an infinite game with a finite view causes trouble. Extending the horizon lowers downside toward zero. He says he does not expect to sell another company.

### POD-0373#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:46
claim: Wealthy people buy and build instead of selling, because a sale shrinks equity through tax and forces a move into a less efficient vehicle.
detail: Sell only if the party is over or interest is gone. Buffett and Munger framing: imagine you may buy only 20 companies for life and you choose differently.

### POD-0373#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:56
claim: Early on, market to make a sale so you can win a customer, not to win a customer for one sale.
detail: If customers do not repurchase or refer, growth rests on brute-force selling that collapses when it stops. With a long horizon he trades short-term growth for learning how to improve the product, accepting less marketing at first. He adds that mistakes love a rushed decision and arbitrary weekly or quarterly targets cut the horizon.

### POD-0373#04 | EVOLUTION | topic: content-brand | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: updates | ts: 00:06:56
claim: He now sees a fixed give-to-ask cadence as transactional and prefers giving for far longer so goodwill compounds.
detail: Goodwill grows faster than revenue because the price is zero and value is above it. A pattern like give, give, give, ask withdraws from goodwill and slows growth; with a big enough audience some people give back without being asked. He links this to why large brands move from direct response toward brand over time.
numbers: Illustration only: about 10% of an audience buying per ask; 1% giving back unprompted at large scale.

### POD-0373#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:22
claim: Hire only on a clear yes and interview many more candidates, accepting discomfort for longer.
detail: He used to hire when someone seemed good enough; now, if unsure about a candidate, the answer is no. He would interview 25 people instead of five. Consider the person for the next decade, not the next day, mainly on values and culture fit; some seasons suit certain people.
numbers: 25 interviews instead of 5.

### POD-0373#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:22
claim: A business is limited by the combined knowledge of everyone in it, so an owner who can do everyone's job caps it at one brain.
detail: Typical for owners at about $3M-$5M and below. Growth needs people smarter or better than you at specific things; freedom and control cannot both be had, because a business that runs without you must rely on others.
numbers: Typical owner range $3M-$5M.
applies_to: Owners at roughly $3M-$5M or below

### POD-0373#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:47
claim: Add slowly, remove quickly, because removing a person is much harder than adding one.
detail: Compromise hires work like a cheap temporary couch you still own five years later. He applied it to dating (asking early whether he could marry the person) and applies it to friends and staff. His four-way trade: fear is short-term pain, regret long-term pain, comfort short-term gain, fulfillment long-term gain; trade fear for fulfillment, not for comfort.

### POD-0373#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: other | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:34
claim: Choose a health routine you could keep for a decade; if it cannot last, it is not worth starting.
detail: Pick the roughly 5% you can do daily for life; liking the activity beats an optimal one you drop. He notes that long workouts take 10% or more of waking hours, and for longevity he cites 10,000 steps, a baby aspirin and not smoking.
numbers: 90 minutes, five days a week, is 10% or more of waking hours (his estimate).
