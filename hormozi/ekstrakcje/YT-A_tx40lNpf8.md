### YT-A_tx40lNpf8#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:46
claim: The higher your close rate, the more you are underpriced, with a stepped ladder of how much.
detail: For salesperson-led sales. At 80% or more you are typically underpriced by 3-4x; 60-80% by 2-3x (a $100 price could go to $200, even $250-$300); 50-60% by 1.5-2x; 40-50% by 1.25-1.5x. A 30-40% close rate is appropriate only if prospects are educated before the call by content, leaving the call for personalisation. With that rate but no such educating machinery, price could still double or triple once it exists. These are his collected rules of thumb, not laws.
numbers: Close rate bands: 80%+ = 3-4x underpriced; 60-80% = 2-3x; 50-60% = 1.5-2x; 40-50% = 1.25-1.5x; 30-40% = fair.

### YT-A_tx40lNpf8#04 | EVOLUTION | topic: unit-economics | also: scaling-constraints | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: updates | ts: 00:06:42
claim: The 3:1 LTV:CAC floor holds only for fully automated models; each step that needs a human raises the required ratio to 6:1, 9:1 or 12:1.
detail: Score attraction, conversion and delivery each as zero (no human) or one (human). Ads into a checkout page with software delivery (a SaaS) is all zeros; a salesperson or a human doing delivery counts as a human step. All zeros: 3:1. One human step: 6:1. Two: 9:1 (ads, a manual person on the phone closing, and service delivery). Three: 12:1. He calls these minimums for scaling big, and says a 6:1 business can still make a million or a couple million a year.
numbers: 3:1 with zero human steps; 6:1 with one; 9:1 with two; 12:1 with three.
applies_to: Anyone deciding how far to scale spend
### YT-A_tx40lNpf8#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: sales-team | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:42
claim: The extra ratio is padding for the lumpiness a human step adds, because every new hire performs worse than the best one.
detail: Once a salesperson's calendar is full you hire another, who will be weaker at first and maybe always. At 3:1 with the star, the second rep pushes you under 3:1, so you need cushion to absorb training and weak performers. He adds that people over-expand before the model is nailed because the number is tied to ego.

### YT-A_tx40lNpf8#11 | BENCHMARK | topic: sales-team | also: sales-process | fw: Basic Sales KPIs | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:25:00
claim: As a rule of thumb, Hormozi has found about 70% calendar utilisation to be the sweet spot: he doesn't let it go above 85% and tries not to let it drop below 60%.
detail: Overbooked reps produce more absolute sales but conversion falls (CAC rises), appointments are pushed further out and at inconvenient times so show rates drop, and reps have no time to work their pipeline or follow up. ### YT-A_tx40lNpf8#14 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:33:54
claim: Net margin can never exceed gross margin, so a low gross margin leaves almost no room for acquisition, overhead or people.
detail: A 50% gross margin cannot reach a 50% net margin because that would allow no acquisition, fixed or admin cost. With an 80% gross margin and a 50% net target, 30 points cover everything else. Small owners underprice because they charge from their own wallet (they feel bad charging, or think the service is easy because they know it) rather than by the value of the solved problem.
numbers: 80% gross minus 50% net leaves 30% for rent, marketing, sales and the rest.

### YT-A_tx40lNpf8#15 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:36:12
claim: The price needed for a given gross margin rises steeply: on a $100 monthly delivery cost, 70% needs about $333 but 80% needs $500 and 90% needs $1,000.
detail: Gross profit per customer then is about $233, $400 and $900. He says that going from 70% to 90% the margin doubles or more, which is why 60% is nowhere near 80%. His line: he will not get into a business with under 80% gross margins; his rule of thumb for services is at least 80%. Low margins can sometimes be a symptom of a commoditized offer or a sales process that doesn't function properly.
numbers: Cost $100: price $333 for 70%, $500 for 80%, $1,000 for 90%; gross profit $233 / $400 / $900.
applies_to: Service businesses
