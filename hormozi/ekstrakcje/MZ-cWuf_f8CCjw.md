### MZ-cWuf_f8CCjw#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: objections-closing | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:55
claim: Saying or doing something truthful that changes behaviour is persuasion, not manipulation; deception is what makes it look like manipulation under the asker's definition, and he sees these words as all meaning changing behaviour.
detail: He starts by asking the other person to define terms. His first marketing rule: state the facts and tell the truth, and if the facts are not compelling, change the reality rather than the story.
### MZ-cWuf_f8CCjw#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: sales-process | also: content-brand | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:15
claim: Instead of claiming to be the best marketer, he cites a verifiable result: a launch doing $106 million in a weekend using the methods in his free books.
detail: A true specific fact adds proof without deception and raises purchase likelihood.
numbers: $106 million in a weekend (launch).

### MZ-cWuf_f8CCjw#03 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:08
claim: Raise motivation by stating true consequences of inaction that make the bad option feel as bad as it is.
detail: Example: stating that being over 50 pounds overweight costs about five years of life, which could mean not meeting great-grandchildren. It works short term by changing the relative value of a reinforcer.
numbers: Over 50 pounds overweight costs about 5 years of life (his claim).

### MZ-cWuf_f8CCjw#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:15
claim: He puts little weight on intent and more on skills that raise the probability of a chosen action.
detail: Good intent can lead to bad outcomes and the reverse. Teaching, selling, persuading and manipulating all change someone's behaviour, which is the only reason to communicate.
