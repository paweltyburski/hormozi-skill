### POD-0053#01 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:47
claim: Selling done-for-you launches in inner-city Washington D.C., he raised the price and closed as many at $600 as at $500 and $400.
detail: He sold about 100 people per gym launch at $500 to $600 each, and kept the upfront money. His belief was that broke people are poor with money and so easy to part from it. The biggest price barrier was in his own head, not the customers'.
numbers: about 100 sales per launch; $500-$600 per person; same close rate at $400, $500, $600.

### POD-0053#02 | CASE | topic: sales-team | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:19
claim: Auditing his mobile sales team's contracts found about 80% with delayed first payments, so he banned payment plans and closing rates did not change.
detail: Eight reps flew to client gyms and wanted the yes, so they offered deferred starts and instalments. He told them that it was either two $250 payments (second 14 days later, as heard) or no commission. Closing rates held overnight.
numbers: 8 reps; about 80% of contracts with delayed first payments; $250 plus $250 after 14 days.

### POD-0053#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:45
claim: Offering yourself a cheaper fallback mostly convinces the seller, not the prospect, and many objections are just questions.
detail: He recalls a seller who volunteered a version a third of the price when he only asked a question; he was about to buy the main one.

### POD-0053#04 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: objections-closing | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:43
claim: Check whether "they cannot afford it" is true by looking at your own staff's spending.
detail: Trainers he calls broke still spend $500 to $600 a month on supplements, clothing, other gyms and programmes. Separately, he mentions a girl who drove Uber two days a week to pay for a gym membership. Money goes to what they prioritise.
numbers: $500-$600 per month spent by broke trainers.
