### LC-10#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: More Better New | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: More Better New: to get more from any acquisition system, first do more of what works, then do it better, and only then do something new.
detail: How Hormozi thinks when acquisition.com invests in a business; first taught to a group of $1–10M/year owners. More = crank working channels to max capacity. Better = raise conversion through testing. New = new placements, platforms, then Core Four methods, by rising risk. Order flips for larger businesses (see profit rule).

### LC-10#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: More Better New | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:10
claim: "What's stopping us from doing ten times more of what already works?" — the answer is the business's single constraint.
detail: If the economics work, often nothing is stopping them: 100 → 1,000 outreaches a day, $1K → $10K/day ad spend. If something is, attack that. Owners rarely ask because they are too close to the business.

### LC-10#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: outreach, content-brand | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:37
claim: Rule of 100: do 100 primary advertising actions every day for 100 days straight.
detail: Warm outreach: 100 reach-outs/day. Cold: 100/day. Content: 100 minutes/day. Paid ads: $100/day — the only one you may scale down if money is short. Hormozi's default prescription for new or starting entrepreneurs.
applies_to: Founders doing their own advertising.

### LC-10#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: operator-mindset | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:36
claim: The Core Four are fully in your control, so a lead shortage means you aren't doing enough of them.
detail: Are you doing 100 reach-outs a day — yes or no? If it takes four hours, your workday starts after — "welcome to owning a small business". Leverage comes later; first know how to do more yourself.

### LC-10#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: sales-team | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:43
claim: Doing 100 a day yourself teaches what a lead is worth and builds enough skill to teach others — and knowing how to make it rain is, in his words, probably the single most valuable skill an owner can have.
detail: With it you never worry about eating again: you can always pick up the phone and make money.

### LC-10#06 | TACTIC | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: content-brand, paid-ads | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:43
claim: Primary actions: post at least one piece of content per platform daily, do reach-outs on every one-to-one channel, and buy any ad space.
detail: Content: short/long video, articles, podcasts, infographics; post more as you improve and spread 100 minutes across platforms — eventually people who got value ask to buy. Cold adds flyers and calls; expect lower response and use automation to exceed your own capacity. Paid: direct mail, seminars, podcast spots, social ads.

### LC-10#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: More Better New | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:01
claim: "Better" means more leads for the same effort, and the only route is testing: do more until something breaks, then make it better.
detail: Volume exposes the weakest step; improving it raises output without raising input — leverage.

### LC-10#10 | TACTIC | topic: scaling-constraints | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:47
claim: The funnel constraint is the step where the same absolute gain gives the largest relative improvement — focus there.
detail: Funnel 30% opt-in, 5% apply, 50% schedule; add 5 points each: 1.16x, 2x, 1.1x. Attack apply. Lifting apply from 5% to 20% quadruples throughput — likely easier than getting 4x the callers.
numbers: Apply 5→10% = 2x; 5→20% = 4x.

### LC-10#11 | TACTIC | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: paid-ads, management-ops | fw: More Better New | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:59
claim: Test one thing per week per platform: you learn what worked, tests don't contaminate each other, you're forced to prioritize, and each test runs long enough.
detail: Several changes at once hide the cause; tests interact (a new landing page may lift volume but cut quality). One slot forces you to pick the highest-impact change. Probably the most important: run long enough for real data, but not so long you delay the next constraint.

### LC-10#12 | TACTIC | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: paid-ads | fw: More Better New | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:12
claim: Call each test with a statistical-significance calculator, keep the winner, move on — 52 improvements a year, run in parallel across independent channels.
detail: Enter before/after results. Parallel tests are fine on independent pathways (Instagram funnel vs YouTube funnel), never two within one pathway.

### LC-10#14 | DECISION_RULE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: paid-ads | fw: More Better New | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:56
claim: Prioritize tests front to back in the order the lead sees them, because early steps convert lowest and so return most per improvement.
detail: ~1% click an ad while ~30% opt in on the landing page, so the front is usually the constraint. Order: call-out, value, offer, CTA, landing-page headline, sub-headline, image. Fixing just a video's first five seconds once yielded ~200x the views.
numbers: Ad click ~1%; opt-in ~30%.

### LC-10#15 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: More Better New | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:49
claim: For businesses under about $1M annual profit he usually advises doing more first; once past $1M in annual profit, he tends to flip to Better-More-New, since improving can be the lowest-cost, highest-return move (his observation, not a hard rule).
detail: Small businesses lack the volume for percentage gains to matter.
numbers: Threshold ≈ $1M annual profit.

### LC-10#19 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: outreach | fw: More Better New | novelty: canon | ts: 00:14:49
claim: At roughly 1% or 10% of your market contacted you can probably keep doing what you're doing; as you approach 20–30%, maybe start expanding beyond it.
detail: Example: Gym Launch's cold outreach had talked to 20,000 of only ~50,000 US micro-gym owners, which he describes as getting closer to saturating the market.
numbers: 20K of ~50K contacted; consider expanding when approaching 20–30%.

### LC-10#20 | FRAMEWORK | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: content-brand, paid-ads | fw: More Better New | novelty: canon | ts: 00:16:06
claim: Add "new" from easiest to hardest: new placement on a current platform, new platform with similar media, new platform with new media, then a new Core Four method.
detail: Placements are lowest risk (Instagram posts → stories; YouTube → community tab; feed ads → story ads). Next, repurpose similar media elsewhere (short video to short video). Then a new medium (long YouTube → tweets). Hardest: a new method (paid ads → outbound team; cold calling → content). Change as little as possible: take a half step, juice it, then the next. Most people overcorrect and break what works.

### LC-10#21 | DECISION_RULE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: More Better New | novelty: canon | ts: 00:18:59
claim: Go new only when more and better are exhausted — when extra effort on the current channel returns less than the same effort on a new one — then scale it with more and better and repeat.
detail: Follow the order placement → platform → Core Four method; measure, scale, rinse and repeat. Lowest effort and risk for the fastest money.
