### YT-hfwZwPGsbIo#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:43
claim: Hormozi once ran nine businesses at a few million a year and was spread thin; focus works like a magnifying glass.
detail: Six gyms, a chiropractor agency, a dental agency and the done-for-you turnaround launch business. Talent is the size of the lens, but only a focused point burns; the more talented you are, the more opportunities you must decline. Choosing costs the novelty of new things; the reward is a big company with teams and systems.

### YT-hfwZwPGsbIo#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:32
claim: Go where customers already pay: if one door keeps being knocked on, crush it and remove the other doors.
detail: Marketplace demand is feedback, so be a bloodhound for value. Almost every business he built ended up different from the original idea. If buyers want only one of three products, do not push two or three. You basically have to say no to every opportunity in order to make one opportunity worth pursuing.

### YT-hfwZwPGsbIo#06 | BENCHMARK | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:09:53
claim: In his experience, a change to the business (sales, leadership, onboarding process) brings about a 20% drop in performance, and it takes four or five months to make up that 20%.
detail: The dip comes from training the team and people dealing with change. When people are left alone they tend to get better at their jobs, so the 5% improvement may arrive anyway. He calls it anecdotal.
numbers: About 20% dip after a change (his experience); 4-5 months to make up the 20%; example improvement of 5%.
### YT-hfwZwPGsbIo#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:10:52
claim: Hormozi's personal rule: he makes a change only if he sees a risk-adjusted 20% improvement, for example a 40% gain he thinks has a 50-50 shot.
detail: Estimating odds for each idea (50-50, 80-20) lets you get better over time at estimating upside. His reasoning, based on his own anecdotal experience, is that a change costs about a 20% dip in performance, so a hypothetical 5% gain for a guaranteed 20% loss is a bad trade.
numbers: Required 40% improvement at 50% probability.
### YT-hfwZwPGsbIo#08 | BENCHMARK | topic: management-ops | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: More Better New | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:08
claim: He estimates roughly 52 funnel tests a year, if traffic volume is sufficient, but maybe only about four true process changes a year in a department.
detail: Changing several things at once ruins attribution. On the people side he guesses about one change a quarter; the bigger the company, sometimes even fewer. He likes Buffett's idea of a 20-investment punch card because it forces you to think about each shot.
numbers: Roughly 1 funnel test a week (with sufficient traffic); maybe about 1 people-process change per quarter.
### YT-hfwZwPGsbIo#09 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:39
claim: Keep a phone list of improvement ideas, each with estimated value and likelihood, and rank them before changing anything.
detail: Write ideas down instead of calling emergency meetings. If an idea still seems good after two to four weeks, take it to the team, who will compare it with the other 50% ideas from the last 30 days. Only run it when the team has spare bandwidth. He accepts that some things stay imperfect because four others drop each time he fixes one.
numbers: Wait 2-4 weeks (about a month) before acting on an idea.

### YT-hfwZwPGsbIo#10 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: NPS | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:29
claim: Track metrics in real time so you can see the cost of a change in NPS, ascension, churn and satisfaction.
detail: Monthly or fudged numbers hide the dip, and entrepreneurs who change many things often never learn which change broke performance.
