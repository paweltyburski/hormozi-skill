### YT-aFoMYz_jWcs#01 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:33
claim: With under $100,000 saved, cut spending hard and use the freed time to earn more per hour, then wait about 36 months.
detail: Steps for a broke starter: eat only discount groceries, buy clothes second-hand, attend only free social events, apply for better-paid jobs, take side work, learn skills that pay a higher hourly rate, and in any new job find the hardest worker and double their activity. Savings buy patience, and patience lets you take long-term bets on education, inventory or software.
numbers: Savings target $100,000; example of doubling activity: 100 doors becomes 200; wait 36 months.
applies_to: Early-career people with low savings and weak skills

### YT-aFoMYz_jWcs#07 | SCRIPT | topic: outreach | also: sales-process, verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:00
claim: To book a local appointment from a Facebook lead, call with upbeat tone: say the first name as a question, name the source, give a landmark, ask what time works, offer to text the address.
detail: Sequence: confirm identity; say the lead signed up on Facebook; joke that you are checking neither side is a crazy internet stranger; place the gym next to a known shop; give a mildly boastful but playful line about the facility; ask for the time; send the address. Light laughter between words shows warmth, makes showing up likelier and lets you pivot on objections. He bounced on a small trampoline between appointments to keep energy.
numbers: 15 to 20 in-person consults a day plus phone setting in 2013; trampoline cost about $100.
applies_to: Local appointment-based businesses

### YT-aFoMYz_jWcs#15 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:58:57
claim: Test a self-limiting story with three questions: logic (define it as behaviour), evidence (how do you know), utility (who cares, relative to goals).
detail: Credited to Dr. Cashy. Translate "I have demons" into an observable behaviour, such as lateness after two months in a new job. Count the actual occurrences, and decide whether it blocks your goals. If it does, change the behaviour in that condition; if it does not, drop it. He defines trauma as a lasting behaviour change from an aversive event, good or bad depending on whether it serves your goals.
