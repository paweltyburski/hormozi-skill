### MZ-WFu3w0Z5m_g#01 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:20
claim: Replace a trait label ("lazy") with the observed behaviours behind it, say what to do instead next time, and reinforce the change immediately.
detail: Steps: recall the first time you applied the label and each instance; name the specific acts (slow replies, late delivery, poor quality); tell the person which acts, not the trait; when they do it right, reward with whatever they value (public praise, lunch). He says undefined adjectives leave the listener not knowing what to change.

### MZ-WFu3w0Z5m_g#02 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:12
claim: To learn a skill, break an expert into observable actions rather than traits like "confident".
detail: Speaking example: stands on one side about 10 seconds, walks to the other, hand in pocket only to make a point, then out. Copying those observables changes how you come across.
numbers: About 10 seconds per side of the stage.

### MZ-WFu3w0Z5m_g#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: Point of Greatest Deprivation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:03
claim: Motivation is the opposite of deprivation, so to change someone's behaviour, deprive them of something good and make the desired act the route to it.
detail: Hunger, sleep and money are the examples. The more variables you control, the more control over behaviour.

### MZ-WFu3w0Z5m_g#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:03
claim: Be clear, not clever; the longer he has been in business the more he has dropped marketing and sales fanciness.
detail: Clear instructions raise the likelihood of the desired behaviour.

### MZ-WFu3w0Z5m_g#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:23
claim: The bigger the purchase the more logical the buyer, and the more expert the buyer the more you can talk features instead of benefits.
detail: An advanced car buyer asks about engine parts; a lottery winner buys the feeling. He uses gym equipment as his own example (show the strength curve). The seller must figure out which buyer they face.
