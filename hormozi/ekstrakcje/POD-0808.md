### POD-0808#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:08
claim: Plan with What, How, Who: every "what" must raise the number of customers, raise lifetime gross profit per customer, or cut risk.
detail: After a conference or workshop, move notes into a fresh page with only three to five items. Teach the three objectives to the team so they can see how decisions are made. Many customers with high profit but high risk make a business worth zero. Then pick the strategy (more, better, new), then name the owner.

### POD-0808#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:35
claim: Test a proposed project (for example a website redesign) by asking which objective it serves, the expected lift against the tested control, the time and the cost, then whether anything else with the same time and money would add more.
detail: Worked case: redesign expected to lift conversion 5%, 8 weeks, a $25,000 design firm. If something else in the same window and budget adds more than 5% to income, the redesign is a good idea but not the best one. Frame refusals as trade-offs, not as bad ideas.
numbers: 5% conversion lift; 8 weeks; $25,000.

### POD-0808#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-team | also: scaling-constraints | fw: More Better New | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:54
claim: Below about $3M revenue the answer is almost always "more"; at larger size compare the gap to benchmark and the odds of closing it against the effort of hiring.
detail: One outbound rep at five sales a month: doubling the reps would probably get you pretty close to doubling sales. With 20 reps closing 30%, lifting the team to 36% is a 20% gain, versus hiring six to keep four who reach KPI. If reps are below KPI, do not hire more; get the current team ready. Companies ping-pong between more and better.
numbers: 30% to 36% close = 20% lift; hire 6 expecting 4 to stick.
### POD-0808#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: More Better New | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:00
claim: Once something is well tested, more of it has the best risk-adjusted return, because changes to a tested control mostly make it worse (Jenga analogy).
detail: Changes carry a guaranteed cost but an uncertain upside. His team's homepage tests on the Skool site: 14 of 16 prominent split tests were worse than the control.
numbers: 14 of 16 split tests lost to the control.

### POD-0808#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:09:59
claim: By Hormozi's rough estimate, a change tends to cost about 20% of a function's execution while people adapt, so his litmus test is to enact a change only if he reasonably expects it to improve things by more than 20%.
detail: Layla's observation: a new sales script drops sales about 20% while reps learn it; the same for a customer success process. Constant change keeps several functions permanently 20% down. Accept that some things stay imperfect.
numbers: About 20% temporary execution drop per change (rough estimate).
### POD-0808#06 | EVOLUTION | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:47
claim: Hormozi's willingness to try new things has fallen with experience; he prefers fewer, bigger swings over everyday bets whose cost he now knows.
detail: Constant change also makes the team feel he never settles.

### POD-0808#07 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:17
claim: If a sound strategy (such as hiring more reps) fails to happen two quarters running, suspect a "who" problem: the owner lacks bandwidth or is not good enough.
detail: "We tried and it didn't work" is usually execution by the owner of the task rather than a wrong strategy. His tolerance for mediocrity has dropped; he assumes that in ten years everyone, himself included, will look inadequate, so he raises the bar for new hires.
