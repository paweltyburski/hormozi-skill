### YT-PTgGfV8Tf00#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Total output equals volume of activity times the leverage of that activity; once hours are maxed, only leverage can grow.
detail: Everyone has the same hours, and volume is a beginner measure. Waking early or sleeping little is irrelevant. A low-leverage task done many times can beat a high-leverage task done once, but time runs out first.

### YT-PTgGfV8Tf00#02 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:16
claim: To raise a CEO's leverage, read their calendar and org chart, hand low-leverage activities to others, and hire where no role exists.
detail: Acquisition.com's CEOs already work every hour, so the change is what they work on, not how much. If many activities belong to a type with no owner on the org chart, create that role.

### YT-PTgGfV8Tf00#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:49
claim: Hormozi thinks it is more valuable to know the roles that exist in the business (promote, sell, product, customer experience), which one you map to, and how you will evolve.
detail: Marketing, sales, product and customer success are not necessarily the same people; he has met brilliant marketers who are bad at sales. The bigger the enterprise, the more specialised the skill. Ask which role you map to and how you will evolve.
### YT-PTgGfV8Tf00#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:07
claim: The founder's path is player, then specialist executive, then coach who recruits top executives and builds a farm system and culture.
detail: The coach's value is the gap between what players do with and without them, so the CEO needs the highest return on time and can turn a business around through standards and values.
