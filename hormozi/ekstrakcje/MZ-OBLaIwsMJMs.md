### MZ-OBLaIwsMJMs#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Guessing what people mean, from a face or an exhale, creates imaginary problems; ask what they mean and translate.
detail: Most conversations end with people having understood different things, so asking the other person to define terms is his life cheat code.

### MZ-OBLaIwsMJMs#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:36
claim: In one-on-ones he asks the other person what action they want from him once the talk ends.
detail: It turns the speaker's intent into a desired behaviour change, strips out feelings and noise, and yields a concrete request such as saying please next time. If he cannot agree, he says so and asks for alternatives.

### MZ-OBLaIwsMJMs#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:22
claim: To judge how good someone is at a job, ask what metrics they track, then how their actions change those metrics.
detail: No metrics is a bad sign. If they name five of the seven he has in mind, that is fine; the follow-up tests whether they understand cause and effect between their work and the numbers.
numbers: Example: five named metrics against seven in his mind.
