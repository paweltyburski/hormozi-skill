### MZ-A8L7BJcf8Qc#01 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A Los Angeles salsa and bachata academy for adults ($400 for a 10-week program, $360K revenue, instructors as constraint) believed it could not scale after a weak Las Vegas pilot.
detail: Hormozi: it is not unscalable, just not scaled; the Vegas pilot failed because no one there knew him and he never advertised. It is the same model as a group-training gym. The bigger the problem solved (skilled instructors), the harder to copy.
numbers: $400 per 10 weeks (20 hours, about $20 per hour); revenue $360K.

### MZ-A8L7BJcf8Qc#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:59
claim: A brick-and-mortar owner whose first location is mediocre should not open a second; two mediocre sites split attention and both become poor.
detail: Often you must shut one and fix the model first. His bet: mispriced or overspending on cost, and overextended.

### MZ-A8L7BJcf8Qc#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: pricing | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:03
claim: Class economics: 25 people at $20 per class hour give $500 per hour, against $160 cost ($100 instructor plus $60 studio rent), a 68% gross margin versus his 80% floor for a service (90% preferred).
detail: Up to 45 fit a class.
numbers: 25 attendees average (max 45); $20 per attendee per hour; cost $160 per hour; gross margin 68%; target 80-90%.
### MZ-A8L7BJcf8Qc#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:04
claim: Owner economics: $360K revenue, $90K direct expenses, $100K overhead, $190K take-home that includes his own unpaid teaching, which is about $140K (roughly 38%) once about $40-50K of instructor pay is deducted.
detail: Hormozi calls about 38% fine for a service brick and mortar, and says the business simply needs to sell more.
numbers: $360K revenue; $190K take-home; about $140K after instructor pay; about 37.5% margin.

### MZ-A8L7BJcf8Qc#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:29
claim: He had not capped Los Angeles at all; 95% of customers are organic and referrals, no advertising has been tried, and adding marketing at about 25% of revenue would cut a 37.5% margin to 12.5%.
detail: The LA metro has about as many people as Australia. Raising gross margin to 80% turns 12.5% net into about 25%, and 85% into about 30%, so fix margin by filling classes.
numbers: 95% organic, 5-10% referrals; margin 37.5% minus 25% marketing = 12.5%; at 80% gross margin about 25%; at 85% about 30%.

### MZ-A8L7BJcf8Qc#06 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:49
claim: Run Instagram ads showing a phone recording of a full class dancing in unison, with a promise like not looking bad at your next dance occasion in 10 weeks, no price shown, sold by phone.
detail: Dance is highly visual. Add secondary benefits such as weight loss; send traffic to a simple name and phone or email page. He thinks the program could sell near $600.
numbers: Possible price about $600.
