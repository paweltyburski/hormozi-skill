### POD-0759#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:48
claim: There are only two ways to build a compounding asset: sell something people never stop buying, or build a network of people who never stop selling.
detail: Recurring subscriptions are not required; repeat purchase counts too. In a brokerage, five agents recruited each year grow to 25 by year five, so the agents are the real customer. Facebook-style businesses keep earning as long as the user stays.
numbers: 5 agents per year, 25 agents by year 5.

### POD-0759#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: Referral Rate minus Churn | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:56
claim: A business that keeps its customers needs half the sales velocity to match one that loses everyone each year, and keeps the saved acquisition cost as margin.
detail: Selling 100, then 200, 400 and 800 customers a year (all lost annually) is one case; the other sells 100, 100, then 200 and still holds 400 customers. Level one: lose no customers. Level two: customers bring customers (viral coefficient).
numbers: example: 400 customers needing 400 sales versus 200 sales in year three.

### POD-0759#03 | CASE | topic: content-brand | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:31
claim: His podcast held flat at about one million downloads a month for roughly 13-14 months, which he read as good enough to retain but not to be shared, so he did not buy ads.
detail: During the growth period he published direct audio content; later effort moved to YouTube and short video, where podcasts rank third tier. He ran an audio-first series as an experiment, with a promise to report if it failed. Advertising a channel that cannot retain listeners only buys more people for bad content.
numbers: about 1,000,000 downloads per month; flat for 13-14 months.

### POD-0759#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: content-brand | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:05
claim: Hormozi recommends not promoting content until it is good enough that you retain and multiply your audience; you get views to build an audience, not the reverse.
detail: He personally likes to advertise things he knows will earn him credit in three years. When retention and sharing are in place, growth becomes inevitable without further permission.
### POD-0759#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:34
claim: Media compounds through the audience it builds, and an audience that has recognised you rarely leaves.
detail: He avoided content for years believing it vanished, which he says kept him poor. Early on, treat 10 viewers like a 10-person lunch and learn; if they click your next piece, you retained them. If one in ten brings one more person each month, the audience compounds.
numbers: 1 in 10 audience members bringing 1 more per month; growth example of about 10% per month.

### POD-0759#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: lead-getters | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:13
claim: Content spreads when sharing gives the sharer social status, so make it good enough that people feel obligated to share it.
detail: Level one is a listener returning; level two is passing it to five people as a gift. Entertainment suits everyone while niche education has fewer people to give it to, for example content for dentists. Parallel to making an offer people feel stupid refusing.

### POD-0759#08 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:31
claim: Spend early time on retention and product quality before marketing; ads on a leaky product are exhausting and demoralising.
detail: At about $200,000 a day in ad spend he found the monthly restart exhausting. After buying a business he now works from the back (product, reviews, referrals, keeping customers) first, then applies marketing and sales. Fix the hole in the bucket while small, so growth needs fewer variables.
numbers: scale example $200,000 per day ad spend.
