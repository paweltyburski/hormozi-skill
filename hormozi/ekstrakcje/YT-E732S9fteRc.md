### YT-E732S9fteRc#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:32
claim: Big talent only chases big opportunities, so a small business struggles to hire top people and a big goal is partly a recruiting tool.
detail: Paraphrasing Blackstone's Stephen Schwarzman: his first filter is opportunity size, the second whether he can find 10-out-of-10 people to pursue it, and starting a small business is harder than starting Blackstone. He also said small and big goals are equally hard, so aim big. Starting niche is fine if the market can grow huge.

### YT-E732S9fteRc#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:19
claim: A 10-point talent scale: 10 is best in the world, 9 the best you can attract, 8 very good and self-starting, 7 functional but needs direction, 6 and below not wanted.
detail: Most businesses are run by sixes and below; if your team is not the best you can get, the opportunity is probably too small. He contrasts the "genius with a thousand slaves" model (do it yourself or underpay a handful of people) with an army of smarter people.
### YT-E732S9fteRc#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:56
claim: Billionaires ask whether customers will keep buying repeatedly; millionaires over-invest in promotion, except in one-off big-ticket categories like solar.
detail: People who made their first money by learning promotion assume more promotion is the answer. If purchase recurs, product quality multiplies the return on marketing. Where one buyer is sold once for 30 years, promotion matters more.
numbers: Solar example: one sale per customer, roughly one purchase per 30 years.

### YT-E732S9fteRc#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:08
claim: Prefer games where waiting wins: success becomes an inevitable result of time, even if you do not know when.
detail: Short-horizon thinkers (can I make payroll or rent this month, what is my next campaign) treat time as a liability. Billionaires ask whether time helps or hinders an opportunity and make decisions on a 10-year view. Brand and reputation compound the same way through integrity.
