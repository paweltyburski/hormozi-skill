### MZ--fzHdm7LGlk#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: retention-ltv | fw: Ascension Path | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: An indoor children's play park at $1.1M revenue (capacity $2M) has cheap play visits and nothing that moves visitors to birthday parties.
detail: 20,000 sq ft, about 36,000 play visits a year, play passes over half of revenue, positioned as "play, not fun". She wants to shift to parties. Hormozi: the two channels look nearly the same on unit math, so the missing piece is a sales motion linking them.
numbers: 36,000 visits per year; play customer LTV about $450 at CAC $15 (word of mouth); birthday party about $550.

### MZ--fzHdm7LGlk#02 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: retention-ltv | fw: Five Upsell Moments | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:19
claim: Seed the next sale at check-in and close it in person at check-out, with the parent present.
detail: Greeter asks everyone "are you here for a birthday party?", knowing they are not, so they learn it exists. At pick-up, while the child is tired and happy, ask about birthdays and other events, and book the date on the spot with information in hand.

### MZ--fzHdm7LGlk#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:52
claim: A teeth-whitening chain with low CAC quadrupled LTV by selling plans of repeat whitening instead of waiting for customers to call back.
numbers: LTV x4.
detail: Hormozi calls 4x LTV large enough to let a business scale; his firm is invested in the chain.

### MZ--fzHdm7LGlk#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:10
claim: Do not make customers want something different; keep the front end they come for and sell the extras with in-person education.
detail: A front end is the hardest thing to find. Give people what they want, then sell what they need. Doubling LTV gets this park to $2M; do one thing, not five.
