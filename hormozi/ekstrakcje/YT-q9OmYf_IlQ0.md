claim: What took you from one revenue level to the next usually will not take you to the next one, and you may be working on something that was once vital but no longer limits the business.
detail: A quick way to see what business you are in: study people 100 steps ahead, not one or two, since those just ahead may be lost too, while those at the top have figured out something you have not.

### YT-q9OmYf_IlQ0#02 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:45
claim: He thought gyms were a fitness-science business but they are a sales and marketing business: the largest chains have mechanised getting people through the door, and managers carry membership quotas.
detail: He signed a lease and had 30 days to find $5,000, which forced sales repetition. People rarely walk in on their own; they must be interrupted by an ad, a parking-lot offer, or a visit to their office with protein muffins and a semi-private-training pitch.

### YT-q9OmYf_IlQ0#05 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:02
claim: In his first software company he believed marketing and sales would be enough, but the real business was product, and an hourly-billed outsourced dev shop had misaligned incentives.
detail: Software sells easily but customers hate it and the seller if it does not solve the problem. The dev shop billed hourly, so it had incentive to stretch work and become hard to leave. It owned the code and saw his revenue grow, and fees rose as he made more money. Lesson: someone technical with aligned incentives must be in the business. He later sold the company to an owner with an in-house team.

### YT-q9OmYf_IlQ0#06 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: unit-economics | fw: Cost to Acquire Talent | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:37
claim: A plumbing business with unlimited demand was limited by finding plumbers, yet offered only a $500 referral bonus on about $300,000 of annual gross profit per plumber.
detail: In a service business the people are the product, and margin is what you charge for their work minus what you pay them. Compare cost of acquiring talent with lifetime gross profit per employee. Options: referral bonus (for example $30,000) paid after the referral is productive at month six so the referrer is invested; paid ads; headhunters, whose 90-day replace-for-free guarantee is typical. Everything the owner does that is not building a talent acquisition system is the constraint.
numbers: ~$300,000/year gross profit per plumber; $500 current bonus; example $30,000.

### YT-q9OmYf_IlQ0#07 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: unit-economics | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:12:48
claim: A very large YouTube media company rated its content 90/100 but its monetization 5/100, and was still spending on content efficiency instead of building a business model.
detail: Moving a weak area from 5 to 25 takes less effort than moving a strong one from 99 to 99.5. Owners keep solving the problem they enjoy rather than the one the business requires.
numbers: Content ~90/100 (self-rated), monetization ~5/100; reach was hundreds of millions of impressions a week.

### YT-q9OmYf_IlQ0#09 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:49
claim: Turn any complaint about the business into an "I don't know how" question, because a question has an answer.
detail: Examples: "no one can sell like me" becomes "I don't know how to get anyone to sell like me"; "there are no good plumbers" becomes "I don't know how to recruit, hire and train them"; "leads are crap" becomes "I don't know how to advertise for good leads". Also, "service is not scalable" really means you are not yet good enough.

detail: The founder was the product; when scaled with less exceptional people, quality falls. The fix is recruiting and training to a standard, not declaring service unscalable.

### YT-q9OmYf_IlQ0#11 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:18:22
claim: As an investor he first thought investing meant many discounted deals, but it is really about saying no at high velocity, since one great company outperforms many mediocre ones.
detail: In commodity businesses (selling cars) profit comes from buying cheaper. Businesses are not commodities, so the edge is screening out most deals; he says you only need one Facebook-scale winner.

### YT-q9OmYf_IlQ0#12 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:20:04
claim: To face a hard problem, price the problem: compute the enterprise value created by solving it and ask whether you would do it for a fraction.
detail: Example: a $10M-revenue business with $3M profit valued at about 5x is worth $15M. If solving the problem takes revenue to $20M and profit to $7-8M, at about 9x it is worth about $72M. The $57M difference makes a problem you would solve for $1M easy to accept. He tells portfolio founders what solving it will lock in.
numbers: $3M profit x 5 = $15M; $8M profit x 9 = $72M; difference $57M; hypothetical.

### YT-q9OmYf_IlQ0#13 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:26
claim: Separate features (inherent to the business model) from bugs (fixable problems); do not burn effort trying to fix a feature, build around it.
detail: Example: gym churn cannot be solved because it is a human problem, so build a marketing machine that never runs out of people; some lapsed members return after two or three years. Sticky businesses (banking, internet providers) have different problems. Every business has both, and a CFO told him the grass looks greener because nobody sees the fertiliser.

### YT-q9OmYf_IlQ0#14 | EXAMPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: operator-mindset | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:30
claim: Entrepreneurs who restart a new business whenever they hit their wall pass the early levels faster each time, then crash at the same level again.

### YT-q9OmYf_IlQ0#15 | BENCHMARK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:31:37
claim: In his opinion, every business has a nearby version that can reach $100M of enterprise value, which for a restaurant requires only about $10M of cumulative profit over a lifetime.

### YT-q9OmYf_IlQ0#16 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:33:56
claim: When choosing between two paths, name the big hairy problem of each, then ask which problem you would rather have and which you are better equipped to solve today.

### YT-q9OmYf_IlQ0#18 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:38:05
claim: Distinguish good hard (an assumption you believe from first principles and iterate on) from bad hard (an assumption proven false), and write down your need-to-believes before a big investment.
detail: Push while the assumptions hold; pivot when data disproves one. Example: running TikTok ads to accountants rests on "accountants are there and can be reached profitably", so poor first results mean iterate on messaging and targeting. Counter-example: a software tool for newspapers failed because the need-to-believe (newspapers survive) proved false. Normal trades such as plumbing, HVAC, lawn care and accounting rarely have a false need-to-believe, so failing to scale them is a "you" problem.

### YT-q9OmYf_IlQ0#19 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:40:30
claim: How long you keep trying is frustration tolerance shaped by past rewards after repeated attempts; the only requirement is not running out of money while you try.
detail: Salespeople follow up a fifth time if they were rewarded for that before (like a dog that begs until fed). Having needed 100 iterations to make an ad campaign work, he is confident that if buyers exist on a platform and can be reached profitably, it will work eventually. Do not conclude failure after the first expensive month.
numbers: ~100 iterations on some ad campaigns.
