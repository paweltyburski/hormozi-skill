### MZ-uTKBbnBSB1s#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: offers, verticals | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: In a commoditized business, pick the one of three vectors to win on: lower risk, more speed, or more ease.
detail: Risk is reputation or guarantees. Speed means faster work or response. Ease means less friction, such as dropping off the keys. Discovery with the owner decides which vector fits; commoditization happens a lot in home services.
### MZ-uTKBbnBSB1s#02 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Conditional Guarantee | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:55
claim: A time-and-budget guarantee turns lower competing quotes into a non-comparison.
detail: Raise price 20%, then answer cheaper quotes with the question whether they guarantee time and cost. Refund the 20% margin if missed, and void it on client changes, so you keep your price rise in all cases.
numbers: Price +20%; refund 20% of the deal.
applies_to: Home services.

### MZ-uTKBbnBSB1s#03 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv, verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:43
claim: Sell a cheap annual maintenance plan that covers repairs up to twice its price.
detail: For turf, a plan priced about $200 a year covers up to $500 of repairs, which are marked up anyway. Pitch it as doubling the turf's life and protecting resale value. Its low price makes yes easy and sets up a return visit and a next sale.
numbers: Example: $200 a year plan, covers up to $500 repairs; or $500 a year plan.

### MZ-uTKBbnBSB1s#04 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:25
claim: Offer the maintenance plan free in exchange for a five-star review, with the crew getting a spiff.
detail: Have the salesperson say ownership runs the special and that the workers receive a bonus for the review. Said by a non-owner about the crew, it is far more persuasive.

### MZ-uTKBbnBSB1s#05 | FRAMEWORK | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops, retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:06:51
claim: Career pathing uses two parallel columns, titles with perks and pay, advanced at set milestones.
detail: Example editor path: junior at $10 an hour, raise to $11 after 30 days, promotion to editor after a milestone, raise after 100 videos and six months, then senior. Roles tripled into junior, regular and senior steps give many progressions and keep staff invested.
numbers: $10 to $11 an hour after 30 days (illustrative); 100 videos and six months; six roles give 12 progressions.

### MZ-uTKBbnBSB1s#06 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: verticals, retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:01
claim: A nail salon chain kept technicians about four years instead of six months through levelled titles and in-house certifications.
detail: Each level required a mini certification allowing costlier procedures and higher billable rates, so staff always felt progress. Technicians without a high school diploma earned about $70,000 a year. He nearly bought the chain.
numbers: Industry tenure about 6 months vs about 4 years; $70,000 a year pay.

### MZ-uTKBbnBSB1s#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:49
claim: Reach scale zero (owner doing nothing in the business) before opening a second location.
detail: Most of the time, owners who divert their time to a second site see the first one decline. In his hypothetical example revenue falls 20% at the original and margin sits at 25% while overhead doubles. Expand when you have surplus money and time.
numbers: Example: original revenue falls 20%, margin 25%, overhead doubled.
