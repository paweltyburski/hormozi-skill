### YT-h9UyQiLjSHg#02 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:12
claim: For mid-priced menus, end prices in .99 instead of round numbers; round prices fit luxury places whose main items cost roughly $29-$50.
detail: Buyers judge price mostly by the leading digit, so $16.99 reads like $16 and he expects no fall in orders. On a $16 average item that is ~6% more revenue, which for a 19%-margin restaurant means about 30% more profit. He presents it as the lowest-cost change: reprint the menu.

### YT-h9UyQiLjSHg#03 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:37
claim: When you turn people away at peak times, hand out a separate peak (weekend) menu priced about $2 higher per dish.
detail: Hormozi likens it to surge pricing: charge more when busy, less when quiet, which also nudges demand toward off-peak days. Customers already accept lunch versus dinner menus with different prices, so a distinct weekend menu does not feel like a markup.

### YT-h9UyQiLjSHg#04 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:07
claim: Lay out any menu so the highest prices come first, each section is ordered by gross profit, and one highlighted "first visit, don't miss" item steers undecided buyers.
detail: Three priorities he balances: price anchoring (most expensive at the top, as in wine-list studies), gross margin per item (two $16 dishes may cost $1 or $6 to make), and the items that bring people back. Ideally the most profitable dishes are also the most loved. He applies the same logic to service menus such as a mechanic's.

### YT-h9UyQiLjSHg#05 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: product-delivery | fw: Crazy Eight | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:39
claim: Prompt a cross-sell in print: "pairs well with" under each dish, and one rotating special on a table tent that stays on the table.
detail: One item only, a clear image and a short story (the team tastes several drinks weekly and picks the winner), styled to match an upscale room. Make it easy for the customer to say yes and hard for staff to forget. On this business, moving alcohol from 1% to 6% of revenue would add about a quarter to profit; one in ten tables is enough.

### YT-h9UyQiLjSHg#06 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:27:32
claim: Identify first-time customers visibly to staff, end their visit with a surprise free item, and hand a manager's card promising another free item next visit.
detail: Ask if it is the first visit and mark the table (coloured napkin or coaster) so everyone knows. Because people remember the peak and the end of an experience, make the ending special with the cheapest dessert. A manager's handwritten business card feels like a personal gift, unlike a coupon; a photo of it on a phone works too, and sharing it with friends is welcome.

### YT-h9UyQiLjSHg#07 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:29:03
claim: Ask for reviews at the moment the check arrives, with an immediate reward (a drink taken off per reviewer) or a manager mentioning the server's bonus for five-star reviews.
detail: Rewards promised for a future visit do not work; it must be immediate. Each person at the table can review, so one table can yield several. Alternative without giveaways: the manager says the server earns a small bonus above 10 five-star reviews a month. Volume matters: he would pick a 4.7 with 13,000 reviews over a 5.0 with 55, and local buyers check reviews before going out.

### YT-h9UyQiLjSHg#08 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:33:08
claim: Set a free-item threshold just above the current average order (e.g. $80 weekdays, $100 weekends when the average is ~$60) to lift order value.
detail: Borrowed from e-commerce free-gift and free-shipping thresholds. He prefers it for takeout, phone and online ordering, where it does not feel awkward and does not add to what servers must remember.

### YT-h9UyQiLjSHg#09 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: offers | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:39:08
claim: If you run a punch card, give a running start (some punches pre-filled) and build loyalty perks from value levers, such as priority when there is a wait.
detail: Hormozi cites studies that pre-punched cards are completed two to three times as often as shorter blank cards with the same punches left. He is split on loyalty programmes for restaurants because good food is the real loyalty driver, while for some businesses (a med spa) the programme is the business. Sign-up also captures the lead source.

### YT-h9UyQiLjSHg#10 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:41:37
claim: Train front-line staff as a few if-then behaviour rules, drilled as short role-play skits before each shift, one skit a day, many repetitions.
detail: Each rule: when X happens, do Y (first-timer greeting and marker, drink special pitch, pairing prompt, review or comeback ask at the check). Pairs act it out in turns, about 20 reps an hour, 5-10 per person in a 20-30 minute pre-shift huddle. Translate each ask into what the server earns in tips. Add a leaderboard for most drink sales, with $50-$100 or first pick of shifts. He cites a McKinsey figure that about one in three strategies succeed, with culture the difference.

### YT-h9UyQiLjSHg#11 | SCRIPT | topic: sales-process | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:43:11
claim: Server upsell structure: after each order item, ask two or three short assumptive questions (extra side, paired drink) using social proof.
detail: Pattern: confirm the choice, offer an add-on with "a lot of people do", then the pairing phrased as "do you want to go ahead and take one of those?". Either-or choices and "most people here get" guide first-timers, which he frames as reducing risk so they get the best experience. A table of five is five small sales exchanges.

### YT-h9UyQiLjSHg#12 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:46:19
claim: Before opening a new location, host a VIP influencer night, free or at cost, in exchange for posts and the right to reuse their videos as ads.
detail: Invite people with large local reach (influencers, forum owners, other business owners who see many customers) because each can tell a hundred people, where an ordinary guest tells two or three. Give a traceable offer (free dessert or drink) so visits can be attributed, then run ads with their faces.

### YT-h9UyQiLjSHg#13 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: scaling-constraints | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:52
claim: Before adding locations, check the unit is "nailed": cost to open, time to profit, time to pay back, opening promotion, and how to hire and build culture at the new site.
detail: Acquisition.com's motto is nail it, then scale it: expanding before the core model is solid may speed up site two but slows site ten. Especially in staff-heavy businesses such as restaurants these questions decide whether the model can be copied.
