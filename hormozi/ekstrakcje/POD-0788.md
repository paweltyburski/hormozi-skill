### POD-0788#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:54
claim: A buyer who does not know your brand judges you by how they have been treated by businesses like yours.
detail: Four-cow analogy: an unmarked cow, a branded cow with an unknown owner, one owned by someone you hate, one owned by someone you love. An unknown brand is treated by the category's reputation, and information marketing has a poor one. A brand known and loved is handled with care.

### POD-0788#02 | DEFINITION | topic: strategy-positioning | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:47
claim: Information changes nothing; education aims to change behaviour, and chasing money alone drifts into information.
detail: Hormozi separates the two. Entering only for money leads to bigger promises and weaker results. Intent cannot be measured, but whether you lie can, and lying is why marketers are disliked.

### POD-0788#03 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:06:07
claim: Burn You Twice close: a prior bad purchase hurts again when it stops you buying the right solution.
detail: Used when a buyer at the finish line recalls a purchase that failed. Steps: 1) say the only thing worse than one bad investment is letting it block a good one, since you are burned twice; 2) tie it to a metaphor, such as swearing off dating after a school romance, which costs you the partner you would later meet.

### POD-0788#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Scarcity | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:08
claim: Earn the right to charge by filling your calendar for free, which creates real scarcity that lets price rise.
detail: Your time has no value until demand exists. Serve until you cannot take more, supply shrinks, word of mouth hits a small supply, price rises. Where demand is weak, the reverse happens: lower price, less supply left. Every product line he starts begins free to see whether it is any good.

### POD-0788#05 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:09
claim: Replace free clients with paying ones one at a time: pay or give up the slot.
detail: When a new buyer appears, tell a free client the slot is wanted by someone paying, and ask them to pay or leave; then take the next in line. Repeat, replacing a $100 payer with a $200 payer as demand grows.
numbers: Example ladder: free, then $100/month, then $200/month.

### POD-0788#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:56
claim: A solo expert scales by raising price or adding capacity (hire, or go one-to-many); his bet is that most would be better served bouncing between price rises and fractionalised versions.
detail: Hiring brings margin on others' time but needs operational skill. Fractionalised delivery (one to four, six or eight) needs fewer operational chops. The approach he outlined brings more key-man risk and keeps the founder involved and working; fine for owners who want income rather than a sellable asset.
applies_to: Owner-operated expert businesses.
### POD-0788#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:16:09
claim: Training a hire over a weekend to deliver a premium service waters it down; a smaller group with the same quality is better.
detail: Milk analogy. Diluting one glass across three tastes bad; pouring the same amount into three shot glasses keeps it strong. If you can teach delivery in a day, either you are not that good or the hire is far worse. Keep improving so each fractional version stays valuable.

### POD-0788#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: retention-ltv | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:46
claim: Never blame customers for failed promises; meet them where they are.
detail: Google story: pitching to Excite, the founders showed Google's university search beat Excite's; the Excite executive said Excite's would match with a better query. Google took it as its edge: the user is never wrong. Reduce risk, time and effort, the lower half of the value equation.

### POD-0788#09 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: operator-mindset | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:57
claim: Test for honesty: if your grandmother or someone you care about were in your marketplace, would you sell it to them?
detail: A tool like a community platform amplifies your ability to help or hurt people. If your real skill is jar pickling, make the group free and sell jars, or charge $5-$8 a month and accept that income. He warns against pretending to have skills you lack in order to charge more.
numbers: Group priced $5-$8/month if skills match.

### POD-0788#11 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: sales-process | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:27:15
claim: A single success story from 100 customers is chance, shown by the coin-flip mailing scam.
detail: Mail 1,000 people, half told heads, half tails, halve the list each flip; after three flips 125 people saw three correct calls. Among 100 people trying to earn online, one will land in the top 1% with no help. Credit success correctly instead of selling that one story.
numbers: 1,000 to 500 to 250 to 125 recipients, three correct "calls".

### POD-0788#12 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: retention-ltv | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:28:54
claim: Track the average result, such as the percentage who reach the first milestone, and set price and ads from it.
detail: Report the share who make a dollar, finish a first painting or land a trick. Example: 10% do it in a $5 programme, one in three in the advanced one. School's ads quote the median instead of a promise. If your price exceeds average value created, adjust it. Tracking itself improves results ("if you don't track, you don't care").
numbers: Median shown in School ads: $1,360; example hit rates 1 in 10 vs 1 in 3.

### POD-0788#13 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:29:54
claim: The 80-20 of compliance: promise nothing, state what has happened and what you will do.
detail: Pattern: "I built three companies to $100M+ enterprise value; founders we work with average a 13x return on equity; I do not promise this for you." Facts about past results, a plan, and the prospect's choice, instead of a guarantee you cannot control.
numbers: Three companies at $100M+ enterprise value; founders' average 13x return on equity.

### POD-0788#14 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:48
claim: Content creators run better communities than direct-response marketers because their reputation compounds.
detail: Creators built an audience by keeping promises over time and care about product quality; ads-first people can always return to the nameless masses. A free community builds goodwill and is a legitimate way to make money.
