claim: Emotion lowers the action threshold, but a rational foundation is what keeps the buyer from regretting the sale days later.
detail: Prospects already want to buy emotionally; the salesperson's job is to give their logical brain a justification. Buyers who purchase only on emotion later phone to cancel, like relationships built without logic. More senior buyers lean toward logic-first decisions.

detail: Selling happens before the ask and closing after it; prospects struggle because they cannot decide without help. He teaches new reps first to expect no and not be surprised.

### YT-NcD2t9qt-fM#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: Anchor Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:29
claim: If quoting the price does not draw a gasp, it is not high enough, and a high first number lets you walk down.
detail: Say the larger number first; after $100,000 a $1,000 figure feels like a rounding error, while after $10 it feels large. Train reps to be comfortable with both the no and the gasp.
numbers: Anchor example: $100,000 then $1,000, versus $10 then $1,000.

### YT-NcD2t9qt-fM#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:32

detail: Selling from the back foot: you stay the same either way, the decision changes their life. Measure yourself on whether you helped, and keep the prospect, not the sale, as the focus. People pay the person who pays them attention.

### YT-NcD2t9qt-fM#10 | DEFINITION | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:21:44
claim: An obstacle arises before the ask and is a disagreement with their framing; after the ask the same thing is an objection, and obstacles are easier to clear.
detail: Example obstacle: "I just want more information." Respond by asking whether they hop on many calls for information or have a problem to solve. Before the sale is on the table you can challenge beliefs safely; after soliciting, everything is objection handling. For "I need to think about it", ask what exactly they will think about.

### YT-NcD2t9qt-fM#12 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: - | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:58
claim: Logical close on "it won't work for me": ask how much proof it would take to make disbelief more unreasonable than belief.
detail: Ask how many success stories they would need to see (ten, fifty), then offer a thousand and let them say when to stop. The excuse then falls away and the real concern surfaces.

### YT-NcD2t9qt-fM#13 | FRAMEWORK | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: Three Core Beliefs | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:28:12
claim: Onion of blame: prospects blame circumstances first, then other people, finally themselves, so the excuse shows how close you are.
detail: The five excuses run from outer to core: time, money, fit, authority, avoidance. "I need to think about it" means you are talking to the decision maker. He derived the three sources from Albert Ellis's cognitive therapy after comparing rival objection lists.

### YT-NcD2t9qt-fM#16 | DEFINITION | topic: objections-closing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:35:44

detail: Examples: saving once rich, paying for a money-making programme once they have money. Answer with empathy ("I was stuck like this too") and walk them through it without attacking.
