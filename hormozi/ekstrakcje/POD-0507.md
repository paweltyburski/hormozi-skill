### POD-0507#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: sales-process | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:38
claim: Reciprocity example: restaurant tips rose 3% with one mint, 14% with two, and 23% when a second mint was placed after turning away.
detail: A small unexpected gift, given in a personalised way, outperforms a standard one. He argues cheap gifts like this are everywhere in business at a few dollars a month. The effect is tied to trust and cooperation in sales.
numbers: tips +3% (one mint), +14% (two mints), +23% (second mint after turning around).

### POD-0507#04 | CASE | topic: lead-getters | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:53
claim: To get a celebrity's attention while promoting his book, Hormozi gave $1M to the celebrity's charity and then met him several times.
detail: Requests and gifts to the person did nothing; giving to a cause the person cares about did. The gift must be proportional to the person: as a child he handed out gum in class.

### POD-0507#05 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:51
claim: Use foot-in-the-door: get a small public commitment first, remind them of it, then ask for a larger step; the commitment must be voluntary.
detail: In a study, residents who displayed a small "drive safely" card later accepted a big yard sign far more often than a cold group. Questions, not pressure, get people to label themselves.

### POD-0507#06 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:41
claim: In discovery, get the prospect to agree to a positive label ("are you the type of person who takes action and invests in themselves?") and call it back at the close.
detail: Steps: ask questions; have them state a label voluntarily; reaffirm it; at the close ask them to act consistently with it. Refusing makes them either defend their self-image or admit they are not that type.

### POD-0507#07 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: sales-process | fw: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:56
claim: Frame every five-star review in the lobby and display before-and-after photos, captured at the start of each customer's journey.
detail: He printed and framed all online reviews floor to ceiling in his gym, half the wall before-and-afters. Capture the "before" at onboarding and ask for the "after" later; show them early in the sales cycle.

### POD-0507#08 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:13
claim: Against "you can't help me", ask how much proof they would need and when their requirement becomes more unreasonable than the proof shown.
detail: Said while pointing to the wall of results. It targets the belief that the seller is good enough.

### POD-0507#09 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:13

detail: If a customer will not give a testimonial, say you did not know you had done so poorly, then ask what would need to change. Either you get an early warning or you get the testimonial.

### POD-0507#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: AAA | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:11
claim: Liking comes from attractiveness, similarity and compliments, and genuine flattery has no ceiling, even when the person knows the compliment is paid for.
detail: A study found people still liked those hired to compliment them. Use acknowledge, compliment, ask to show you listen. Send a short pre-call questionnaire to find similarities faster. He kept his own mood high with a cartwheel before handshakes and later a mini trampoline during 20+ consults a day.
numbers: 20+ in-person consults a day.

### POD-0507#11 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: sales-team | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:44
claim: Build authority with credentials and track record, and transfer your authority to staff, such as by certifying all trainers two or three times.
detail: He paid for recognised certifications for trainers and also issued internal ones, so asked about credentials he could say all were double or triple certified. He notes US fitness certifications are not strictly accredited. People believe authority with fewer filters.
numbers: trainers double or triple certified.

### POD-0507#12 | CASE | topic: promotions-launches | also: sales-team | fw: Scarcity | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:22:31
claim: During three-week on-site turnarounds with eight salespeople, about two thirds of sales came in the last third of the period, with the same lead flow.
detail: Time limits pushed people to sign at the end. Where capacity was limited to 80 members, sales also sped up as the remaining spots shrank.
numbers: 21 days; 8 salespeople; two thirds of sales in last third; capacity 80.

### POD-0507#13 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: pricing | fw: Scarcity | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:23
claim: Use loss language ("don't miss out") and state real limits; luxury brands hide shipment quantities and ration entry to stoke scarcity.
detail: People weigh losses more than equal gains. A merch drop with 100 signed hats and never reprinted beats an ordinary hat listing. Scarcity must be real, and showing remaining units increases desire.
numbers: 100 signed hats example.
