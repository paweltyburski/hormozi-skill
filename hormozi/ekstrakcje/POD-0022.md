### POD-0022#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:49
claim: When a prospect says they need to think, ask "what would you need to see to believe this will work for you?"
detail: Once value is agreed, the second question is belief. Do not accept a vague stall; ask for the proof they need, and the answer sets what conviction you must supply.

### POD-0022#02 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: content-brand | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:37
claim: Manufacture the ideal proof (a prospect who watched ten friends succeed) by choreographing exposure to real customers before the appointment.
detail: Give leads a customer Facebook group to browse, then send a testimonial page or video and make watching it mandatory before they come in, so they arrive pre-framed.

### POD-0022#03 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: content-brand | fw: Sales Aids | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:23
claim: Make the testimonial page varied: men and women, toning and large weight loss, short and long timeframes, and one who kept it off two years.
detail: A single pre-watch sizzle reel is fine if you can produce it, otherwise a series of clips.
numbers: 2 years kept off (example).

### POD-0022#04 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:12
claim: Cover the sales room walls floor to ceiling with testimonials so doubts about reaching the goal are answered by the room.
detail: His gym licensees do this; you cannot boost yourself, but third-party proof can. He says most people are sold once they see about 200 people like them succeed.
numbers: about 200 testimonials at the point most people are sold.

### POD-0022#05 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:19
claim: After belief in the programme, ask what reason would stop them personally, then answer it with stories of similar people.
detail: Steps: 1. ask what would stop them; 2. give stories of others in the same situation (a mother who hired a nanny two days a week, one who committed to 5am); 3. ask "is that the only reason?"; 4. repeat for each. Limiting beliefs appear in B2B too.

### POD-0022#06 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:34
claim: Answer "I never stick with anything" by treating motivation as results plus ease, and show how easy the system is.
detail: He says his system is colour coded and uses your hand, so non-English speakers and 12-year-olds have done it, then checks with light humour that they can read and are over 12.
numbers: example: 12-year-olds can do it.

### POD-0022#07 | EXAMPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:12
claim: To the "my husband does not support me" objection, tell of a client whose husband forbade it, who joined anyway and was later apologised to.
detail: Then say change must come from within: change your story and your life changes.
