claim: A tech boot camp wants an attractive affiliate offer without exceeding its CAC.
detail: Lead sources are already diverse. The owner finds a 20% commission uninteresting to partners.
numbers: Price $12,000; CAC ~$1,200 (10%); revenue heading to ~$5M this year; ~20% of leads from SEO, ~20% affiliates, ~20% student referrals.

### MZ-jBfIY0nTBqc#02 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:54
claim: Give affiliates a narrow piece of the core product as a lead magnet they can sell at any price and keep all the money.
detail: Like a bookkeeper peeling off one narrow problem. For a boot camp, one phase of the process such as a resume review. Affiliates sell it or bundle it; it feeds leads into the main offer. Hormozi dislikes plain 20% deals.

### MZ-jBfIY0nTBqc#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:50
claim: Paying referrers in hours of your own service can cost far less than paying a platform per lead.
detail: A gym giving two free massages to every sign-up costs about $50 in labour per showed customer, versus $100-$200 per show on Facebook.
numbers: Masseuse cost $25 an hour; about $50 per showed lead vs $100-$200 on ads.
