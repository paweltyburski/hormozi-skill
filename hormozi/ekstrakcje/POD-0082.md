### POD-0082#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:46
claim: The most members a recurring base can ever reach equals monthly new recurring signups divided by monthly attrition rate.
detail: He calls it the pie equation. The top is signups into recurring billing only (referrals plus cold traffic), not front-end challenge or low-barrier buyers. The bottom is monthly attrition. If nothing changes, the base converges on this ceiling, so the future is predictable and decisions can be made without emotion.
numbers: Example: 2 referral + 8 cold signups per month at 10% attrition gives a ceiling of 100 recurring members.

### POD-0082#02 | DEFINITION | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:32
claim: Attrition is the share of last month's billed members who were not billed again this month; new signups never enter the calculation.
detail: A common error is netting signups against losses (100 members, 90 stay, 12 join, "I grew, so no attrition"). Attrition is measured only on the prior month's cohort, so growth from new signups cannot hide it.
numbers: 100 billed last month, 90 billed again = 10% attrition, even if 12 new people joined.

### POD-0082#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:18
claim: Owner-operated micro gyms average about 10% monthly attrition on recurring billing and roughly 10 new recurring signups a month, so most sit near 100 members.
detail: The average gym he meets is stuck around 100 recurring members, which matches the ceiling formula. The plateau is arithmetic, not a marketing mystery.
numbers: 10% monthly attrition, about 10 signups per month (about 2 referral, 8 cold), ceiling about 100 members.
applies_to: Micro gyms and other owner-operated recurring-billing businesses

### POD-0082#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:44
claim: Growth toward the ceiling is front-loaded and slows sharply as the base grows, because attrition takes a bigger absolute bite each month.
detail: Start at 100 members, add 30 a month, 10% attrition against a 300 ceiling. Month one nets +20, month two +18, month three +16. Most of the visible growth lands in about the first six months; near 250 members, 30 added minus 25 lost nets only +5, then +4, +3 and so on.
numbers: Ceiling 300 (30 signups / 10%). Net gains +20, +18, +16. At 250 members: 10% loss is 25 per month.

### POD-0082#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: promotions-launches | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:58
claim: Lever one is more signups: a front-end programme feeding recurring billing lifts the ceiling in proportion to signups.
detail: Sign up 10 people a week (40 a month) into a defined-length front-end programme and keep 75% of them on recurring, which he says is the average for gyms in his programme. That gives 30 recurring signups a month, so at unchanged 10% attrition the ceiling moves from 100 to 300.
numbers: 40 front-end per month x 75% retained = 30 recurring signups; ceiling 300.

### POD-0082#06 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:02
claim: Lever two is a simple contact routine that he says cuts attrition from 10% to 5%.
detail: Staff reach out to every member about every two weeks (a short text pulse check twice a month is enough), send a handwritten card monthly, and track attendance so anyone absent by day three of the week gets a call. With the same 10 signups a month, halving attrition doubles the ceiling. It costs effort and a system, not money.
numbers: 10% to 5% attrition: ceiling 100 to 200 at 10 signups/month. Contact: about every 2 weeks, card monthly, call by day 3 of the week.

### POD-0082#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:14
claim: The lower attrition goes, the more each point is worth, because the ceiling is signups divided by a shrinking number.
detail: At 10 signups a month, 10% attrition caps at 100, 5% at 200, 3% at about 333. His Legacy programme signs about 45 a month: at 1% churn the ceiling would be 4,500, at 10% only 450 (he already had 500 members). So a five-point cut is not a 5% gain but a doubling.
numbers: 10 signups/month: 10% = 100, 5% = 200, 3% = 333. 45 signups/month: 1% = 4,500 vs 10% = 450.

### POD-0082#08 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:12:03
claim: Combining both levers (double signups, halve attrition) quadruples the ceiling, and client-financed acquisition is what removes the marketing-budget limit on signups.
detail: Going from 10 to 20 recurring signups a month and from 10% to 5% attrition moves the ceiling from 100 to 400. He says recovering acquisition cost at the sale makes sales staff, not ad budget, the bottleneck. 10% to 5% is within reach of anyone who talks to every member; below 3% needs deliberate systems, good staff and training.
numbers: 20 signups / 5% = 400 members, 4x the 100 baseline.

### POD-0082#09 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:11:18
claim: A gym owner in his programme was adding about 100 recurring members a month at under 2.5% churn.
detail: Joey Huber had about 600 recurring members and, at that growth rate, expected 1,000 within six months. Hormozi attributes it to delivery systems at scale: regular outreach to everyone, handwritten cards and a monthly event.
numbers: about 600 members, about +100 per month, churn under 2.5%, target 1,000 in six months.
