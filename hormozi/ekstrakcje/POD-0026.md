### POD-0026#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: A service business is a garden, not a machine: it can run without you for about 30 days, 90 days is a stretch, and a year is unrealistic for an owner-operated service gym.
detail: Planting and watering lets it grow without constant presence, but you still weed, market and replace seeds, meaning employees and clients need ongoing attention. He knows nobody with truly passive income, including in real estate. The exception he names is a facility-usage gym, which is a different model from a service-led micro gym.
numbers: 30 days away: doable; 90 days: a stretch; 1 year: he knows of none for an owner-operated service gym.
applies_to: Owner-operated micro gyms selling service.

### POD-0026#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: retention-ltv | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:03
claim: Hormozi says that if he thinks about a client a lot and gets a turning feeling in his stomach, he confronts them; if the feeling doesn't go away, he removes them if it's within his control.
detail: His answer to when to fire a member or client is: as soon as you think about them a lot. First step is confronting them, and removal follows only if the feeling does not go away, and only if it is within his control.
### POD-0026#03 | CASE | topic: product-delivery | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Sales to Fulfillment Continuum | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:17
claim: Gym Launch moved from doing the selling for gyms (done for you) to teaching owners to do it (done with you), because scaling a sales team was slow and margins were thin.
detail: With six salespeople the service sold out six months ahead within two weeks, so he was telling prospects the first slot was seven months away. Adding reps meant travel, hotels, rental cars, insurance and fronting ad money, so revenue was large but margin low. A second reason: clients were resentful. Owners earning small incomes saw a peer make far more from the same help and disliked him anyway. Teaching also scaled faster and with more impact.
numbers: Six salespeople, sold out six months in two weeks; typical gym owner take-home about $20,000 a year; one client made $50,000 in 21 days.

### POD-0026#04 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:06
claim: Judge a coach or service employee by how often customers publicly praise them and by zero complaints.
detail: He expects near-daily posts about the employee on social media, customers unprompted telling him an employee deserves a raise, and no complaints. Cameras and good rapport with customers fill the gaps.
numbers: Target: praise posts almost daily; complaints: zero.

### POD-0026#05 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:12
claim: Tell customers you promise exceptional service and ask them to report anything less, so they police employees for you.
detail: Since you cannot be everywhere, set the expectation up front and make it easy for people to say so, giving you feedback on staff you cannot watch.

### POD-0026#06 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:03
claim: Vet who you pay to learn from with three checks: what they have done, whether it was for people like you, and whether they took those people where you want to go.
detail: He pays practitioners thousands of dollars an hour for two hours showing how they actually do something. In his first three years he reinvested about three quarters of income in learning.
numbers: About 75% of income spent on learning for three years.
