### YT-A4L3byKcYQg#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Once a niche business reaches about $1M-$10M, the market can be enlarged in three ways: go up-market, go adjacent, or go down-market.
detail: A buyer or banker will ask about the total addressable market early, since a small one is a hidden cap. Enlarging it changes your delivery and prospects, so do it with care. Think of the customer as one unit with people below it and organisations above it.
numbers: Relevant at roughly $1M, $3M or $10M in revenue.

### YT-A4L3byKcYQg#02 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: lead-getters | fw: Leverage | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Going up-market means serving the organisations above your current customers (associations, franchises, licensors), which sells hundreds of clients at once and raises contract size.
detail: Example: from helping salon owners to serving whoever serves salons. It needs experience, demonstrated results and evidence your system works. He calls it one of the most profitable and least tried paths because owners fear it.

### YT-A4L3byKcYQg#03 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:08
claim: Going adjacent means carving a neighbouring segment where your best practices still apply, such as hair salons to nail or lash salons, or to the physical products those businesses buy.
detail: Selling physical products or going direct to consumer is a bigger change of business nature. If it triples the customers you can market to, it triples potential sales. It matters most when you hit a bottleneck.

### YT-A4L3byKcYQg#04 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:09
claim: Going down-market means serving the people below your customer, typically at one fifth to one tenth of your price, while up-market is typically five to ten times your price.
detail: Down-market suits an offer such as a certification that lets someone service more customers or charge more. Hormozi tried it and disliked it.
numbers: Up-market price about 5-10x; down-market about 1/5 to 1/10.

### YT-A4L3byKcYQg#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: verticals | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:51
claim: For a gym coaching company, down-market is personal trainers, up-market is franchisors, and online fitness is adjacent and partly down-market.
detail: Many online fitness businesses are smaller than gyms because they are often run by business opportunity seekers.

### YT-A4L3byKcYQg#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:59
claim: A "3.5" option is to turn an improvement offer into a business opportunity offer, which widens appeal but lowers prospect quality, as quality falls at each step down the ladder.
detail: Business opportunity means helping people leave a job or vehicle they dislike for something new with a low barrier to entry.

### YT-A4L3byKcYQg#07 | DEFINITION | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:01
claim: Vertical integration (owning every step of a supply chain) is the position that creates large enterprise value, but each step adds operational complexity.
detail: Gym example: certify trainers, help them open a gym, help it grow, then advise on franchising; may also own processing, CRMs or physical products sold through the base.

### YT-A4L3byKcYQg#08 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:10
claim: Do not mistake a bottleneck on one channel for a limit on the market; first add another channel or raise lifetime gross profit per customer.
detail: Example: in a $60B health and beauty space, a $4M-a-year business has not run out of market. Raising back-end value lets you spend more on the same channel.
numbers: $60B space vs $4M a year revenue.

### YT-A4L3byKcYQg#09 | EVOLUTION | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: updates | ts: 00:08:52
claim: When moving markets, address pricing accordingly: going up-market you cannot just charge twice as much because there are far fewer customers who will expect much more, and going down-market he adjusted his price but kept the service the same.
detail: His admitted mistake was adjusting price for the lower market while keeping the same time and effort per client, which made the service unviable.
