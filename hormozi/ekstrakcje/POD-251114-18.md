### POD-251114-18#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: paid-ads | fw: Attraction Offers | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:50
claim: Freemium is an acquisition strategy, not a business model, and suits near-zero-marginal-cost products with funding.
detail: The free product must spread by word of mouth. Named examples: online storage, ad-supported music, video hosting, email. Advanced and mostly used by funded firms; novices should avoid it.

### POD-251114-18#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: offers | also: - | fw: Attraction Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:36
claim: The free product must cost almost nothing to serve, give continuous value and leave something to upgrade for.
detail: In B2B, one of the key ways is to give something that exposes a hole your paid product fills. In consumer markets, it typically means limiting use so regular users would want more.
### POD-251114-18#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit | fw: CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:22
claim: True CAC under freemium is cost to serve a free user divided by upgrade rate.
detail: Revenue per paying user should be about three times that to profit.
numbers: 5 cents/month serve cost / 1% upgrade = $5/month CAC; need over $15/month revenue per paid user.

### POD-251114-18#04 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:15
claim: Freemium fails through low conversion, low shareability or high cost to serve.
detail: Check whether free users upgrade, whether they value it enough to tell others, and whether servicing them for free costs more than paid users bring in.
