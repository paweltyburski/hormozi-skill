### POD-0588#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Acquisition.com's ideal investment is a company with $2M to $10M of EBITDA and a founder who truly wants to scale.
detail: Stakes are mostly 33%, 49% or 51%, with a couple of early deals at 20%; one holding is a majority. Unlike a traditional private equity firm that buys a majority, the founder matters more, since a minority partner must work with them.
numbers: $2M-$10M EBITDA; stakes 20%, 33%, 49%, 51%; 1 majority holding (as of mid-2023).

### POD-0588#02 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:40
claim: Some founders say they want to scale but stop once income doubles or triples.
detail: One CEO whose profit was tripled began golfing daily and said he already earned more than ever. Hormozi wants founders aiming at 20x or 50x, not comfort. The gap between $1M a month and $10M a month is huge, and founders often assume they will simply do 10 times as much.
numbers: Profit tripled; targets of 20x-50x; $1M vs $10M a month.

### POD-0588#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:43
claim: Any one of five red flags stops a deal immediately; no three-strikes rule.
detail: Flags named: dishonesty, discrepancies between numbers, a culture of fear in the organisation (the other two not stated in audio). Also, he personally says that if he doesn't look forward to talking to someone, that is a big red flag for him. Most flags concern the founder, not the business.
numbers: 5 flags; 1 is enough.
### POD-0588#04 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: finance-exit | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:31
claim: Usually you can spot a founder who will keep going by their language: a big mission, or a genuine wish to test themselves or get very rich (as long as it is true and their personal needs are already met).
detail: Positive signs: wanting to build an amazing workplace, change an industry or community, test their limits, or openly wanting a billion-dollar company when personal needs are already met. Red flag: they talk in small numbers for growth and final size, because you cannot make them believe more than they do.
### POD-0588#05 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:16
claim: Test honesty by whether the leader knows and admits where problems are in each department.
detail: If everything is "amazing" and numbers are rounded up, they are not close enough. Hormozi's own CEO check: if he did not know what was going wrong in every department, he was too far away. The interviewer (not Hormozi) added that micro-controlling everything is the opposite extreme and also a flag, and that self-deception is, in his opinion, the greatest downfall of any CEO, manager or salesperson.
### POD-0588#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:16
claim: Hormozi says that when a founder wants to partner on one business but also runs three others, his own response is to tell them to close the others and then he'll work with them.
detail: Sometimes founders do that and come back, which he reads as a sign they are in it; others refuse, and he tells them to go chase five rabbits. Shiny-object syndrome is one of the business red flags, along with losses, no growth path and excess complexity.
numbers: Example: 1 deal plus 3 other businesses.
### POD-0588#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:54
claim: Partners must accept a smaller slice of a bigger pie; many say they do but do not believe it.
detail: He cites Elon Musk holding about 20% of Tesla as an example (his claim) of giving equity to help build something far larger. Convincing a founder who does not already believe this is hard.
numbers: Example: about 20% stake.

### POD-0588#08 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:28
claim: Companies under $10M in sales and $2M in profit trade at a median of about 2.5x profit, and over 80% involve seller financing.
detail: The 2.5x is total enterprise value, not cash. Buyers of owner-dependent businesses add no value and expect to pay the median. Example: $1M profit sells for $2.5M; sell half and with heavy seller carry the cash at closing may be a few hundred thousand dollars. The figures come from SBA statistics, he says.
numbers: Median 2.5x profit; 80%+ seller financed; $1M profit example selling half = $1.25M, mostly deferred.

### POD-0588#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:17
claim: Founders overvalue a business by measuring effort put in instead of an asset that grows without them.
detail: Headline exit multiples do not apply to a profit-heavy company where the founder with a few helpers is the business. Value requires an asset that can grow on its own, which takes time.

### POD-0588#10 | EXAMPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:04
claim: An offer for an Instagram account dependent on its owner's face asked for 5 to 6 times profit, which he considered absurd.
detail: The account had large following and modest net income (the figure was garbled in audio); a social account tied to one person is hard to value at a business multiple.
numbers: Requested 5-6x multiple.

### POD-0588#11 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:47
claim: After product-market fit, lock one path and ask whether doing only this for 10 years would reach the outcome; before it, keep iterating.
detail: Before buyers exist, change things until some buy. After, refuse other opportunities, because people say no to big, slow money by saying yes to fast money. He rejected selling a course for the same reason.
numbers: Horizon 10 years.

### POD-0588#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:39
claim: Big things take about five to seven years of seemingly little progress, so combine unshakable faith with paranoia about the present.
detail: Hormozi calls the stance hope for the long run plus paranoia that the thing could die now, and attributes the idea to Jim Collins.
numbers: 5 to 7 years.

### POD-0588#13 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:47
claim: Acquisition.com's thesis, chosen after 18 months of thinking, joins social media (proprietary deal flow) with hands-on value-add investing.
detail: Typical investors do one of two things: buy very cheap, or buy where they hold niche expertise, so a 10x outcome makes the entry price matter less. His firm combines proprietary deal flow with real value added.
numbers: 18 months of planning.

### POD-0588#14 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:37
claim: In investing the goal is not getting a yes but an agreement inside a box you defined in advance.
detail: Operators are used to pushing for yes. In deals, raising price and changing terms to win a yes can produce a terrible deal. The hardest part of his shift from owner to investor.

### POD-0588#15 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:23
claim: Investing feedback loops are long, so measure growth in percentages and play games in the meantime.
detail: A deal takes 90 days to 6 months to close and 1 to 2 more years to know if it was good. He started investing in summer 2020. Comparing year 2-3 of a new venture with year 7-8 of an old business in absolute dollars is discouraging.
numbers: 90 days-6 months to close; 1-2 years to judge; first deal summer 2020.

### POD-0588#16 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:19:46
claim: Different businesses place pain at different times; choose where you want it and expect it.
detail: Info businesses are easy to start but hit a ceiling around $1M-$3M a month; software is painful and unprofitable early, then compounds at no extra cost once it reaches critical mass. Choosing pain now yields lasting results; pain later gives short bursts.
numbers: Info ceiling at $1M to $3M/month.

### POD-0588#17 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:26:31
claim: Successful businesses end up as investment firms with three layers of capital reallocation.
detail: Layer 1: inside the business (new offers or lines). Layer 2: strategic acquisitions that add value, for example buying the financing company or the manufacturer to integrate vertically. Layer 3: any outside place giving a higher return. He chose to focus on this end state.

### POD-0588#18 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:23:59
claim: Hormozi says the advice others gave him was what they would do, and he is not them; for him, investing where his experience is (business) made sense.
detail: After his sale he spoke to at least 40 people worth $100M to billions and got conflicting advice. Buying one huge building suits someone with 30 years in real estate, like Grant. He balked at the last step of four real estate deals worth $15M-$30M, but writes a $5M cheque for a business easily, since he knows its risks.
numbers: 40+ conversations; deals of $15M-$30M; cheques of $3M-$5M.
