### POD-0167#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: operator-mindset | fw: Activation Points | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:30
claim: Churn is a lagging indicator, so manage the leading indicators that cause it.
detail: A bad churn figure reflects problems from months earlier, and a fix takes three months or more to show. Track the events before churn and make them the goal of onboarding in weeks one and two. He says his view shifted after studying software companies.
numbers: 3+ months before a retention fix shows in churn.

### POD-0167#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:20
claim: When gyms start using the retention outreach, churn typically rises about 50% in month one (he first says doubles), then falls to about 3% by month three and stays under 3%, taking about 12 weeks.
detail: Month one outreach shakes the tree: people who meant to cancel do so. Then churn halves each month. It takes nerve to hold through the bad first month. The routine is Gym Launch's retention system (name unclear in audio).
numbers: Typical monthly churn example: 8% to 12% (month 1), 6% (month 2), 3% (month 3), then stays under 3%.
applies_to: Gyms and similar monthly-billing businesses
### POD-0167#03 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: Six Ways to Increase Value | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:13
claim: Hot selling set an unstated income expectation, so clients who earned well still felt let down.
detail: Early Gym Launch marketing showcased big testimonials; clients who made about $20,000 in month one were unhappy because they expected $50,000. He blames the company. Fix: sell cold, because over-delivering against an inflated expectation still disappoints.
numbers: Actual about $20,000 in month one vs expected $50,000.

### POD-0167#04 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: content-brand | fw: Six Ways to Increase Value | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:58

detail: For weight loss, show average transformations too, so over-delivery registers as delight.
numbers: Show the bottom 30% of results.

### POD-0167#09 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: Cohort Analysis | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:23
claim: Find the activation point by comparing earlier actions of people who converted or stayed against those who left.
detail: Take a promotion, challenge or trial. Form two groups: those who converted at the end, and those who stayed six months. Check what they did early (for example six visits in two weeks) versus cancellers. It rarely works on the first try, so iterate.
numbers: illustrative threshold only: 6 visits in first 2 weeks.

### POD-0167#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: retention-ltv | also: management-ops | fw: Activation Points | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:53
claim: Make the weekly team question "how many customers reached the activation point", not "what was churn".
detail: Churn is an output; process change must act on the cause. Centre huddles and resources on the share reaching the activation experience.
