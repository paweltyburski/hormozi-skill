### POD-0008#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:34
claim: Every outbound marketing piece should pass a seven-point test, taken from Dan Kennedy's direct response rules.
detail: The seven: 1) always carry an offer, 2) put a deadline on it, 3) give clear instructions for the next step, 4) spend nothing on branding, 5) make it measurable so return per piece is known, 6) follow up with everyone who responds, 7) write strong copy. Material that cannot be tracked is money shot into the dark.
applies_to: Gyms and small local businesses, but stated as valid for any business.

### POD-0008#02 | TACTIC | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:37
claim: Spell out the next step in the copy and preframe each following step on every page so the funnel matches what was promised.
detail: Format depends on medium (direct mail: text a short code or call; Facebook: click then opt in). Early on, people commented "I'm interested" under his ads because the link was never mentioned. His own funnel announces the application page after opt-in, then a thank-you page says to book the call and show up. Matching the promise builds trust; unknown next steps are the main fear.

### POD-0008#03 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:40
claim: A deadline does not have to sit on the lead magnet itself; it can come from a scheduled price rise.
detail: His free case-study download had no end date, but the paid service had a deadline because its price went up on a fixed rhythm, so buying sooner was cheaper.
numbers: Gym Launch raised its prices roughly every 8 weeks (2017).

### POD-0008#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: content-brand | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: He paid $5,000 for a logo and website, got nothing back, and now says to build pages with a funnel tool.
detail: Offered as proof that designed identity does not produce sales. Awareness cannot be monetised or traced to a buyer; only a tracked offer can. A phone call with a gym owner who had hired a branding firm triggered the episode.
numbers: $5,000 spent, $0 return (2017 or earlier).

### POD-0008#05 | CASE | topic: lead-getters | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:51
claim: A business owner paid $2,000 per month for six months to a mastermind and could not say whether her revenue had grown.
detail: Hormozi asked what she earned when she joined and what she earns now; she had never compared. His point: any serious service provider can state to the penny the added revenue clients got, and a buyer should demand the same measurability.
numbers: $2,000 per month, six months.

### POD-0008#06 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: unit-economics | also: other | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:51
claim: Ask what the business made before a paid programme and what it makes now.
detail: Used on the mastermind member to test whether spending had any return. If the answer is "I didn't really think about that", the spend was unmeasured.
### POD-0008#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:58
claim: "Huge opportunity" style ads draw people aware of the promotion but unaware of the problem, which weakens positioning.
detail: Copy must either hook an existing problem or make the reader aware of the one they have, then solve it. Plain recruitment-style ads like "we are looking for 15 people" are not copy. Hormozi dislikes them for fitness marketing.

### POD-0008#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: money-models | fw: Ascension Path | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:51
claim: A free offer pays off only through the follow-up that ascends people to paid services.
detail: Free can be profitable, but money comes from the choreographed sequence after the opt-in: raising awareness of the problem and of his ability to fix it, then a next step. Value letters and ascension are cited as the mechanisms; without a defined next step prospects drift away and forget you.
