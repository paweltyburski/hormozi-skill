### YT-_x6n-CmZqjo#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:50
claim: To get time from someone far ahead, bring achievements; the cutoff is probably around 5x your own level, and below that you may have to pay through coaching or masterminds.
detail: Hormozi rejects "buy a millionaire lunch" advice since you offer nothing. He says access probably scales with your own results: multiply your income by about five to estimate who might take your call. He thinks programmes and masterminds exist because people without prior achievement have to pay.
numbers: At just under $100M a year in revenue he got a call with a $1B-a-year founder; example cutoff: $100,000 earner reaching someone near $1M.
### YT-_x6n-CmZqjo#02 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:47
claim: Hormozi's company sat at $30-40M a year for three years until he focused on people and delegating; it then paced about $85M.
detail: He asked the founder why. An owner feels like the source of everything, but influence thins across layers of management. Gap 30M to 100M is about giving up control of high-level decisions to people who want authority and credit.
numbers: $30-40M annual revenue for three years, then pacing about $85M (target $100M).

### YT-_x6n-CmZqjo#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: hiring-talent | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:45
claim: The mentor Hormozi spoke with (a billionaire entrepreneur) said a business that will grow and be self-sustaining must have three superstars: one over acquisition, one over delivery, one over operations and shared services.
detail: Per the mentor, one person gets new clients, one delivers on promises, and the third handles bills, payroll, legal and IT. He said talent comes in just stars and superstars.
### YT-_x6n-CmZqjo#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:35
claim: If a senior candidate does not strike you as a superstar immediately, do not hire; people never convert later.
detail: The founder said he had misjudged people as superstars who weren't, but never the reverse. Hormozi's version: "good enough" hires block excellence. Superstars solve and implement problems without input and want authority, responsibility and credit.

### YT-_x6n-CmZqjo#05 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:33
claim: Give CEOs of each company a profit share plus the right to buy a growing stake; profit is the CEO's responsibility.
detail: The founder shared 5% of profit only with CEOs and let them buy in larger chunks over time. In one large company the CEO started at 5-10% and now owns 30-something percent. Hormozi: profit centres each run by a specialised driver form a conglomerate (a doorknob, door, frame and insurance seller, not just more doors).
numbers: 5% of profits to CEOs; one CEO went from 5-10% to 30-something percent ownership.

### YT-_x6n-CmZqjo#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:44
claim: Be willing to give up part of the pie to grow it; align incentives and partner with people whose skills differ from yours.
detail: Early on, Hormozi partnered with people like himself because they were fun, when he should have paired acquisition skill with a delivery and an operations owner. Do not give equity for services you can pay for, since the earliest equity is the most expensive.

### YT-_x6n-CmZqjo#07 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:15:37
claim: A founder stuck at $100M for 4-5 years and burnt out split the company into about a hundred pieces with profit shares and phantom equity, then reached $500M and $1B.
detail: He and his partner largely checked out and let the best drivers lead. Five years later revenue was $500M and five years after that $1B. He called it luck and said key decisions that made it work were ones he opposed. Hormozi's reading: make others wealthy and be humble.
numbers: $100M for 4-5 years; $500M five years later; $1B five years after that.

### YT-_x6n-CmZqjo#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:07
claim: As a company grows you need fewer hunter-drivers and more superstar tenders; growth is stepwise and each plateau is cleared by an experienced hire.
detail: Plateau-then-jump is normal. Constraints are removed by bringing in someone who has already solved that problem. Hormozi believes everyone is about three hires away from the growth they want.

### YT-_x6n-CmZqjo#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:58
claim: The guest founder told Hormozi he is more concerned with return of capital than return on capital, and that he never bought below an 8 cap rate.
detail: The founder holds a couple hundred million of multifamily property through the company, sharing equity with a partner; tenants cover the mortgage. He preferred slightly pricier, lower-risk buildings over maximising IRR. Hormozi guesses this ties to the leverage he might use, since more debt lifts returns and risk. Hormozi: rich people own the lottery game, not the ticket; halving net worth hurts more than doubling helps.
numbers: The founder's minimum cap rate was 8; roughly $200M of real estate.
applies_to: Owners investing profits into property
