### POD-0479#13 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:39:46
claim: When evaluating an investment, count how many "need to believe" statements must hold, and prefer few of them, each highly likely.
detail: Each extra need-to-believe lowers the chance of the outcome. If a business depends on one near-certain thing, they will write a check. Personally he applies it reversed: ask what amount of action would make it unreasonable not to succeed.
numbers: Example: 10,000 cold calls, or half a paycheck spent on real advertising for a year or two, would make failure unreasonable.

### POD-0479#16 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:36:58
claim: Holding the frame in a sale means a clear agenda: say why you are on the call, agree you understand their problem, then offer a frame for the decision.
detail: A prospect who responded to content has already said they have a problem, which gives the seller the frame. Control comes from clear communication, not alpha behaviour; who leads depends on context. The goal is to convert desire into a decision using criteria both agree to.

### POD-0479#18 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: Three Core Beliefs | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:39:04
claim: The aim of a sales call is for the person to decide, not necessarily buy, and he peels objections from outer layers inward.
detail: Order he describes: first circumstances (time, money, product fit), then other people (spouse, partner), then the prospect's own avoidance and fear of a mistake. The underlying model comes from Albert Ellis.

### POD-0479#19 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:40:53

detail: People do not join calls just to gather information, so that line hides a problem. Ask what they are trying to solve and then label the problem back to them.
