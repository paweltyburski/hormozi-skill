### POD-0102#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: The top reason micro gyms fail is not knowing what business they are in: they are not selling classes, since premium chains offer similar classes and more amenities for less.
detail: Nicer chains sell at about $99 a month with high-intensity classes, bigger facilities and extra amenities, while the actual average for CrossFit, bootcamp and semi-private sites is about $118. A micro gym cannot win on facility, equipment or class quality; its advantage is accountability and premium service. He researched what gyms did in the 2007-2009 recession.
numbers: Premium chains from about $99/month; micro-gym average about $118/month.

### POD-0102#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: retention-ltv | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:56
claim: Hormozi says most gyms aren't actively doing anything to foster true accountability, and then are surprised by consistent 10%+ month-over-month churn or trial customers who don't stay.
detail: The three things sold in the consult are fitness, nutrition and accountability, with accountability the overarching umbrella. Many owners say community is why people stay, which he grants is true, but most do nothing active to foster accountability.
numbers: Consistent 10%+ month-over-month churn is what he mentions gym owners being surprised by.
### POD-0102#03 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:39
claim: Build a routine of accountability touches: check on members who have not shown by Wednesday, and make contact twice a month outside class.
detail: Also: send flowers or a message for a birthday or a death in the family, a handwritten card every month, members events, charity activities, greeting everyone by name, and value given outside the gym. These must be processes, not intentions, so they happen consistently.
numbers: No-show check by Wednesday; non-class contact at least twice a month; handwritten card monthly.

### POD-0102#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:06
claim: Premium service puts the membership high on the buyer's hierarchy of needs, so it survives job loss, pay cuts and downturns.
detail: Classes are what people come for, the burger; everything outside the class is why they stay. Spending on equipment (even $30,000) does not beat chains that spent millions on facilities.
