### POD-0766#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: retention-ltv | fw: Four R's | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:09
claim: In his gyms, Hormozi did 48 back-to-back one-on-one nutrition consults on one Saturday after each challenge sale, and earned about five times the supplement revenue per member of rival gyms.
detail: After selling roughly 50 people into a challenge, he booked two consults every 30 minutes for 12 hours and sold supplements as part of onboarding. Competitors pitched a room instead, closing perhaps 30% to 50% and averaging about $50 per head, while he averaged about $250 per head. The tiring repetition also gave fast feedback loops that made him a strong seller.
numbers: 50 challenge buyers; 48 consults in 12 hours (2 per 30 minutes); about $250 per head versus about $50 per head, around 5x.
applies_to: Gyms and local services.

### POD-0766#02 | CASE | topic: promotions-launches | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:47
claim: A business whose live event grossed about a million dollars automated it into recordings and saw return on ad spend collapse, when repeating the event monthly would have been far more profitable.
detail: The event returned more than 10 to 1 on ads. The team found the event too much work and tried to automate. Hormozi's advice was to ask what the most absurd version would be and then run it monthly, with the public co-founder showing up and the integrator running the rest. Claimed upside: about $10M a year.
numbers: About $1M per event; ROAS above 10:1 live; monthly events implied roughly $10M a year.

### POD-0766#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: offers | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:55
claim: Ask "what is the most absurd version of this, and what if I did meet every customer one-on-one?" before ruling out unscalable work.
detail: The habit is to dismiss high-touch ideas because they are work. Doing them pays twice: more money immediately and more skill. Hormozi also cites Y Combinator founders doing unscalable things (hand-recruiting in supermarkets) before they have something valuable.

### POD-0766#04 | TACTIC | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: offers | fw: Lead Magnet Steps | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:38
claim: A beginner with a weak PDF lead magnet should offer about five hours of personal help instead, because the alternative is no customers.
detail: Hormozi's own PDF works because of his reputation and books; a newcomer lacks that. The objection "I can only take 10" is answered by solving scale later with the money and information gained. Example: a beginner web designer offers five hours to improve someone's website.
numbers: Five hours of one-on-one time; capacity of about 10 clients.
applies_to: Beginners with no audience.

### POD-0766#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:24
claim: Unscalable one-on-one value is strong because it moves every Value Equation variable at once: likelihood up, effort down, delay irrelevant.
detail: With personal attention the likelihood of success rises, the customer works less because you do it, and value arrives in real time. It supports premium prices. The extra profit and information then fund scaling: split the offer into components costing the same time but valued less, and automate those, then scale everything except the most valuable part.

### POD-0766#06 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:11
claim: To find what to sell, work with people one-on-one for free and then ask what working together would look like if you charged a given price, letting them describe the offer.
detail: The conversations also provide face time with customers. If you fear time wasters, qualify: free to them does not mean free to you, so reserve the costly free service for the ideal prospect and make others earn it by meeting criteria.

### POD-0766#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Trim and Stack | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:47
claim: When trimming an offer, do not cut the unscalable element without also losing its price, because price rises with value; cost out the extreme version first and then decide.
detail: Most people trim too much or the wrong thing. Start from the most insane version and cut down. Many sellers refuse extreme services without ever costing them.

### POD-0766#08 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: Four R's | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:12:12
claim: An online trainer could fly to each client for a one-day, in-person onboarding if the price were $25,000 a year; the trip costs a day and about $500.
detail: Plan it ahead, fly in and out the same day to cut the roundtrip from about $1,000 to about $500. Selling five a month would make a million-dollar business that is nearly all profit; triple the sales and the cost per customer is still a day and $500. Onboarding in person raises review, referral, retention and resale. The point: pick a price at which you would be happy to do it.
numbers: Price $25,000/year; trip cost about $500 (from about $1,000); five sales a month roughly $1M a year (his figure).

### POD-0766#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Sales to Fulfillment Continuum | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:51
claim: Starting with the premium hands-on tier gives you the brand credibility and customer knowledge to release a done-with-you and then do-it-yourself version later, as Tesla moved from Roadster down to cheaper models.
detail: When you later launch a scalable version, you can say your top tier is full and offer the same methods with you doing less. Hands-on delivery teaches all the problems customers meet.

### POD-0766#10 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:08
claim: Imagine the offer the best-fit customers would jump at, write down its true cost in time and money, then either charge a price you are happy with or give it free only to the people who are absolutely insane.
detail: He says trainers, physical therapists, chiropractors, dentists and roofers all have a product more people would want that no one costs out because they dismiss it at once.
