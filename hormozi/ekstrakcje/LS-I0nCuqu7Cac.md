### LS-I0nCuqu7Cac#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:06:33
claim: Mental toughness as four measurable behaviour stats after a bad event: tolerance, fortitude, resilience, adaptability.
detail: Tolerance: how much hardship before your normal behaviour breaks (fuse length). Fortitude: how far behaviour changes once it breaks (a walk outside versus quitting a job). Resilience: how fast you return to baseline. Adaptability: whether the new baseline is above or below the old one. He defines trauma as a lasting behaviour change after an aversive event, which can be positive. Measuring each component turns toughness from a trait into a trainable skill; telling someone to "toughen up" does not help.

### LS-I0nCuqu7Cac#02 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:26:16
claim: Stop a bad-decision snowball by noticing the moment your behaviour departs from normal and acting as you would if you were not upset.
detail: Steps: (1) recognise you have started acting differently; (2) instead of justifying it, recall what happened the last time you went down that road; (3) ask what you would normally say or do and do that while still feeling upset; (4) treat the slip as a chance to show yourself higher fortitude. Feelings are allowed; the goal is that only you know you are upset. Sleep and food raise all four stats, so poor biology lowers tolerance, but the choice between stimulus and reaction remains (Frankl).
