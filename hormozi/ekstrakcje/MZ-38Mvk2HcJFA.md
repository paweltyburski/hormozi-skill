### MZ-38Mvk2HcJFA#01 | TACTIC | topic: other | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: He decided to marry by auditing how his measurable life stats had changed since meeting her, not by how he felt.
detail: A mentor told him to look at his stats: business up, eating better, training, less stressed. Translating the relationship into changes in his own results made the decision easy; his proposal was a statement that it made sense to marry.

### MZ-38Mvk2HcJFA#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: other | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:44
claim: Falling in love is a temporary state of impaired judgment, so the marriage decision should be rational enough to still hold after it ends.
detail: The speakers describe falling in love as dosing yourself for roughly six to 24 months, during which you cannot trust your own decisions. Choose so that the match makes sense without emotion, then it survives when the feelings fade.
numbers: 6-24 months of the infatuation window.

### MZ-38Mvk2HcJFA#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: other | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:46
claim: If you want several children, the fertility window leaves little slack, so plan the sequence early.
detail: Worked arithmetic: four children about 18 months apart take roughly six years; with pregnancy risk rising after 35, the first child must come near 29, marriage near 28, meeting around 26. He adds this only applies if you want kids, and that betting on future cures is poor reasoning.
numbers: Four kids at ~18-month spacing = ~6 years; age 35 cited as the limit.
applies_to: People who want children.

### MZ-38Mvk2HcJFA#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: other | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:48
claim: Do not expect one partner to meet every need; a good partner helps you become your best self, other needs can come from friends.
detail: A mentor whose wife was not in his business said he did not want to talk shop with her, that is what peers are for. He rejects the soulmate idea and the fallacy of a perfect pick. Test: does this person make me better, and are there non-negotiables clashing (kids, travel)?

### MZ-38Mvk2HcJFA#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: other | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:15:42
claim: It is easier to build a life around a partner early than to fit a partner into a finished, curated life.
detail: The lamp analogy: furnishing an empty house around one lamp is easy; finding a lamp that suits a house you spent 15 years perfecting is hard. Older, more settled people also drift from satisficing to maximizing and raise the bar.
numbers: About 15 years of building a house in the analogy.

### MZ-38Mvk2HcJFA#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: other | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:33
claim: The person you marry marries two people, who you are and who you want to become, so check they love both.
detail: Going through hardship together before success (she saw him lose everything twice before marriage, she 23 and he 26) removed the doubt whether she loved him or his status. The partner either prevents the growth or is a rocket pack for it.
