### YT-MA-5y9s80Qs#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: Goodwill (Price-Value-Cost) | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:32
claim: Netflix priced below video-rental stores while giving far more value, yet kept very high margins because each extra customer costs almost nothing.
detail: That produced a big customer surplus and left room to lower price further.

### YT-MA-5y9s80Qs#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:25
claim: A business with high cost of goods and big customer surplus needs little marketing but runs on thin margins and grows only 10-15% a year.
detail: Typical of old-school service businesses that are operationally efficient yet cannot reinvest much. A generic mom-and-pop business has decent margin and decent value, nothing remarkable.
numbers: Growth about 10-15% per year.

### YT-MA-5y9s80Qs#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:34
claim: A large enough customer surplus makes the business grow on word of mouth, quadratically instead of linearly, with acquisition cost near zero.
detail: Diagnose in order: first how to provide the most value, then how to cut cost of goods drastically (a penny to make, ten cents to sell, ten dollars of value).
