### MM-14#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Making more offers is not unethical; unethical selling means recommending what helps only the business, and he says bad selling is rooted in deception (lying about qualification or quality).
detail: Ethical sellers recommend what helps both customer and business. The lie is either about whether the customer qualifies or about the quality of delivery. Bad selling is rooted in deception, good selling in education — the customer should know what you know. Test: if your mother had all your information, she should buy what you'd recommend.

### MM-14#02 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: pricing, verticals | fw: Buy X Get Y Free | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:37
claim: Nashville's Boot Factory sold "buy one pair, get two free" by pricing three pairs into the first — and bachelorette parties bought in groups.
detail: As a Vanderbilt freshman Hormozi thought the store (car-sized boot, flashing lights) was foolish. Ten years later it had expanded. Each boot was marked down to about $600 for one pair — three ordinary leather pairs at a normal $200 each. Bachelorette parties piled in: the bride and two friends each got a pair.
numbers: $600 for one + two free = $200/pair.
applies_to: Retail / physical products, especially one-time buyers.

### MM-14#03 | DEFINITION | topic: money-models | also: pricing, offers | fw: Buy X Get Y Free | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:24
claim: In Buy X Get Y Free, customers buy something to get other things free, which turns a discount into a far stronger "free" offer while still selling full-priced units.
detail: More free items and higher value work better: buy one get two beats buy two get one. Free draws far more attention than discounts, but a single product can't just be given away, so most businesses run seasonal discounts instead. Selling several units at once lets the discount appear as free units: 33% off becomes "buy two, get one free" — they still buy two at full price. Fits anything people sensibly buy more of or want longer access to (00:11:50).
numbers: 33% off ≡ buy two get one free.

### MM-14#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Buy X Get Y Free | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:37
claim: T-shirt math: Buy X Get Y can be a pure reframe or a reframe on top of a discount.
detail: Reframe: three shirts for $30 vs "buy one for $30, get two free" — same economics, far more attention. With discount: three for $20 vs "buy one for $20, get two free" — effectively one third off.
numbers: $10/shirt reframe; $6.67/shirt with discount.

### MM-14#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Buy X Get Y Free | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:45
claim: 18 months of service for $1,800 converts best framed as "buy 6, get 12 free".
detail: "Buy 12 get 6" is good, "buy 9 get 9" better, "buy 6 get 12" crazy — identical price and service, but the most free stuff wins. Give more free than paid and adjust pricing until it works (00:07:25).
numbers: 18 months for $1,800 in all versions.

### MM-14#06 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics, promotions-launches | fw: Buy X Get Y Free | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:30
claim: A grand-opening "buy six months, get six free" collects months of full-price prepayment, justified by a small high-margin extra.
detail: Some of his service businesses took a year to recoup acquisition cost; buy-six-get-six brings many customers and six months' prepayment each. Add a tiny high-margin differentiator during the opening (e.g., text access to the gym owner) so the package justifies a higher price — you may collect close to a year's normal price. Raise prices before giving things away, and never give away margin you can't afford (00:07:25).
applies_to: Recurring service businesses, new locations.

### MM-14#07 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: Buy X Get Y Free | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:25
claim: Free items needn't match the paid item — several cheaper free items can beat one expensive one, so test.
detail: Buyers count items: "buy one shirt, get three pairs of socks free" (same cost as one free shirt) may beat "buy one shirt, get one shirt free", especially when socks are the next logical need. Likewise test "buy one, get two free" against a plain 33% discount — same economics, usually better conversion.

### MM-14#08 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: finance-exit, retention-ltv | fw: Buy X Get Y Free | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:00
claim: Don't sell long prepayments if you can't manage cash or need the money to survive, and cap annual prepay at about 10% of recurring customers.
detail: Spending prepaid cash immediately leaves you unable to deliver and ruins your reputation. Keep it in a separate account (use an accountant) and pay yourself monthly as you deliver. Never use the play to keep a failing business alive. The 10% cap is psychological, not mathematical: all-annual makes sense on paper, but most owners can't handle the responsibility, and businesses keeping monthly recurring cash flow do better.
numbers: Cap at ~10% of recurring base (e.g., 10 of 100).

### MM-14#09 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: finance-exit | fw: Buy X Get Y Free | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:13
claim: A healthy business can sell capped prepayments to existing customers to fund equipment instead of taking on credit.
detail: E.g., prepay about 10 customers to buy gym equipment or an industrial oven for a bakery rather than drawing a credit line. Also suits grand openings and fast cash.

### MM-14#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Buy X Get Y Free | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:19
claim: Long-term prepayers are your most likely buyers of more, so keep making them offers.
detail: Owners fear upselling prepaid customers (he did too), but annual prepayers self-select as hyper-buyers and bought the most add-ons (Big Booty Bootcamp, deadlift seminar, Lean by Halloween, etc.). They forget they paid, the gym feels free, and earners' wallets replenish.

### MM-14#11 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Buy X Get Y Free | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:50
claim: If customers buy only once, pack as much as possible into the first transaction.
detail: The Boot Factory maximised ticket size because visiting bachelorette parties rarely return; with one shot, make the ask as big as possible.

### MM-14#12 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv, unit-economics | fw: Buy X Get Y Free | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:53
claim: Size the package beyond your normal customer lifespan so the offer extends how long customers pay.
detail: If customers usually stay three months, sell "buy two, get two" priced to cover four months — they commit longer than they would have stayed.
numbers: 3-month norm → 4-month package ≈ 33% more revenue per customer.
