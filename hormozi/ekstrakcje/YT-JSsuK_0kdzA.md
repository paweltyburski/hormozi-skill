### YT-JSsuK_0kdzA#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:47
claim: After a forced shift to remote service, many customers will not return to the old format, because convenience wins.
detail: In late 2020 Hormozi predicted that reopened gyms would see fewer people than owners expect and a slow recovery, since remote training was pulled about ten years forward. He compares owners' denial to Blockbuster rationalising the "experience" against Netflix. Advice: do not wait; learn to sell remote while waiting, then keep the revenue stream.
applies_to: Local fitness businesses in the 2020 closures

### YT-JSsuK_0kdzA#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: sales-process | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:44
claim: Remote gym sales got twice as many leads into a sales conversation, which outweighed a lower phone close rate.
detail: Remote gyms got 40% of leads into a sales conversation against about 20% before, and closed 29% on the phone against 42% in person. Twice the at-bats still nets more sales. He also cites a steady 11% of all leads closed, higher than most gyms in person.
numbers: Lead to conversation 40% remote vs 20% in-person; close rate 29% phone vs 42% in-person; overall leads closed about 11%.

### YT-JSsuK_0kdzA#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: paid-ads | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:28
claim: A wider geographic radius gives far more leads at lower cost, but needs a trust cue.
detail: Reach grows with the square of radius (his pizza analogy), so a 25-mile radius reaches many times the people of a 5-mile one (he says 10 to 50x; the pure geometry gives more). Nationally run ads can get leads near 25 cents. The hard part is getting a stranger to buy on the first call; a semi-local appeal adds one or two trust points. Ad impression costs had already normalised, so cheaper leads came from radius, not cheap ads.
numbers: National ad leads around $0.25 each (his figure); 25-mile radius with no drop in sales percentage.

### YT-JSsuK_0kdzA#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models, verticals | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:15
claim: Remote fitness at average numbers paid back ad spend almost at once and built recurring income in about 30 days.
detail: Spending about $1,500 returned about $6,000-$6,600 in front-end cash within 30 days (he says both). Owners were breaking even on the first transaction versus 2:1 to 4:1 in person, with an 80% gross margin on the back end. Worked example: about $2,200 in ads yields roughly 50 clients; with 60% going to continuity at $200 per month this is a $10,000-a-month business. He adds that at Gym Launch he loses money in the first 30 to 60 days (unclear in audio).
numbers: $1,500 spend to ~$6,000-$6,600 cash in 30 days; $2,200 ads to 50 clients; 60% continuity at $200/month = $10,000/month; 80% back-end gross margin.
applies_to: Remote fitness coaching, 2020 averages
