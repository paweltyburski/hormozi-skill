### YT-0BZ7-PZR8jE#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: paid-ads | also: content-brand | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:34
claim: Build data hooks by turning three wheels: what share of clients, what outcome, and over what time.
detail: Work from customer-success data and ask how to say each stat in the most compelling way. He bases whole campaigns on statistics sliced this way.
### YT-0BZ7-PZR8jE#02 | CASE | topic: paid-ads | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: His best campaign was "one year later": clients of more than a year compared on their stats before joining and now.
detail: The differences ran to $250,000, $500,000 and $1M extra revenue a year. Cuts included the average client, the top 20% (over $400,000 more a year) and the bottom 80%.
numbers: Gains of $250,000 to $1M a year per client; top 20% added $400,000+.

### YT-0BZ7-PZR8jE#03 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:22
claim: State shares as "1 in 5.5" rather than 18%, or invert them (4.5 of 5.5), and use medians so most people place themselves above average.
detail: "One in 5.5 gyms is over $1M run rate" sounds closer than 18%. Inverting plays on typical outcomes; an average also implies half are above it.
numbers: 1 in 5.5 gyms (about 18%) at a $1M-a-year run rate.

### YT-0BZ7-PZR8jE#04 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: retention-ltv | fw: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:56
claim: Use long durations (one or two years later) as proof that clients stay and succeed, which newer rivals cannot show.
detail: A long-tenured client probably did well, particularly in education businesses, so results over a year or two double as retention proof. You can also slice the first week, 30 days or two months and pick the strongest cut.

### YT-0BZ7-PZR8jE#06 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:26
claim: Show an increase four ways: percent, absolute amount, total result, or relative to a cost the buyer knows.
detail: Pick the version that reads strongest; he prefers the relative form, such as what $15,500 covers in monthly rent for a micro gym (around $2,000 a month; his arithmetic in the audio is garbled).
numbers: $15,500 against assumed rent of ~$2,000 a month.

claim: Express outcomes as a frequency: with about 50 seven-figure gyms in a year, one appeared roughly every 7.7 days.
detail: The same works for any result (every two days another client loses 20 pounds). The count of about 50 in 2019 or 2020 is from memory in the audio.
numbers: ~50 seven-figure gyms in a year; one every ~7.7 days.

### YT-0BZ7-PZR8jE#08 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: offers | fw: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:29
claim: Move the outcome threshold until the share achieving it is a strong, truthful number, then market that.
detail: For weight loss, "70% lose over 12 pounds in six weeks" can beat "10% lose over 30". Tracking this data gives a competitive edge since most rivals do not track results, but claims must be compliant.
