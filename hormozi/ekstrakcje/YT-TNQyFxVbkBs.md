### YT-TNQyFxVbkBs#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: If you have customers and growth is slow, the product is the problem: people already know about you and are not telling others.
detail: Owners say "more people need to find out about it", but existing customers have found it and chose not to refer. A billionaire friend told him that if it will not grow on its own over time it is not worth doing; ultra-wealthy people favour one-off effort that compounds. Needing constant promotion signals a product that is not good enough.

### YT-TNQyFxVbkBs#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: product-delivery | also: pricing | fw: Delivery Cube | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:29
claim: Ask what the product and experience would have to be if it cost 10 times more (or $100,000-$1M); this exposes value.
detail: The question forces you to design much more value. He pairs it with a second question on profit.

### YT-TNQyFxVbkBs#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: product-delivery | also: unit-economics | fw: Delivery Cube | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:29
claim: Ask: if the product cost one tenth as much but you had to make it better, what one-time assets would you create that keep providing value at scale and profitably?
detail: He says this question hits on the profit of the product, pointing to high-margin, one-time investment type things that make the product more valuable.
### YT-TNQyFxVbkBs#04 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: retention-ltv | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:36
claim: Imagine you could sell only one more customer and all further growth had to come from that customer's unprompted referrals; what experience would have to happen?
detail: This targets customer experience: everything around the product that turns a buyer into a raving fan who cannot help mentioning it. The super wealthy people he knows pretty much exclusively talk about product, and usually the really small people he knows only talk about promotion, which should only exist to get the product right, then be juiced.
### YT-TNQyFxVbkBs#05 | FRAMEWORK | topic: lead-getters | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:05:35
claim: Six ways to get customers: paid media, earned media (following), your contact list, manual outbound, affiliates or partners, and word of mouth; only word of mouth compounds.
detail: The first five scale roughly linearly (double outbound staff, double customers; doubling ad spend at best doubles customers). Word of mouth multiplies: two people tell two, who tell two. A product good enough for that also eases churn, bad reviews and bad-fit customers.

### YT-TNQyFxVbkBs#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:05
claim: Small entrepreneurs try to get customers to make sales; bigger ones make sales to get customers.
detail: Quoted from Dan Kennedy. The first optimises for money fast (what do I say to get a yes); the second for a long relationship providing value, a compounding view of time. Hormozi names this a core difference between millionaires and billionaires he knows.
