### YT-sEQNnsaeiAs#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Most businesses are worthless because they are jobs, not because owners cannot market a sale.
detail: He says 99% eventually close and only 1% are sold. The step from employee to self-employed usually lowers pay for a long time, because you are now every part of the machine and not yet good at each; having an LLC or staff does not make you an owner.
numbers: 99% close, 1% sold; ~30 million US small businesses.

### YT-sEQNnsaeiAs#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: management-ops | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:07
claim: Sellability sequence: stop doing fulfilment, replace acquisition with a repeatable channel, productize the service, then hand off every management hat.
detail: (1) Stop delivering. (2) Prove a buyer could expect reliable customers: pick a channel (referrals, affiliates, manual outbound, owned media, earned media or paid) where known input produces known output; being the one who still does marketing or sales means it is not independent. (3) Productize: custom work is valued as a service business. (4) Replace the hats you still wear: head of sales, marketing director, chief product lead, and your own decision-making. Then see yourself as a shareholder or board director, not the owner.
numbers: 6 channel options; takes 3-10 years.

### YT-sEQNnsaeiAs#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:47
claim: A custom, owner-dependent business sells only with a large earn-out, which leaves you working for years while the buyer holds the upside.
detail: With a three-to-five-year earn-out you work for similar pay, the buyer keeps the upside, and if milestones are missed the buyer keeps the business and you get none of the money. Being the spokesperson also leaves the company worthless outside you.
numbers: earn-out of 3-5 years.

### YT-sEQNnsaeiAs#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:39
claim: Owner profit (seller's discretionary earnings) contains unpaid wages for the roles you fill; replacing them lowers margin and that is fine.
detail: As you replace yourself, margin often falls but you get 100% of your time back and the business becomes sellable. You may need to hire six people to replace your six jobs, and several hires will fail along the way.

### YT-sEQNnsaeiAs#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:45
claim: Hiring quality moves in stages: early staff are worse than you and must be trained; later hires have done the role elsewhere but need to learn your way; at multi-figure scale they are better than you.
detail: Gross margin and volume must be big enough to pay for talent. At roughly $10-30M+ a year people arrive with solutions preloaded; then the owner's role shrinks to recruiting people and casting the vision. A great marketer will not work for someone less skilled, so the owner needs clear values and a distilled vision, or hires churn.
numbers: hyper-growth stage ~$10M-$30M+ per year.

### YT-sEQNnsaeiAs#06 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:03
claim: If you stepped away and did not answer calls for two months, would the business still run?
detail: Most owners think it runs itself; list the small fractional tasks you do, quantify them, write processes, hand them off and iterate until it works.
numbers: 2 months without contact as the test.
