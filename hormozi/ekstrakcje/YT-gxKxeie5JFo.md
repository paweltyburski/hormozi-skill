### YT-gxKxeie5JFo#01 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:51
claim: He lent $2.5M to buyers of a condemned 334-unit foreclosure building and layered four protections against default.
detail: Protections: personal guarantees from two buyers whose net worth exceeded the loan; three offers of $5-5.6M on the building (about double the loan); a $300,000 earnest-money deposit from the top buyer; and verification that he was in first position on the title, using the cash to clear the last lienholder. Control of structure is why he likes private lending.
numbers: loan $2.5M; offers $5M-$5.6M; earnest money $300,000.

### YT-gxKxeie5JFo#02 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:29
claim: Price a bridge loan at 4% a month, double the rate if it runs past four months, with a two-month minimum carry.
detail: 4% of $2.5M is $100,000 a month, about $3,300 a day. The deal was expected to close in 60 days. The minimum carry guarantees at least $200,000 even if repaid at once. A rate step-up compensates for rising risk when deals drag.
numbers: 4% per month = $100,000; after month 4 rate 8% per month ($200,000); minimum $200,000.

### YT-gxKxeie5JFo#03 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:14
claim: The deal ran over 100 days and the borrower flagged his own lawyer's error to repay roughly $75,000 more than the invoiced $275,000.
detail: His lawyers sent a repayment figure of $275,000 instead of the agreed amount (about $300,000 plus $3,300 a day, near $350,000). The borrower's lawyer advised staying quiet; he disclosed it and dropped that lawyer. Hormozi dropped his own for a skill error. Total returned was about $2.85M. After roughly 50% tax at ordinary rates he netted about $175,000. Trust built this way leads to bigger deals, he argues.
numbers: invoiced $275,000 vs about $350,000 owed; total repaid about $2.85M; about $175,000 after tax.
