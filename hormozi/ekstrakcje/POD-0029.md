### POD-0029#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:56
claim: Once a gym is at full capacity, either accept the revenue ceiling or raise prices on the legacy members too.
detail: Space and sessions are limited, so only price can add revenue. Fear is mostly emotional: low payers who leave cost little, and an acquisition system can refill the spots. Example: an owner signed up 100 front-end members and had moved the first 24 to $150 a month, while 100 old members paid $67 to $97 for unlimited boot camp.
numbers: Example rates: new $150 a month versus legacy $67-$97 a month.
### POD-0029#02 | SCRIPT | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:57
claim: Tell legacy members that the old deal cannot be honoured, release them from it, and offer a new agreement in person.
detail: Steps: (1) treat the agreement as two-sided and release them from the old contract; (2) admit you lacked information when you set the price; (3) say profit is now a core value, since without it the business cannot reinvest or survive; (4) thank them with their own results; (5) say you chose a call or a visit instead of an email; (6) give the new rate and say you want them to stay.

### POD-0029#03 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:13
claim: Treat existing members as the most qualified prospects you have: call or meet each one rather than mass emailing.
detail: With few members, close the increase over the phone or in person across a week, and chase the rest by phone over the weekend.

### POD-0029#04 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:21
claim: Hold the new price firm and, if needed, offer flexible billing terms instead.
detail: Ask whether biweekly or weekly payments aligned to pay cycles would help. This signals the owner is trying to make it work while price stays.

### POD-0029#05 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:10
claim: Quote the increase per week, such as $15 a week instead of $60 a month.
detail: It sounds smaller. Compute the true figure as monthly membership revenue divided by paying members.
numbers: $60 a month = $15 a week.

### POD-0029#06 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:13
claim: The blended average membership of about $100 a month does not work for most gyms.
detail: Over 70% of his client gyms were near $100 a month. Only gyms that serve about 40 people per session could make $99 work. Otherwise profit is too thin to reinvest.
numbers: Blended average ~$100 a month at 70%+ of his gyms; viable at $99 only with ~40 people per session.

### POD-0029#07 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:28
claim: Raising a 120-member gym from $100 to $160 and losing 20% still lifts monthly revenue.
detail: Revenue goes from $12,000 to roughly $15,000 with 96 members, and 24 freed spots can be refilled, so revenue rises while serving fewer people. He expects only 10 to 20 percent to leave and calls it about a $40,000 yearly raise.
numbers: 120 x $100 = $12,000; 96 x $160 = $15,360; expected loss 10-20%; gain ~$40,000 a year.

### POD-0029#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Virtuous Cycle of Price | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:24
claim: Do not copy competitors' prices; micro gyms fail because they set prices by looking around at others who are also broke.
detail: Large-group and boot camp gyms that follow the neighbours end up with no profit.
