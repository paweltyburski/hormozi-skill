### MZ-X-1e1cgIMvs#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A day spa selling $99 monthly memberships can copy the six-week challenge with the same end state, then flip buyers into membership at week three or four.
detail: The spa also offers a year prepaid at a discount. A software company he owned ran a six-week stress-release challenge with one float-tank visit a week for $600 and did well. Cash up front, then a product upsell 24-48 hours later.
numbers: Challenge $600 for six weeks; membership $99/month; spa CAC about $91.

### MZ-X-1e1cgIMvs#02 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:19
claim: Advertise the valuable challenge, then offer it free as a gift for becoming a member so it becomes the onboarding offer.
detail: Break even on the front end so you can keep buying traffic. Premium of the paid package was about 40% over membership cost.
numbers: $600 for six weeks versus $200/month; almost two-thirds took membership, about one in three paid $600 only.

### MZ-X-1e1cgIMvs#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:58
claim: The spa's experience business returns about 9:1 on acquisition cost while its membership business is about 1:1.
detail: Experiences average about $600 with roughly $60-$70 cost to acquire (as heard); growth through membership is preferred for scale.
numbers: Experience business about 9:1; membership business about 1:1; average experience $600.
