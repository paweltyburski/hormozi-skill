### YT-zNiXk_3C_Io#01 | DEFINITION | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A problem is anything that threatens the business's future survival, while a missed opportunity is something you are not doing that could expand the business.
detail: Problems: customer complaints about the product, a payment-processor relationship turning into a red flag, a sales team whose conversion is dropping. Missed opportunities: retargeting across platforms, a rebuilt email follow-up campaign, a new product or channel. Most owners file both on one undifferentiated to-do list, which breeds overwhelm. Opportunities are fun, new and tied to what you love (product people see product ideas, marketers see marketing ones); problems are unpleasant repair work.

### YT-zNiXk_3C_Io#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:24
claim: Chasing opportunities grows the business but also grows its unsolved problems; solving problems grows it through getting better rather than doing more.
detail: Strategy is described as allocating limited resources against unlimited opportunities. His advice is to put the biggest share of time on problems arising daily, since a business without problems grows by default. He also notes that small problems are easier to solve than big ones, so sequence them accordingly.

### YT-zNiXk_3C_Io#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:24
claim: Present versus future focus is a dichotomy to manage by percentage of attention, not a problem to solve once.
detail: Hormozi admits he over-indexes on the future (a large new project for 2025) and had cut his content output to nearly nothing for two weeks, with recording sessions underprepared, until his partner told him he was not engaged in the day-to-day. He now deliberately over-emphasises the present, since without today working the future never happens. A founder normally lives three, six or twelve months ahead; the risk is losing sight of today.
numbers: Roughly one recording session in two weeks while distracted.

### YT-zNiXk_3C_Io#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:00
claim: Show a number only if a change in it would change what the team does; ask what you would do if the metric were maxed out, and stop watching it if the answer is nothing.
detail: Reports from direct reports tend to over-include data because they know the boss likes numbers. Each metric should have a known behaviour it informs, and each meeting should end in a change of behaviour. Otherwise it is noise, and the team's limited attention is wasted.

### YT-zNiXk_3C_Io#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: management-ops | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:19
claim: A platform vendor proposed shorter content because a larger share of viewers finish it; Hormozi's answer was to ask how that metric ties to more or more valuable customers.
detail: He would have accepted the change had the vendor shown that higher average view or listen duration produces more shares. Without that link between the metric and customer acquisition or customer value, he does not care. The test is the link to getting more customers or making customers worth more.

### YT-zNiXk_3C_Io#06 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:12:07
claim: Fix each problem with a four-part record: current versus desired condition, the action as a hypothesis with its cost, whether the action was actually done, and whether the expected result occurred.
detail: Example: churn is 5%; hypothesis that a one-to-one outreach to each new customer will lower churn. Part three checks execution (you can reach the end of the period and find you never did it), and part four checks the outcome, because the action can be done at a cost in time and money while the metric stays the same. Opportunities, by contrast, are purely hypothetical projections of revenue uplift, while you will probably have significantly more data around problems.
numbers: Churn example at 5%.
### YT-zNiXk_3C_Io#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:07
claim: A second business grows faster than the first because you already know the solutions to the problems growth creates; the stall comes from unsolved problems you do not know how to fix.
detail: Owners drift to opportunities they know how to execute because problems are unpleasant and unfamiliar, and after enough rounds the accumulated problems stifle the company.

### YT-zNiXk_3C_Io#08 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:20
claim: Move all missed opportunities onto a separate shared idea list and revisit it only after current problems are solved; many ideas will lose their appeal by then.
detail: Hormozi keeps a company-wide "big money-making idea list". He has more ideas than capacity and gets to about one in 10 to 20. When you wait two months, you find that many ideas no longer look good, and the problem-solving gives better information about which are real. Do not pitch an idea to the team the same day, since you oversell it; sleep on it. If 90% would be dropped later, pushing them on the team was a mistake.
numbers: He implements roughly 1 in 10 to 20 ideas; about 90% would lose appeal after two months (his hypothetical).

### YT-zNiXk_3C_Io#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:20
claim: A 10% chance at a 100x payoff is only a good repeated bet if you can afford many tries; with limited capital the chance of ending with nothing is high.
detail: He agrees with Bezos that long-tail returns justify bold bets, but points out the flaw: in a casino with a $10 minimum and only $20, you get two swings, so a 1-in-10 bet loses entirely about 80% of the time (his round figures; the exact chance of losing both is 81%). Business owners have limited swings and resources, so the highest-probability bets are the problems in the business today, which pay off when solved. Only when current problems are solved should the idea list be reopened.
numbers: Two swings on a 1-in-10, 100x bet; he states about 20% win and 80% lose.

### YT-zNiXk_3C_Io#10 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:19:24
claim: Fixing onboarding in one portfolio company added roughly $3M profit, improved satisfaction, cut refunds and chargebacks, and lifted ascension rates by more than 40%.
detail: Presented as a problem that, once solved, immediately made more money; he says it should have been prioritised earlier because an early great experience compounds into reputation. Applies to physical products, software and services alike.
numbers: About $3M extra profit; ascension rate up over 40%; refunds and chargebacks reduced.
