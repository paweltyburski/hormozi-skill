### MZ-36FJXlTFzW4#01 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: scaling-constraints | fw: More Better New | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: When taking over a business, audit every conversion point (about 20 between lead and deal) and tweak six or seven of them.
detail: Moving a rate from 28% to 38% came from three or four small changes. The next stage, say 50% to 65%, is compounded the same way, adding percentage points step by step.
numbers: 28% to 38%; about 20 conversion points between pickup and deal; 50% to 65% as a +30% relative jump.

### MZ-36FJXlTFzW4#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Advanced businesses do not do fancier things; they do the basics the founder did alone, but at scale.
detail: A solo operator profits because he does everything right; trouble starts when hired helpers are not taught well or incentivised. Scaling is reproducing the founder's standard, not adding sophistication.

### MZ-36FJXlTFzW4#03 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: verticals | fw: Perceived Hidden Costs | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Ask the customer what sucks about the process, price the ones you control and wrap them into the offer.
detail: Sound more informed on the phone by naming the other costs the customer will face and covering them. He says wholesalers are lazy about this.

### MZ-36FJXlTFzW4#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:49
claim: Employer health coverage is a retention arbitrage: it costs the company little but employees value it far above its cost.
detail: A company paying about $400/month per person can retain staff against an offer of $15,000 more a year without cover, since staff have not priced the benefit.
numbers: about $5,000/year cost to employer versus about $10,000 perceived saving; $15,000 competing raise.
