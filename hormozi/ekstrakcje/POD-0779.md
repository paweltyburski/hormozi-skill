### POD-0779#01 | DEFINITION | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Eight Business Functions | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: He defines IT as how a business gathers, stores, analyses and displays data, and data as the base for prioritising resources.
detail: Spreadsheets are fine below roughly $3M revenue, sometimes up to $10-15M, since they are flexible and cheap, but they break easily, are hard to organise and permission-heavy. Data becomes a constraint, sometimes quickly with growth, prompting a CRM move.
numbers: spreadsheet empires work below about $3M, up to about $10-15M revenue.

### POD-0779#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:53
claim: Poor data lowers sale value: buyers ask for data first and discount a business run by gut.
detail: Messy data signals unprofessionalism, but more importantly implies decisions were made by intuition. Gut cannot beat data across departments as a company grows. Data must also be pushed to the owner in curated form.

### POD-0779#03 | CASE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:07
claim: Of four large IT implementations, two failed when he delegated them and two succeeded when he was closely involved.
detail: The first failure: told a tech specialist to set it up; the specialist interviewed department heads and built it without understanding the whole business. The next succeeded with his deep involvement. He then repeated delegation and it failed again. Like hiring a vendor for a custom workflow and staying absent. The person who knows the business best should shape what data is collected, start to finish.
numbers: 4 mega implementations: 2 good, 2 poor.

### POD-0779#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:50
claim: Early on a duct-tape techie is fine; for growth hire a deliberate IT lead who maps how each change affects other systems, and pay well.
detail: The deliberate type slows you down by drawing a flow chart of what a change touches. The yes-man who instantly does what you ask often upsets the rest of the team and breaks things. Strong IT people are high-leverage and one may suffice for a long time. Keep a frequent feedback loop so they think strategically.

### POD-0779#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:51
claim: Teams use an internal IT department like a vendor, so it needs prioritisation by value.
detail: Example: automating a 30-minute weekly task takes a day from a specialist paid about three times as much, equal to about 45 weeks of the requester's time on that task, with nothing gained. Prioritise by value.
numbers: 30 minutes/week task; one day of work by a role paid ~3x; about 45 weeks of the task's time.
