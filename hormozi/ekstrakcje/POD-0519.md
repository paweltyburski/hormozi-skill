### POD-0519#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: - | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:32
claim: Worked example of why to attack the weakest funnel step: in a 50% / 5% / 50% three-step funnel, adding 10 points to each step helps very differently.
detail: Lifting the 5% step to 15% triples the business; lifting either 50% step to 60% adds only 20%. Same effort, far larger return at the constraint.
numbers: 5% to 15% = 3x; 50% to 60% = 1.2x (+20%).

### POD-0519#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: scaling-constraints | also: - | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:37
claim: Sign you are working on the wrong problem: 16-hour days with no growth in revenue or business size.
detail: Founders tend to work on what they enjoy rather than the actual constraint, so they solve real but non-binding problems. When an owner says "I need more customers", the usual real problem is that existing customers keep leaving.

### POD-0519#03 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning, verticals | fw: 80/20 Customer Analysis | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:32
claim: Appointment-setting business (builds outbound teams for agencies, coaches, consultants): says it needs more customers, but its customers are low quality, not skilled or profitable enough, and leave early.
detail: Hormozi asked which past clients earned the most from the appointments set and were easiest to work with. Easiest path: niche down to that type and package offers and messaging for it. Higher-leverage path: partner with or learn the best client business and own the economics, since the setter keeps rebuilding the same work monthly and gets no credit for last month's teams.
applies_to: Service businesses of about $5-10M a year selling to weak customers.

### POD-0519#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:43
claim: If you must sell 10 new customers each month just to stay the same size, you are on a treadmill; choose a vehicle in which this month's work still pays in five years.
detail: Hormozi separates the quick fix (niche down, good for 1-2 years) from the 10-year play. Service fees carry a low multiple and a poor customer base is hard to sell; owning a share of a bigger business turns monthly effort into stacking assets.

### POD-0519#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:35
claim: Reasoning for the setter: a $5-10M appointment-setting firm becomes one department in a $50-100M business, whose outbound marketing alone might cost $5-10M a year.
detail: He proposes a 50-50 partnership with the best client, or spending a year or two learning that business to own both sides. Teams then add up: building 50 outbound reps a month inside one company gives 300 still-working reps after six months, instead of resetting each month.
numbers: Marketing department of a $50M business is roughly $5-10M a year; 50 reps per month gives 300 after 6 months.

### POD-0519#06 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: lead-getters, retention-ltv | fw: Buy vs Build | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:23
claim: HVAC marketing agency with about 50 small clients in 50 local markets: clients are unsophisticated, do not call leads fast enough and churn in three to five months.
detail: Hormozi's view: the agency's skill equals that of a $50-100M HVAC company, but it captures only the service fee while the client earns 5-10x the fee. Options: start an HVAC firm itself, partner with a 10+ year industry veteran, partner with its top clients for a share, or buy an HVAC company and use the marketing skill on it. Stepping back first can be faster for the 100-story goal.
numbers: Clients typically earn 5-10x the agency fee; churn in 3-5 months; example share when partnering with a strong top client: perhaps 30% rather than 50%.

### POD-0519#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:16
claim: The fastest way to build a $10M business is often not the fastest way to build a $100M one, because the foundation differs.
detail: A 10-storey foundation cannot carry 100 storeys, so entrepreneurs who picked the wrong vehicle are stuck. A 100-storey foundation can take as long as an entire 10-storey building, so rivals appear to move faster above ground.

### POD-0519#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:52
claim: The size of your slice of the pie matters more than its shape; trading ownership for a much larger business is the better bet.
detail: Hormozi spent years obsessed with owning 100%. Partner terms for a veteran might be 5%-50% of the deal depending on experience; the more people you make rich, the more you make.
numbers: Average founder at IPO owns about 12.5% of the company; a veteran partner might receive 5%-50%.

### POD-0519#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: verticals | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Riches in the Niches | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:38
claim: Agencies that sell lead generation or outbound/closing teams plug naturally into a client's business and are better absorbed or partnered; creative and branding shops make better stand-alone companies.
detail: Branding clients have higher LTV and more valuable agencies result. Lead-gen firms alone rarely become valuable, except when they own a large media asset that produces free leads (for example ranking first nationally for a service and selling the leads).

### POD-0519#10 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals, content-brand | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:14:36
claim: Creator with about 30-40M subscribers and no product: focused on better videos (97 to 99 of 100) while the goal was more money; sponsorships and ad revenue are the least efficient way to monetise.
detail: Sponsors earn a large arbitrage on the creator's audience: a $100,000 payment per video might earn the sponsor about $1M. The creator already has a marketing department of a $100M+ business (about 100M views a month with no new posts) but zero sales, product or customer success. The constraint is going from 0 to 20 in those, not 97 to 99 in media.
numbers: Sponsor pays $100,000 per video, may earn about $1M; roughly 100M views a month with no new posts.

### POD-0519#11 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: Buy vs Build | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:06
claim: Creator path 1: take equity from recurring advertisers instead of cash, so the creator joins an existing company with product, sales and service already built.
detail: Even 10-20% of a business worth $100M that grows to $200-300M beats being paid video to paycheck. Owning the product also lets the creator integrate it into content and build a consistent brand association rather than hard ads for whoever pays that month.
numbers: Example: 10% of a $100M company grown to $200-$300M.

### POD-0519#12 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:56
claim: Creator path 2: clone the best-selling advertiser product category, fix the complaints seen in buyers' comments, put your name on it, and recruit the advertiser's top executives with equity.
detail: Like Amazon, copy what already sells. Offer 70% to the creator and give each of three senior executives about 10%, since they earn little or no equity now and know that around 50% of the sales come from the creator's channel. Fastest; doing everything yourself is longer but bigger. Each choice trades off new skills needed.
numbers: Example split: creator owns about 70%, three executives get about 10% each; about 50% of the advertiser's sales come from the creator.

### POD-0519#13 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: paid-ads, verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:03
claim: Creator path 3: if the creator only wants media, rebrand as a high-end creative ad agency, since viral-video skill is worth more than audience size.
detail: Influencers are paid by free impressions to their audience, but the real money comes when a brand puts large ad spend behind a proven winner, moving it from 5M audience views to 100M cold views. Ad spend has no reach limit while profitable, so skill is monetised without audience limits. This is the lowest operational risk.

### POD-0519#14 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:21:42
claim: Problem-framing questions he uses: 1) what problem are we trying to solve, 2) over what time horizon, and playing out scenarios to their natural cap, 3) which problem would we prefer or be better equipped to solve.

### POD-0519#15 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:14

detail: One company is even easier because the back end is standardised and there is one point of contact. Wealthier people are paid on work done years earlier, others only on last month's.

### POD-0519#17 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:26:30
claim: Three founders is the optimal number (Y Combinator research), with one handling operations and finance, one product and industry depth, one marketing and acquisition.
detail: The first split into thirds is the largest dilution (two thirds given away at once). The alternative is to build all three skills yourself and keep 100%, which costs years: compare 10 years to gain all three against 3 years for one skill and 7 years building with partners.
numbers: Three founders; two thirds given away on day one in a three-way split.
