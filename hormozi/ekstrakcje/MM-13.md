### MM-13#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Decoy Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Mentor John's 22 tanning salons used a $5 five-day VIP pass that converted most visitors into $19.99/month memberships once they learned one session can't give a real tan.
detail: $5 VIP gift cards went on car windshields. Customers don't know how long tanning takes, so staff asked what shade they wanted and gave the "turkey talk": you can't cook a turkey faster by cranking the heat — you burn the outside and leave the inside raw; tanning likewise needs multiple sessions. Then: "That shade needs this many sessions — $25 each, or take your $5 off a $19.99/month unlimited membership." Nearly everyone converted to EFT (recurring billing).
numbers: $5 pass; $25/session vs $19.99/month unlimited.

### MM-13#02 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals, paid-ads | fw: Decoy Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:35
claim: When Gym Launch's ads stopped working, a "Free 21-Day Transformation" decoy beat every tested offer, with most people choosing the premium.
detail: Three or four years in, his team tested new offers across five mini beta groups. The winner, on arrival, showed two options. Free: one workout a week plus an at-home nutrition course. Premium: unlimited workouts, personalised nutrition, grocery instructions, and a guarantee of 10–15 lb lost in 21 days or another 21 days free. People came for free and bought the premium.
numbers: 5 beta groups; 10–15 lb in 21 days guarantee.

### MM-13#03 | DEFINITION | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: Decoy Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:38
claim: A decoy offer advertises something free or discounted; when leads ask about it, you present a far more valuable premium offer beside it, so everyone buys one or the other.
detail: The advertised item can be free or cheap (e.g., $21 transformation; credit the $21 toward a $421 program and pay $400 more). The premium adds features, benefits, bonuses and guarantees; side by side, its value is obvious. It is an assumed close — every lead buys something — so acquisition becomes cheap and profitable for any business. Steps (00:05:36): (1) advertise a lesser, smaller or simpler version as the decoy; (2) present both, emphasise the premium, and close with its bonuses, value and guarantees. The model lives entirely on the upsell.
numbers: 70–80% of leads should take the premium when structured properly.

### MM-13#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: offers, paid-ads | fw: Decoy Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:36
claim: Upselling is legitimate as long as the advertised free thing is still offered — so advertise the result, not the vehicle or extras.
detail: If the ad says only "Free 21-Day Transformation", you choose vehicles and prices in the presentation; list the premium inclusions in the ad and you must give them free. The attraction offer only has to win engagement, so sell the outcome and benefits, not features (00:07:46).
quote: Upselling is as American as apple pie.

### MM-13#05 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: offers, product-delivery | fw: Decoy Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:46
claim: Build the decoy by stripping the premium: fewer components, an older model, or less personalisation — remove the costliest features.
detail: Start from what you already sell and peel off the most expensive-to-deliver features, as far as is reasonable (00:13:59). The decoy exists for contrast.

### MM-13#06 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Decoy Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:36
claim: Lemonade-stand example: any business can pair an unappealing free version with an obviously better premium.
detail: Ad: "Free week of lemonade for first-time customers." Free: warm, powdered, artificially sweetened, may upset digestion. Premium: organic, vegan, gluten-free, imported Italian lemons, cold-distilled, shipped to your door. Everyone takes the premium.

### MM-13#08 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing, paid-ads | fw: Decoy Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:46
claim: One discount can be advertised four ways — percent off, dollars off, free time, or bundle price.
detail: Lawn care at $100/month sold as a $900 annual prepay: "25% off", "$300 off", "three months free", or "one year for $900 instead of $1,200". Useful because decoys are often discounts.

### MM-13#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: Decoy Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:46
claim: If many people take the decoy, it is too good and the premium not good enough — widen the contrast until the premium is obvious.
detail: Bigger contrast means more premium buyers. You should make money fast; if not, increase the contrast (00:13:59). He says discounted offers have higher "up shove" rates than free ones (garbled, probably show-up rates; unclear in audio), so if you have that problem, use a small paid version such as $21 (00:07:46).

### MM-13#10 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Decoy Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:51
claim: If you have the rate problems he names (garbled in the transcript, probably show rate; unclear in audio), present the premium offer first where possible; almost no one remembers what they opted in for, only that it solved their problem.
detail: If a lead asks about the decoy you are legally obliged to present it, and if they remember it, tell them. Leading with the cheap option works against price anchoring; if you insist, use the sorting question.

### MM-13#11 | SCRIPT | topic: money-models | also: sales-process, objections-closing | fw: Decoy Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:51
claim: Ask "Are you here for free stuff or lasting results?" to earn permission to pitch the premium; if they want free, present the decoy and contrast immediately, then ask which gets them there faster.
detail: (1) Sorting question — nearly all say results, so go straight to the premium; if they say yes, stop and close. (2) If "free stuff", describe the decoy and, without pausing for a yes, say "and…" and move into the premium. (3) After both, ask "Which will get you to your goal faster?", "Which would you prefer?" or "Which is more likely to help you reach your goal?" Everyone picks the premium. (4) Close. Asking before offering lets them ethically choose the premium themselves.

### MM-13#12 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Decoy Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:31
claim: Present the premium with calm certainty, as if choosing it is a formality — tone and body language signal what to pick.
detail: Be genuinely excited that it is better for them; talk as if they'll obviously take it ("this is where everyone gets results — not sure why we offer the other one"). Then "Let me get your ID and card." No hype; friendly, almost bored. Avoid questions inviting a no, such as "You don't want this, do you?"

### MM-13#13 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Decoy Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:25
claim: When someone takes the decoy, surprise them with a few low-cost premium features — it builds goodwill, proves your sincerity, and creates the next sales moment.
detail: "I'll throw this in even though it's premium, because I want you to get results." Gym example (00:04:25): a one-workout-a-week member gets a free nutrition consult in two days, where you sell about $300 of supplements. Decoy buyers are still customers — upsell them again, e.g., halfway through.
numbers: ~$300 supplement sale at the bonus consult.
