### SC-02#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: The $100M Scaling Roadmap splits growth into ten stages by headcount; at each stage, each of eight business functions has one constraint and one step that graduates you to the next stage.
detail: The stages: 0 Improvise (no company yet), 1 Monetize (solo, first dollar), 2 Advertise (solo plus freelancers), 3 Stabilize (1–4 employees), 4 Prioritize (5–9), 5 Productize (10–19), 6 Optimize (20–49), 7 Categorize (50–99), 8 Specialize (100–249), 9 Capitalize (250–500).
Each stage lists headcount, the founder's role, leadership structure and how many companies are that size. Then, for product, marketing, sales, customer service, IT, recruiting, HR and finance, it gives the constraint and the step that graduates you. A one-line "bottom line" sums up each stage. Later stages are more complex and get longer training.

### SC-02#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:50
claim: The founder's role changes at every stage, from researcher to chairman, as layers of leadership build up underneath.
detail: Role and leadership by stage:
- 0: researcher (you know nothing).
- 1: starter.
- 2: doer, "in the dirt", leading only freelancers.
- 3: trainer of the first full-time hires.
- 4: manager of the first team layer.
- 5: director — about 1.5 layers, two managers plus some direct reports.
- 6: leader — two full layers, directors and managers.
- 7: executive — three layers.
- 8: president — four layers, including a full executive team.
- 9: chairman — division leaders or CEOs run mini-businesses, and you allocate resources between them.

### SC-02#05 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:50
claim: Stage 0 Improvise: you have nothing, and you graduate by getting people to try something you give away free.
detail: Constraint → graduation step:
- Product: nothing to sell → make something free.
- Marketing: nobody knows about it → tell people about the free thing.
- Sales: you cannot sell → get people to try it free.
- Customer service: no customers → serve the free ones, for free.
- IT: no tech → computer, internet, microphone, phone, email.
- Recruiting: you do not know how to work → recruit yourself and build work capacity.
- HR: personal and business assets not separated → form an entity.
- Finance: personal money is business money → open a business bank account, fund it, write off expenses.
Bottom line: nothing is happening yet.

### SC-02#06 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: sales-process | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:08
claim: Stage 1 Monetize: the business makes no money yet, and you graduate (and officially become a company) by making the first sale.
detail: Constraint → graduation step:
- Product: not good enough to sell → fix it into a V1 using feedback from free users.
- Marketing: nobody knows you have paid stuff → tell people, and use free ad credits.
- Sales: you cannot sell → use free customers' results to sell paid ones; offer to solve problems for money, no scripts yet.
- Customer service: free users do not use or like it → talk to them to find the hidden costs.
- IT: no basic software or social accounts → find free tools via groups, forums and search.
- Recruiting: missing skills or time → friends, family, freelancer platforms.
- HR: freelancers do not deliver → basic vendor agreements and expectations.
- Finance: no way to collect money → set up a payment processor and get the first deposit.

### SC-02#07 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:13
claim: Stage 2 Advertise: new customers arrive inconsistently because the solo founder advertises inconsistently, and you graduate by letting more people know about you, every day.
detail: Constraint → graduation step:
- Product: paying customers expect more, and it is unreliable → make it good enough in an unscalable way.
- Marketing: inconsistent lead flow → Rule of 100, advertising daily.
- Sales: inconsistent conversions → a setting and closing script (CLOSER).
- Customer service: paying customers complain → a method for disappointed customers (the "angry boat"), plus testimonials via the Epiphany Bridge script.
- IT: no money for tech → free tools, free trials and credits.
- Recruiting: part-timers are overwhelmed → convert them to full-time, or post on job boards.
- HR: full-timers treated as contractors → W-2 for employees, 1099 for real contractors (US).
- Finance: no tracking or tax plan → QuickBooks or equivalent, quarterly tax savings, check the bank daily.

### SC-02#09 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: hiring-talent | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:16
claim: Stage 3 Stabilize (1–4 employees): too much work for one person, and you graduate by getting help (the first couple of people on board).
detail: Constraint → graduation step:
- Product: no time to fix everything → fix the top complaint.
- Marketing: warm leads used up, new ones colder → long-term nurture by email or content.
- Sales: time wasted on bad leads → basic qualification, outsourced setting and calendaring.
- Customer service: new customers feel lost → consistent onboarding.
- IT: unused tools piling up → keep the useful ones, cut the rest, and build a basic pipeline (come in, schedule, show, convert), even on paper.
- Recruiting: weak applicants → learn to write job descriptions and review résumés.
- HR: taxes not withheld → payroll provider (avoids liens; stop paying via Venmo or PayPal).
- Finance: informal payments → processing, bookkeeping, invoice and payment management.

### SC-02#10 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:56
claim: Stage 4 Prioritize (5–9 employees): you are trying to be everything to everyone, and you graduate by niching down and picking one customer, or best customers, to serve.
detail: Constraint → graduation step:
- Product: feedback from any paying customer pulls it in too many directions → a specialized V2 priced for a niched avatar. Most go wide when they should go deep.
- Marketing: too many unqualified leads → better, more creative free stuff for volume, plus qualification friction so leads self-select.
- Sales: speed to contact drops, no metrics → manual KPI spreadsheet.
- Customer service: no metrics → KPIs, and customer notes centralized in a CRM.
- IT: team on different tools → shared platforms, password manager, project-management and chat tools (monday.com, Asana, Slack, LastPass).
- Recruiting: bad candidates → proper interviews and reference checks.
- HR: unclear conduct (PTO, sick days, dress) → handbook and policies.
- Finance: lumpy cash from one-time costs → P&L, cash-flow statement, basic insurance.

### SC-02#12 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: paid-ads, lead-getters | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:43
claim: At 5–9 employees a second acquisition channel is premature (owners think they need one when they are doing the first poorly); at 50–99 he recommends looking at a second channel, once relying on one makes lead flow volatile, funded by referrals and segmented nurture.
detail: At 5–9 people, owners often think they need a second channel when they are simply doing the first one badly. At 5–9 the marketing step is better free offers and qualification friction. At 50–99 (ts 00:23:48), when one channel makes lead flow volatile:
1. Build a much stronger referral process.
2. Segment nurture by lead score (leads are scored by now but not yet emailed differently), which raises email returns.
Both cost almost nothing, and that cash funds the second channel. Choose the platform most similar to the one that already works.

### SC-02#13 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: money-models | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:14:54
claim: Stage 5 Productize (10–19 employees): low LTV:CAC means too little money per customer, and you graduate by selling customers a second, premium product.
detail: Constraint → graduation step:
- Product: customers have nothing else to buy → connect customer success to product to learn what to build next.
- Marketing: qualified leads too expensive → spend more, now that LTV:CAC is higher; add lead scoring, attribution and conversion-rate optimization.
- Sales: unrealistic promises cause refunds → sales aids (VSLs, webinars, decks), weekly game tape, NPS/CSAT/refund rate by rep.
- Customer service: inconsistent answers → CS playbook and an ascension path.
- IT: staff on personal devices leave with leads → company-owned hardware and software, basic dashboard.
- Recruiting: senior roles hard to fill → promote from within, referral bonuses equal to cost to acquire talent.
- HR: bad firings expose you → termination policy and process.
- Finance: unsure how much to invest → budget, save for big expenses, simple forecasting, track write-offs.

### SC-02#14 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models, paid-ads | fw: LTV:CAC | novelty: canon | ts: 00:16:26
claim: A second product sold to the same customers raises LTV:CAC, which is what lets you afford expensive qualified leads.
detail: At 10–19 people the niched offer pulls in qualified leads, but they are expensive and cap ad spend. The diagnosis is too little money per customer, so the fix is on the product side: a second, premium product. It raises customer value, which then funds more ad spend, lead scoring, attribution and conversion work.

### SC-02#15 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: sales-process, retention-ltv | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:16:26
claim: Hold a weekly game-tape review across sales, customer service and marketing, so what is promised matches what is delivered, and back it with NPS, CSAT and refund rate by rep.
detail: This fixes customers being sold unrealistic expectations, which leads to refunds, bad reviews and inconsistent CS answers. The departments review real interactions together and align on promises versus delivery. The metrics are Net Promoter Score, customer satisfaction and refund rate, ideally cut by sales rep to see whose customers are unhappy.
applies_to: Productize stage (10–19 employees).

### SC-02#16 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: - | fw: Peter Principle | novelty: canon | ts: 00:18:11
claim: To fill manager and senior roles at 10–19 people, promote from within deliberately (not to people's point of incompetence), and pay referral bonuses as large as your cost to acquire talent.
detail: The Peter Principle is promoting people until they reach their level of incompetence. Cost to acquire talent (CAT) is the hiring version of CAC. Be willing to spend it, and pay employees a referral bonus equal to it.
applies_to: Productize stage.

### SC-02#17 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: retention-ltv | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:19:28
claim: Stage 6 Optimize (20–49 employees): everything is inefficient, and you graduate by growing through doing everything better rather than more.
detail: Constraint → graduation step:
- Product: two products split attention → an improvement pipeline for each, with separate owners.
- Marketing: colder traffic raises CAC → an ad assembly process for more creative volume.
- Sales: close rate drops and gets inconsistent (refunds fall, but so do closes) → training systems, one-on-one coaching, team cadence.
- Customer service: churn rises → segment customers by cohort and activation point.
- IT: leavers take IP, data and access → cybersecurity, password protection, onboarding and offboarding.
- Recruiting: roles outgrow your network → headhunting, multi-stage interviews.
- HR: senior talent expects a full package → benefits, retirement, advanced compensation.
- Finance: spending without growth, money missing → scale AR/AP, a balance sheet, quarterly taxes.

### SC-02#18 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:22:40
claim: Stage 7 Categorize (50–99 employees): every system is overwhelmed and the business is disorganized, and you graduate by triaging everything into proper buckets.
detail: Constraint → graduation step:
- Product: improvements too slow → focused, feature-specific teams, one problem at a time.
- Marketing: one channel makes lead flow volatile → a second channel, funded by referrals and nurture segmented by lead score.
- Sales: leads not worked consistently → better schedule coverage, a lead-nurture checklist, and kudos for high show rates as well as close rates.
- Customer service: reactive firefighting → a proactive journey tied to milestones, with a focus on renewals and ascensions.
- IT: data and access scattered → network tech, cloud, a data host, centralized hardware.
- Recruiting: too many open roles to track → an applicant tracking system (a CRM for candidates) and specialist recruiting firms.
- HR: scattered employee data → an HRIS (needed around 100 people).
- Finance: the team spends badly → departmental budgets, cards with limits, expense approval, insurance, projections.

### SC-02#19 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:27:31
claim: Stage 8 Specialize (100–249 employees): nobody can know everything, and you graduate by creating dedicated people and teams for specific tasks.
detail: Constraint → graduation step:
- Product: feature bloat overwhelms customers → survey them, prune, and sell the pruned parts as add-ons.
- Marketing: broad creative dilutes the brand and raises CAC → standardized messaging, big free value, positive brand associations.
- Sales: premium-product close rate drops → best leads routed to the best closers.
- Customer service: product journeys conflict → one blended journey with proactive cross-sells.
- IT: departments outgrow general software → specialized stacks per department, ticketing, SLAs.
- Recruiting: inconsistent standards hurt culture → standardized selection, with a final fit check by the founder.
- HR: performance falls and complaints rise → group onboarding, documented performance management, liability insurance, reinforced values.
- Finance: paying retail, idle cash → renegotiate rates, specialist finance roles (tax), external audits.

### SC-02#23 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:31:49
claim: At 100+ employees, use your size to renegotiate every vendor rate, hire specialized finance roles, and bring in outside auditors, because much financial wrongdoing comes from inside.
detail: 1. Renegotiate everything; few companies reach this size, so expect special treatment.
2. Add specialists, especially in tax, to reduce risk.

### SC-02#24 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: finance-exit, hiring-talent | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:32:55
claim: Stage 9 Capitalize (250–500 employees): you are not sure where the next big growth will come from, and you graduate by making a big bet on the future.
detail: Constraint → graduation step:
- Product: changing the cash cow only hurts it → find the next rising star through M&A or R&D.
- Marketing: attribution and compliance get hard → brand-first omnichannel advertising, blended ROAS, a compliance process.
- Sales: too many products for one rep → salespeople specialized by product, lead type, process and territory.
- Customer service: support breaks under volume → triage into specialized reps.
- IT: an outdated stack → specialized tech roles, segmented stacks, possibly an ERP.
- Recruiting (the transcript mislabels it "marketing"): top talent responds only to the founder → the founder recruits stars personally.
- HR: employee churn grows → strategic workforce planning.
- Finance: books not credible to lenders, investors or buyers → prep for sale.

### SC-02#25 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery, finance-exit | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:32:55
claim: At 250+ people, changing the cash cow makes it worse, so growth must come from a new "rising star", bought through M&A or built through R&D and planned one to three years ahead.
detail: The cash cow made the company's money and name, so changes now tend to damage it, yet you still need something new to grow. At this size, moving the needle means capturing a whole market or trend. So bet on what the market will need one, two or three years out, and have it ready when the market gets there. Funding that bet is why finance at this stage turns to prep for sale and credit lines.
applies_to: Capitalize stage.

### SC-02#29 | BENCHMARK | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: Strategic Workforce Planning | novelty: canon | ts: 00:37:03
claim: Using 250 employees and about 5% monthly churn as his example, he says you could be losing 12.5 to 25 people a month and recruiting that many just to break even, which is why strategic workforce planning is the step.
detail: Employee churn works like customer churn. With 250 employees the speaker says you "might lose 5%" and then quotes 12.5 to 25 people lost per month, all to be recruited just to break even (5% alone is 12.5; the 25 end would imply 10%, which he does not state). Strategic workforce planning forecasts the people side of growth. It asks: what capacity will we have, whom must we backfill, where are the skill gaps, which redundancies do we want, and can we move people around?

### SC-02#31 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: Prep for Sale | novelty: canon | ts: 00:38:54
claim: At the Capitalize stage, a large company's finances must look credible enough for others to lend, invest or buy, so you go through what he calls a prep for sale (some steps are optional, as he says "consider").
detail: The money may be needed to fund the next rising star.
1. Get a quality of earnings (QoE) report.
2. Talk to investment bankers, who will tell you everything wrong with the business.
3. Audit the capital structure and entities, with legal review.
4. Consider hedging big investments against market trends or commodity prices to smooth profit.
The payoff is credibility, e.g. a $20M credit line for a big move or as a reserve. Cleaning the house always improves it.
numbers: Example $20M credit line.
applies_to: Capitalize stage.
