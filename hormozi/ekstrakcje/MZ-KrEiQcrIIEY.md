### MZ-KrEiQcrIIEY#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Education is worth nothing the day after it is learned, so a membership must sell consumable inputs, not just knowledge.
detail: Success means the customer no longer needs the lesson. Sell the system once, then sell the monthly inputs the system needs, like ink after a printer. Sticky versions: hair extensions after training, supplements after sales training.

### MZ-KrEiQcrIIEY#02 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: verticals, paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:49
claim: Gym Launch handed members pre-tested ad creative every month as its consumable value.
detail: The company spent about $50K per shoot, produced ads and tested them in 10-20 representative markets, sampling markets by ethnicity, country and operator quality. From about 100 ads it kept the top five and gave them to licensees, who could run proven ads instead of making their own. Price captures testing losses avoided plus money made.
numbers: ~$50K per shoot; 100 ads tested, top 5 handed out; 700+ locations; 10-20 test markets.

### MZ-KrEiQcrIIEY#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: product-delivery | also: offers | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:32
claim: Value test for membership content: hand them the result if you can; else do the work for them; at level two, carry the risk too.
detail: Handing over customers was the ideal but out of scope, so the next step was winning pages and ads tested by the company ("a winning scratch-off ticket") for a machine the customer paid once to learn. Gym Launch fronted labour and cash.

### MZ-KrEiQcrIIEY#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: verticals | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:23
claim: A wholesaling educator emailed members a monthly PDF of pre-vetted properties matching their deal specs, so one deal pays for the year.
detail: His team found, sorted, listed and rated properties; members only had to visit the address.

### MZ-KrEiQcrIIEY#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:23
claim: A print-on-demand educator kept churn near 2% by showing weekly or monthly, with his tool, trending products to print and delivering the sorted list.
detail: The rule behind it: pre-chew the work. There is always more research, analysis and distillation you can repackage into something easy to use.
numbers: ~2% churn (period unclear in audio).

### MZ-KrEiQcrIIEY#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: offers | fw: Primary vs Secondary Features | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:35
claim: Adding features that members do not use does not add value and can reduce it.
detail: Community itself is consumable and measured by willingness to pay, so extra components must be things people actually consume.

### MZ-KrEiQcrIIEY#07 | FRAMEWORK | topic: sales-process | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:09:01
claim: Three-level accountability pitch: peer (same step, same stage), alumni (one step ahead cheering) and expert (has solved your problem many times).
detail: In weight loss about 75% of what was sold was accountability, not information, because customers know what to do and don't do it. Hormozi told the coaches on this call that they have all three: other members, finished testimonials and themselves.
numbers: ~75% of weight-loss pitch spent on accountability.
applies_to: Communities, coaching and programmes where adherence is the issue.
### MZ-KrEiQcrIIEY#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:21
claim: "Magical product" test: the complexity happens in the background and the customer's effort is one press of a button.
detail: A friend from software uses this lens; giving a recipe when buyers lack flour, eggs and oven is the gap where money is made. Figure it out for them first (level one), then at scale (level two).
