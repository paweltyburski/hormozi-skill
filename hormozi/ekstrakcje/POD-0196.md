### POD-0196#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: sales-process | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:52
claim: Close rates on remote phone sales climb after roughly 20 pickups, because fitness pros used to in-person selling must learn tonality and energy management on the phone.
detail: The rise is gradual, not a switch at call 20. The words are easy; how they are said separates good from bad sellers. With 10-15 calls a day, call 14 must be as fresh as call 2.
numbers: About 20 phone pickups to cross the learning threshold; 10-15 calls per day.

### POD-0196#02 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: money-models | fw: Continuity Bonus Offer | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:04
claim: Offer two options at the close (about $199 up front or a continuity membership), so the question becomes which one, not whether.
detail: Most buyers chose continuity over the upfront fee. Closing one or two a day gives 45-60 continuity members at about $200 a month by month end, at roughly 80% gross margin after paying a trainer about 20%. It also gives a built-in down-sell.
numbers: $199-$200 one-time or about $200/month; 45-60 members after a month; 80% gross margin.

### POD-0196#03 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Anchor Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:51
claim: Set the gap between the two options by your goal: a very high option A pushes people into continuity; equal prices push cash up front.
detail: To get about 90% into continuity, make option A $500-$700 so option B looks like a great deal in a bargain-minded market. To get more cash, price A and B equally (for example, $200 now with no continuity or $49 a week for the first month), and most pay the $200. Cash-strapped owners should push continuity.
numbers: Option A $500-$700 for ~90% continuity take-up; equal prices for cash up front.

### POD-0196#04 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: Feature Downsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:50
claim: To grant a lower price ethically, trade something back: waive the commitment, ask for a testimonial, or ask for a friend within four weeks.
detail: First check you would still accept the member at the lower price. Do not strip the daily check-in service, since it keeps customers. Offer in steps: $49 a week for a 12-week commitment (half of the $99 a week normal remote price); $39 with a testimonial; $29 with testimonial plus a friend. Asking something back makes repeated refusal sound unreasonable.
numbers: Normal remote price $99/week; $49, $39 and $29 per week as stepped concessions; 12-week commitment; friend within first 4 weeks.

### POD-0196#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:06
claim: Within the community, revenue per phone pickup is going up by about $5, which he says is about 10% a day.
detail: The best sellers moved from $199 to $299. Gyms were selling $200-$300 on a one-call close to cold traffic, with six to ten pickups a day. Half-off offers did not generate lead flow; lead flow came from free offers, which can still be upsold to a superior paid programme.
numbers: +$5 per pickup is about +10%; $199 to $299 price; 6-10 pickups a day.
### POD-0196#06 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:11:23
claim: Treat a 90-day window like a launch: Orange Theory requires about 250 sign-ups before a studio opens, and 250 remote members at $200 a month could be a business in itself.
detail: Gym Launch worked on 90-day timetables. Closing two or three people a day into continuity builds quickly.
numbers: 250 sign-ups before opening; 250 x $200/month = $50,000 a month.
