### YT-aO56wn5sBSA#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: You can only buy things or lend; lending risk drops with three features: top of the capital stack, transparency, and liquidity.
detail: Top of the stack means you are the preferred creditor paid first if things go wrong, as a mortgage bank is. Transparency means access to books and reporting. Liquidity means you can get in and out quickly. These came from a mentor who sold a company for $3B. Pair them with yield, equity growth, tax advantage and capital preservation as the four lenses on every investment.

### YT-aO56wn5sBSA#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:07
claim: Very rich people care more about return of capital than return on capital, because zero times any number is zero.
detail: One bad loss can erase a lifetime of good decisions, so downside is considered first. He says this is repeated by everyone worth hundreds of millions he knows.

### YT-aO56wn5sBSA#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:13
claim: He puts cash from his portfolio companies into three buckets: index funds (probably around half of his wealth), multifamily real estate, and cash plus speculation.
detail: The companies (about $85M a year in revenue then) are asset-light and cash generating. Indexes are truly passive and let him allocate cash almost immediately each month. Multifamily is used because he would need to buy about two single-family homes a day to place the cash. The third bucket is dry powder for opportunistic deals such as a $2.5M 90-day note expected to return $300,000-$400,000, deals that arrive because people know he holds cash. The risk sits in his operating companies, not in this portfolio.
numbers: indexes probably about 50% of wealth; $2.5M note returns $300,000-$400,000 in about 90 days.
### YT-aO56wn5sBSA#04 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:56
claim: Allocate new cash quickly, since waiting for deals leaves money idle, but avoid rushing decisions.
detail: Real estate has a delay for finding, offering, negotiating, financing and escrow. A house rule he quotes is that mistakes love a rushed decision.

### YT-aO56wn5sBSA#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:25
claim: If you qualify as a real estate professional, he says you can take depreciation against regular income; in his example he might take about 40% accelerated depreciation on a $10M building, which would probably save about $1.8M of tax.
detail: He gives the requirement as 750 or more hours a year. Example: a $10M building and $10M of income, with about 40% accelerated depreciation ($4M), leaves $6M taxable. He says this would probably save about $1.8M, which he can put into indexes or other investments.
numbers: 750+ hours/yr; about $4M depreciation ("might"); probably about $1.8M tax saved.
applies_to: US high-income real estate investors.
### YT-aO56wn5sBSA#06 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:50
claim: Borrow against an index portfolio at low rates to fund other investments.
detail: Secured loans of 50-60% of portfolio value at about 1.5%. On $10M that is $5-6M at an annual carry of $50,000-$75,000, versus a $500,000 return if invested at 10%. He also quotes diversification as protection against ignorance, which he says does not apply as fully when you actively run niche companies you understand.
numbers: loan-to-value 50-60%; rate about 1.5%; $5M carry about $50,000-$75,000 a year.
