### YT-h6y0nYVZgwE#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:06
claim: Spend the first four hours of every day on whatever the current biggest problem is, and switch topics only once it is solved.
detail: The protected block is for the constraint itself: practising closing if sales is weak. Most people skip it and firefight all day. Looking back after a few months, the only real progress he has to show is the work done in that block, while fires get put out anyway and tend to fade if ignored.
numbers: 4 hours every day, ideally before staff arrive or after they leave.
### YT-h6y0nYVZgwE#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:55
claim: Whatever lead method you rely on, convert it into daily hours of work, about four hours a day.
detail: For paid ads the daily work is the creative side: scripting, hooks, studying winning ads in your niche, learning a phone editing app. The platform handles distribution, but money is spent every day before anything else, because ads are how the message reaches people. Under roughly $100,000 a month, the main job is simply letting people know you exist.
numbers: Rule of thumb 4 hours per day per activity. Audience poll: about a quarter to a third rely mainly on ads, about 10% on outreach.

### YT-h6y0nYVZgwE#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:38
claim: For a local business, content mainly raises conversion of people already referred or advertised to, not new customer volume.
detail: Platform algorithms distribute globally, so most viewers sit outside the catchment area. Its local use is as proof: a prospect reached by outreach, referral or ad clicks to your page and sees a friendly, real place near them. A business with no content should post about one piece a week to look alive; going beyond that makes content the primary channel, which needs volume.
numbers: 1 piece per week as a minimum; a baseline of 2 pieces per day if content is the main channel; his own team posts about 400 pieces a week.
applies_to: Local service businesses such as gyms

### YT-h6y0nYVZgwE#04 | FRAMEWORK | topic: content-brand | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:05:41
claim: Brand is built in three ways only: what you say, what others say, and what customers experience; the third decides whether the first two pay off.
detail: At the start there are no customers, so you speak. The first buyers leave reviews, which reassure the next ones. The first two are advertising. The third is the product. If the delivered experience matches the promise, buyers become reviewers and the cycle strengthens; if it falls short, the earlier claims backfire. For a local business, brand is therefore delivery.

### YT-h6y0nYVZgwE#10 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: scaling-constraints | also: sales-process, pricing | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:25:19
claim: Diagnose the business in order: awareness, then schedule, show, close, then price, then churn.
detail: Check traffic first. If leads come but do not schedule, show or close, that step gets the daily block. If everyone closes but money is thin, price is too low. If price is high but customers drain out, improve the product and service.

### YT-h6y0nYVZgwE#11 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:38
claim: In a sale, whoever is asking questions is winning; sidestep an objection by asking what the prospect wants out of the question.
detail: A prospect cannot oppose a question, only a statement. Many questions (certifications, workout style) were suggested by a friend, and the buyer cannot say why they matter. Ask which certifications they seek, or what their ideal workout is and how that works for them now. Often the answer reveals why they are really there. Practise by role-play with a friend for fast feedback.

### YT-h6y0nYVZgwE#13 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:23:41
claim: Hold exit interviews, run a pulse check every two weeks, and track attendance to cut churn.
detail: In exit interviews about half of leavers can be saved by admitting a gap and offering the support they lacked. Contact customers about every two weeks with a non-sales touch point. Track attendance, and when someone misses a booked session, tell them they were missed; attendance drifting from three visits a week down to cancellation is visible in the data.
numbers: About half of departing customers saved via exit interviews; contact roughly every 2 weeks.

### YT-h6y0nYVZgwE#15 | EXAMPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Open to Goal | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:27:02
claim: Reverse engineer the goal from activities per customer, then convert it to a daily count.
detail: Example 1: 30 posts a month yield 5 customers, so 6 posts per customer; for 25 customers need about 150 posts, so 5 a day. Example 2: 100 outreach contacts yield 1 customer; 10 customers means 1,000 a month, about 30 a day; 50 customers needs about 150 a day. With a new acquisition target, he first asks for the activity count per customer and says start tracking if unknown.
numbers: 6 posts per customer; 100 reach-outs per customer; 150 outreach per day for 50 customers a month.
