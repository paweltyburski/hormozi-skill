### LS-9OsUfwTTslc#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:16
claim: Acquisition.com sets goals in a table: the problem solved, a hypothesis, the input X, the expected output Y, and after the period whether X and Y happened.
detail: The problem must chunk up to one of three buckets: more customers, more money per customer, or lower risk to those two lasting (which raises enterprise value). A goal that cannot be tied to one is probably not worth the limited resources. The hypothesis is "if we do X, Y happens" (for example, doubling videos should raise site clicks). Review can show X done but Y missed, for instance if quality fell, so the hypothesis gets amended. Done every quarter and in annual planning.
numbers: Three buckets; two follow-up columns after each quarter.

### LS-9OsUfwTTslc#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:46
claim: Set goals on 100% controllable activities and measure the percentage of days you did them, not the outcome.
detail: Example: a new employee asked which gym programme to follow; the answer was binary, did you go. First month, count the days; next month raise the count; add content only after attendance is stable, since programming is pointless if you cannot stick to it. If all activities are done and the outcome misses, you still kept your word, which builds trust in yourself. He cares little if the team misses outcomes while doing all it committed to.

claim: For a solo creator, drop to one good piece a day and move the saved time into giving away free consulting time as the lead magnet.
detail: One good video earns more than ten mediocre ones, but skill comes from volume, so quality and quantity pull in opposite directions.
