### YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: product-delivery | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:45
claim: Two otherwise identical marketing agencies differ in value purely on time delay and client effort.
detail: Agency A needs about four hours a week of client-recorded ads plus three weekly one-hour meetings, and the phone rings only after 60 days. Agency B gets a lead within seconds of payment and asks nothing from the client. Same product and promise, so the second is far more valuable.
numbers: A: 3 meetings per week at 1 hour, ~4 hours per week of client content, first leads at day 60. B: first lead within seconds of payment.
applies_to: Agencies and other B2B services

### YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc#02 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: product-delivery | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:45
claim: A simple way to pick a product is to see what everyone sells and deliver the same thing in half the time.
detail: He cites a mentor's shortcut. Inside portfolio companies the standing question is how to compress time to value in service delivery, and the answer is usually found by looking at the delivery steps, not the promise.

### YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc#03 | CASE | topic: offers | also: product-delivery | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:54
claim: A book publisher with a slow process added a 7-day mini book boot camp to deliver a tangible result first.
detail: Writing a real book takes long, so the company built a one-week program in which each customer ended with a small published book. It was labelled honestly as not the book that makes them a millionaire, but it proved the concept and broke the belief that they could do it. The full book was sold afterwards.
numbers: 7 days to a published mini book.

### YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc#04 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: product-delivery | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:45
claim: Time delay has two parts: the wait from payment to delivery, and the wait until the customer first feels any benefit.
detail: Even if the headline result takes a year, early side benefits (community, feeling more upbeat in a gym) deliver value fast. So you can shorten the second part without shortening the main result. A long process is not a reason to skip it; ask what the customer can hold in their hands first. Real estate example: during a 90-day sale, add help with cleaning, inspection, repairs and lawn care.
numbers: Example sale cycle: 90 days.

### YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc#05 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: offers | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:48
claim: To cut customer effort, turn repeated activities into a streamlined, predictable routine.
detail: Look for steps repeated with every customer, predict what will happen, merge three onboarding calls into one, then replace the one call with a checklist. The goal is a seamless route to the result with as little from the customer as possible.

### YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc#06 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: offers | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:34
claim: Big-company moats sit on the bottom of the Value Equation: Netflix beat Blockbuster on time and effort, and Amazon did the same.
detail: Movie watchers wanted the same outcome from both and were equally sure of getting it, but Netflix removed the drive, browsing and late fees. Amazon sells the same goods as everyone else, shrank both the purchasing effort (one click) and the delivery time, and added reviews to raise believed likelihood. He admits he spent the first half to two thirds of his career pushing the top side (bigger promises, testimonials, endorsements) and now thinks the fortunes are made below the line, because it is much harder to copy operational speed.

### YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc#07 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:16
claim: Same promise, very different prices: the dream outcome sets the category, the other three variables set the price within it.
detail: A $50,000 liposuction and a $5 ebook promise a flat stomach, so their gap comes from likelihood, time and effort. Across categories, the outcome itself matters: investment and business opportunities sell dearer than weight loss, which is part of why B2B out-prices consumer offers. Two surgeons with the same procedure: the one with 10,000 operations can charge more than the one with a single operation, even though he spends less time.
numbers: Surgeon A 10,000 operations vs surgeon B 1; $50,000 vs $5.

### YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc#08 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: sales-process | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:12
claim: The four Value Equation variables double as the structure of any marketing claim.
detail: A strong claim states the dream outcome, a number of people who got it, how long it took, and the conditions. Draw the figures from customer success data, such as the share of customers who reach an outcome by a date under a stated condition. Use them in headlines, emails, ads, sales pages and video hooks.
numbers: Example claim for chiropractors: 70% reach over 100 patients in the first 30 days without ad effort.
applies_to: Services with customer result data

### YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:10
claim: A seller competes mostly with inaction and doubt, so the job is to lower the action threshold.
detail: The comfort of not buying is high, so friction and perceived risk must be reduced until stepping over the bar is easy. Guarantees are one tool: you carry part of the risk because you have done this many times and the buyer has not.

### YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: offers | also: unit-economics | fw: Unconditional Guarantee | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:17:36
claim: Judge an unconditional guarantee by net sales, not by the extra refunds it causes.
detail: If it lifts sales by 20% and refunds jump from 1% to 5%, you still gain roughly 15% net, so treat it as arithmetic rather than a feeling about refunds.
numbers: Sales +20%; refunds 1% to 5%; net about +15%.

### YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc#11 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: objections-closing | fw: Conditional Guarantee | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:17:36
claim: An agency conditional guarantee ties the refund to client behaviour the agency needs.
detail: Client delivers content by noon every Friday before the campaign for the first eight weeks; if they then do not earn at least twice their fee in those eight weeks, the money comes back, or the agency keeps working until the fee is recovered.
numbers: 8 weeks; 2x fee threshold; content due Fridays by noon.
applies_to: Marketing agencies

### YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc#12 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: retention-ltv | fw: Stacking Guarantees | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:18
claim: Pair a 30-day unconditional guarantee with a 90-day conditional one and sell it as an informed decision.
detail: A hesitant buyer is told the only way to be sure is to experience the product, so the first 30 days work as a trial. He expects more closes and more refunds, with a net gain in sales.
numbers: 30-day unconditional plus 90-day conditional.

### YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc#13 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: retention-ltv | fw: Conditional Guarantee | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:30
claim: Build guarantee conditions from the journey of your best customers, using the fewest stipulations that drive success.
detail: Study who got the best results, what they did and whom they talked to, and keep the two or three milestones they all hit. Each extra stipulation weakens the guarantee. Internet marketing example: buy a domain, build a website and spend a small amount on ads, with an ROI refund promise at day 90. Weight loss example: attend every workout and send photos of every meal for 90 days.
numbers: 2-3 stipulations; 90-day window; ad spend example $1 to $100.

### YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc#14 | BENCHMARK | topic: offers | also: retention-ltv | fw: Conditional Guarantee | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:18
claim: By his rough recollection ("I think"), only about 1-2% of people comply with writing their plan in a document each day and reporting at day's end for about six weeks.
detail: He jokes that for coaching-type businesses you could guarantee results on condition that the customer does this for six weeks, since he figures about 99% of people wouldn't do it anyway. He calls it a "fun one".
numbers: 1-2% compliance over about six weeks (his estimate).
applies_to: Coaching and similar businesses
### YT-5MHQr-Z17Hc#15 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: content-brand | fw: Unconditional Guarantee | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:17
claim: State the guarantee in plain, specific terms instead of a vague "results guaranteed" line.
detail: The vague phrase is ignored like banner blindness and means different things to different people. Spell out the exact outcome, the deadline and the action, without even using the word guarantee. His example: a specific waist-loss result by a date, otherwise a check for the gap between the price paid and what the buyer thinks it was worth.
