### POD-0071#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:50
claim: His two best-earning gyms sat in the poorest and one of the richest markets in his area, and both worked on the same two-tier model.
detail: One site had a median income of about $31,000, others about $60,000 and $90,000 and the second best about $120,000. The tiers were large group at $150-$225 a month and one-to-four semi-private at $500-$700. The poor area ran mostly large group, the rich area mostly semi-private, with some members always at the lower tier. Even in the poor area members with worn-out cars paid $400-$500 a month for semi-private because it was a priority.
numbers: Median incomes ~$31K, ~$60K, ~$90K, ~$120K; large group $150-$225/month; semi-private $500-$700/month.

### POD-0071#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: retention-ltv | also: pricing | fw: Ascension Path | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:18
claim: In middle and wealthy markets, not offering a higher tier after the first sale leaves money on the table; in poor markets a small upgrade rate still pays because the base is bigger.
detail: Poorer areas are denser, give cheaper and more plentiful leads and larger member bases, but a lower upgrade rate, and lifetime value may be slightly lower. He likes them as the fastest to grow because competitors often lack a second tier.
numbers: Poor market: about 300 members on automatic billing, 20-30 upgrading to $500 (roughly 7-10%); wealthy market: perhaps 30% upgrade to $500-$600 out of about 180-200 members.
### POD-0071#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:35
claim: By his definition, market income matters when the ticket is super high; he calls a $1,000 a month minimum ticket very high ticket (and $2,000 a month, probably, super high).
detail: For a minimum ticket that high, the model suits a truly wealthy market. Otherwise, a flexible model that adjusts the mix of tiers to the market adapts; a less flexible model is less likely to win in a competitive environment.
numbers: Minimum ticket of $1,000 a month is "very high ticket".
