### YT-ETEFhDICm5o#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Sales questions are either mining (extract information) or agreement (propose and ask for a yes), and agreement questions come as yes-based, neutral or no-based.
detail: Yes-based: "sound great?" Neutral: "fair enough?" or "does that sound reasonable?" after a concession (a reciprocal yes, taken from Belfort). No-based: "would you be opposed to moving forward today?" (idea from Chris Voss).

### YT-ETEFhDICm5o#02 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:02
claim: No-based asks: "would you be opposed to moving forward", "would it be unreasonable to start today", "are you against it", so "no" means yes.
detail: People feel safer and more autonomous saying no and avoid vulnerability and decision fatigue. Same logic as the furrier's "you don't want anything else, do you?", which he says got 95% to take an upsell.
numbers: 95% upsell take rate.

### YT-ETEFhDICm5o#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:38
claim: He suggests rephrasing a couple of the key milestone questions, or leaning toward no-based wording when a prospect is near the edge; it is not for mining questions.
detail: One counter example: with decision fatigue, people may say yes just to be done, and it depends on the size of the purchase.
