### POD-0103#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:33
claim: Lower the barrier to entry only as far as retention and stacked recurring revenue let you absorb the lost upfront cash.
detail: Small gyms copying big-gym low-barrier marketing get into trouble because less cash upfront per customer strains the business. First secure cash-positive acquisition and retention, then reinvest and widen the offer. Goal over time: maximum volume plus maximum lifetime value; the balance of upfront cash against volume is a seesaw.

### POD-0103#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Gym Launch started with half-up-front or full prepayment, then stepped its payment terms looser as recurring revenue grew.
detail: Early on a high-risk financing firm made buyers pay more so Gym Launch got cash up front. Later: three payments, then a few thousand down, then a small start payment. By early 2019 about $1,000 to start, with roughly two weeks' grace before the second payment.
numbers: financing added about 25% to the price; down payment $3,000 to $4,000 as an intermediate step; $1,000 start, about 2 weeks grace (2019).

### POD-0103#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:58
claim: Gym Launch ran cash-negative for the first six weeks after an opt-in, and its recurring base alone covers operations at a high margin.
detail: Clients stay very long, so heavy up-front cash is unnecessary. Existing recurring revenue alone would still support the operation at that margin.
numbers: cash-negative 6 weeks from opt-in, then first profit; recurring alone gives 60-70%+ margin.

### POD-0103#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:39
claim: A gym with strong retention should be stacking $5,000 to $10,000 per month in recurring revenue.
detail: Lowering the entry barrier (the "green play" in Gym Legacy) gets more people onto recurring faster, at the cost of less cash up front; with strong retention this should stack up the recurring revenue. Unsolved retention means you never scale massively.
numbers: $5,000-$10,000 per month recurring target for a gym.
applies_to: Gyms with strong retention.
### POD-0103#05 | EVOLUTION | topic: money-models | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:54
claim: He had long bashed low barrier offers partly for polarising marketing, and now says they work for businesses with stacked lifetime value.
detail: His old bashing of low barrier offers does not match the reality of about 99% of struggling or non-growing gyms, which lose money on acquisition and rarely have the cash to market that way. With cash reserves or stacked retention and recurring revenue, low barrier offers can work.
