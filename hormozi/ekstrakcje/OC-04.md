### OC-04#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: - | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:49
claim: An offer may fail to convert simply because it is not valuable enough, and fixing that requires understanding what creates value in the prospect's perception.
detail: Offer component three, after market and price. Value can be "created out of thin air" by changing perception; the Value Equation is his model of what drives it.

### OC-04#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: offers | also: pricing, product-delivery | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:49
claim: Value = (dream outcome × perceived likelihood of achievement) ÷ (perceived time delay × perceived effort and sacrifice).
detail: Top (raises value): (1) dream outcome, how meaningful the end result is; (2) perceived likelihood, how strongly they believe they will get it from you. Bottom (lowers value): (3) time delay until first and final result; (4) effort and sacrifice, what they must do, give up or suffer. Ideal: perfect outcome, 100% likelihood, instant, zero effort; unreachable but always a direction. For each variable ask how to recombine for more value. All are perceptions: prospects must be told, not just served.
quote: Increase the desire and promise, decrease the time and effort and sacrifice.

### OC-04#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:21
claim: A lot of times you do not need a wildly unique business; you just need to be really good at a core thing and solve a unique problem in a nuanced way.
detail: None of his businesses were really unique. Dream outcome means the result is meaningful to the prospect: value lies in closing the gap between where they are and the "mountain top".

### OC-04#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: offers, strategy-positioning | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:07
claim: The more meaningful the dream outcome, the more people will pay — so to make more money, solve more expensive problems.
detail: Dream outcome explains price differences across problems: making someone $1M is more meaningful for many people than losing 20 pounds, so they would pay more for it. His takeaway: to make more money, find more expensive problems to solve.

### OC-04#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:07
claim: People pay more for a surgeon on his 10,000th operation than one on his first, even though the experienced one takes less time — because perceived likelihood of success is higher and risk is lower.
detail: Between a fresh graduate and a veteran, nearly everyone pays more for the veteran despite less time spent; the premium buys lower risk of a bad outcome.

### OC-04#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: retention-ltv | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:14
claim: Every client success you create raises future perceived value, because repeated results make the outcome look like a repeatable system rather than luck.
detail: One eight-figure business is a proof point; five make the method look repeatable. About 0.4% of businesses (1 in 250) reach eight figures, so five by chance is 0.4% multiplied five times, implying a working framework.
numbers: ~0.4% (1 in 250) of businesses reach eight figures (per Hormozi).

### OC-04#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: content-brand, sales-process | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:59
claim: What counts is perceived likelihood, so you must communicate your success rate — results nobody knows about add no value to the offer.
detail: Even 100% client success adds nothing if prospects do not know. Early on most of his effort went into perceived dream outcome and likelihood (e.g. testimonials).

### OC-04#08 | DEFINITION | topic: offers | also: - | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:59
claim: Time delay has two parts: how long until the customer sees an initial result and how long until they reach the final result.
detail: Both lower value; "tomorrow you'll have a six-pack" beats "wait a year". Shorten either one.

### OC-04#09 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:00
claim: An easy way to beat an established market is to take the main thing everyone does and deliver it in half the time.
detail: Time is in the denominator, so cutting delivery time is one of the simplest ways to create value in a crowded market.

### OC-04#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:00
claim: Fast beats free — even if free competitors exist, you can win by being faster.
detail: People take an Uber instead of walking, and FedEx instead of cheaper USPS when it must arrive overnight. Free players do not stop you charging if you are meaningfully faster.
quote: Fast beats free.

### OC-04#11 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: offers | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:45
claim: When the final result inevitably takes a long time, design in early progress and show it to the customer along the way.
detail: Trigger and display progress during the journey to raise perceived value though total time is unchanged.

### OC-04#12 | DEFINITION | topic: offers | also: - | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:45
claim: Effort and sacrifice means all the additional costs, inconveniences and "sweat" the customer must take on to achieve the result.
detail: Everything beyond paying: changing habits, discomfort, time on tasks. The more the offer removes, the more valuable it is.

### OC-04#13 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:36
claim: CoolSculpting sells for $2,500–$5,000 while online workout programs get $10–$20 a month for the same outcome, because it wins on likelihood, time and effort.
detail: Both promise a flatter stomach. CoolSculpting: very high perceived likelihood, minimal time delay ("no surgery, no downtime"), near-zero effort (no diet, workouts, soreness, 5 a.m. alarms). Weights or a Beachbody-type subscription: eat less, be sore, much effort. "Wake up skinny" versus "suffer for it": same dream outcome, wildly different value and price.
numbers: CoolSculpting ~$2,500–$5,000 per treatment vs online workouts ~$10–$20/month.

### OC-04#14 | EVOLUTION | topic: offers | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:31
claim: Hormozi says he used to focus a lot on the top of the equation (desire, perceived likelihood) but now thinks much more about "getting the bottom to zero" (time delay, effort and sacrifice), which he sees larger enterprises focusing on and which in theory creates infinite value.
detail: Early on he raised desire and perceived likelihood (testimonials, outcome messaging), the commonly taught approach. Big businesses mostly drive time delay and effort toward zero. He now asks how to make the product more convenient, seamless and immediate than anyone else. He says that in theory this creates infinite value.

### OC-04#15 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: product-delivery | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:31
claim: Amazon and Netflix illustrate getting the bottom of the value equation to zero: reviews and one-click ordering cut risk and effort, next-day delivery cuts time, and recommendations cut the risk of wasted time.
detail: Amazon: reviews reduce the risk of a bad purchase, one-click cuts effort, next-day and same-hour delivery cut time. Netflix versus Blockbuster: no driving or browsing, instant viewing, and taste-based recommendations reduce the risk of wasted time.
