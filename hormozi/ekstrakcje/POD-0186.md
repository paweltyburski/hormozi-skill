### POD-0186#01 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:21
claim: End every price in 9s rather than 7s and add 99 cents to every transaction; across hundreds of transactions that is real money.
detail: In a challenge with about 200 sign-ups he realised that pricing at $497 instead of $499.99 gave up about $600. Standardising a $167 membership at $169.99 can be introduced as simplification, with no sale needed. The extra is about $600 a month, $7,200 a year, on top of a typical gym owner's pay.
numbers: About 200 sign-ups x $3 = $600; $600/month = $7,200/year; $167 to $169.99.
applies_to: Gyms and other high-transaction-count local businesses.

### POD-0186#02 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:24
claim: Take a backup card at sale; he claims it can halve churn through an administrative step.
detail: If 10 members would cancel and 5 have a second card billed at $170 a month, that is $850 a month, possibly repeating. His estimate is about $10,000 a year for a typical gym.
numbers: Claimed churn 10% to 5%; 5 saved x $170 = $850/month; about $10,000/year.

### POD-0186#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:28
claim: Billing on a four-week cycle gives 13 payments a year instead of 12, matching a biweekly payroll that already produces 13 payroll runs.
detail: Monthly billing against biweekly payroll means one month of payroll is absorbed each year. Adding about 8% to revenue when an average gym margin is about 12.5% nearly doubles profit. Only about 10% of a mastermind room did this. His estimate is about $20,000 a year in the running example.
numbers: 13 four-week cycles vs 12 months = about 7.8% more; average margin 12.5%; about 10% of room already on this cycle.

### POD-0186#04 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:41
claim: Collect by ACH rather than card to save about 2% in processing, and know that CRM payment gateways earn mostly from processing.
detail: Gateways take partnership payouts of 1-1.5% on your processing, while a stand-alone local processor might charge about 50 basis points. 2% of $1M a year is $20,000, roughly a 20% raise on a 12% margin. His four tactics together total about $37,000 a year, equal to about $300,000 of extra revenue at a 12% margin.
numbers: 2% saving = $20,000 per $1M/year; gateway rebates 1-1.5% vs about 0.5% local processors; total about $37,000/year.
