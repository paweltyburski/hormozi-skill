### POD-0793#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: The strongest outside multiplier on an employee's effectiveness is how much their significant other helps them reach their goals.
detail: Hormozi and his wife reviewed people who joined their companies and judged them only on the support they got from their spouse. They could call where each couple would end up, and the result mattered more than starting position. Some low-level staff with all-in partners outperformed stars whose partners derailed them. He is considering adding this to the interview process.
numbers: Prediction by spouse support alone matched outcomes in essentially every couple reviewed (he says 100%).
applies_to: Owners hiring or promoting people for long-term performance

### POD-0793#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: hiring-talent | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:07
claim: Spouse support sits on a continuum from actively destroying to passively destroying, neutral, and supportive.
detail: Actively destroying: tells you to quit the job that supports the family, so talking about work is punished. Passively destroying: well-meant pulls away from work ("forget it, let's go out"), same outcome with different intent. Neutral comes in two kinds: a partner on their own path who gives you room, or one who simply does not interfere. Supportive: takes interest, covers the home, treats you two as one unit and frames your work as loving them.

### POD-0793#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:06:45
claim: Ask whether having this person in your life raises or lowers the odds that you hit your goals.
detail: He backs the core question with five zoomed-out tests. Would you take it as a compliment if someone said you resemble your ex or current partner? Would you let your future child date someone like them? Can you be yourself without acting? Do you like the real person or their potential? Are you fulfilled with them, or only less lonely?
numbers: Five questions.

### POD-0793#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:38
claim: An executive coach had him score a relationship by results in his life, not by chemistry.
detail: At about 26 or 27, unsure about his future wife because there were no fireworks, he went category by category with the coach: fitness (she got him to the gym), food (she cooks to his goals), money (she saves and earns, and handles work so he can sell). Every stat was up. He switched his test from butterflies to whether the person helps him become who he wants to be.

### POD-0793#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: hiring-talent | fw: A Players | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:13
claim: Mediocre "B" partners hurt like B players at work: good enough to keep, but they slow you down.
detail: He cites Frank Slootman's view that B players, not obvious C players, destroy a company, because nobody removes them. He applies it to relationships: a partner along for the ride is a weight on your ankle. You can still run, but you spend energy on managing the relationship and waste potential. Leaving also gives them the chance to find someone who sees them as an A.

### POD-0793#06 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:27
claim: Before deciding a partner is unsupportive, tell them specifically what behaviour would help instead of what to stop doing.
detail: Many partners want to help but do not know how. Name a few concrete behaviours for a given situation, for example when you come home stressed, ask them to listen or help you solve it instead of saying work is not worth it. If you have said this clearly and they have no interest, then consider other options.

### POD-0793#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:31
claim: Accept real trade-offs between family and work, but do not accept a relationship that costs your goals and gives no fulfilment.
detail: Working every hour would likely produce more, but it is a personal choice, and many rich people have families. Trades are fine. The failure is paying the cost (fewer goals) and not getting the benefit (a person who makes you better). He also says a partner will rarely become excited about your work if they are not already; his own first date with his wife was four hours about ads, funnels and offers.
