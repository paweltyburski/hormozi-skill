### YT-ZuJryiwxjDw#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: pricing | fw: Riches in the Niches | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: US wealth is extremely concentrated, so selling to people with money is a structural choice, not a slogan.
detail: He scales US household net worth to $100 shared by 100 people. The top 10% of earners take 40% of income; for wealth, the bottom 50 hold about $2.50, the next 40 about $28, the next 9 about $38, and the top one person about $32, more than the bottom 90 combined.
numbers: Household net worth $163 trillion; top 10% earn 40% of income; top 1% hold ~32% of wealth, top 10% ~69-70%; one in ten Americans has $1M+ net worth.

### YT-ZuJryiwxjDw#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: unit-economics | fw: 80/20 Customer Analysis | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:29
claim: Profit follows a power law inside the 80/20 rule: 20% of customers give 80% of profit, 4% give 64%, 1% give 51%.
detail: Applying the Pareto split recursively. One top customer often doesn't cost much more to serve than the other 99, so more service for few people is significantly more profitable. It works only if your model lets them pay more.
numbers: Top 20% of customers = 80% of profit; top 4% = 64%; top 1% = 51%.
### YT-ZuJryiwxjDw#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:46
claim: Selling a $100 thing to a buyer with a $1,000 budget loses $900; selling a $1,000 thing to a $100 buyer loses $100.
detail: Underpricing for rich buyers costs more than overpricing for poor ones, and the lost amount is mostly profit. Expect 99 of 100 prospects to refuse the expensive option and chase the one who accepts.

### YT-ZuJryiwxjDw#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: Anchor Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:59
claim: Make each new tier 5-10x the price below, expect about 20% to take it, and want each tier to roughly double revenue.
detail: Example: ten customers, eight at $10 a month and two at $50: the bottom eight customers (80%) give $80, the top two give $100, doubling revenue. If overhead is covered by the base, the extra $100 can add far more than $10 of profit, taking profit from $10 to $90. If a tier does not add roughly another full revenue, it may not justify the extra operational constraint.
numbers: Tier step 5-10x price; about 20% take rate; profit in the example $10 to $90 a month.

### YT-ZuJryiwxjDw#07 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:54
claim: When a 5-10x upsell sounds like too much work, move value or price: do less or charge more, preferably charge more.
detail: Ask what you would do to blow people away at 10x the price. The cost is often only about 5% of that price, so margins are high. Only serve fewer people if you are happy making more money from them. Expect one in five or one in ten (even one in a hundred) of the right avatar to say yes; the sweet spot is the most money, not the most yeses.
numbers: Cost to deliver the 10x tier maybe 5% of its price; take rate 1 in 5 to 1 in 100.

### YT-ZuJryiwxjDw#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:29
claim: Start at the top with the unscalable high-ticket offer; wealthy buyers are often easier to serve per dollar.
detail: Top-down launches (the Roadster before the cheaper models) give a stronger brand story and manageable volume than bottom-up. A $100,000 payment is 1% of a $10M net worth and 10% of $1,000 for a poor buyer, so the rich buyer is less demanding per dollar, and one $100,000 customer replaces 1,000 at $100. Serving the masses at low prices needs heavy capital and automation; about 78-80% of US businesses are services without that.
numbers: 78-80% of US businesses are service-based (as stated).

### YT-ZuJryiwxjDw#11 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:25:47

### YT-ZuJryiwxjDw#13 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:30:37
claim: To avoid choking on a big price, write it down or show it on a calculator, or say "it's expensive" before stating the number.
detail: The pre-frame works either way: a rich prospect imagines their own number, a poor one braces for a large figure; both then accept it more easily. Take a breath, let the prospect breathe, then deliver the price.

### YT-ZuJryiwxjDw#14 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: sales-process | fw: Lead Scoring | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:31:52
claim: Use your close rate to estimate how much pricing headroom you have.
detail: A very high close rate probably means price is too low. You might see the rate drop when you raise price, but he says revenue can still rise. Below 30%, he says get better at selling, which could mean improving the offer or talking to better customers; pitching a high-ticket offer to unqualified people lowers close rate.
numbers: Close rate 80%: probably 3-4x room in price; 60-80%: probably 2-3x; 50-60%: probably 1.5-2x; 40-50%: probably 1.25-1.5x; 30-40%: priced about right; under 30%: get better at selling. (His example: dropping to about 35% at 4x the price gives 120% of the prior revenue.)
