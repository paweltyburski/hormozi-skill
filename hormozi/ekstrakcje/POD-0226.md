### POD-0226#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:55
claim: In a service business some churn is normal, and obsessing over it past the point of diminishing returns delays growth.
detail: He cites subscription companies with heavy churn that are still hugely profitable (Spotify, which he thinks has around 15% monthly churn, unverified). Churn in small-business customers includes firms closing, like unemployment never reaching zero. He lost growth time by chasing churn instead of pushing spend.
numbers: For service clients who are small businesses, getting churn below 10% is very hard (period not stated).

### POD-0226#02 | TACTIC | topic: unit-economics | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:56
claim: Model the business as a vertical sheet of eight levers projected six months out, starting from a column of current volume.
detail: Levers: cash collected up front in 30 days, ongoing service price, front-to-back-end conversion, back-end churn, leads, appointments scheduled, shows, closes. Use six-month averages of real data, not last week's or a best week. Re-enter month zero as things change. Beyond six months a projection is useless. Not building it means flying blind.
numbers: Use 6-month averages for close rate and lead counts; projection horizon 6 months (12 maximum).

### POD-0226#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:24
claim: Pick the one lever to push by two tests: biggest effect within the goal window, and highest certainty you can actually move it.
detail: Move each knob in the sheet (halve churn, price +10%, front-end price +10%, more appointments). Leave the other levers alone at acceptable levels.
numbers: Example: show rate 70% to 80% takes similar effort to lifting leads from 100 to 150 (+50% business).
### POD-0226#04 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:38
claim: For a new performance-coaching division, the sheet showed that restoring marketing spend, not fixing churn, price or close rate, would reach the goal.
detail: Churn, price points and sales were at acceptable levels. Marketing spend had fallen to about a third of earlier levels, carried by word of mouth. At current volume the division would grow only 15-20% top line against a larger goal. Plan: return ad volume to earlier levels and hire a few more salespeople, and ignore everything else.
numbers: Spend about one third of earlier levels; projected growth at current volume 15-20% top line.

### POD-0226#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: paid-ads | fw: Three Levels of Advertising | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:55
claim: A service model is ready for scaling spend once first-30-day return is about 4:1 to 5:1 over acquisition cost.
detail: Below that, keep fixing the model: the gap between profit per customer and acquisition cost is too thin. For software the equivalent is about 1:1 within three months. Then double or quadruple spend and see what breaks. He says the gap is profit against cost to acquire.
numbers: Services 4:1 to 5:1 in the first 30 days; software about 1:1 within 3 months.
applies_to: Service businesses and software.

### POD-0226#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:29
claim: Absolute profit beats ROAS: lower ratio at much larger spend earns more than a high ratio at tiny spend.
detail: Small operators refuse to drop below 10:1. He would take 3:1 on a million dollars over 10:1 on ten thousand. Likewise a funnel converting 35% is acceptable; raising ad spend 25% is easier than lifting conversion to 40-45%.
numbers: 3:1 on $1,000,000 vs 10:1 on $10,000; funnel at 35% considered good enough.
