### LC-01#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:57
claim: The offer answers "what do I sell?"; leads answer the next question, "who do I sell it to?" — and leads are the leading indicator of sales.
detail: $100M Offers covers what to sell or how to make it more valuable; the Leads material covers who to sell it to. For a company wanting more sales, the first leading indicator is the pool of people it can sell to. The course runs from big picture to tactics Hormozi used to scale, and sell, his companies.

### LC-01#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:59
claim: Having lots of leads makes it very hard to be poor — having this one skill is, in his words, one of the biggest pieces of business insurance you can have.
detail: With plenty of leads you can make many mistakes and still have people to sell to, so lead generation works as business insurance. Leads do not appear by themselves; you have to force them to find you, through advertising.
quote: Lots of leads makes it very hard to be poor.

### LC-01#03 | DEFINITION | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: - | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:59
claim: Advertising is the process of making something known — ideally to people who do not know you yet.
detail: A deliberately broad definition: anything that makes your offer known counts, not just paid ads. Referrals, outreach, paid ads and affiliates are all methods of advertising.

### LC-01#05 | CASE | topic: operator-mindset | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:55
claim: A gym owner told his members to refund through Hormozi and re-sign directly at half price, creating about $150,000 of refunds in a week that had to be covered within 30 days.
detail: Hormozi's early business flew teams to gyms for turnarounds, paying travel, ad spend, commissions and per diems, and filled gyms with clients paying around $500 each. One owner stood on a chair and told the new clients to charge back and join him for half price. He needed $150,000 profit in 30 days, more than he had ever made in a month. His first ideas only squeezed the existing model (upfront launch fees, revenue share from past gyms, supplement upsells, prepaid future launches) and none penciled out. His lesson: he had created too big an incentive for financially desperate owners to cut him out.
numbers: ~$500 per client; ~$150,000 refunds in a week; $150,000 profit needed in 30 days; rent ~$1,000/month.

### LC-01#06 | CASE | topic: lead-getters | also: paid-ads, verticals | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:03
claim: Turning Layla's small online personal-training business into a direct-to-consumer offer with a sales letter and paid ads produced about $1,000 a day within 14 days.
detail: Layla was serving her clients online for about $3,000–4,000/month. Hormozi proposed selling fitness direct to consumers, cutting out gym owners, with Layla as the face. In 48 sleepless hours he wrote a sales letter and ran ads to it to book sales calls; within 14 days it did about $1,000/day. His projection: eight field reps at that rate ≈ $240,000 in 30 days, ~$150,000 after commissions and ad spend. Because he could generate leads on demand, a new business sold within two weeks.
numbers: ~$1,000/day within 14 days; projected 8 reps ≈ $240,000/month, ~$150,000 net.

### LC-01#07 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals, pricing | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:38
claim: When gym owners said "I just need leads," Hormozi stopped doing turnarounds and sold them the know-how instead — $60,000 collected in one day and $215,000 in total with no delivery cost.
detail: Calling to cancel next month's eight launches, the first owner (credit cards maxed, house refinanced) said he only needed leads, not someone to fly out and close. Hormozi offered to teach him and named $6,000, hoping for a no; the owner agreed. He raised the price each call ($8,000, $10,000) and kept hearing yes — about $60,000 in a day. He then offered 30+ previously launched gyms "keep all the money, I'll show you how," reaching ~$215,000 collected with no fulfilment travel, covering the refunds. Diagnosis: the value was real, the model was wrong — the origin of the Gym Launch licensing model. Takeaways: a repeated customer request shows what they will pay for; teaching instead of doing removed cost and refund exposure; raising price call by call finds the market price.
numbers: $6,000 → $8,000 → $10,000 per gym; ~$60,000 in one day; ~$215,000 total.
quote: We had value here — we just didn't have the model right.

### LC-01#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: operator-mindset, paid-ads, outreach | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:11
claim: The ability to get leads on demand gives you endless chances to get the business right — Hormozi ties the repeated recoveries after nosedives in his career to it.
detail: Each nosedive was survivable because he could get customers on demand to test something new. His skills: paid ads for local weight-loss leads for gyms; referrals, paid ads and outreach for gym-owner leads (outreach gave the first sales); affiliates for supplement buyers; paid ads for agency leads. Different businesses used different advertising methods. He calls the skill a "get out of jail free card," worn at the edges from use, and the course aims to transfer it.
