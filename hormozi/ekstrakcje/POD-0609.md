### POD-0609#04 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:10:49
claim: Acquisition.com serves portfolio companies through pods: one portfolio operator leads a group of subject matter experts, and each pod handles several businesses.
detail: Experts are managing directors for people, customer success, sales and marketing. The pod does value creation for the portfolio, and the structure is duplicated per niche (today professional services and brick-and-mortar chains). A move into software would mean building the same pod with software-background experts. Outside consultants come in for special projects.

### POD-0609#05 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:36
claim: In a company around $10M revenue and $3M profit, find the department that does not exist yet, headhunt a person from a business two to three times larger, and have the expert deploy the playbook with them.
detail: At this size (roughly 30 staff) the missing function usually needs to go from zero to one. The value is created inside the portfolio company, not at the holding company.
numbers: ~$10M revenue, ~$3M profit, ~30 employees; recruit from firms 2-3x larger.
applies_to: Small companies just below private-equity size.

### POD-0609#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:36
claim: Rare senior experts are affordable when their cost is pooled at the holding company, while on a single company's books they would hurt the P&L.
detail: High-skill work needs deep judgement more than hours, so the experts are fractionalised across several companies. Playbooks that work in three portfolio companies are copied to the next, and learnings move across the portfolio faster than any one firm could learn.

### POD-0609#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:25
claim: A small company's bad culture comes from having no practices, while a larger company's comes from systems that reward the wrong things, and the second is a harder turnaround.
detail: Leila: the first is built from scratch and is fixable; the second needs a different skill and they have done it only once. Culture is the slowest piece, and replacing people around a founder does not help if the founder does not change.

### POD-0609#08 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:14:03
claim: A real estate team owner repeatedly put a successor in his seat, and each time the business drifted when conditions changed and he had to step back in.
detail: The successors understood the org chart and processes, and did well while deals flowed, but could not lead people through a new environment. Customers came for the founder's standards, so conversion fell when a replacement lacked that. Another successor eased off once he got a taste of the money, before the business reached escape velocity. Hormozi's reply: hire people who share your values or are open-minded enough to adopt them.

### POD-0609#09 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: scaling-constraints | also: hiring-talent | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:36
claim: To choose the next hire, ask which artery, if clogged, prevents the most blood loss, and where a person placed there gives the highest ROI.
detail: Often, many owners want to hire to get themselves out of pain. Better wording of the question: what is not happening that, if it were, would make the business 10x better, or what is happening that needs to stop?
### POD-0609#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:36
claim: Before handing off a skill you do well, check whether the business can bear a replacement being worse at first.
detail: Example: you close at 80% and the new person closes at 50%. In the beginning, he says the answer is usually that the business cannot support that.
numbers: Close rate 80% (owner) vs 50% (new hire) in the example.
### POD-0609#11 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:28:00
claim: A founder of a two-month-old company with warm leads and good conversions asked whether to hire an operator or a marketer; the advice was to keep the skill he loves and is best at, and remove everything else from his calendar.
detail: He markets four or five companies and wants a project manager to hold the daily agenda. Leila's view: project manager is a catch-all for roles that are missing elsewhere. numbers: Dave Ramsey ~$300M a year; example sales manager at ~$600,000 a month.

detail: When Leila joined, Hormozi's company did about $2M a year with little profit; what she added was leadership, not spreadsheet detail. Operators have been placed in nearly all portfolio companies, pattern-matched on this.

### POD-0609#14 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:25:46
claim: Hiring is a learnable skill that most owners lack because they judge it from very few attempts, so interview many candidates before deciding on a role.
detail: Hormozi has hired thousands of people. Owners often have about 30 interviews against thousands of sales calls. Leila asks owners who say they do not need a sales manager to interview ten and then decide. Ignorance of what good looks like is the issue, not stupidity. If weak, defer to a recruiter and watch what they do.
numbers: ~30 interviews vs 1,000-2,000 sales calls for a typical owner.

### POD-0609#15 | BENCHMARK | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: Internal Core Four (Recruiting Mirror) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:26:43
claim: Leila says about two thirds of strategies fail through execution, so only roughly a third are carried out; the best companies execute 78%.

### POD-0609#16 | FRAMEWORK | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:32:14
claim: Track talent like marketing: time to fill, employee return, and a two-sided fit score taken 90 days after hire.
detail: At 90 days manager and employee each rate fit from 1 to 10. In his example, if one says 0 and the other 10 the result is 0, and both saying 10 gives 10. Acquisition.com's current two-sided fit is 8.7, which he describes as the average of both sides. The 90-day wait matters because managers often judge a new hire after two weeks. Talent funnel mirrors customers: application generation, nurture, interviews, offer, onboarding, retention.
numbers: Two-sided fit measured at 90 days, 1-10 each side; company score 8.7.
