### POD-0155#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Business problems usually come from a big, obvious, unglamorous failure, not from an advanced technical cause.
detail: When a result drops, owners hunt for hacks (bidding, algorithms, courses) instead of admitting the plain cause: weak ad, weak offer, unclear message, average product. Hormozi's first question about any problem is what the obvious thing is that the owner does not want to face.

### POD-0155#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: paid-ads | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:48
claim: If a campaign fails, it is usually the creative, the offer and unclear messaging rather than bidding or audience settings; if a page fails to convert, it is usually a headline that does not connect to the ad.
detail: A landing page whose headline does not connect to the ad, or that looks and feels different from it, is a usual cause of poor conversion, though he says fast load times matter too. Make copy clear enough for a third-grader.
### POD-0155#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-process | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:27
claim: A poor scheduling rate on the thank-you page is probably caused by inconvenient available slots, not load times or a fancy scheduler.
detail: Example: offering only 30-minute slots every other day makes it unlikely the prospect can attend.
### POD-0155#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:27
claim: Weak sales numbers are probably due to skipping the basics rather than a missing super secret selling technique.
detail: The basics he lists: label the pain, ask why they came in today, find what past solutions missed, make a relatively compelling offer, add a guarantee so a normal stranger will sign.
### POD-0155#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: Ascension Path | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:44
claim: If nobody buys the upsells (semi-private, nutrition, accountability), Hormozi says it is probably because the first thing they got wasn't that good.
detail: He imagines the customer thinking they don't want to risk something else when they're not that satisfied with the first purchase. Owners may see their sessions as a success because people are visibly working out, but on average they might just be average. Ask what someone much dumber than you, or a person off the street, would say about why no one comes.
### POD-0155#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:41
claim: When something that worked stops working, check the people first: compare each person's start date with when the numbers moved.
detail: Example: the data looks fine until a new person starts on Wednesday and the show rate falls. Asking staff whether they follow the script gets "yes" by default, so use data. Then check whether people are still doing the original actions.

### POD-0155#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: management-ops | fw: Goodwill (Price-Value-Cost) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:27
claim: Complacency kills results slowly because the feedback is delayed: drop the actions and the effect appears months later.
detail: Examples: creative output slipping from 10 ads a week to 5, 3, then fortnightly; member check-ins stopping as the gym grows, so attrition rises a couple of months later once stored goodwill runs out.
numbers: 10 ads a week falling to 5, 3, then every other week.

### POD-0155#08 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:44
claim: Ask yourself: if you were your own business coach hearing this, what would you tell you?
detail: Hormozi says Layla asks him this; he sometimes feels he coaches others' businesses better than his own and sometimes breaks his own rules. Pretend another owner presented the same problem.
### POD-0155#09 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:34
claim: Scale by locking each pipeline stage in order (leads, schedule and show, sales, retention, ascension), then raising lead volume and repeating.
detail: Get every stage stable at today's volume, with percentages holding, before returning to the top. Then test the next volume; it breaks something again. The cycle never ends, so fix one stage at a time.
numbers: Example volume steps: 10, 15, then 50 leads a day.

### POD-0155#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:19
claim: Troubleshoot in the order problem, then person, and redesign the process only after both are ruled out.

Owners of businesses at 10, 20, 30, whatever million, sometimes come back saying they were not doing what he had taught them; his reply is that they should probably check, because it probably is not happening.
