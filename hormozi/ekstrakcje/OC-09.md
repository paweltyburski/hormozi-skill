### OC-09#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: unit-economics, paid-ads | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Scarcity and urgency make prospects decide faster, which shortens the cash conversion cycle so ad spend comes back sooner and marketing stays profitable.
detail: Offer component eight. Prospects who buy too slowly keep your ad money out; shorten decision time with urgency (time) and scarcity (units).

### OC-09#02 | DEFINITION | topic: offers | also: promotions-launches | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Urgency limits time (decide by a date, countdown timers); scarcity limits quantity (how many spots or units are left).
detail: Urgency: decide by a date or time (countdown timers, "offer ends X"). Scarcity: limited spots, seats or units. Separate levers, usable alone or together.

### OC-09#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: promotions-launches | also: offers | fw: Urgency | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:31
claim: There are four ethical sources of urgency: rolling cohorts, rolling seasonal promotions, time-limited pricing or bonuses, and exploding opportunities.
detail: 1. Group: rolling cohorts with a fixed start. 2. Season: calendar promotions (Valentine's, New Year, spring, summer) that really end. 3. Pricing and bonuses: available only during this promotion. 4. Opportunity: one that genuinely shrinks or disappears (most powerful). None requires lying.

### OC-09#04 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: sales-process | fw: Urgency | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:31
claim: Use rolling cohorts — a group that always starts on a set day — to give every prospect a reason to decide today, whatever day it is.
detail: Use your natural cadence (daily, weekly, monthly, or a cohort every six months): "our next group starts Monday". It claims only that this group starts soon. In his brick-and-mortar experience: on a Friday, "we start Monday"; on a Monday, "you've got seven days to get ready, next Monday we go live". Always a reason to decide now.

### OC-09#05 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: offers | fw: Urgency | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:39
claim: Rolling seasonal urgency: run the same core offer under a different seasonal name each period so each promotion genuinely ends.
detail: Valentine's special, New Year's special, "spring forward" in March, April showers, get-ready-for-summer. "Our Valentine's special ends in three days." Next month a very similar offer runs under a new name, so the current one really ends.

### OC-09#06 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: pricing, verticals | fw: Urgency | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:34
claim: Create urgency by rotating bonuses and price points between promotions — different on the surface, identical in the underlying business model.
detail: Bonuses: with 10, offer five in month A, another five in month B, the first five again in month C. Pricing: rotate the front-end hook ($19, $29, $1 special, free start or free first session). No real business difference, but it looks new and prompts action. Most important in local markets with limited audiences; vary by month, name and price.
numbers: Front-end variations: $19, $29, $1 specials, free first session.
applies_to: Especially local businesses with small audiences.

### OC-09#07 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: offers | fw: Urgency | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:20
claim: The most powerful urgency is an exploding opportunity — a real reason the opportunity will shrink or disappear, so the sooner you act the more you capture.
detail: Example: arbitrage between platforms (buy on eBay or Craigslist, sell on Amazon higher). You can buy anytime, but the gap keeps shrinking, so the sooner in, the more of the wave you ride. Any unique marketing method or market you exploit can be framed this way.

### OC-09#08 | FRAMEWORK | topic: offers | also: promotions-launches | fw: Scarcity | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:08
claim: There are three ways to create scarcity: a limited supply of seats or units, a limited supply of bonuses, and products that are never available again or one of a kind.
detail: 1. Limited seats/units: real capacity or stock. 2. Limited bonuses: a fixed number, especially physical premiums. 3. Never again / one of a kind: limited editions, single drops. Alone or with urgency; it rests on supply and demand, so you limit supply deliberately.

### OC-09#09 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: scaling-constraints, sales-process | fw: Scarcity | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:54
claim: Every business already has real capacity limits — define them and tell prospects, which creates honest scarcity and signals social proof.
detail: A million customers tomorrow would overwhelm you, so you have a natural limit; market it. If your one media buyer is 75% loaded: "25% capacity left before I hire, get in today." It also shows others already bought. He calls unused excess capacity the "sawdust" of your business.
numbers: Media buyer at 75% capacity → 25% remaining.

### OC-09#10 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: verticals | fw: Scarcity | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:48
claim: Limited physical bonuses — t-shirts, bands, orthotics — work especially well for scarcity because people readily believe physical supply limits.
detail: "Free if you buy today, but we only have X left; otherwise I don't know if we'll get more" turns "I'll think about it" into a decision today. Genuinely limited quantities beat unlimited ones.
applies_to: Especially brick-and-mortar businesses.

### OC-09#11 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: verticals | fw: Scarcity | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:51
claim: For physical products, limited one-time drops sell more than a permanent catalogue — e.g. 100 units of a single t-shirt design that sell out.
detail: One-of-a-kind items fetch extreme prices (art, limited editions). Do one-time flavour drops; in apparel make 100 of one design and sell out, which outsells listing every shirt. He predicted NFT-style digital scarcity would grow.

### OC-09#12 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: promotions-launches | fw: Scarcity | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:36
claim: A sold-out charity event at Arnold Schwarzenegger's home cut the number of tickets and raised the price from about $10,000 to $25,000 — "whenever demand increases, cut supply."
detail: At the $25,000-a-ticket event he met a major donor in exotic watches and jewellery. After a sold-out year, the charity cut tickets (roughly in half or a bit less) and raised the price from about $10,000 to $25,000. Rule: when demand rises, cut supply; especially true in the high-end space.
numbers: Ticket price ~$10,000 → $25,000; supply cut by up to ~50%.
quote: Whenever demand increases, cut supply.

### OC-09#13 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Scarcity | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:38
claim: Chanel and De Beers engineer scarcity deliberately — Chanel stocks only one or two of each item per store, and De Beers restricts diamond supply to protect margins.
detail: Chanel stores carry one or two of each item, unannounced assortments, no online sales; an item you like may be gone tomorrow. De Beers, with about 75% of world diamond supply passing through its mines, limits supply to keep margins high; diamonds are less rare than rubies or sapphires. Control the supply-to-demand curve and you set the price; use it if demand for your time is unlimited.
numbers: De Beers ~75% of world diamond supply (per Hormozi).

### OC-09#14 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: sales-process, product-delivery | fw: Scarcity | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:19
claim: In service businesses, express scarcity as spots left this month, a total client cap, a weekly limit, or a per-cohort limit — always based on your real capacity.
detail: (1) Spots left: an agency handling 10 new clients a month with 8 sold has two spots left before hiring; also signals demand. (2) Total cap: "I only take 100 clients." (3) Per-period cap: 10 new clients a week. (4) Per-cohort cap, whether cohorts start in two months or every six months. Derive the number from real capacity and never lie. Products: Amazon's units-left display.
numbers: Example agency: 10 clients/month capacity, 8 sold → 2 spots left.

### OC-09#15 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: offers | fw: Scarcity | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:19
claim: For a high-end program, cap membership and name the group after the cap — it creates scarcity on the way in and makes members reluctant to give up their spot.
detail: Limit an exclusive program to a fixed number and put it in the group's name; scarcity for new buyers and better retention in recurring offers.
