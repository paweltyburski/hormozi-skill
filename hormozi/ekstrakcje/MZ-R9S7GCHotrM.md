### MZ-R9S7GCHotrM#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:47
claim: Define a vague problem with three questions: what does it mean, how do you know, and why does it matter (logic, evidence, utility).
detail: A marketing agency said the chiropractor market was "burnt out". Pressed to define it, the claim meant a lower click chance on ads, which they could not prove. Even if true it would not change behaviour, so the data was irrelevant.

### MZ-R9S7GCHotrM#02 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:19
claim: Reframe the problem into a question you can act on: not "how do I market to a burnt-out market" but "how do I raise the click chance on my ads".
detail: Learning means same condition, new behaviour. Similarly "should I start a retail product" often means "how do I reliably recruit stylists".

### MZ-R9S7GCHotrM#03 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Penny Gap | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:51
claim: An agency with seven paying clients can run a free "beta programme" for past customers to generate proof.
detail: Tell prior customers you did not give enough proof and offer to prove it at no charge, with only an ad account card required. Change one element so calling it a beta is honest. Solve for being really good and the rest follows.
numbers: Seven paying clients.
