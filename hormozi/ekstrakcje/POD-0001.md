### POD-0001#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Low Barrier Offer (LBO) | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:35
claim: A $21, 21-day promotion earns only $6.30 per lead and leaves the gym net negative after attrition.
detail: Spend $1,000 for 100 leads (about $10 each), convert 30% by phone into the $21 promotion: $630. The cycle takes about six weeks. About 25 show; half might stay (about 12). Meanwhile about 15 existing members churn, so membership falls and the front end lost about $400.
numbers: $1,000 ad spend, 100 leads, 30 sales x $21 = $630 ($6.30/lead); about 12 stay; about 15 lost to attrition in six weeks.

### POD-0001#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: Payback Period | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:28
claim: Big-box gyms spend about $150 to acquire a customer paying $29 a month for about 36 months, with a five-month payback; he says most small gym owners lack the cash flow to float that.
detail: Copying the free-month model without that cash cushion is a trap. A typical small gym has about 100 members on recurring billing, 10% monthly attrition (10 lost a month), and signs up only about that many.
numbers: CAC about $150; $29/month; 36-month lifetime; payback 5 months; average small gym about 100 members at 10% monthly attrition.
applies_to: Small gyms and boot camps.
### POD-0001#03 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:45
claim: A $600 six-week offer with a $150 supplement upsell earns about $135 per lead versus $6.30, 21 times as much.
detail: Same $1,000 and 100 leads, 20% close at $600 is $12,000. A 50% margin supplement upsell adds roughly $75 per buyer, bringing the total to about $13,500, a 13.5:1 return. Higher revenue per lead lets this gym buy more and costlier leads and outbid rivals, and even a 5x rise in costs still leaves 4:1.
numbers: 20 sales x $600 = $12,000; total about $13,500 on $1,000; $135 per lead; 21x; 5x cost increase still about 4:1.

### POD-0001#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: paid-ads | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:21
claim: Daily cash-flow example: $50 a day brings 15 leads and 3 sales at $600, so the ad account is repaid by day four.
detail: Starting with $500 in the account, three days cost $150, then $1,800 lands on day four and again each day, so the budget grows and nobody needs a fixed marketing budget. Customers fund the next customers.
numbers: $50/day; 15 leads; 20% close; 3 sales x $600 = $1,800 a day.

### POD-0001#06 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: money-models | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:12:35
claim: A new gym can open at full capacity from customer cash: set aside $5,000, sign a lease, and presell 200 members at $600 within 30 days.
detail: The presale cash, about $120,000, paid for flooring, paint, equipment, front desk and signage, starting from an empty bank account. He opened six gyms this way.
numbers: $5,000 reserve; 200 members x $600 = $120,000 before opening; 6 gyms.
applies_to: Gym start-ups.
