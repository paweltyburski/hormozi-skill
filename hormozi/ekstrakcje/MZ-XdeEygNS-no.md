### MZ-XdeEygNS-no#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A balloon and flower decoration company grows by over-delivering at events, but struggles to close high-ticket clients.
detail: Each event is made bigger than paid for, to move up-market. About 80% of customers come from Instagram ads, the rest from referrals.
numbers: Revenue $400K, goal $1M; ~80% of customers from paid ads; LTV:CAC 58:1.

### MZ-XdeEygNS-no#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: pricing | fw: Riches in the Niches | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:17
claim: It is important to zero in on an avatar if delivery is materially different; if it costs the same and you must charge less, he says he is not a fan.
detail: With a painting analogy, one product can serve many sizes. If you would take only bigger events but cannot get them, that is a lead-gen problem, not a model problem.
### MZ-XdeEygNS-no#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:17
claim: Add a second, smaller segment only after owning a large slice of the market, with a lean process of equal margin.
detail: Hormozi puts that stage near $30M to $100M a year; at $400K, focus on getting more of the right customers.
numbers: Roughly $30M-$100M a year as the threshold.

### MZ-XdeEygNS-no#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:51
claim: With a very high LTV:CAC, ask what stops you from spending more on ads.
detail: At 58:1 the advice is to push spend, forget small clients.
numbers: LTV:CAC 58:1.
