### POD-251114-10#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: outreach | fw: CAC | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:54
claim: A cold-email channel works out at $500 CAC.
detail: Add the emailer's pay, the software, and a commission on each of eight sales, then divide by the eight new customers.
numbers: $3,000 emailer + $200 software + 8 x $100 commission = $4,000; $4,000 / 8 = $500.

### POD-251114-10#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: content-brand | fw: CAC | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:47
claim: A content channel with a two-person media team works out at $1,100 CAC.
detail: Payroll for both media staff plus commissions, divided by the ten customers produced.
numbers: 2 x $5,000 payroll + 10 x $100 commission = $11,000; / 10 = $1,100.

### POD-251114-10#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: paid-ads | fw: CAC | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:35
claim: A paid-ads channel works out at $3,500 CAC once buyer pay, software and commissions are included.
detail: Ad spend alone would understate it; the media buyer, tracking software and per-sale commissions all count.
numbers: $4,000 buyer + $20,000 ad spend + $1,000 software + 10 x $1,000 commission = $35,000; / 10 = $3,500.

### POD-251114-10#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: - | fw: CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Owners usually undercount CAC, though it can be known exactly by channel and month.
detail: They report ad spend only, treat content as free or count outbound commissions alone. A sale thought to cost $200 may cost $500, enough to separate a $1M and a $10M month. In investor diagnostics, one channel often beats the rest and gets the spend.
numbers: $200 believed vs $500 actual per $1,000 sale.
