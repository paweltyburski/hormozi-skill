### POD-0773#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:54
claim: Upside and guaranteed pay trade off: the larger the fixed base, the less upside you should expect.
detail: Advice relayed from a serial founder: take no salary if you can, but have participation in growth tied as closely as possible to what you do. The base disproportionately eats into upside. Hormozi failed to close two deals with entrepreneurs wanting to join Acquisition.com because they ignored this.

### POD-0773#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:00
claim: Entrepreneurs often raise their lifestyle to their income, so they demand a risk-free baseline equal to what 100% risk previously gave them.
detail: That mismatch of risk appetite blocks upside. A person whose personal burn rate is high cannot take business risk. No competent buyer can grant matched salary plus full upside; the person currently carries 100% risk and wants 0%, which is theirs to ask but not the employer's to grant.

### POD-0773#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:03
claim: To get meaningful upside inside another company, offer to work for little or nothing at base and take a percentage tied to your impact.
detail: Lowering the organisation's risk is what earns the share. Typical vehicles: stock, shares, profit interest or options. This is how many employees inside organisations become very wealthy.

### POD-0773#04 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:51
claim: A skilled single-skill marketer should either learn the other business functions or cut lifestyle and negotiate impact-linked upside.
detail: Marketing alone limits an entrepreneurial career. The second route is to join an organisation with lower personal costs and a stake tied to results.

### POD-0773#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:45
claim: In their mid-to-late 20s he and his partner earned over $10M a year but lived on under about $200,000, so they could make big bets.
detail: He treats personal lifestyle as almost all liability. Citing Charlie Munger, a person gets roughly six to eight once-in-a-lifetime opportunities, and people often get greedy on upside while fearing the downside. In his opinion, living defensively is what allows aggressive big swings.
numbers: Income over $10M per year; spending under about $200,000 per year; about 6-8 big opportunities per lifetime (Munger).
### POD-0773#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:04
claim: Spend less than you earn, and take your limited risk budget where upside is highest, not in consumption.
detail: Treat risk as a bucket; consumption has almost no upside, so minimise risk there and maximise it where returns are best.

### POD-0773#07 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:15
claim: A $1M-$5M entrepreneur who wants something bigger can join a company they love, name its constraint they can solve, and forgo pay for upside.
detail: Alternative is to learn every skill, which just takes longer. Hormozi says his own outsized rewards came from taking risks that look risky to others but not to him because he understands them.
numbers: Entrepreneur range cited: $1M to $5M per year revenue (unit unclear in audio).
