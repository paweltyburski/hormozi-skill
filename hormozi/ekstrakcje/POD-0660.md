### POD-0660#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit, unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:08:41
claim: To judge a deal, he walks the business as a pipeline from click to renewal, estimates a fixable multiplier at each stage, and multiplies them.
detail: Each stage has a benchmark from processes he has already tested. Example: appointments showing at 30% where 80% is achievable gives about 2.5x, a mispriced offer that can double gives 2x, and churn cut from 10% to 3% gives 3x. Together that is roughly 15x. He then adds a confidence score: the easier the changes are to implement, the more he wants the deal.
numbers: Show rate 30% to 80% (2.5x); price 2x; churn 10% to 3% (3x); product about 15x.
applies_to: Acquirers and operators prioritising which funnel fixes to make.

### POD-0660#03 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: money-models, retention-ltv | fw: Crazy Eight | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:55

### POD-0660#14 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:32:08
claim: Output equals repetition volume times leverage, so with no leverage you make up for it with volume until skill creates leverage.
detail: Leverage is the gap between what you put in and what you get out; a podcast has a lot because a few hours reach huge audiences. A beginner has none, so must do a very large number of repetitions; skill then produces leverage (the same-length video gets far more views). Beginners should think less and do more; the more advanced one is, the more one thinks before acting (the Buffett example).

### POD-0660#16 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:38:21
claim: Good advisors need three things: aligned incentives, competence for your problem, and complete context.
detail: Missing one makes it fail: competent and informed but misaligned can ruin you; aligned and competent but uninformed gives generic advice; aligned and informed but incompetent is like a loving parent with no investing skill. Paid coaches and therapists failed him on all three (incentive to keep him paying, 60 minutes of setup, less business skill). His fix is "Solomon", his 85-year-old self, consulted in a chat document in the first hour of each Monday; sessions last 7 to 45 minutes until the issue resolves.
numbers: Sessions 7 to 45 minutes, weekly.

### POD-0660#21 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 01:04:20
claim: To a client citing an excuse (such as kids taking all their time), agree it is a valid reason they will not succeed and watch them push back.
detail: Steps: 1. Say they are right, it is not their fault and they will not succeed. 2. Let them argue against you; the person who argues has shown up. 3. Ask whether they want permission to fail. 4. Reframe the excuse as the reason to act. He notes the practical alternative is to tell them to restructure the day and sacrifice time, and that some people just need to be told what to do. Used in Q&A.
applies_to: Coaching and consulting clients who give circumstances as reasons for inaction.
