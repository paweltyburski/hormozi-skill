### YT-ONV__y1z7MI#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:22
claim: A market has four positions (bargain, best value, premium, luxury), and only in luxury does a higher price raise the quantity sold.
detail: Best value is the Target-versus-Walmart type, premium is Lexus or BMW. In luxury the price has no cap, part of the value is the known cost, and it carries the buyer's status.
applies_to: Consumer and luxury brands.

### YT-ONV__y1z7MI#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Judge a price against the buyer's wealth: a $50,000 burger cost a $100M earner less work than a $12 lunch cost Hormozi at $7 an hour.
detail: $100M over 2,000 hours is $50,000 an hour. Empathise with buyers of $50,000 jackets; if the gap to a $100 jacket is trivial to them, they want the best.
numbers: $100M over 2,000 hours = $50,000 an hour; lunch $12 at $7 an hour.

### YT-ONV__y1z7MI#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: sales-process | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:09
claim: For wealthy buyers, speed and convenience dominate the Value Equation; the cost is the time and pain you demand, not the money.
detail: Do the same thing in half the time and accept any contact channel (email, text, WhatsApp, phone). Solving wealthy problems earns wealthy prices.

### YT-ONV__y1z7MI#04 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:55
claim: An agency serving only Fortune 100 firms handles $1.2B a year of ad budget, fronts the spend on a credit line, and charges 6%.
detail: Fronting $300M in a month earns $18M, because the client wants it effortless.
numbers: $1.2B annual budget; 6% fee; $300M month gives $18M.

### YT-ONV__y1z7MI#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: Virtuous Cycle of Price | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:29
claim: Higher prices raise perceived value even for identical product (a wine study) and attract less demanding customers.
detail: People ranked three identical wines by price. Pricier contracts mean fewer payment plans; a $50 buyer may be spending a big share of net worth. Cheap, demanding customers bring chargebacks, expensive ones bring testimonials.

### YT-ONV__y1z7MI#06 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: Virtuous Cycle of Price | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:47
claim: A $5 item sold at $8 leaves $3 to run the business, while at $50 it leaves $45 to deliver more and hire better people.
detail: Margin funds reinvestment and top talent. There is no advantage in being second cheapest.
numbers: $5 cost: $3 left at $8, $45 left at $50.
