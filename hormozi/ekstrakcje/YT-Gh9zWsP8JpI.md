### YT-Gh9zWsP8JpI#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:02
claim: Sell a defined-length program tied to a promise before any recurring fee, because people want an outcome, not a membership, and pay more once than monthly.
detail: The higher front price also funds acquisition, and the program can break even.

### YT-Gh9zWsP8JpI#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:58
claim: People weigh monthly cash flow more than total price, so more people accept a monthly price than a larger one-time sum.
detail: $18,000 sounds heavy, but $1,500 a month is easy; he says you sell more at $1,500/month than at $10,000 once.
numbers: $18,000 vs $1,500/month; $10,000 one time.

### YT-Gh9zWsP8JpI#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Big Head Long Tail | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:46
claim: After a $6,000 program, a continuity priced near $1,500 a month feels like a downsell though it totals more, especially if it gives more for less up front.
detail: The high front price anchors perceived value of the recurring offer, so continuity can be priced higher.
numbers: $6,000 program; ~$1,500/month continuity.

### YT-Gh9zWsP8JpI#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:51
claim: Raising the barrier raises prospect quality, and better prospects make the same product achieve better results, so the product itself becomes more valuable.
detail: An identical service works better for an able customer than an unable one. The higher price also funds over-delivery during onboarding.
