### MZ-DUe2RxPf3lM#01 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics, hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:57
claim: A coaching business with organic personal-brand leads saw close rate and margin fall after raising prices and adding staff.
detail: The owner does all closing, charges higher rates with more testimonials, yet sales are below target (a revenue plan of 30K versus 20K; exact meaning unclear in audio). Margin fell from 80% to 37.5% after hiring a brand-acquisition person, more teams and several six-figure employees. Hormozi sees a pricing tweak, not a deep problem.
numbers: Margin 80% to 37.5%; 45 clients so far.

### MZ-DUe2RxPf3lM#02 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:27
claim: Make the offer easier to say yes to by lowering the front-end price and adding a point or two of back-end share.
detail: He calls this an ethical offer change that should raise sales velocity, and says he is not normally a price-cutting adviser but wants the owner feeling good again.
numbers: Front-end price from about 20K to 10K (as heard).

### MZ-DUe2RxPf3lM#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:59
claim: Do not change avatars unless the current market is saturated; a switch is among the biggest changes in a business.
detail: With only 45 clients the market is untapped, and the whole brand is built around the first avatar. Use more sandpaper and fewer hammers: small tweaks.
numbers: 45 clients.
