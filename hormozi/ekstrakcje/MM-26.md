### MM-26#01 | DEFINITION | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Continuity offers give ongoing value for ongoing payments until the customer cancels — sell once, get paid again and again; the fourth money-model prong after attraction, upsell and downsell.
detail: They raise profit per customer, give one last thing to sell and stabilise cash flow. In the money model their job is to maximise lifetime value per customer: lower absolute gross profit, often higher gross margin. Hormozi puts them last, but they can attract, upsell, downsell or re-engage — there are no rules (00:01:35). Three best combinations, covered next: continuity bonus, continuity discount and waived-fee offers (00:11:32).

### MM-26#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Selling a $1,000 product as $50/month can bring in four times the buyers and the same per-customer revenue, at the cost of far less cash up front.
detail: 100 prospects: at $1,000, 10 buy = $10,000 now, nothing later. At $50/month, 40 buy and stay 20 months = $2,000 now and $40,000 over time, plus 4x the customers to upsell. The downside — little cash now — makes continuity weak as a stand-alone attraction offer, so Hormozi places it after attraction, upsell and downsell offers have collected cash today.
numbers: 10 × $1,000 = $10k vs 40 × $50 × 20 months = $40k; 4x customers.

### MM-26#03 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing, retention-ltv | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:35
claim: Downsell the upsell: after a short, high-priced front offer, sell a longer commitment with more value at a lower weekly or monthly rate.
detail: Instead of raising the price of the next offer, extend the term and lower the rate — buyers of the first offer see an even better deal. People think about monthly cash flow more than total term. Gym Launch: $16,000 for 16 weeks ($1,000/week), then "five times the value for less" on a longer term — eventually three-year contracts with much higher total value but lower monthly payments. Gym challenges: $600 for six weeks ($100/week), then membership at $200/month (~$50/week). Commitments can significantly raise LTV if you deliver value.
numbers: $16,000/16 weeks; $600/6 weeks → $200/month.

### MM-26#04 | SCRIPT | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:24
claim: A script for converting a happy front-end customer into a long-term continuity member.
detail: (1) "Have you enjoyed everything so far?" (2) "Can I share how you could pay a little less and receive a lot more?" (3) "Since X, Y and Z have gone well for you, we'd like to have you with us long term — if you're committed for the long run, we'll give you even more for less. Are you in?"

### MM-26#05 | FRAMEWORK | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: LTGP | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:10
claim: Back-of-napkin LTV: lifetime revenue = monthly price ÷ monthly churn; lifetime gross profit = lifetime revenue × gross margin.
detail: Gross margin = (price − cost of goods) ÷ price. Example: $100/month, $20 cost of goods → 80% margin; at 10% monthly churn lifetime revenue is $1,000, so LTGP is $800 per customer.
numbers: $100 price, $20 COGS, 80% GM, 10% churn → $1,000 revenue, $800 LTGP.

### MM-26#06 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models, unit-economics | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:06
claim: Even a low-value subscription on a one-time product usually lasts about three months, tripling that customer's LTV — if half of buyers take it, revenue doubles.
detail: If half of buyers take a recurring option on a one-time-purchase product (e.g., physical products) and half stay at 1x, revenue doubles; he adds "not bad, just by making it available".
numbers: ~3 months typical subscription life → 3x LTV; 50% uptake → ~2x revenue.

### MM-26#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: LTGP | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:56
claim: Cutting monthly churn from 10% to 3% more than triples lifetime revenue — conquer churn to get big LTGP.
detail: Churn = share of customers present at the start of a month who cancel during it. $100/month at 10% churn = $1,000 lifetime revenue; at 3% ≈ $3,300 (Hormozi rounds to $3,000). It is not "only 7 points" — it is a 70% cut in churn. Customers weigh today's price far more than term, which is why continuity works.
numbers: 10% → 3% churn: $1,000 → ~$3,000+ lifetime revenue.

### MM-26#08 | FRAMEWORK | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv, unit-economics | fw: Big Head Long Tail | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:43
claim: Big head, long tail: charge a large upfront payment followed by a small recurring fee — customers protect the low rate they "bought", so churn is tiny.
detail: From a mentor named John: his tennis club charged $100 down plus $10/month versus $19.99/month without the down payment; members phoned before their cards expired to keep the $10 rate. Offer "$1,000 now and keep a $50/month rate, or $200/month" — many pay the grand, and the $50 tail churns so little it can out-earn $200/month. The bigger the offset between upfront and continuity, the better (test the numbers). Upfront cash (e.g., $5,000 down, $200/month) funds CAC and commissions; sunk cost keeps cancellations very low (00:09:16).
numbers: $100 + $10/mo vs $19.99/mo; $1,000 + $50/mo vs $200/mo; $5,000 + $200/mo.
quote: You can shear a sheep for a lifetime, but skin it once.

### MM-26#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: verticals | also: money-models, pricing | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:38
claim: Hormozi thinks the main reason many education and media businesses have poor continuity is a price-to-value mismatch — information is worth a lot before you learn it and almost nothing after.
detail: Fix: price one-time value one-time (high up front, or annual), and charge continuity only for consumable value. Gym Launch: the installed sales system was one-time value; monthly consultant calls reviewing metrics, monthly new plays and new tested ads were consumables. Pricing continuity on the blended value means high churn. Annual billing also buys a year to develop the next offer for the next problem (sales → marketing → onboarding).

### MM-26#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: retention-ltv | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:16
claim: Enterprise value is future cash flow discounted by how likely it is, so retained recurring revenue earns a far higher multiple than transactional revenue.
detail: Two companies with $10M revenue and $3M EBITDA: all-transactional might sell at ~4x ($12M); all-recurring, depending on retention, 8–15x ($24–45M). If customers stay, buyers (investors) pay. Personally, continuity means sleeping well: on the 1st, bills are covered and profit locked in; everything else is gravy (00:11:19).
numbers: $3M EBITDA × 4 = $12M vs × 8–15 = $24–45M.

### MM-26#11 | DEFINITION | topic: finance-exit | also: retention-ltv | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:21
claim: Recurring revenue (memberships, e.g., Netflix) differs from reoccurring revenue (habitual repeat purchases, e.g., Coca-Cola); both are valuable if repeat behaviour can be demonstrated.
detail: Example: a sauce brand Hormozi has bought for about four years, three or four bottles a month — such a company can show the average buyer purchases ~20 more bottles, which makes it valuable.
numbers: ~20 repeat bottles per average buyer (illustrative).
