### POD-0780#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Many business "isms" (never work with a spouse, only you can sell or handle the money) are rules built on someone's single aversive experience, and one counterexample disproves them.
detail: He treats this as trauma in the literal sense: a negative event that permanently changes behaviour. Better to say "I lack the skill to work with family" than to condemn everyone who does.

### POD-0780#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:00
claim: A private equity firm's door sign banned husband-wife partners because one couple had defrauded them; Hormozi argues the real variable is skill and fit, not marital status.
detail: Spouses cannot be forced to testify against each other, which the partner felt raised fraud risk. Hormozi's counter: any two people over 20 years rarely work well together, and most partnerships and marriages fail anyway, so avoid broad generalisations that block steps you may need later.

### POD-0780#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:21
claim: Hold beliefs strongly but loosely: commit to them because of the data, and change the conclusion as soon as new information arrives.
detail: Danger starts when people wrap their identity in the belief and become immovable.

### POD-0780#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:51
claim: As a company scales, the founder must make identity fluid: identity is the bundle of skills and behaviours others use to describe you, so changing behaviour changes who you are.
detail: A loose link between identity and beliefs speeds becoming the person the business needs. Diagnostic: list skills or behaviours you lack, and strong beliefs blocking the actions needed to grow.
