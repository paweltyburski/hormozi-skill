### YT-hnfh2jqNVEQ#02 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: retention-ltv | fw: 80/20 Customer Analysis | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:17
claim: To find whom to qualify for, compare the last 90 days' worst customers (exits, escalations, least results) with the best.
detail: At Gym Launch the best did better if they were gym owners, full-time in the gym, had a signed lease or their own building, and had employees. Employees matter because implementing new systems takes time on top of the existing business.
numbers: 90-day look-back window.

### YT-hnfh2jqNVEQ#03 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: Six Ways to Increase Value | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:18
claim: After adding qualification, Gym Launch's sales fell by half but escalations went from 22 to zero in four weeks.
detail: The new criteria were at least 25 clients, a signed lease, a full-time owner and ideally one employee. Admissions dropped from over 100 gyms a month to 40-50. The newcomers paid more, made more and needed less team time.
numbers: Sales from 100+ to 40-50 gyms a month; escalations 22 to 0 (compared four-week periods); minimum 25 clients.

### YT-hnfh2jqNVEQ#06 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:19
claim: A brief experiment selling year-long access up front with a 90-day sprint worked worse than the defined-end programme used for three years.
detail: A defined end is easier for people to grasp and clarifies the skills to learn, so they reverted after the trial.
numbers: 12-month upfront offer tried briefly; defined-end programme used for three years.

### YT-hnfh2jqNVEQ#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: GROWTH Onboarding | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:26
claim: Customers decide whether to stay at two points: between sale and start, and during orientation.
detail: Choreograph the orientation by demonstrating each skill with them and having them repeat it until they are self-sufficient. If onboarding is rushed, lengthen it, since it multiplies lifetime value.

### YT-hnfh2jqNVEQ#09 | DEFINITION | topic: objections-closing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:30
claim: "Selling hot" is setting expectations so high that most customers cannot reach them.
detail: Counter it by selling toward behaviour and sustainable change, and by moving the prospect to a realistic time frame. Customers who earn $20,000 in a first month can still be disappointed if told to expect more, as can someone losing five pounds in 14 days.
numbers: Examples: $20,000 first month; five pounds in 14 days.

### YT-hnfh2jqNVEQ#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-process | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:38
claim: Sell off the average, and in an ideal world you would probably try to sell on the bottom 25% of results, so everyone feels they overachieve.
detail: Gym Launch stopped leading with its best cases and publishes averages instead, noting half do worse. The average he quoted was about $16,000 in the first six weeks (metric unclear in audio).
numbers: Average ~$16,000 in first six weeks; in an ideal world, probably sell on the bottom 25% of results.
