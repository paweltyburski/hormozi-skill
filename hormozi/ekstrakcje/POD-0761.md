### POD-0761#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: People tend to fill their plate with problems they know how to solve, which are usually lower impact, so the big problem goes unsolved.
detail: Triggered by a department issue that stayed unresolved three weeks running, he recalled Peter Thiel enforcing a single-focus policy. Big leaps come from harder problems with more variables and parties. Without ruthless prioritisation, the pay and work hours spent give a poor return.
numbers: Issue persisted three weeks in a row.

### POD-0761#02 | DEFINITION | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:22
claim: Strategy means prioritisation of limited resources among unlimited options; a better strategist sees plays that are not on your list.
detail: A first-level strategist ranks the existing list well. A second-level one spots items with a higher return that others cannot see, and that is when people call strategy brilliant. The same rule at manager level: regularly and ruthlessly strike the to-do list down to the one most important item.

### POD-0761#03 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:26
claim: To speed up a stuck item use two levers together: eliminate everything else on the owner's plate and raise the check-in frequency.
detail: His "hammer" is telling the person this is the only thing that matters, asking what they did on it since yesterday. His "screwdriver" is moving a weekly meeting to daily or twice daily until done. The frequent check-in signals the boss believes nothing else matters and creates quick positive reinforcement. The two must be paired, or the person faces conflict.

### POD-0761#04 | CASE | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:57
claim: A customer service rep asked to write a new SOP by day's end failed because 16 tickets arrived, so priority must include taking other work off them.
detail: Unless told to defer tickets (or given overtime), the rep rationally handles the queue. He argues it is acceptable to upset 16 customers if the SOP issue is upsetting 500. People want to do well; they get conflicted when prioritisation is left to them.
numbers: 16 tickets versus 500 customers affected.

### POD-0761#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:05
claim: Team to-do lists only grow and drift from strategy because people lack context, so share financials and drivers.
detail: He and his wife publish nearly everything internally. He cites Sam Walton calling secrecy about financials and delayed ownership a mistake. More leadership-minded people then see how their role ties to growth and can decide faster. Not everyone cares, which is fine.

### POD-0761#06 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:31
claim: Go through each direct report's full task list in a one-on-one to prune what is no longer important, including your own forgotten assignments.
detail: He assumed his team was perfectly productive, but on review found that one person had spent about a quarter of their time on a project he himself had forgotten and no longer needed. He treats each person as a tree that needs regular pruning toward their one point of leverage.
numbers: About 25% of one person's time on a forgotten project.

### POD-0761#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:18
claim: Skip routine output (an email, a video, a podcast week) when the big priority needs the capacity, provided the long-term cadence is not killed.
detail: He dislikes streak rules and cares about their spirit: one missed week costs little relative to not finishing the big item. He keeps a trump card to "stop the trains". To turn down opportunities he tries to save them somewhere so they do not feel lost.
### POD-0761#08 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: Impact vs Investment Matrix (RICE) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:05
claim: From five good ideas pick one and solve it; the rest may become irrelevant or be replaced by a new number one.
detail: In the product roadmap, solving the biggest item often removed one or two others because the problem was solved or the requests stopped. New priorities sometimes outranked all remaining ones. Spreading across five could have left none done.
numbers: Example list of five candidates, choose one.

### POD-0761#09 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:35
claim: Give a split-day schedule and end-of-day updates when a role cannot drop its routine duties completely.
detail: Example: customer service staff only answer phones from nine to noon for a week and spend the afternoon on the priority, sending a daily update for positive reinforcement. A weekly cadence can be too slow: helping with three decisions in one day can save about 21 days of delay.
numbers: Phones 9:00-12:00; 3 decisions in a day save about 21 days.
