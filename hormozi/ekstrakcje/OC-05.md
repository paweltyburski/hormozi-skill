### OC-05#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: product-delivery | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:46
claim: An offer may fail because the delivery does not actually solve the customer's real problems, even when the marketing claims it does.
detail: Offer component four: people market that they solve a problem, but their fulfillment and delivery do not actually solve it. The process he uses starts from the prospect's dream outcome and perceived problems.

### OC-05#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: offers | also: product-delivery | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:46
claim: Hormozi builds a Grand Slam Offer by identifying the dream outcome, listing all perceived problems, turning every problem into a solution, and then packaging the solutions into a delivery system.
detail: 1. Identify the dream outcome. 2. List all perceived problems, everything the prospect thinks will get in the way, found by walking every step they must take. 3. Turn every problem into a solution so every way to fail is eliminated. 4. (Next lesson) Package solutions into a delivery system: choose vehicles, trim and stack. Success makes customers love you and refer friends.

### OC-05#03 | SCRIPT | topic: offers | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:32
claim: State the dream outcome as: [specific outcome] in [specific time period] without [biggest pain or fear], optionally prefixed with the avatar.
detail: Structure: avatar + measurable outcome + time frame + "without" their biggest fear. Make the gap feel easy to cross. Examples: "lose 20 pounds in six weeks without any risk"; "I help moms with more than three kids lose weight within six months without going to the gym or changing what their family eats". If you cannot say it clearly, write it out until you can; otherwise you claim to help anyone.

### OC-05#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:21
claim: Hormozi calls it one of the easiest recipes for success, especially when starting out, to solve a problem you have already solved for yourself, do it so well that others notice, and climb to the next problem when people ask how you did it.
detail: His sequence: got in shape, people paid him to help; started a fitness business (eventually six gyms); gym owners asked how, so Gym Launch; licensing, education and consulting businesses asked how, so he helped them; now Acquisition.com. Each solved problem creates a new audience. He notes you can also identify markets and go after them directly, but for someone starting out this is one of the easiest routes, and being your own prospect helps you know the dream outcome and obstacles.

### OC-05#06 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: product-delivery | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:25
claim: To list perceived problems, walk through every intricate step the customer must take to succeed with your product, and treat each step as a problem.
detail: Ask what each step to success is and what you struggled with. Weight-loss eating steps: grocery shopping, packing and unpacking food, cooking, prepping and portioning, eating, cleaning dishes, feeding the family, eating out. Each step is its own problem with several components, hence endless opportunities.

### OC-05#07 | FRAMEWORK | topic: offers | also: sales-process, objections-closing | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:14
claim: Every perceived problem shows up in four flavours that mirror the value equation in reverse: "not worth it", "won't work for me", "too hard", and "takes too long".
detail: 1. Dream outcome: "it's not worth it". 2. Perceived likelihood: "won't work for me", "can't stick with it", "too many stores", external factors; external factors are the most service-specific, the rest are common. 3. Effort and sacrifice: "too hard", "confusing", "won't enjoy it", "I'll be bad at it"; counter by making it easy, simple, fun or gamified, making them feel good at it. 4. Time delay: "takes too long", "not convenient". Each step-problem can carry five or more of these; reverse each. Many are changed by communication alone.

### OC-05#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: strategy-positioning, content-brand | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:46
claim: Solving problems no competitor even mentions positions you uniquely and makes prospects feel understood, which raises their belief that you will succeed for them.
detail: Addressing every perceived problem shows you understand them better than anyone, raising perceived likelihood and positioning you where no one else is.

### OC-05#09 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: content-brand | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:16
claim: Turn each problem into a solution by simply reversing the objection, phrased as "how to [do the task] [without the obstacle]".
detail: Grocery shopping example: hard, confusing or "I'll be bad at it" becomes how to make buying healthy food easy and enjoyable for busy moms; too slow becomes how to buy it quickly; expensive becomes how to buy it for less than your current grocery bill; unsustainable becomes how to make it less effort than buying unhealthy food; family needs or kids' refusal becomes buying for you and your family in the same store; travel and eating out becomes buying healthy food while traveling. Repeat for every problem ("chunking down"), then re-bundle into distilled bullets. A plain mechanical reversal, reused in marketing and sales copy; packaging comes in part two.

### OC-05#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:07
claim: If your solution takes less effort than the customer's current behaviour, they are likely to stick with it, because people always go to the path of least resistance.
detail: Decision energy is limited, so people keep the easiest option. Make the new behaviour (buying healthy food) easier than the old one and adherence is likely; claim it in copy.

### OC-05#11 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: sales-process | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:03
claim: Solve every perceived problem, because you cannot know which one a given prospect is holding — and addressing all of them maximises both conversion and customer success.
detail: Each prospect holds a handful of unknown reasons; covering every problem maximises conversion and ensures success.

### OC-05#12 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: retention-ltv | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:03
claim: A shortcut for finding more problems: map the customer's journey before and after they work with you and ask what the need just before and just after your solution is.
detail: Besides the core process, check what they need right before they come and right after, since they are solving those too. "Lubricate" the path so no obstacle is unaccounted for; successful customers refer friends.
