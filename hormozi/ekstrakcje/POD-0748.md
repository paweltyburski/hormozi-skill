### POD-0748#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:27
claim: Offering your time for free is worth nothing to someone who already has money; it is worth something to people slightly ahead of you who cannot afford many hires.
detail: A stranger asked to do anything for Hormozi for free, in return for being taught. Hormozi replies that he pays money precisely so he does not have to pay with his own time. Start with someone one or two steps ahead, not a top figure whose standards are far above your skill.

### POD-0748#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:24
claim: A wealthy founder already has all the personal help he needs and trains new assistants through the older ones.
detail: Hormozi and his wife have four executive assistants, trained by the senior ones to take up as little of their time as possible. A volunteer offering coffee and trash duty adds nothing.
numbers: 4 executive assistants.

### POD-0748#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: lead-getters | fw: Perceived Hidden Costs | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:44
claim: Even free labour costs the host onboarding time and carries no promise of upside, which is why first customers and first jobs often refuse free work.
detail: A hire with proven skill is a trade the employer understands. A zero-skill person asking to be taught costs money and time with no return, so free does not remove the cost on the receiving side.

### POD-0748#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:55
claim: Opportunity is hunted, not awaited, and a pitch must be framed as a gain for the other person.
detail: Becoming the best at one specific skill makes being hired close to guaranteed, so do not bet your life on a chance meeting. Bring a skill beyond your hands and time.
