### LC-06#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Content monetises through reciprocity: give repeatedly and people feel indebted and want to give back — treat goodwill like a bank account of deposits and withdrawals.
detail: Gary Vaynerchuk's "jab, jab, jab, right hook." Signals it is working: people ask what you have for sale or how to support you; others ask you to promote their product, meaning you are building a brand. If no one ever asks you to push their product, he says it is because you lack goodwill and they can see it.

### LC-06#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:34
claim: Mature media platforms maximising monetisation run about 3.6–4 parts content to 1 part ads: TV about 47 minutes of content to 13 of commercials per hour, Facebook about one ad per five posts.
detail: All ads would kill the audience (the asset); no ads would not pay the bills. TV uses cliffhangers before breaks so viewers wait out the ads. Both are mature cash cows harvesting an existing audience, so their ratio maximises monetisation, not growth.
numbers: TV 3.6:1 (≈47:13 min per hour); Facebook ≈4:1 posts.

### LC-06#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:37
claim: If you only want to harvest, use a 3–4:1 give:ask ratio; to keep growing, give far more — fast-growing platforms run few or no ads for years.
detail: TikTok gave for years with few or no ads; goodwill drove sharing, sharing drew users who shared more, and growth compounded. Maximum monetisation is not maximum growth; the ratio depends on your life-cycle stage. Hormozi's version: not "give, give, give, ask" but give until people say "can I pay you for something?"
quote: Give until they ask.

### LC-06#04 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: outreach | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:57
claim: Advanced version: give in public, ask in private — your public reputation stays purely about giving, and sales conversations happen only when people message you.
detail: Your reputation among the 99.9% who never buy stays pure goodwill, which compounds fastest. Asking publicly slows growth; patience earns more when you finally ask. Spectrum: 100% ask gets no attention; 100% give gets attention but no income; ~4:1 monetises without growth; stay on the giving side. Any ask leaves the maximum-growth path — acceptable, given bills and a team — so make each count.
quote: Asking for money publicly is deciding to slow your growth.

### LC-06#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: offers | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:08
claim: If what you sell is exceptional, an ask can itself deposit goodwill — you make money and still build the brand.
detail: Most products are not exceptional, so asks usually withdraw. Hormozi sells his books at the lowest price Amazon allows ($2.99 digital) because each purchase grows the brand. Brand over everything: reinforce it with free content and with product value.
numbers: Kindle ~$2.99.

### LC-06#06 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Integrated vs Intermittent Asks | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:16
claim: Ask like a TV commercial: briefly interrupt your own value to mention what you sell and who it is for, then go straight back to giving.
detail: "By the way, here's this thing — if you're this type of person it'll work well for you. OK, back to it." If the purchase delivers more value, the next "commercial" converts more, creating a compounding cycle — why brands with goodwill are so valuable.

### LC-06#07 | FRAMEWORK | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Integrated vs Intermittent Asks | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:57
claim: Asks go in content two ways: integrated (a short ask inside each piece, keeping the ratio high by time) and intermittent (occasional whole pieces carrying an offer between many pure-value pieces).
detail: Integrated suits long content: a 30-second ask in 60 minutes is about 59:1, far above 3–4:1. Intermittent suits short-form, where a proportional ask would last a second: many pure-give pieces, then one (still hook-retain-reward) with an offer — e.g., one in 11 or one in 51. Audiences accept that you pay your bills.
numbers: Integrated ~59:1 by time; intermittent 10:1 to 50:1 pieces.

### LC-06#08 | CASE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:45
claim: A friend's fast-growing podcast stopped growing and then shrank after he started asking too often — each ask earned less, so he asked more, a vicious cycle.
detail: Valuable content made it blow up; his first offer made a lot, so he offered more and more often. Returns per ask fell, bills pushed more asks, and the podcast shrank. He should have returned to pure giving, rebuilt goodwill, then asked again. Hold asks back and ask with an open hand.

### LC-06#09 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Integrated vs Intermittent Asks | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:47
claim: Place integrated CTAs after a valuable moment or at the end first, check audience growth does not slow, and only then add a second or third.
detail: Add ask placements one at a time while watching growth.

### LC-06#10 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:47
claim: In written content, the P.S. is the most-read part after the headline — put your call to action there.
detail: Readers read the top, scroll to see the length, and read the bottom. Make headline and P.S. (or P.P.S.) your strongest lines.

### LC-06#11 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Integrated vs Intermittent Asks | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:28
claim: A lead magnet CTA in content works best when the magnet continues that piece's topic — viewers have already selected themselves as having the problem.
detail: After content on getting leads: "I have 11 more tips — grab a visual of them on my site"; the thank-you page then shows a paid offer. For skateboard-cleaning content, offer skateboard cleaning: a free bottle, PDF, video, call, or "ship it in, I'll clean it free" (possibly a customer for life).

### LC-06#12 | SCRIPT | topic: content-brand | also: offers | fw: Integrated vs Intermittent Asks | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:55
claim: A core-offer CTA in content reuses the outreach offer: "If you've enjoyed my content, I'm looking for five people who want [outcome] in [time] without [effort]; if you don't get it I'll refund you and work with you until you do — if that sounds fair, DM me / book a call / reply."
detail: Elements: tie to the content, a limited number, dream outcome and time frame, no effort or sacrifice, a guarantee with a reason ("I want everyone to have an amazing experience"), a clear response route. The CTA can be lead magnet or core offer, placed integrated or intermittent.

### LC-06#13 | EXAMPLE | topic: content-brand | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:13:43
claim: Hormozi's books end each chapter by pointing to free online downloads and swipe files — cross-promoting lead magnets inside existing content.
detail: Likewise YouTube viewers are pointed to the book. Action step: choose integrated or intermittent asks, choose lead magnet or core offer, then get back to giving.

### LC-06#14 | FRAMEWORK | topic: content-brand | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Depth-then-Width | novelty: canon | ts: 00:14:31
claim: Depth-then-width: post on one relevant platform, post regularly, maximise quality and quantity there, then add the next platform and repeat — his likely best first shot if you are resource-constrained or have never made content.
detail: Maximising means short-form sometimes up to about 10 posts a day (e.g., Twitter, TikTok), long-form roughly every few days to weekly before the audience can't consume more. Pros: best return on effort; audiences compound as they grow, so effort where you are good beats starting over. Cons: no omnipresence, and single-channel dependence — risky if edgy posts get you shut down or throttled while the business relies on that lead flow. Master one platform before several.
numbers: Short-form sometimes up to ~10/day; long-form roughly every few days to weekly (once every ~5 days to once a week).

### LC-06#15 | FRAMEWORK | topic: content-brand | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Width-then-Depth | novelty: canon | ts: 00:16:55
claim: Width-then-depth: post regularly on one platform, spread to the most similar platforms by reformatting the same content until you are on all relevant ones, then maximise them all — he says it might be the better call for more experienced creators (beginners: depth first).
detail: One short video reformatted for TikTok, Reels and Shorts costs little extra; text adapts across LinkedIn and Facebook. Pros: broader audience faster, cheap repurposing. Cons: more skill, attention, time and labour, and lots of bad content everywhere early on. Choose by current skill.

### LC-06#16 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: operator-mindset | fw: Five Topic Sources | novelty: canon | ts: 00:17:41
claim: Hormozi's routine as a non-full-time creator: twice a month he records about 30 short clips from his topic sources plus 2–4 longer videos expanding his best tweets — which grew a 5–6 million audience in two years.
detail: He finds topics via the five sources (calendar, tweets), records the shorts, and the same day records long videos unpacking tweets that had stories or examples. Start simple; add volume later.
numbers: 2 sessions/month; ~30 shorts + 2–4 long videos each; ~5–6M audience in ~2 years.

### LC-06#17 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:18:52
claim: People overestimate the time content takes and underestimate how good it must be — aim for content people would send to someone, because platforms reward sharing.
detail: Ask "how do I make this valuable enough that they say 'I didn't know that' or 'I should share this'?" Platforms want new users, so they reward content shared off-platform that brings people in. Aim for the overlap between what you like discussing and what your audience wants.

### LC-06#18 | CASE | topic: content-brand | also: paid-ads, verticals | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:20:06
claim: At Gym Launch, paid ads underperformed because Hormozi had stopped making gym-specific content 12 weeks earlier — a survey showed 78% of customers had consumed at least two long-form pieces before buying.
detail: At a quarterly meeting Layla asked, "What did we stop doing 12 weeks ago?" Alex had switched from gym-specific to general content. The survey revealed he had credited ads for results driven by content goodwill; ads only harvested an audience content had warmed. Content lifts every method — outreach prospects who check your profile and find value reply and believe more — and keeps delivering indefinitely.
numbers: 78% of customers consumed ≥2 long-form pieces before buying; ad drop ~12 weeks after the content change.

### LC-06#19 | FRAMEWORK | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:20:56
claim: Hormozi's seven content lessons: say "how I" not "how to"; people need reminding more than teaching; grow from puddles to oceans; content creates sales tools; free content retains paying customers; there is no such thing as too long, only too boring; avoid pre-scheduling posts.
detail: Lesson six repeats Part I (LC-05): people have higher standards, not shorter attention spans — focus on value, not the algorithm. The others have their own atoms.

### LC-06#20 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:20:56
claim: Frame content as "how I" rather than "how to" — sharing what you did instead of prescribing takes ego out and stops people trying to disprove you.
detail: "How I built my seven-figure agency," "my favourite way to generate leads," not "the best way." With no universal claim, the audience simply listens.

### LC-06#21 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:22:12
claim: People need to be reminded more than taught — you tire of your message long before your audience absorbs it, so repeat it for years.
detail: One in six of Hormozi's surveyed audience did not know he had a book after two years of near-daily posts. A Ford executive, tired of an ad after three months, learned it had not launched yet. After one post about something, he says only about 1% of your audience knows. People return to creators to be reminded (be patient, work hard), so repetition serves them.
numbers: 1 in 6 unaware of the book after ~2 years of near-daily posts.

### LC-06#22 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Puddles-Ponds-Lakes-Oceans | novelty: canon | ts: 00:24:15
claim: In his opinion, start content in the smallest pond where you are clearly credible, become the biggest fish there, then move to a lake and the ocean — a guppy in the ocean is screwed.
detail: A small local business making general business content has a low likelihood of succeeding (he says it's not that it shouldn't), because tycoons make content and he would pick the more credible voice. A plumber becomes king of his town's plumbing puddle, then local business (pond), brick-and-mortar chains (lake), general business (ocean). What he perceives as the number one mistake: a 20-year-old life coach giving universal advice. Fix: talk only about your own struggles and specific people you helped ("Here's Sarah and how we tackled her anxiety") — indisputable. Widen later: "across everyone I've worked with…"

### LC-06#23 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: content-brand, paid-ads, objections-closing | fw: Greatest Hits | novelty: canon | ts: 00:26:21
claim: Content that unexpectedly generates inquiries is gold: run it as a paid ad, and build a "greatest hits" list mapping content to common objections so salespeople can send the right piece.
detail: Do not necessarily make more of it (it sits closer to a right hook). Reuse it: (1) as an ad — content the platform already proved often makes the best ads; (2) as sales tools — collect pieces answering specific concerns ("too long", "too complicated", "works in my area?"), label each with problem solved and benefit, and let reps send one or two between calls. Prospects consume more and close more.

### LC-06#24 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:27:37
claim: Free content retains paying customers: they do not separate where value came from, so valuable free content makes them stay longer.
detail: A paying customer consuming your free content just feels they got more than they paid for. Giving a lot away does not alienate payers; it confirms they chose right.

### LC-06#25 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:28:39
claim: Avoid pre-scheduling posts — Hormozi's team found manual posts perform better, so they prepare ahead but publish by hand.
detail: His unproven explanation: pressing "post" yourself means feedback within seconds, which triggers a final check of caption and headline; scheduling a month removes that moment.

### LC-06#26 | EXAMPLE | topic: content-brand | also: outreach | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: canon | ts: 00:30:09
claim: Your first post can be an announcement with an ask, since you have probably given value for a long time — Hormozi's 2013 first post announced his Free Training Project and a few open roster slots.
detail: Treat it as a "state of the union" about a life change. His post: a self-deprecating opener (bad with tech, loves training), a year of training people free for a $500–1,000 donation to their chosen cause, a new website of client transformations, "a few slots open — drop me a note." He had worked privately through DMs for a year first. With no following, give longer before asking. Just start.
numbers: 2013; ~1 year of private outreach before posting.

### LC-06#28 | BENCHMARK | topic: content-brand | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:32:44
claim: Measure content monthly by audience size (followers, reach) and growth rate: growing means you did well, growing fast means better.
detail: Size: 1,000 → 1,500 followers is +500; reach 10,000 → 15,000 is +5,000. Rate: 500 new on a base of 1,000 in a month is a 50% growth month. Measuring takes maybe 60 minutes once a month. Rule of thumb he got from "Dr K": the more you measure, the more ways you can win.
numbers: 1,000 → 1,500 followers in a month = 50% growth; maybe ~60 min/month.

### LC-06#29 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:33:40
claim: Every account starts at zero and early growth percentages are usually huge — he suggests comparing yourself with your own past and your real alternative, not with people far ahead (his opinion, what has worked for him).
detail: Going from 10 to 50 viewers is a win. Hormozi treated 10 views as a room of 10 people — efficient compared with pitching weight loss one-to-one. It feels small only against someone with 10–100x your time. Compare month to month and ignore everyone else.

### LC-06#30 | DECISION_RULE | topic: content-brand | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:34:33
claim: You control only inputs, and output metrics mean something only if inputs are consistent — fix a posting cadence and an ask cadence per platform, then start and do not stop.
detail: The ask cadence is what turns audience into engaged leads. Hold both steady so monthly measures are meaningful.
