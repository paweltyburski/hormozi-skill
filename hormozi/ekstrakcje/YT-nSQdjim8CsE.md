### YT-nSQdjim8CsE#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Four Types of Offers | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:28
claim: A typical gym "low barrier" front end, a $21 21-day trial rolling into a $99 membership, could spend only about $53 per lead even in a best case.
detail: Walkthrough: 100 leads at $20 each cost $2,000. In a good local market 25% buy the $21 offer (about $525). A third of those convert to the $99 membership (8 people, about $800 up front). They stay roughly five months, adding about $4,000. Total about $5,325 per 100 leads, so the ceiling on spend per lead is $53, before rent, trainers and admin. The model has no bridge between the trial and the membership.
numbers: 100 leads x $20 = $2,000; 25% close at $21 = $525; ~35% (a third) trial-to-membership; $99/month; average stay ~5 months; total ~$5,325 or ~$53 per lead.
applies_to: Local gyms; mechanics apply to any trial-to-membership business.

### YT-nSQdjim8CsE#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Win Your Money Back | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:11:06
claim: His six-week-challenge model earned about $19,500 in 30 days from the same 100 leads, letting him spend roughly $245 per lead against $53.
detail: Assumptions made deliberately worse than reality: same $2,000 lead cost (free offers usually cost about half), only 15% of leads buy a challenge at $500, so $7,500 up front. Then 12 of those 15 buy supplements averaging $200 (about $2,400, roughly 80% gross margin, about $2,000 profit) within 48 hours. Add the year-long conversion and prepay steps below. The effect is a roughly 5x higher affordable ad spend, paid back 8-9 times on a card within 30 days.
numbers: 15% convert at $500 = $7,500; 12 of 15 buy ~$200 supplements = $2,400 (~80% GM); total ~$19,500 in 30 days plus ~$5,000 later = ~$24,500, ~$245 per lead; ~$8-9 back per $1 ad spend.

### YT-nSQdjim8CsE#04 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Rollover Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:13
claim: Credit the $500 challenge payment toward a year of membership at the end of the challenge window, which got about 70% to continue versus about a third for a $21 trial.
detail: A $21 credit is meaningless leverage; a $500 credit toward a year is real money, so he roughly doubled the continuation rate. With better pricing knowledge he moved the monthly rate from $99 to about $250 less credit, roughly $200 a month in a large group-training studio. Of 15 buyers about 10 stayed, producing about $2,000 monthly recurring and about $10,000 over five months.
numbers: ~70% of challenge buyers continued (10 of 15); $99 membership became ~$200/month net; ~$2,000 a month recurring from 10 members.

claim: Once members are committed for a year, offer a further 10-20% off for paying the whole year in advance.

Examples of his offer stack: Win Your Money Back (attraction), supplements as classic upsell, credit toward a year as rollover upsell, paid-in-full as buy 10 months get 2 free.

### YT-nSQdjim8CsE#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: paid-ads | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:28:20
claim: Whichever advertiser earns the most gross profit per customer can outbid all others for an avatar's attention and effectively monopolise it.
detail: His reasoning at the level of Gym Launch's market: equipment dealers, supplement sellers, agencies, builders and apparel firms all bid for the same gym owner's feed. The one with the strongest cash flow per customer can raise the price of attention until others cannot compete. Gym Launch applied a superior money model twice: it taught gyms one, and it ran one itself to out-advertise other vendors selling to the same owners. Differences lie in how money flows, how fast and to whom.

### YT-nSQdjim8CsE#07 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:26
claim: Seeing a former classmate's supplement stores earn $80,000 and $130,000 a month pushed him to sell supplements to gym members, which let him advertise again when CAC reached $500.
detail: Context: his $99 membership with CAC rising to $500 left him out of pocket and he would need to carry a loss for months. He bought stock, struggled to sell it, then found a way to sell a few hundred dollars per new member within 48 hours. This turned the economics and allowed him to open more locations. Competitors then spent only enough for leads and had to wait a month to reload.
numbers: Stores reported at $80,000 and $130,000 a month (claimed, not verified); gym CAC rose to ~$500 versus $99/month membership; supplements ~$200-$300 per member in first 48 hours (a few hundred dollars).
