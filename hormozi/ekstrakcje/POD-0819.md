### POD-0819#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:11:02
claim: The founder's earlier custom-goods marketplace was a bad model, and outside money turned a nearly profitable business into one he no longer controlled.
detail: A buyer and maker had around 60 back-and-forths over specs on every custom item, and the platform had to sit between them to earn a ~10% fee. It was nearly profitable at about $60,000 a month, up from ~$35,000 a year, on a small convertible raise (about 7% dilution). Later rounds ($18M including $4M secondary) left him with about 8% and investor pressure; he says every business he has had since was bootstrapped. Hormozi's view: ask whether a normal business really needs capital before raising it.
numbers: Revenue ~$35,000/year to ~$60,000/month in 12 months; marketplace fee ~10%; $500,000 convertible ~7% equity; ended owning ~8%.
applies_to: Marketplaces and custom, made-to-order goods

### POD-0819#02 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:23:46
claim: A Kickstarter launch reverse-engineered the platform's "verified" badge, which pushes a campaign to strangers rather than only the founder's contacts.
detail: He studied which campaigns earned the badge and found it followed early momentum, so he contacted other founders and drove sales to land quickly. With the badge the campaign reached random people who didn't know him. The target was $25,000 of presales.
numbers: $40,000 sold on day one against a $25,000 goal; final $210,000 of presales = 1,100 boxes.

### POD-0819#03 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:27:40
claim: Presell a "butcher's selection" so you decide the contents after the demand is known and hold no expensive inventory in advance.
detail: Inventory of premium cuts was costly and sales per cut unknown, so buyers purchased a box and the mix was chosen later. A Wisconsin firm already ran cutting and pick-pack-ship, which allowed shipping all 1,100 boxes in about a week.

### POD-0819#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:43:36
claim: A physical subscription box runs about 30% gross margin, a third of it spent on operations, leaving about 20% for marketing and profit; net margin was about 6.5%.
detail: The founder wants to stay at or above 30%. Operations (salaries, technology) take about 10 points. The CPG playbook is to market heavily for years to build the name, then cut marketing and harvest. Hormozi compares it with software, except the hard part is supply chain. The guest founder (not Hormozi) says subscription box firms are not valued at software multiples because of physical product.
numbers: Gross margin ~30%; operating costs ~10% of revenue; ~20% left for marketing and EBITDA; net margin ~6.5% last year.
applies_to: Consumer physical products and subscription boxes

### POD-0819#09 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:53:58
claim: At 3% monthly churn on about 430,000 customers, the business must add about 12,000 customers a month just to stand still.
detail: One percent of the base is about 4,300 customers, so numbers get large as the base grows. Hormozi translates this to roughly 400 a day leaving. To grow the company needs more top-of-funnel customers each month than the leakage.
numbers: 430,000 customers; 3% monthly churn (about 30-ish% per year); about 12,000 sign-ups a month to stop treading water.
### POD-0819#10 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:47:47
claim: A founder who does not want to raise, list or sell can still cash out shareholders through yearly profit-funded share buybacks.
detail: Each year the company repurchases shares with profit, priced by an independent 409A valuation, and recently also ran a reverse Dutch auction: it sets a pot of money and holders state the price at which they will sell, which shows an actual market value. Valuation came out around 1x revenue, which he considers low but irrelevant when not selling. About 30% owned by others, roughly a $150 million obligation, handled by asking holders to be patient. During COVID almost all marketing was switched off and profit bought shares.
numbers: Buyback valuation about 1x revenue; ~30% owned by others (~$150M).
applies_to: Private bootstrapped founders with minority investors

### POD-0819#11 | EXAMPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:51:40
claim: Paying one-time help in equity at a very low company valuation later cost millions to buy back.
detail: He gave away stakes for $75,000 of work, valuing the company at $750,000, each 10%. He later bought both holders out when the valuation was about $48M, one of them receiving $200,000 a month for two years. The work was one-time, not ongoing.
numbers: 10% for $75,000 of work (valuation $750K); buyout at $48M valuation; $200,000 a month for 24 months.

### POD-0819#13 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 01:00:13

### POD-0819#16 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: management-ops | fw: CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 01:23:39
