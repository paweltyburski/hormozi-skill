claim: Take a partner only for a skill, money or time you lack; partnering because you like each other just dilutes equity.
detail: Caller was considering a partnership with a person to cover his weakest function (lead generation). Hormozi, who has had eight failed partnerships, says the early lust phase makes people skip the ugly topics: roles, responsibilities and exit terms should be defined first.
applies_to: Owner with a 10-year-old business
### YT-DAm0EOes5to#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:11
claim: People want equity for only two reasons, control or a payout on exit; if neither applies, offer a revenue or profit share instead.
detail: A need for a skill does not require an owner; you pay an accountant without making him a partner. Ask the prospective partner what he wants; if it is a share of what he brings in, write a performance agreement. Profit share favours the owner, revenue share favours the partner; negotiate between them. Equity otherwise brings liability.
numbers: Marketing providers for e-commerce commonly take about 10% of top-line sales attributed to their marketing, with no equity.

### YT-DAm0EOes5to#03 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:01
claim: Structure a performance partner's equity as phantom equity that only pays on a sale and is forfeited if he leaves, paired with profit sharing.
detail: Caller wanted to grant up to roughly a third of an S-corp as the partner hits million-dollar milestones while keeping the majority. Hormozi called that a performance relationship with kickers and proposed phantom equity (no real shares, pays only if the company is sold, lost on departure) combined with profit or revenue share.
numbers: Caller's cap: up to about one third of the company across milestones.

### YT-DAm0EOes5to#04 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:28
claim: Unwinding a 50/50 LLC where the partner stopped selling: transfer his equity to you and take a promissory note for half the debt.
detail: Agency owner and a partner ran an LLC with debt; the partner did the front-end sales but brought none in six months, while the owner did sales and delivery. Advice: the partner signs over his shares (or dissolve and form a new entity, saving about $300), and the debt is split via a simple one-page promissory note from borrower to lender, no interest, amount, instalment dates and default terms. The partner can still sell the owner's product for commission.
numbers: Promissory note: one page; new entity filing saved about $300.

### YT-DAm0EOes5to#05 | SCRIPT | topic: management-ops | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:30
claim: For a hard conversation, say to the person exactly what you would say about them behind their back, then ask what they think is fair.
detail: Sequence: state the facts of the broken agreement (you were to do sales, you did not, we both still owe), then ask "what do you think is fair". People often propose the same fix you had in mind; confirm it and sign immediately. Hormozi calls this the biggest hack in relationships.
