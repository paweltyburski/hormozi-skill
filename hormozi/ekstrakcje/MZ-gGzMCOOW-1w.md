### MZ-gGzMCOOW-1w#01 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A pre-foreclosure flipper ($3M a year for three years, $1.9M last year after a divorce, $750K of high-interest debt, goal $10M) may miss payroll next week.
detail: She switched from American callers to trained virtual assistants, keeping a skilled team on payroll. Two closings worth about $200K arrive within 30 days. The model itself did not change.
numbers: Revenue about $3M/year then $1.9M; debt $750K; two closings about $200K in 30 days.

### MZ-gGzMCOOW-1w#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:10
claim: Keeping extra capacity through a dip is a deliberate trade: you pay a bridge cost now to scale faster later, and he doesn't necessarily see anything wrong with the business.
detail: The alternative was letting the team go, which would have made it harder to reach the higher revenue. He sees it as a risky decision the caller already chose, and notes the pre-foreclosure model hasn't materially changed.
### MZ-gGzMCOOW-1w#03 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:17
claim: Raise cash by pre-selling to existing assets, such as offering investors a better deal on the next house if they front the cash, instead of borrowing.
detail: He lists what you have (hundreds of past investors, past homeowners) and asks what they would pay for. Futures are prepayments, trading a spiff for debt. Bridge loan or hard money is the fallback.
