### MZ-JSpRbQVDS-s#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: People do what they were rewarded for in the past; a so-called trigger is just a circumstance that repeats a rewarded behavior.
detail: His example: he got angry when his wife cried, she stopped crying, and the anger was rewarded. Ask why the behavior happened the first time.

### MZ-JSpRbQVDS-s#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:28
claim: When a behavior is blocked, people cycle through other behaviors (an extinction burst) until one works, so repeated tantrums mean one was once rewarded.
detail: A child at the candy aisle asks, begs, then screams. Reward the absence of the tantrum, not the tantrum.

### MZ-JSpRbQVDS-s#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:15
claim: Latency makes causality hard to see, so immediate rewards beat delayed ones unless someone was rewarded for long effort before.
detail: Work ethic generalises across domains if long effort once paid off. Most people never had a long reward cycle. He does not know how to get people to try the first time.
