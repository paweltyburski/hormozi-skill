### MZ-Mk2DEeIOmvk#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:07:07
claim: Emotional discomfort on a given day is not a sufficient reason to change what you are doing.
detail: He treats feelings as weather. Across a year, about 10% of days are bottom-decile days for no reason, roughly three per month, and the same number are top-decile days. So when a bad day hits, he asks whether it is simply one of those days. Founders who react to a bad day by changing many things can wreck a fledgling business. If you can have a bad day and still do good work, count it a good day. He uses this graph with his sales team.
numbers: About 36 bottom-10% days per year, roughly 3 per month; roughly 3 top-10% days per month.

### MZ-Mk2DEeIOmvk#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:26
claim: He deliberately sells measurable business outcomes and makes no promise about happiness.
detail: His promises are quantitative: more people finding your content brings more sales, faster cash collection grows the business, and an offer people are embarrassed to refuse converts. Where those outcomes lead in someone's life is beyond his scope, and he says many people sell happiness but he will not.

### MZ-Mk2DEeIOmvk#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:56
claim: Strivers often learned in childhood that love is earned, which locks them into a loop of working and earning.
detail: Drawing on Arthur Brooks, he describes high achievers whose parents withheld approval except after achievement. The reward loop makes earning feel like the way to get more approval. Brooks also stresses that roughly half of subjective wellbeing is genetic.
numbers: About 50% of subjective wellbeing is genetic (per Brooks, as cited).
