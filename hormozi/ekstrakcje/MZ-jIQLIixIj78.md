claim: Following the norms gives a normal outcome ("life beta"); an exceptional outcome requires reasoning for yourself and accepting outsider status.
detail: He sees high agency, plus a focus on leverage, return on time and effort, as the common thread among billionaires he has met. The more outside the return wanted, the more willingness to disagree with convention.

### MZ-jIQLIixIj78#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:58
claim: The co-host reports from interviews with eight billionaires two necessary components: a non-consensus position held for years, plus a compounding advantage.
detail: Compounding examples: network effects, a marketing advantage, a financial advantage. Holding the position was described as painful for about nine years. Claim comes from the co-host, not Hormozi.
numbers: Eight billionaires interviewed; about nine years against consensus.

### MZ-jIQLIixIj78#03 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:41
claim: For a big bet, list the "need-to-believes" and ask others to attack the assumptions; if only one is needed and it is likely, push chips in.
detail: Questions: which assumptions might be wrong, how many can be wrong while the bet still works, how wrong can I be and still be right.

### MZ-jIQLIixIj78#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:29
claim: Conviction comes from hours spent understanding and stress-testing an idea, and wavering means the idea is not your own.
detail: Example: someone told to buy a coin at $100 sells at a small gain; someone who spent 10-200 hours studying it holds. People ask when to buy and rarely when to sell.
numbers: 10-200 hours of study (his range).
