### POD-251114-20#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Classic Upsell | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:47
claim: A gym owner gave refusers a free home programme and booked them into a free nutrition orientation where supplements were sold.
detail: Almost all non-members attended; they spent more on supplements than regular clients, so no prospect left unsold. He then used the free programme as a front end, upselling an in-person version or monthly supplements.
numbers: Refusers spent 50% more on supplements than regular clients.

### POD-251114-20#02 | DEFINITION | topic: money-models | also: - | fw: Attraction Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:38
claim: Paired upsell makes thing A free in exchange for buying B; independent upsell gives A free and encourages B.
detail: The free item must have a low incremental cost; the upsell is the next natural need.

### POD-251114-20#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Classic Upsell | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:55

detail: Free storage month plus a lock sold only by the facility; a free 28-day weight-loss programme plus a supplement package; four free physiotherapy sessions plus supports and oils; free coaching while using your software; a free real-estate book with shipping, audio, contracts and training upsells.
numbers: Supplement package $400.

### POD-251114-20#04 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Attraction Offers | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:44
claim: A 200-plus weight-loss clinic chain charged $5 a month for service if clients bought its supplements, bars and meals.
detail: Doctors and nurses staffed it, yet the consumables carried the profit; the guarantee depended on continued purchasing. Hormozi concluded people pay more readily for tangible goods than for professional service.
numbers: $5 a month versus a normal $100 a month, for a year.

### POD-251114-20#06 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models | fw: Classic Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:12
claim: Free plus a $300 upsell can out-earn selling a $99 product directly.
detail: Value per free unit: take rate times price times margin. Meeting 100 people: selling 30 at $99 versus giving all 100 the free item and upselling 80 gives more money, customers and referrals.
numbers: 80% take x $300 x 80% margin = $192 per free unit; 30 x $99 versus 80 x $300.

### POD-251114-20#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: paid-ads | fw: Trial with Penalty | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:03
claim: With cold traffic take a card and trial commitment on the first transaction, then upsell on the second.
detail: Otherwise there are many no-shows. Anyone who does not buy the upsell is unlikely to stay, so it predicts back-end conversion better than anything else.
