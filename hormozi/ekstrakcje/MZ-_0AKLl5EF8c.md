### MZ-_0AKLl5EF8c#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A dance school with 800 students in a county of 100,000 wants to double from $2M to $4M revenue in a single location.
detail: Students re-enrol each year, with about 200 new ones. Ads (Meta and Google) run about $1,500 a month, seasonal sign-up promotions. Profit is $500,000 on $2M.
numbers: 800 students; ~200 new per year; $2M revenue; $500,000 profit; market of 100,000 people.

### MZ-_0AKLl5EF8c#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: CAC | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:10
claim: Ad spend of $20,000 a year for about 200 new students gives a CAC near $100, against a $1,600 annual price.
detail: Year-one return is about 16:1, and with three more years of retention about 64:1.
numbers: CAC $100; first-year revenue $1,600; 16:1 year one; 64:1 over four years.

### MZ-_0AKLl5EF8c#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: paid-ads | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:25
claim: In a 100,000-person market he guesses efficiency would start falling somewhere around $7,500-$10,000 a month on Meta, and with Google you would know when you have maxed out the keywords.
detail: By his guesstimate that would still be a true tenfold increase in ad spend from the current budget.
numbers: ceiling guess $7,500 to $10,000 per month on Meta in a 100,000-person market.
### MZ-_0AKLl5EF8c#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: paid-ads | fw: Payback Period | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:25
claim: Even if return drops from 16:1 to 2 or 3:1, keep scaling, because customers pay up front.
detail: Upfront collection makes the lower ratio acceptable.
numbers: 16:1 falling to 2:1 or 3:1.

### MZ-_0AKLl5EF8c#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:48
claim: The bottleneck is people to work leads, so hire one full-time person for about $60,000 a year to unlock more spend.
detail: Profit is $500,000; he notes that the hire would take it from $500,000 to about $440,000.
numbers: $60,000 per year hire against $500,000 profit.
### MZ-_0AKLl5EF8c#06 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: scaling-constraints | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00

detail: Capacity check first, then market size and ad share.
