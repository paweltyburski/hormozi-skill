claim: A burger sold at $0.25 profit cannot support a business, but stacked upsells lift profit per order eight to eleven times.
detail: At a $2 burger and $0.25 profit, the shop would need about 10,000 burgers a day to survive. Fries, then a drink to make it a meal, take per-order profit to about $2 (8x), and a supersize for a dollar more takes it to about $3 (11.6x). The itemised increments in the audio do not add up exactly (unclear in audio). Point: the biggest seller is not the biggest profit.
numbers: $0.25 base profit; ~$2 (8x) with meal; ~$3 (11.6x) with supersize; 10,000 burgers a day needed without upsells.

### POD-250819-05#10 | FRAMEWORK | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: Decoy Pricing | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:08:40
claim: The "magnetic middle": adding a third, deliberately priced option nudges buyers toward the option you want them to buy.
detail: Cinema example with small $5, medium $8 (not the "rational" $7) and large $9: the people who would normally take the medium will probably take the large. To push people into the medium, price small at $6, medium $7 and large $9, which moves most would-be small buyers to medium. Small and large loyalists do not change.
numbers: Version A: $5 / $8 / $9. Version B: $6 / $7 / $9.

### POD-250819-05#23 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: Decoy Pricing | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:22:29
claim: The Economist's late-1990s digital subscription cannibalised print until a print-only decoy at the bundle price was added.
detail: It sold digital at $59 and digital plus print at $125, and print sales fell as customers chose the cheaper digital. Adding a print-only option at $125 made the $125 bundle the obvious choice. Keep each option's price above your margin floor.
numbers: Digital $59; print $125; digital plus print $125.

### POD-250819-05#24 | SCRIPT | topic: sales-process | also: pricing | fw: Anchor Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:26:40
claim: Anchor upsell in five steps: present the anchor, expect the gasp, come to the rescue, present the main offer, ask how they want to pay.
detail: The rescue is a question about whether the customer cares what makes the premium premium. The salesperson has the cheaper option ready before the reaction. A bigger gasp meant more purchases; if there is no gasp, the premium is probably priced reasonably, so move straight to payment.
numbers: Anchor at 5 to 10 times the core price.
