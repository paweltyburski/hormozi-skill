### POD-0491#08 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:31:03
claim: A new business gets a monthly processing cap, so contracts must be run in stages; ask for the limit to be raised once you show volume.
detail: He did about $200,000 in the first month (around $7,000 a day) without a processor. Friends refused to process for a 10% cut. One processor opened a $50,000 limit that is per month, so he ran $50,000 then $50,000 again two days later when the month rolled, barely covering costs. Limits rose to $100,000, then $200,000 a month, and the second month produced small profit.
numbers: $200,000 sales month one; $50,000/month starting limit; later $200,000/month; first-month profit about $100.

### POD-0491#09 | CASE | topic: product-delivery | also: retention-ltv, verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:33:23
claim: Gym owners told members to refund after a launch, either from overload or to rebuy at half price, producing $150,000 of refunds that Hormozi's processor account had to absorb.
detail: One owner whose membership went from 70 to 270 clients in a month lacked infrastructure and told everyone to refund. Later two owners told members to refund and sign up directly at half price because the launcher had left, and the owners held the client relationship. Layla's insight: selling more would only create more refunds. Lesson: the launcher carries the processing risk while the gym holds the customer.
numbers: 70 to 270 clients in one month; $150,000 refunds; half-price rebuy.

### POD-0491#10 | BENCHMARK | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Offer Stack | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:34:09
claim: The launch model used a six-week paid program whose revenue paid for the launch costs he lists, handing the gym paying, qualified customers who, if done right, should convert about 70% on the back end.
detail: Price was about $500-$600 for six weeks. Revenue paid for marketing, sales, hotel, rental car, ad spend and commissions; the rest was profit. Because customers paid, they show up and follow instructions. Gyms have mostly fixed costs, so fulfilment needs little extra staff.
numbers: $500-$600 per six-week program; ~70% back-end conversion if done right.
### POD-0491#11 | CASE | topic: sales-process | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:35:42
claim: Facing refunds, Hormozi proposed an online sales model with no fulfilment: Layla's weight-loss program sold by phone to test whether his reps could do the same.
detail: Layla earned $3,000-$4,000 a month online with essentially no cost. Hormozi wrote the sales copy and an ad campaign within 48 hours; within days she sold about $1,000 a day by phone. His plan: eight sales reps each at that rate, without travel, giving about $8,000 a day and roughly $150,000 after commissions, and no gym owner could tell customers to refund.
numbers: $3,000-$4,000/month baseline; $1,000/day per seller; 8 reps = $8,000/day; ~$150,000 after commissions (period unclear in audio).

### POD-0491#12 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:37:23
claim: The first licence sale came from naming a deliberately high price, $6,000, hoping to be refused; the buyer accepted, and calling seven more owners yielded $60,000 in one day.
detail: A gym owner who had missed the next launch begged for help and asked to be shown how his friend's gym was done. Hormozi priced his scripts and system at $6,000 to get a no, then raised prices on the next calls ($8,000, $10,000) and found demand elastic. He realised he was still in the gym business, just selling the system.
numbers: $6,000 first sale; next prices $8,000 and $10,000; $60,000 in sales on day one.

### POD-0491#13 | CASE | topic: product-delivery | also: verticals, sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:40:04
claim: The licence package was assembled from materials he already had, with only the ad documentation missing; the first 30-40 buyers each collected about $30,000 in extra cash in their first 30 days.
detail: Contents: professional videos on servicing customers, nutrition consults, supplements and membership back end; an hour-long sales training and script built for one product at one price; the ads and a copy-paste landing page with the city name changed. Buyers were given the ad and page that already worked. Results are cash collected, not contract value. The first month brought about $200,000 cash and about $400,000-$500,000 in sales.
numbers: ~$30,000 additional cash per gym in 30 days; first month ~$200,000 cash on $400,000-$500,000 sales; 30-40 early buyers.

### POD-0491#14 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:40:50
claim: Sales training works best when built for a single product, price and room, because the builder has money riding on whether the learner closes.
detail: His training covered exact setup: chair placement, how the office looks, a tour joke, seating the prospect so they face the weights. It was based on about 4,000 of his own sales conversations. He contrasts this with generic sales courses written to justify a course price.
numbers: ~4,000 personal sales; 1-hour training.
