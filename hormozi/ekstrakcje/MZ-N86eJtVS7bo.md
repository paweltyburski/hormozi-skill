### MZ-N86eJtVS7bo#01 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A German dating coach sells a $4K, six-month programme; happy clients leave once they find a partner, and many are business owners.
detail: He asked how to raise lifetime value. Hormozi's view: the aim is that clients no longer need you, like an app meant to be deleted, so the value is one-time and should be billed once.
numbers: $4K for 6 months.

### MZ-N86eJtVS7bo#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: retention-ltv | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:09
claim: When a retention attempt fails, he asks how many times it was tried before blaming the market, and presumes an offer issue.
detail: The owner had tried a post-result retention message about 50 times; clients feel confident and see no problem. Hormozi suspects the offer, since every relationship has problems that can be named.

### MZ-N86eJtVS7bo#03 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Problem-Solution Cycle | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:27
claim: Sell around the next problem that the first solution exposes, such as keeping the relationship once it starts.
detail: His default: a customer arrives with one problem, you solve it, it creates another, and you sell something that naturally pairs with the original offer. If one approach failed 50 times, test two others.

### MZ-N86eJtVS7bo#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:24
claim: Giving clients what they ask for (here, business coaching after dating) makes more money faster but makes the company a very hard business to sell if an exit is a goal.
detail: He thinks about enterprise value: could a buyer package this asset with a clear narrative for one avatar? He calls going from helping find a partner to improving profit super weird. Fine if no exit is planned.
