### YT-ZZZNsDs2G_s#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: The money ladder ranks people by when they get paid: employees after working, then self-employed on half now and half on delivery, then paid first, then banks, then insurers.
detail: Employees front time and effort and wait two to four weeks. Contractors may split 50-50, and stronger ones are paid up front or in full, as with a doctor before surgery. Banks are preferred creditors in the capital stack and are repaid first when things go wrong.
numbers: Employee pay delay 2 to 4 weeks; contractor split 50-50.

### YT-ZZZNsDs2G_s#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:33
claim: Insurers sit highest: they are paid for years before they might ever fulfil, the opposite of a depreciating asset.
detail: He imagines signing 500 families at $1,000-2,000 a month, about $1M a month, with delivery uncertain. The oldest companies in the world are insurers. The top rung is a fixed percentage off the top line, which he jokes about with franchisors and tithes.
numbers: Example: 500 families paying $1,000-2,000/month = about $1M/month.

### YT-ZZZNsDs2G_s#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:26
claim: On his money ladder, people higher up get paid more up front, and he says a lot of that is because of demand and how good you are; he stresses watching where money flows and who gets paid when.
detail: He changed the way his agreements were structured after this view.
