### POD-0239#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:46
claim: After Gym Launch grew from $6.8M to $28M in a year, starting a supplement company instead of adding markets cost about a year of growth.
detail: He weighed Gym Launch international (China, Southeast Asia, South America) against a supplement company and picked the latter because it felt easier. Adding channels or markets for the core avatar would have kept things simple; he believes the core could have reached $100M. It took about a year to re-simplify.
numbers: $6.8M to $28M in one year; about 1 year lost to re-simplifying.

### POD-0239#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:12
claim: Monthly revenue plateaus at about new customers per month times lifetime value if nothing changes.
detail: Back-of-napkin forecast: the curve keeps rising but flattens toward that ceiling, an asymptote. Example: 10 sign-ups a month at $2,000 LTV, currently earning $10,000 a month, will grow toward $20,000 and stop. First decide whether the back end or the front end limits you.
numbers: 10 new/month x $2,000 LTV = $20,000/month ceiling (first example garbled in audio).

### POD-0239#03 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: unit-economics | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:01
claim: An agency at $200K a month wanting $1M a month was shown a plan of two small tweaks that reached about $960K.
detail: It sold 30 customers a month at $12,000 LTV, so would cap at $360K a month. Front end: 30 to 60 customers (Facebook +50% to 45, with higher ad cost and 1-2 salespeople, plus restarting a dropped outbound method for about 15). Back end: LTV 12K to 16K via a 25% price rise and more availability. 60 x 16K = $960K. Small adjustments, not an overhaul.
numbers: 30 x $12K = $360K cap; plan 60 x $16K = $960K per month; price +25%.
applies_to: Agencies on his software at $100K+/month.

### POD-0239#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: paid-ads | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:39
claim: His "good enough for growth" bar is a lifetime value to acquisition cost ratio of at least 10:1, ideally 20-40:1, not required upfront.
detail: Gym Launch's ratio was 33:1 on lifetime value, and it often lost money in the first one or two months of a customer. Typical competitors had LTV of $5,000-$6,000 against about $1,000 CAC and spent 20-30% of cost on marketing, against under 5% of revenue at Gym Launch.
numbers: LTV:CAC at least 10:1; Gym Launch 33:1; competitors' LTV $5-6K vs CAC about $1,000; marketing under 5% of revenue.

### POD-0239#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:53
claim: Acquisition cost is often relatively similar across businesses of a type, so lifetime value is what differs; reference points by customer type.
detail: Cost to acquire a small-business owner is in most markets about $500-$1,500; a local B2C customer might be around $100. A chiropractor with five visits at $50 has only $250 LTV, a bad number, while $2,500-$4,000 is healthy.
numbers: CAC $500-$1,500 (small business owner, in most markets); might be around $100 (local B2C); chiropractor LTV $250 poor vs $2,500-$4,000 good.
### POD-0239#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: More Better New | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:17
claim: Once the back end is good enough, grow by adding acquisition channels for the same avatar, not by adding services or customer types.
detail: Owners often fear a market is tapped: an agency selling 20 a month in a market with about 100,000 clients was not. Options he lists for the gym market: more marketplaces, channel partners who hold gym lists (equipment, flooring, financing, CRM firms), outbound email, calls or messaging. Outbound often has lower cost than inbound but scales more slowly, which can suit fulfilment capacity. Aim entrepreneurial restlessness at learning new channels.
numbers: 20 clients a month in a 100,000-client market.

### POD-0239#07 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Opening questions for a consulting day: what would add $5M to the bottom line, or $10M of revenue, without adding people?
detail: Pricing is one of the first things he raises; if you price appropriately, many times it can add $2-3M to the bottom line with no other change. The goal is to decrease complexity, keeping the intervention as simple as humanly possible.
numbers: Pricing alone: many times $2-3M to the bottom line (example scale).
