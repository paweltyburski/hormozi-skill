### POD-0118#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Structure client communication around four end actions: connect, refer, give a testimonial, resell or ascend.
detail: These are results the client should take, not a touchpoint list (there are hundreds of scheduled touchpoints already). Staff must know the four milestones and the processes must make each one happen. He took the model straight from how he ran his gyms. Refer and testimonial are interchangeable in order, depending on the client; the transcript cuts off before the resell part.

### POD-0118#02 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Get a new client connected to people inside the community in the first days, when risk of leaving is highest.
detail: The first 7 to 10 days carry the highest risk of leaving, mostly from overwhelm and confusion. Give each newcomer an accountability buddy and an accountability coach who expect them on a named day, and have the trainer message before the first session (who they are, what time, how to recognise them). Push them to community events early, since people with connections stay longer and get better outcomes.
numbers: First 7 to 10 days = highest churn risk.

### POD-0118#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:35
claim: People refer a business to raise their own status, so a referral is only given when the service is good enough that the friend would thank them.
detail: A referral risks the referrer's relational capital. If the business is merely adequate, the friend may think less of them, so most satisfied customers fall in a zone of not good enough to recommend, not bad enough to leave. To get referrals the experience must be exceptional, not just competent.

### POD-0118#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: lead-getters | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:53
claim: Some buyers refer in the first week, before any testimonial; give those hyperactive buyers an outlet right away.
detail: These early advocates tend to be influencers and ascend all the way up. Most buyers wait and test the product before spending their reputation. Referral and testimonial are interchangeable in order, depending on the type of customer. He cites referring about 20 people to a shirt brand within a week of buying.
numbers: example: about 20 referrals in the first week (his own shirt purchase).
### POD-0118#05 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:54
claim: Give clients a fixed template for posting results; people hold back testimonials for fear of getting the format wrong.
detail: Ask for stats framed as feedback ("so we can help, show us your numbers") and show what a good post looks like. In his gym-owner group the posts all report the same fields: spend over a number of days, leads, booked appointments, shows, closes at a price, total revenue. A common template also lets staff diagnose (low show rate, low close rate) from the post.

### POD-0118#06 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:29
claim: Make testimonial collection someone's full-time job and feed client wins into daily department huddles.
detail: One person collects testimonials for all parts of the business. Each department's daily huddle reviews client wins; screenshots of praise go into the next day's huddle so the named employee gets kudos. The weekly company meeting also starts with client wins.
numbers: 1 full-time testimonial collector.

### POD-0118#07 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:29
claim: Reward support staff with status for unsolicited client praise and make kudos count part of their scorecard.
detail: Staff cannot ask for praise; the goal is every interaction so good that the client volunteers a post. Count kudos as an objective for customer service roles and present them to the group. If trainers lack a reward system for kudos, this is why client praise stays rare.

### POD-0118#08 | EXAMPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:16
claim: Ask "who referred you?" on every application; at a summit three quarters of the room had been referred by another gym owner.
detail: He asked his Legacy summit audience who came through a referral and about 75% raised hands. His point: they are stronger at delivery and fulfilment than at marketing and sales (the funnel had not changed in two years), and referrals multiply the effect of marketing.
numbers: about 75% of summit attendees referred; funnel unchanged for 2 years.
