### YT-C_SgvSvJZdk#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Solve problems backwards: list what you do not want or what would guarantee failure, then work away from it.
detail: Borrowed from Munger. Instead of reasoning forward to a goal, start from the outcome to avoid and remove its causes. The whole video is structured this way: the eight things that guarantee misery or failure (chemicals to alter mood, envy, resentment, unreliability, overspending, learning only from own mistakes, quitting early, cracking under pressure) are each inverted into a habit.

### YT-C_SgvSvJZdk#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:03
claim: Reliability, meaning doing what you said and showing up, is a larger success lever than talent or speed.
detail: Hormozi tells of a classmate with no obvious natural advantages who did every extra-credit task and attended every teacher session, and who got into a better college than Hormozi (Duke against Vanderbilt). The lesson he took: consistent showing up beats raw ability, and if you get this one trait right it is hard to stay unsuccessful.

### YT-C_SgvSvJZdk#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:34
claim: Wealth is the ratio of income to expenses, not a number, and the spending side is the one you can change immediately.
detail: If you can live on 1% of income you are effectively wealthy for 100 years on one year of income. Two routes: raise income or cut spending; spending is controllable today, and the surplus can then be invested in yourself to grow the top line. Hormozi says anxiety falls as the share of excess over expenses rises.
numbers: He lived on about $30,000 a year when he had $1M in the bank and income above $1M a year (about 3% of take-home); he tries to live on around 10% of income.

### YT-C_SgvSvJZdk#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:54
claim: Your personal yardstick for "a lot of money" resets as you earn more, so comparison never ends and envy is pointless.
detail: Early on his unit was burritos and $100,000 felt like the wealthiest he had been; now his base unit is $1M. Someone is always doing better (Buffett and Munger said the same), and how others play has no effect on you, so envy is self-inflicted suffering. A multi-billionaire friend told him the more he makes the poorer he feels.

### YT-C_SgvSvJZdk#05 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:08:25
claim: Where you learn from changes with your stage: free labour for practitioners, then paid coaches, then masterminds, then networks and equity-paid advisors, with heroes at any level.
detail: 1) With no skills, trade your time as extra hands to a small business owner earning roughly $100,000-$200,000 a year. 2) Then pay coaches (his first 3-4 years). 3) Then masterminds of peers. 4) Past eight figures a year, networking plus advisors who receive a minority stake so they are incentivised to teach. 5) Heroes you never meet (books, speeches) work at every level.
numbers: Network and advisor mode began once revenue passed eight figures a year.
applies_to: Early-stage founders deciding whom to learn from

### YT-C_SgvSvJZdk#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:25
claim: Offers of "half the business" or "I will work free if you teach me everything" from people with no skills are poor trades for an experienced operator.
detail: Hormozi gets such messages. He sees no value coming to him, since the person brings no skill and wants an education; the one fair offer from a beginner is free hands on tasks a small business needs done, which then builds skill.

### YT-C_SgvSvJZdk#07 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:59
claim: Negative visualization: imagine an annoying event repeating a thousand times and notice you would stop caring, then adopt that reaction now.
detail: From Epictetus via a friend. Hormozi applied it to a pricey shirt that came out of the wash ruined: if it happened every wash, expectations would reset to match reality and the misery vanish, so skip ahead to that state. Works for repeated stressors, including behaviour of people close to you.

### YT-C_SgvSvJZdk#08 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:38
claim: For a hard new habit, set the baseline expectation at the worst case so good days are a bonus rather than the norm.
detail: Example is working out: expect to be tired, busy and unmotivated every single time. If you feel that way you are not surprised or derailed, and if you feel good it is a happy coincidence. Many of us create invisible expectations and then crumble under pressure we put on ourselves.

### YT-C_SgvSvJZdk#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:59
claim: Quitting after a few failures guarantees the worst outcome, and counting failures means nothing.
detail: Munger's point as Hormozi relays it: everyone fails many times, not once or twice, and anyone who quits early loses hope and purpose for a long time afterwards. Keep going and do not treat the number of failures as a signal.

### YT-C_SgvSvJZdk#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:27
claim: Hormozi lowers pressure by remembering that everything built will eventually disappear, so no single outcome is a big deal and meaning is chosen.
detail: He frames the mind as a meaning-making machine: nothing has inherent meaning, so you choose what to care about, pick activities you enjoy, and drop expectations and envy. In business it takes the heat out of upset customers or copycat competitors. He notes it may feel depressing to others, in which case skip it.
