### POD-0187#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:58
claim: Burnt bridges earn nothing, so disagree with ideas on their merits without attacking the person.
detail: If asked about a method he disagrees with, he explains why the math does not work, separated from who proposed it. Phrase it as a difference in approach that you can respect.

### POD-0187#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:24
claim: Several people who publicly criticised him later asked to resell his products.
detail: He had helped many of them earlier. He also made deals with people one connection away from someone he disliked, and says staying quiet instead of criticising publicly earned him significant money, since a public attack would have been repeated to the counterparty.

### POD-0187#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:37
claim: One cold front-line employee can colour the whole customer's experience of the business.
detail: His restaurant example: a scowling host leaves the guest rating the meal two stars even though the food was fine. The same applies to a front desk or trainer. Every business has staff who ignore the script when the owner is away.

### POD-0187#04 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:13
claim: When served badly, assume the owner did not intend it and give private feedback instead of lashing out.
detail: The likely intent was good service poorly executed. Owners should correct the problem, but expect no grace from others. One suggestion is to leave a five-star review with a note asking the owner to respond so you can fix the issue, instead of a one-star review.
