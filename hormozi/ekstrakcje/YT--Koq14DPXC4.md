claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: a lash-studio owner spending about half a percent of revenue on ads, with about half her clients saying they came from Google, is in his view probably underspending rather than failing at marketing.
detail: His reading of this case: either the marketing does not work or it is starved of budget; since she reports anecdotally that half of clients name Google, he bets it works and her CAC is probably very low, so she should spend much more ($12,000 a year on a $2.3M business; 1% would be $23,000). ### YT--Koq14DPXC4#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:05
claim: Where capacity is short only at certain days or hours, raise prices for those slots, and frame it as peak prices with a discount on other days.
detail: Higher peak prices shift some demand to quiet days, which smooths staff utilisation, frees peak slots, and lets those slots fill again at the higher price. He proposed +20% on Thursday to Saturday; the owner chose 10%, and he accepted it because the owner has to be convinced of the price herself. On a 28% margin he estimated ~5% more revenue lifting profit by about a fifth.

### YT--Koq14DPXC4#04 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing, retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:50:36
claim: Pair a peak-price surcharge with a membership whose headline benefit is exemption from it: create the problem, then sell the solution.
detail: When the owner worried regular clients would be annoyed by weekend pricing, Hormozi made non-surge pricing for members the strongest membership benefit, making the membership far more compelling than the discount-and-perks version.

### YT--Koq14DPXC4#05 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: sales-process | fw: BAMFAM | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:30
claim: Book the next appointment before or during the service rather than after, and train it as a sale, not admin.
detail: After the service, clients want to leave; during an hour in the chair they have time. Prepare one or two answers for "I'll book later" (pull up calendars together, card already on file, offer two specific dates). Shortening the gap between visits raises yearly visits sharply; getting 80-90% to rebook would change the business. He adds that membership is a harder sale than rebooking, so rebooking comes first.

### YT--Koq14DPXC4#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:50
claim: With a cheap prepaid front-end promotion, he cares more about the close rate on the upsell than on the promo, because the promo is not where the money is made (the promo close rate is still worth tracking in case it falls).
detail: Prepaid promo buyers who already put a card down are effectively pre-closed. He calls the promo a lead magnet and says that, if it only breaks even after labour, rent and materials (his hypothetical), the real business is the step-one-to-step-two take rate. ### YT--Koq14DPXC4#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit, scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:44
claim: An owner who works in the business of a model they intend to scale should be paid the market rate for that role, or the margins and expansion returns are overstated.
detail: Unpaid owner labour inflates profit. To open more units you must backfill that role, so its cost belongs in the return on capital. His example: $300K profit on $50K to open looks great until $200K must go to a capable operator, leaving $100K; still maybe good, but the real figure. If one person covers four quarter-roles, the team structure needs fixing.

### YT--Koq14DPXC4#08 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:46
claim: To end a stalled partnership choose between a larger share for the working partner, a shotgun offer, or, where partners co-own several units, an equity swap.
detail: Shotgun: name a price and terms (e.g. paid over 2-3 years) at which you will either buy or be bought. Equity swap: each takes full ownership of one co-owned location; clean, no cheque that stings monthly, friendship kept. The working partner has leverage because they can stop working. Hormozi recommended the swap even if she got the weaker site, since focus will grow it. Hormozi says the swap has no tax (US context, unclear). For a partner leaving, a buyout in exchange for helping replace her.

### YT--Koq14DPXC4#10 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:27:33
claim: Build a technician career ladder of six titles with a pay step about every three months over two years, each step unlocking training in a new skill that can be charged for.
detail: Example ladder: junior, regular and senior technician, then junior, regular and senior artist, commission rising from the starting rate in small increments (e.g. 40, 42.5, 45, 47.5, 50%). Three-month gaps keep the next reward close; six months feels too far. The top performer after two years can be placed in a new location with a profit share.

### YT--Koq14DPXC4#11 | EXAMPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:30:46
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: instead of giving lash technicians ownership (she had given one a third), give a profit share paid as a bonus. General statement: equity carries four things, risk, control, profit and the proceeds of a sale, and he doubts such staff want the risk.
detail: Back into a target on-target earnings figure, then design a path: after two years the best technician runs a new site for 10% of its profit in year one and 20% in year two, while still doing services. Because it is a bonus, it can be withdrawn if they underperform. Make candidates compete for the slot.

### YT--Koq14DPXC4#12 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: offers, lead-getters | fw: Free Giveaways | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:36:35
claim: Make each membership bonus worth the price on its own, and add a monthly members' prize draw in which the referrer of the winner also wins.
detail: Prefer free extras to discounts when your delivery cost is low. Too few or too many bonuses both fail; aim for a few that each justify joining. A prize of about $200-$500 a month is cheap, gives something to promote, and pushes members to refer for more chances.

### YT--Koq14DPXC4#13 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:39:56
claim: Upsell regulars to membership as a default: "you've been in six times and haven't been getting what you should; it costs no more", then card on file, then prepay at checkout.
detail: Sequence: rebook, offer a free immediate bonus, frame the membership as formalising what they already do, close with the card on file. At checkout: "want today free?" by prepaying 12 months (the discount equals today's bill), downsell to six. He cites the opt-out organ-donor study for making the desired choice the default. Train it by daily 30-minute role-play with immediate corrections.

### YT--Koq14DPXC4#15 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: paid-ads, lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:47:18
claim: Use a fixed check-in checklist in the waiting area: ID first, confirm card on file, sign-in with a "how did you hear about us" dropdown and referrer name.
detail: Asking for ID first feels routine and less intimidating, then confirming the card follows naturally. The sign-in gives ad attribution and referral tracking, so ad spend can be raised with confidence. The waiting time before the service is the most underused asset of service businesses: rebook, pitch the upsell and ask for reviews there.

### YT--Koq14DPXC4#17 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:49:34
claim: Rank actions with ICE (impact, confidence, ease), which Hormozi maps to the value equation by splitting ease into speed and effort.
detail: Impact matches the dream outcome, confidence matches perceived likelihood, and ease becomes time delay plus effort, giving four variables. He defines strategy as prioritising resources: put them into the fewest moves with the highest probability of high return; easy, fast, high-impact items go first.
