claim: A spouse's interest in your business must be their own; if present, make it easy to learn, and expect them to insert themselves (shadow calls, take tasks).

### POD-0612#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:11:03
claim: Hormozi, relaying a Dave Ramsey/Graham Stephan example, describes matching your assets to the circle of your knowledge, experience, resources and connections as what gave him permission to focus his own investing on business.
detail: In the story he relays, Graham said about 85% of his knowledge was real estate and 85% of his assets were real estate, and Dave called that a perfect distribution. Hormozi had asked wealthy people what to do with money and got different strategies; he concluded that since he knows and is good at business, his circle would be 100% business.
### POD-0612#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:46
claim: Buying companies earning $2-5M profit a year is risky for outsiders but not for operators with an information advantage, who suffer fewer and smaller surprises.
detail: Acquisition.com buys what it knows: scaling brick-and-mortar and consumer and professional services. Knowing where problems hide lets returns beat the risk.
numbers: Typical entry size $2-5M profit per year.

### POD-0612#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:01
claim: Absolute gains grow with age of a business: going from year 3 to 4, or 10 to 11, beats years 0 to 1, and taking a $20M business to $25M makes you richer than building from zero to $5M.
detail: First-year learning is greatest, but the biggest returns come late, so restarting with a greener-grass idea is usually wrong. Switchers loop through optimism, pessimism and despair.
numbers: $20M to $25M versus $0 to $5M.
