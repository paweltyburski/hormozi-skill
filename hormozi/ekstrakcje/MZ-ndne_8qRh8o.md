### MZ-ndne_8qRh8o#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: 80/20 Customer Analysis | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A $1.2M agency doing paid media and SEO for every kind of client should drop small paid clients and sell SEO and AI search to bigger ones.
detail: SEO clients pay most and stay longest. Hormozi says to position upmarket and to target clients with at least $5M revenue, ideally over $10M, where the top 20% earn $10-40M. A niche will emerge later.
numbers: $1.2M revenue, goal $8M; target clients above $5-10M revenue.

### MZ-ndne_8qRh8o#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:52
claim: A generalist agency path caps near $3-5M because margins compress, talent is scarce and best clients are lost when handed to new reps.
detail: Right moves at a sticking point are counterintuitive; if the intuitive move were correct you would already have done it.
numbers: Cap at $3-5M.

### MZ-ndne_8qRh8o#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:22
claim: A small-business agency price of about $400-$600 a month, set to their worst month, can hold 30-40 month lifetimes with cheap acquisition.
detail: Pricing to what they will say yes to creates a churn factory. The low price must come from a low cost base (Walmart), not from charging less for the same work. For a service business not going the SMB volume route, he says the only way is up-market.
numbers: $400-$600 per month; 30-40 months LTV.
