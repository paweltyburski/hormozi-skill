### YT-uaLNfijnp-8#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:46
claim: A three-location dental implant practice was capped because one doctor (the owner) did every diagnosis and every procedure.
detail: Run by a son who joined a year earlier and the father-dentist. Three locations, one doctor, staff shuffling between sites; the one associate was leaving and had never filled a schedule. When the father takes a break the business stops. Hormozi's verdict: straightforward, supply constrained, so fix delivery capacity before anything else.
numbers: Past 12 months revenue ~$2M, profit ~47% (~$960,000). Three-year goal $15M revenue, ~$5M profit. Capacity 25-30 implant patients a month.
applies_to: Professional practice with one skilled owner-practitioner.

### YT-uaLNfijnp-8#03 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:48
claim: Keep the owner only on the highest-skill steps and hand the rest of a multi-visit treatment to associates, which doubles capacity.
detail: Owner keeps diagnosis, extractions and bone graft. Implant placement, checkups and the final fitting go to associate doctors, who can learn placement. Hormozi calls this the greatest point of leverage. He also says not to fire the departing associate if decent, since a full schedule of his visits doubles potential revenue.
numbers: Capacity 25 to 50 patients a month, provided lead flow rises. 90 days later two associates were hired and took the simpler work.

### YT-uaLNfijnp-8#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:53
claim: A price that has not moved for years is not an increase when raised; it is only catching up with the money supply.
detail: The father had not raised prices for three to four years. Over that time money supply grew by more than 20%, so a 20% rise was just right-sizing. Because most patients pay over time or finance, the monthly difference is small for them but material for the practice.

### YT-uaLNfijnp-8#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:28:43
claim: Keep raising price until conversion multiplied by price falls, not until you sell less.
detail: Selling fewer units after a rise can still be a win. A 50% price rise with 10% fewer sales should be kept. First there is a zone where close rate holds at the higher price, then one where it drops a little and the maths still works. It is arithmetic, not feeling.

### YT-uaLNfijnp-8#11 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Virtuous Cycle of Price | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:47
claim: For risky, personal-outcome purchases (implants, cosmetic work) buyers read a higher price as lower risk, so demand can rise with price.
detail: Buyers choosing a procedure for the rest of their life pay up to cut the risk of a bad result; the extra monthly cost is trivial against an inferior outcome. Hormozi compares it to a $50 tattoo parlor. Many businesses sell around this kind of risk without noticing and leave pricing power unused. The extra margin funds better delivery and better doctors.

### YT-uaLNfijnp-8#12 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: money-models, unit-economics | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:11:29
claim: Switch from pay-per-visit to payment up front, offering card or financing, with a payment plan only as the downsell.
detail: Script structure: there are two ways to pay, card or financing; either way payment is due now so the process can start; the reason is that materials for the case are pre-ordered. A prepay discount is optional and Hormozi makes it seem odd to decline. Only 30-50% used financing; he expected 80-90% could. Preferred for the cash conversion cycle.
numbers: Treatment plans over $15,000. Cash collected averaged ~$160,000 a month before and was on track for ~$500,000 a month 90 days later; best prior month $290,000.

### YT-uaLNfijnp-8#14 | SCRIPT | topic: sales-process | also: content-brand | fw: Sales Aids | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:18:34
claim: Show a short video sales letter in the waiting room (5-10 minutes) before the treatment-plan conversation.
detail: Sequence: promise of the video; the pains they are avoiding; proof from three or four different avatars; the big idea (old way versus our way and why others have bad outcomes); then each common objection as what people think, why wrong, what is true, proof; finish with before-and-after proof. A price range may be given. Every booked patient also gets the VSL. Result reported: fewer questions, no downside.

### YT-uaLNfijnp-8#15 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:59
claim: A sales motion has two levers, qualification and education, and in-person selling gives no downside to educating more before the ask.
detail: Qualification makes the right people show up, education sets the mindset. If sales take too many calls or questions, educate earlier. The usual cost of education is losing prospects who drop out, but someone already seated in the office cannot drop out. Result: shorter conversations, faster purchases, more prepaying.
