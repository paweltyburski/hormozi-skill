### POD-0100#01 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: Peter Principle | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:44
claim: Gym Launch repeatedly promoted star technicians to manager and five of them failed.
detail: In a 90-minute review of its 70+ staff, the team found five cases where an all-star in a role was promoted and flopped. Firing is the worst result, since you need a new manager plus a replacement for the star. The shared trait was pride: they took everything on to be indispensable, then suppressed those below them so no one matched them.
numbers: over 70 employees; five failed promotions; review lasted 1.5 hours.

### POD-0100#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:35
claim: A prideful manager breeds a team of clones, passive aggression and fake communication, which can wreck the culture.
detail: Such a subordinate smiles to your face and acts differently behind your back, so you get two lines of dialogue. They then hire people like themselves. Signs: always has the quick answer, thinks they are the smartest in the room, a cup too full to learn.

### POD-0100#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:51
claim: Separate taking pride in the work from being prideful; in his experience, the prideful person tends not to grow because growth requires admitting a gap.
detail: Pride in work means wanting to give your best; prideful means believing it is best because you made it. Prideful people can suppress those beneath them because they don't want anyone to be as good as they are, which hurts them as managers. Sometimes the person who is medium on loudness and quietly does the work is willing to grow because they lack ego; the loudest is not always the best.
### POD-0100#04 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:05
claim: In interviews, give a scenario, ask what they would do, then keep asking why and watch for irritation.
detail: A prideful candidate becomes annoyed at repeated "why" questions and lashes out, which exposes them early.

### POD-0100#05 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:39
claim: Tell people to their face what you say about them behind their back, and never say behind a back what you have not said to them.
detail: A director worried about how a person would react to not being promoted. Hormozi's advice: say exactly what you told me. Voice the worry openly ("given how you reacted before, I was unsure how you would take this"). This gives them a chance to grow and builds real bonds. Applies to customers too.

### POD-0100#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:03
claim: Moving up requires emptying your plate by coaching successors, which runs against survival instinct.
detail: Survival says hold as many tasks as possible so they cannot do without you. In a growing firm you grow by training the people below to take your work, freeing you to take things from above. Gym Launch has four levels: frontline, lead, manager, director.
numbers: 4 levels.
