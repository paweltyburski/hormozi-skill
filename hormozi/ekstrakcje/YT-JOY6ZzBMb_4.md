### YT-JOY6ZzBMb_4#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent, product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:02
claim: Build roles from three variables: expected customer inflow, the activities each customer needs, and how long each takes; then put them on role calendars.
detail: Steps: list desired outcomes along the click-to-resell pipeline; list the activities needed for each; set time per activity; scale by inflow; block the hours on each person's calendar. As it becomes too much for one person, hand whole pieces of an activity type to the next person.
### YT-JOY6ZzBMb_4#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: management-ops | also: verticals, product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:36
claim: Online-fitness coach load: 10 new clients a week means about 1.5 hours per client for sales call, nutrition orientation and ascension chat, plus daily fulfilment per client.
detail: Fulfilment at about 2 minutes per client per day, capped at 100 clients, is about 3 h 20 min a day (blocked as 90 minutes morning and afternoon). Onboarding for 10 new clients is about 5 hours a week; halfway and transformation meetings add more. Total is roughly six hours a day for a coach. Vary onboarding slots (Mon/Wed/Fri vs Tue/Thu) so customers can choose.
numbers: 30 minutes each x 3 meetings; ~2 min/client/day; 100-client cap; ~6 hours/day.

### YT-JOY6ZzBMb_4#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:06
claim: Before hiring, compare the modelled load with the person's actual hours; someone at 40% utilisation asking for help should explain what else fills the day.
detail: Part-time roles on full-time pay appear as a company grows. Often the odd tasks come from people not knowing what is valuable; decide whether to fix the process, drop the task, or add clients to their roster.
numbers: Example utilisation figure: 40%.
### YT-JOY6ZzBMb_4#04 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:57
claim: Run a time study: an employee logs what they did in each 15-minute block for a week.
detail: Efficiency rises while people are being logged. Use it when numbers look off to find waste, such as hours spent on email, or dishonesty. Then judge each activity as valuable or not.
numbers: 15-minute blocks; one week.
