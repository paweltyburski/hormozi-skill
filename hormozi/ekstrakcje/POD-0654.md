### POD-0654#06 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:11:48
claim: Region beta paradox: if you only act when things pass a threshold of badness, a mildly bad situation can keep you stuck longer than a terrible one.
detail: Walking a mile or less but driving longer distances means a two-mile trip is quicker than one mile. The analogy is a so-so job, a lukewarm relationship or a flat with mould. Things not bad enough to trigger action persist; a worse situation lowers the action threshold, and people then do everything they knew they should. Each plateau after a jump becomes comfortable again.

### POD-0654#10 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:18:24
claim: Anxiety cost: every moment spent thinking about an unmade decision is time and attention you could have got back by deciding.
detail: Pulling the deadline for decisions from the end of the week to the end of the day multiplies pace: four decisions take about a month at one per week but four days at one per day. Deciding within an hour raises the gap further. It supports doing the hard task first.

### POD-0654#16 | CASE | topic: promotions-launches | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:37:41
claim: He judged readiness for his book launch by effort: nothing more could be done, shown by 19 full rewrites and a scaled-up rehearsal routine.
detail: He wrote at the start of each day for two years and then reasoned that a talk to 500,000 people merits 50 times the preparation of a talk to 10,000. Rehearsal loop: run the talk in the head, then aloud with a recording, play it back with the slides, and fix every stumble or missing visual each day. He accepts that he can then do fewer things, but each is done.
numbers: 19 full end-to-end rewrites; 6 am to noon daily for 2 years; 500,000 registered for the event; 900 slides in 60 minutes; 50x the preparation of a 10,000-person talk.
