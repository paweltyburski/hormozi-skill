### YT-R7Fin9HFhoM#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Four growth chapters, each with a different job: $0-1M one product on one channel, $1-10M multiple products, $10-30M professionalising operations, $30-100M+ more people with owner-like drive.
detail: He notes the stage boundaries are rough (the first jump is sometimes closer to $1-3M) and that bottlenecks are hard to understand before you own a business.
numbers: $0-1M, $1-10M, $10-30M, $30-100M+ annual revenue.
### YT-R7Fin9HFhoM#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:49
claim: To pass roughly $1M a year you need one reliable system for acquiring customers with one product on one channel, and owners who stay stuck at $100-300K usually still acquire clients haphazardly.
detail: Then the arithmetic is simple: a $1,000 product needs 100 sales a month. He adds that many owners at this stage also stall on personal chaos, not strategy, and wreck a working system through interpersonal drama.
numbers: $1,000 product x 100 per month = $100,000 a month.

### YT-R7Fin9HFhoM#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics, leads-fundamentals | fw: Upsell Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:10
claim: From $1M to $10M, add a back end of cross-sells and upsells to raise LTV, then use the extra LTV to afford broader channels.
detail: The question "what else should I sell these people" is the correct one. Not new businesses, but the next thing the same customer needs. His example: 100 customers a month at $1,000 LTV becomes $500,000 a month if LTV rises five times to $5,000. Higher LTV then funds channels that were previously unaffordable.
numbers: LTV $1,000 to $5,000 (5x); 100 customers a month; ~$500,000 a month.

### YT-R7Fin9HFhoM#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:55
claim: Between $10M and $30M the founder's "special sauce" dilutes, so the company must professionalise HR, legal, accounting and IT, accepting corporate hires and structure.
detail: At about $10M it is a big small business running on spreadsheets and Trello boards, with support complaints rising. Structure also protects against liability, because lawsuits and contracts start to matter at this size. The risk is a pendulum: too much corporate control kills innovation.

### YT-R7Fin9HFhoM#05 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:35
claim: Hormozi's own companies sat at about $28M, $37M and $32M in successive years (as he states, unclear whether per year) and he blames the missing step from $30M to $100M on needing more people with initiative.
detail: He says the business was built on his own drive, and growing past $30M needs more people who can take initiative and seize opportunities, not just him.
numbers: ~28, 37, 32 ($M, three successive years; units not fully clear in audio).

### YT-R7Fin9HFhoM#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: A Players | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:34
claim: Do not hire entrepreneurs; hire intrapreneurs, who like driving change but want a safe paycheck.
detail: Hormozi says hiring entrepreneurs repeatedly failed. Either the person has lost their drive and complains about pay, or they succeed and leave with as much of the business as they can. Intrapreneurs get a safe base with variable pay and room to experiment.

### YT-R7Fin9HFhoM#07 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:09:14
claim: Structure past $10M: a central core (HR, legal, IT, finance) with spokes of profit centres, each with its own product and acquisition, led by sub-CEOs.
detail: Core operations make everyone paid, taxed and contractually covered. Each product line is a profit centre run by someone with entrepreneurial drive, as Bezos calls his directors CEOs of their businesses. Founder spirit cannot stretch across a large organisation, so it is delegated.

### YT-R7Fin9HFhoM#08 | CASE | topic: product-delivery | also: money-models, verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:11:14
claim: A new product line should reuse existing skills, like the "boiler room" sales-drilling add-on for gym clients, not a big new undertaking like his supplement company.
detail: Gym clients asked for sales help, so the team that drills its own reps each morning offered a paid add-on to drill the client's reps. It shows clear ROI, so it is sticky and high margin, and took little investment. Another natural line: running ads for them as done-for-you. He says launching the supplement company first was the harder order.

### YT-R7Fin9HFhoM#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:55
claim: Hormozi would not recommend launching a new product division until you have a true leader as smart as you, because people massively screw up when they try to manage several product lines themselves.
detail: Growth is slow because it depends on people. Since the owner is an imperfect manager, hire people who come with "batteries included" and need less management.
