### POD-0060#01 | DEFINITION | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:08
claim: A finite game has known players, fixed rules and an agreed objective; an infinite game has known and unknown players, changeable rules and the aim of continuing to play.
detail: Baseball is finite. The concept comes from a Simon Sinek video.

### POD-0060#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:39
claim: Finite versus infinite players are unstable: the finite player runs out of resources or will, as in Vietnam and Afghanistan.
detail: Two players of the same kind are stable. The Cold War ended when Russia ran out of resources, not because it was beaten.

### POD-0060#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: Many-Sided Die | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:12
claim: Business is an infinite game, so "number one" is arbitrary and the goal shifts from outplaying competitors to outlasting them.
detail: Whether you rank by revenue, profit, headcount or square footage is a choice; he says this gave him peace about competitors.

### POD-0060#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:45
claim: Judge big investments on a very long horizon, and build one piece at a time so the team can keep up.
detail: Example: buying a huge warehouse for supplements looks costly over 6 to 12 months but not over 50 years. Going too fast means the team cannot catch up, a lot of things get blown up, and the business and customers suffer. The long view also shapes customer service and reputation.
numbers: Horizon compared: 6 to 12 months versus 50 years.

detail: Finite goals sit inside them (weight target), but the real question is what comes next.
