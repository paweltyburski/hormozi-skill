### MZ-rhVfAss4IrU#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Riches in the Niches | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: There are five ways to expand who you sell to: upmarket, downmarket, adjacent, broader, narrower; a sixth is just getting better.
detail: Start niche, then expand in sequence; a large business eventually does all five but a given owner picks one. Longer-running peers at a larger meeting had simply not stopped improving and had a bigger addressable market.

### MZ-rhVfAss4IrU#02 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:32
claim: Upmarket means higher-value, lower-churn deals but longer, harder sales.
detail: Examples: salons to multi-location chains, small business to enterprise. Enterprise sales may take half a year or more. Raising customer quality raises company quality.
numbers: Enterprise deals can take 6 to 18 months to close.

### MZ-rhVfAss4IrU#03 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:09
claim: Downmarket means endless emotional buyers but poor retention, so it suits you if marketing and sales outrank delivery.
detail: Examples: salon owners to stylists. These buyers pay inconsistently and have high expectations. A CRM vendor for tiny businesses had churn almost entirely from customers closing.
numbers: About 30% of these businesses fail each year; CRM churn 4% monthly.

### MZ-rhVfAss4IrU#04 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:33
claim: Adjacent markets (nail salons, physical therapists, dance studios) allow copy-and-paste with new language, but need an insider from the new vertical.
detail: It is slow, vertical by vertical, but extracts the most value per effort, and one move can double or triple market size.

### MZ-rhVfAss4IrU#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:17
claim: Go broader only after champions and case studies exist in several verticals and the product can still deliver significant value.
detail: Broader means generalising across all verticals (hair to beauty). It can multiply the market overnight, but it is more templated and you compete with specialists. Going too broad too soon is common.

### MZ-rhVfAss4IrU#06 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: 80/20 Customer Analysis | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:58
claim: A private-equity pricing head grew acquired companies about fivefold by making all customers resemble the top 20%.
detail: They analysed the top 20% of customers by value and their common traits and channels, and steered everyone toward that profile with little extra infrastructure. His portfolio companies often triple or quadruple profit this way.
numbers: Company grown about 5X; 3-4X profit in portfolio examples.

### MZ-rhVfAss4IrU#07 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: 80/20 Customer Analysis | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:09:47
claim: To go narrower: survey customers, pick those who spent most and stayed longest, find two to five common traits, then rewrite marketing and force prospects through the buying path the best customers followed.
detail: Narrowing raises prices and profit and cuts servicing cost without moving upmarket. Fewer prospects only matters at $10M revenue or more; saying no to others is required.
numbers: Two to five common traits; concern about fewer prospects only above about $10M revenue.
