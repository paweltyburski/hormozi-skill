### MZ-vOKmfdLbgwk#01 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:24
claim: A gym-wear e-commerce brand grew $10M to $80M in a year, targets $500M, and is cash-limited by supply chain.
detail: Growth came from large creators acting as affiliates plus ads on their pages and smaller pages. Restocks sell out within days; quality defects fell from 8% to 2%. The owner asks whether to scale or build a back end.
numbers: $10M to $80M revenue year over year; defects 8% to 2%.
applies_to: Physical-product brands

### MZ-vOKmfdLbgwk#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:10
claim: When growth outruns cash, there are three doors: throttle growth, take debt, or sell equity.
detail: Throttling keeps equity but gives up speed. Debt looks at downside, not growth, so the lines will not be nearly big enough. Raising equity lets outside investors fund growth. With this caller's strong growth story, he said they would probably get a favourable valuation, and he would probably raise.
### MZ-vOKmfdLbgwk#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:25
claim: A big revenue target is arbitrary; prefer giving up some growth to making sure the business survives.
detail: Risk appetite has no correct answer. A made-up goal like $500M should not push you to risk the company; a lower range can be near-guaranteed.
