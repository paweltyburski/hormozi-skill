### POD-0027#01 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:46
claim: A gym straining under 20 sign-ups can move from one-on-one to group or semi-private training, which raises capacity and margin.
detail: One-on-one maxes out at about 20 clients; group has better margins and much higher capacity, limited by floor space and equipment. Upgrading to a semi-private facility is a modest fix that multiplies income without other changes.
numbers: Semi-private upgrade about $2,500, claimed to give about 4x income.
applies_to: Small studios hitting capacity limits.

### POD-0027#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: sales-team | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:37
claim: He learned selling without training, through about 4,000 one-on-one closes.
detail: His sales skill came from volume of repetitions. Mentors he names: Russell Brunson's materials, Dan Kennedy's books and Alex Charfen for operations and scaling. He says scaling came from trying many things that failed.
numbers: About 4,000 one-on-one sales conversations.

### POD-0027#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: unit-economics | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:35
claim: There are three ways to grow any business: get more customers, get them to pay more per transaction (ascend them), or get them to buy more times (retention).
detail: He uses this "pie" to place products: his cold-traffic acquisition programme is one slice of the first way, the full gym programme covers the others.

### POD-0027#04 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:35
claim: A done-for-you internal promotion kit for a gym includes emails, text blasts, social posts, posters with a clipboard sign-up, and a one-page pitch summary for trainers.
detail: Posters go on the front door and in the bathroom, where people see them. The trainer sheet covers the promotion, its price point and two sentences to memorise so trainers pitch before and after each session. The programme ran such promotions year-round.

### POD-0027#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:26
claim: Partners running a business together should write down who does what and keep roles separate, even if one has to learn the skill the other is better at.
detail: Alex acts as CEO, the rainmaker who builds sales processes, products and marketing; Layla acts as COO, creating processes and keeping problems from reaching him. She originally was strong in sales and took over operations out of necessity, then came to like it. He gave up managing the sales team to her to keep roles clear.
