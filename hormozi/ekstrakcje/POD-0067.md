### POD-0067#01 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:54
claim: Park as far away as possible and leave the nearest spaces for customers, and have trainers do the same.
detail: Owners and trainers often take the best spots because they arrive early when it is dark. He did this himself when he did not care much about customers and wanted to show he was the boss. Many micro gyms have limited parking and city disputes. Walking in having just put customers first primes the team to serve them. Retail also parks staff far away because transaction speed matters.
numbers: Example: three of the seven spaces assigned by the landlord were used by owner and trainers.

### POD-0067#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:49
claim: Minor frustrations such as a hard-to-find parking spot are small damages that you should remove where you control them.
detail: He cites a Disney saying that every tragic moment needs 39 magic moments to offset (his citation). A few mornings of hunting for parking count as one of those moments.
numbers: 39 magic moments per tragic one (Disney, as he quotes it).

### POD-0067#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:26
claim: Start the standard with something small like where you park; the other habits then follow, as with fixing broken windows.
detail: A leader picking up pebbles sets the tone for everyone. Next come bathrooms and greetings, for example saying hello within ten seconds and smiling. Ask trainers why they park where they do, and own up if you did it too.
numbers: Greet within 10 seconds.
