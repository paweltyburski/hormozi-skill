### POD-0333#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: The year he started a second business, revenue rose but profit fell.
detail: At 28 the company made $28M with $17M profit. He started another business, thinking he was outside day-to-day operations. The next year revenue was $37M and profit $13.4M. He attributes it to split attention and the skills he had then, not just reinvestment.
numbers: $28M revenue / $17M profit, then $37M / $13.4M.

### POD-0333#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:47
claim: A neighbour who sold his trash company for multiple eight figures planned to build the same company again and sell it to the same buyer.
detail: Reason: he had learned more while working for the acquirer for three years and knew how to scale from scratch. He is a blue-collar founder from Wisconsin, not an MBA, who scaled through word of mouth and service. Depth beats novelty.

### POD-0333#03 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:22
claim: An agency coach at $5-6M considered expanding into general business coaching because people were asking; Hormozi called that exactly what strategy means refusing.
detail: Know who the avatar is and is not; being all things to all people means being nothing to anyone. A high-school friend with a contracting business offered roofs, repairs, flips and real estate: Hormozi pointed to about 200 million US homes with roofs and billion-dollar roofing companies.
numbers: About 200 million US homes.

### POD-0333#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:48
claim: Better begets bigger, not the reverse; the biggest gains come from years ten and twenty in one business.
detail: The first year teaches the most but does not separate you from the field. Like Olympic sprinters separated by tenths of a second but with gold-medal rewards, small skill gaps yield winner-take-all outcomes. Use time as an asset and do the boring volume. If you are not growing, confront what is not happening, such as weak word of mouth.

### POD-0333#05 | EVOLUTION | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:12
claim: In his first five years he ran about 11 businesses, and returns came only after committing to one.
detail: These included six gyms with different models, a chiropractor agency and a dentist agency. He believed one would take off, but none could while he held them all. Success is often emotionally, not intellectually, hard, like eating less than you burn.
numbers: About 11 businesses in the first 5 years.

### POD-0333#06 | EXAMPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:16
claim: Executives tire of an ad long before the market sees it; do not change it for your own boredom.
detail: Story attributed to Henry Ford: after three or four months of passing a campaign he asked when it would stop; the CMO said it had not yet started running.

### POD-0333#07 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: operator-mindset | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:03
claim: To reach the next level ask whether you lack a belief, a trait or a skill.
detail: Check: is a belief about the world wrong, is a character trait undeveloped, or is a skill missing? Overloading the business with projects may be recreating chaos from your history.
