### YT-gpKz22P84iM#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent, scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:49
claim: Talent Grid, three levels: know what to do (individual contributor who does), know how (manager who teaches others to do it), know who (leader who systematizes: builds the machine that builds the machine).
detail: The grid grades yourself, a team member or a department so you see what to do next. He stresses systematizing, not delegating or handing off. People often stay stuck at a level because of beliefs about themselves, so identity must stay fluid.

### YT-gpKz22P84iM#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:10
claim: A manager does not have to be better at the task than an individual contributor but must understand how it works; leaders are a different, higher level, needed at about the multi-million scale.
detail: He says leaders become essential around roughly $3M a year to open the next growth stage, and that leaders must be engaged to reach $10M+ (figures as stated, loosely).
numbers: About $3M a year when leaders become necessary; $10M+ requires engaged leaders.

### YT-gpKz22P84iM#03 | DEFINITION | topic: management-ops | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:18
claim: His test for a truly delegated business: he is not in its Slack channel, only one lead can reach him, and he holds one weekly meeting with the company's head.
detail: Not having to answer a message right away does not mean the business is systematized; if your phone still buzzes with decisions, you are still involved.
numbers: One meeting per week with the head of each portfolio company.

### YT-gpKz22P84iM#04 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Eight Business Functions | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:23
claim: Each quarter, grade each department (marketing, sales, product, shared services) on what, how, who to find bottlenecks.
detail: Example for sales: do you sell, teach selling, or have a machine that trains reps so you can hire one to three salespeople a month and run them through it? Compare with marketing, where you may still be doing the work.
numbers: Example throughput: 1-3 new salespeople per month through a training machine.

### YT-gpKz22P84iM#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:12
claim: A founder's strengths are usually the bottleneck, because you readily hand off what you don't know but cling to what you do well.
detail: The sequence for a strength: do it yourself, teach someone to do it, teach them to teach others, and then build the machine that teaches for you.

### YT-gpKz22P84iM#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:49
claim: Few people can jump from one level to the next, so fast-growing companies often have to replace long-tenured managers with real leaders.
detail: He cites Reid Hoffman on this. Great leaders can be poor individual contributors and the reverse.

### YT-gpKz22P84iM#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:39
claim: To bring in people operating at a higher level, the founder must grow in skill and character, since people follow someone they aspire to be like.
detail: Someone who feels they know more than their boss will not be influenced by them.

### YT-gpKz22P84iM#08 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:24
claim: Discount an owner's verdict on his own team if the verdict keeps flipping: many founders call their team amazing, then reverse months later.
detail: He tells such owners he does not trust their judgment, because past claims proved wrong. About 95% of owners he speaks to start out praising their team (his estimate).
numbers: About 95% of owners praise their team first (his rough estimate); in one example he saw a reversal nine months later.
