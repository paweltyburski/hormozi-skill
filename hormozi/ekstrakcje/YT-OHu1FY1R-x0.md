### YT-OHu1FY1R-x0#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:48
claim: Rank ten life categories (community, romance, parenting, family, recreation, friends, career, physical health, spiritual health, personal growth) and accept that time is zero-sum.
detail: Everyone's order differs and others will call yours wrong. Adding time to one takes time from another; with about 12 usable hours a day, even 1 hour 10 minutes per category per day is not feasible. Choose your regrets or they will be chosen for you. Check each ranking against effort actually given. His own corrections: more time with his wife and more friendships, even at some cost to career hours.
numbers: ~12 usable hours a day after 4 hours of living tasks; ten categories at ~1h10m each.

### YT-OHu1FY1R-x0#02 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:00
claim: His one tactical tip is to choose activities that check several life boxes at once.
detail: Lifting with colleagues who are friends covers recreation, some career growth and physical health, so more time can be blocked for it.

### YT-OHu1FY1R-x0#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:27
claim: Youth is optionality, adulthood is commitment: options have no value until exercised, so cut off alternatives and accept the criticism.
detail: One-way doors (marriage, children) hold much that is best. Keeping options open is a form of endless delayed gratification. His maturity measure: the number of commitments made and people who rely on you. He also notes that people are made uneasy by anyone who changes priorities or does not live as they would.

### YT-OHu1FY1R-x0#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:44
claim: Choose the most dominant game to optimise; the metric you optimise can decide who wins.
detail: A founder who sold a company for about $10B (name unclear in audio) said MySpace lost to Facebook because MySpace optimised total users while Facebook optimised monthly active users. He applies this to life: some people make one category the whole game, whereas the larger game is winning at life across all ten.
numbers: Sale price about $10B (unclear in audio).
