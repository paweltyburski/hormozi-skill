### POD-0765#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:25
claim: Do not publicly commit to a send cadence; send when you have something good, and brand the series with a fixed subject-line name.
detail: A public cadence forces weak emails out. He sends roughly twice a week of ones he writes himself and more if there is material, and skips when there is nothing worth sending. All issues carry one recurring title so the series becomes a brand people look for. Later he says the team sends three or four a week overall.
numbers: about 2 per week personally; 3-4 per week across the company.

### POD-0765#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: operator-mindset | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:56
claim: Send the tactic you wince at sharing; it is the gritty, recent result that others do not know.
detail: Content everyone already knows is useless. The wince is a test for worth. Fear of copying comes from scarcity thinking; he expects to find a better one next week, which forces him to keep improving.

### POD-0765#08 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:22
claim: Write a backlog of emails in one sitting to start; he wrote 24 in half a day to a day.
detail: Having 24 ready gave a baseline of twice a month without public commitment. If unsure, write 12 for one a month. The first sale then reinforces the habit. Start while the list is small: one woman made her first email sale with eight people on her list. His first email went to about 600,000 people, which made it scary.
numbers: 24 emails in 0.5-1 day; first sale at 8 subscribers (anecdote); first send to about 600,000.

### POD-0765#10 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:55
claim: Prefer plain text and compress: a good 200-word email is usually an 800-word draft cut down.
detail: At least for B2B, text-only emails with little fluff tend to do better. Rewrite multiple times, cut three sentences to one. His quality signal is how often readers screenshot and share the email, not the per-email stats.
applies_to: Mainly B2B.

### POD-0765#11 | TACTIC | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: paid-ads | fw: Nurture | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:41
claim: Segment the list by asking opt-ins to self-identify, accepting a lower opt-in rate for much more revenue per subscriber.
detail: He runs two segments, a general list and a larger-company list, with very different responsiveness. A cited HubSpot study attributes a roughly 8x return gain to segmentation. Trading about a 10% lower opt-in for 8x revenue is worth it. Four segment-specific emails to four lists beat one email to everyone, so total sends rise while sends per person fall.
numbers: HubSpot figure cited as 791% (about 8x) return; opt-in rate cost about 10%.
