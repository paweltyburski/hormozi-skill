### YT-RVbvhPGFi6E#01 | DEFINITION | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: AAA | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A reframe is the one to three sentences said after any prospect response other than yes, which raise the chance that the next thing you say leads to a purchase.
detail: Used from $100 products to million-dollar services, in person, by phone, by video or from stage. Reps who jump straight to an overcome sound combative; the reframe comes before the close.
numbers: price range from about $100 to $1M+.

### YT-RVbvhPGFi6E#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:46
claim: Answer a trap question with a question about the question, because the person who asks the questions controls the conversation.
detail: Blind questions (how many trainer certifications? how many support emails?) let the prospect judge any answer and refuse. Ask which certification they want, or why they want more questions and what they fear.

### YT-RVbvhPGFi6E#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:41
claim: The best closers ask for the sale most often, so every ask must preserve rapport to allow many asks.
detail: Asking repeatedly in a combative way makes prospects hate you and stop buying.

### YT-RVbvhPGFi6E#04 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: AAA | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:28
claim: Acknowledge by repeating their words, associate the question with what top customers ask, then ask your next question.
detail: Repeating shows listening and buys thinking time. The association (a question many of our best customers ask) turns a step away from buying into one toward it. Only ask questions whose answers you know, like a lawyer.

### YT-RVbvhPGFi6E#05 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:04
claim: If asked about NASM or ACE certification, ask why those specifically, agree they are good, then explain what you have and why you chose it.
detail: Most people have never been asked about their question and often do not know why they ask. This lets you answer without failing a hidden requirement.

### YT-RVbvhPGFi6E#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:41
claim: Rule 1: a prospect believes almost nothing you say and almost everything they say, so get them to state the conclusion.
detail: Never tell them they are a good fit; ask until they say it. The worst question to ask is "do you have any questions?", which hands over control and invites objections; train it out of the team.

### YT-RVbvhPGFi6E#07 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:55
claim: When pressed to answer, say that asking the secretary about your heart surgery is no substitute for the surgeon, or that a mechanic cannot diagnose without looking under the hood.
detail: Salespeople often know less than the people who deliver. Closing line: anyone who answers on the first call without an assessment is just telling you what makes you buy.

### YT-RVbvhPGFi6E#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:50
claim: Rule 2: nobody can disagree with a question, so be like smoke and never voice disagreement with the prospect.
detail: You do not win sales by winning arguments. People do not change their minds when insulted or in a bad mood. If you cannot answer, ask more questions about theirs, and you remain judge of whether they fit.

### YT-RVbvhPGFi6E#09 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: What Would It Take | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:39
claim: Answer "I need to think about it" with a set of questions: main considerations, decision variables, main concern, biggest fear, what would make this a no, what would it take to say yes.
detail: Others: "no time" gets acknowledgement, praise for planning, then "what would make it a good time". "Need to talk to my spouse" gets "what parts do you think they would disagree with". "I hate this feature" gets "why not". Statements that sound like deal enders are not; keep asking.

### YT-RVbvhPGFi6E#10 | EXAMPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:12:57
claim: Rule 3, tell them what their question means: a wealth advisor answered "I already have an advisor" with 90% of clients who switch already have one.
detail: It turned a reason to refuse into membership of the buying group. Prospects have had this conversation far fewer times than you, so never be surprised by their lines; practise.
numbers: 90% of switching clients already had an advisor (as said).

### YT-RVbvhPGFi6E#11 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:47
claim: Bad selling is selling an unqualified prospect; with a qualified one, state facts and ask as many times as needed.
detail: Be comfortable with conflict and de-escalate. If you only ask when it is the truth, you have an ethical duty to help them decide.

### YT-RVbvhPGFi6E#12 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: Three Core Beliefs | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:15:20
claim: Rule 4, use straw men for tough truths: say what you told someone else, a past customer or a respected third party, rather than lecturing the prospect.
detail: Three forms: "someone asked the same this morning, may I share what I told them"; "that makes you like Sarah, who had a similar business and..."; "my CEO said this today". Asking to wear a coach hat or sharing what worked for you fails when you are younger than a B2B prospect. Use it when you must break a belief such as "I don't have time" (really priorities).

### YT-RVbvhPGFi6E#13 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: retention-ltv | fw: HEAR | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:29
claim: Validating the person's reasoning in sales works like the angry boat in service: affirm it, do not call it a stupid question.
detail: Prefacing closes with associations like "that's smart" lets you say nearly anything and reach the point faster. The association gives them a label to live up to, such as "family man", which you bring back at the close.

### YT-RVbvhPGFi6E#14 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:14
claim: Rule 5: keep childlike curiosity; say "huh", smile (tone follows posture), seek to understand, and keep a note to keep the human first.
detail: "Huh" buys two or three seconds. Frame: imagine you travel back and must convince your past self to buy a stock that will soar; you would show total empathy. Close by making the prospect right, not by being right; be a faceless guide.

### YT-RVbvhPGFi6E#15 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: Five concern types | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:16
claim: Further along in the sale he usually asks "what are you afraid of having happen?"; on the first objection he would ask about their main concern and decision variables.
detail: The early questions let you triage; the fear question gets to the heart of the issue fast. He says this kind of line only works with rapport. If they say they need more time, he asks what would make it a no.
