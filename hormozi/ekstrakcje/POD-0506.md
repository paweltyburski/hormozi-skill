### POD-0506#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: pricing | fw: Riches in the Niches | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: In a small niche, selling a high-price implementation beats selling a cheap information product to the same buyers.
detail: With around 50,000 micro gyms, 2,000 owners at $1,000 gives $2M, while 2,000 owners at $100,000 gives $200M. The reason to build an implementation business was also that fixing acquisition revealed the other problems.
numbers: 50,000 micro gyms; 2,000 buyers x $1,000 = $2M vs x $100,000 = $200M.

### POD-0506#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45

detail: After the acquisition system came the other gaps in a gym: price, membership options, floor layout, coach pay and the sales hire. The first product was an accelerator for cash and customers; the second bought the owner time back and turned the job into an asset.

### POD-0506#08 | DECISION_RULE | topic: content-brand | also: finance-exit | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:35
claim: He chose organic content over paid because deal flow for long minority partnerships must come inbound from people who already trust him.
detail: He had used five of the six lead methods (paid, content, cold outbound, warm outbound, referrals, affiliates) in earlier companies and had never built an organic brand. Investing in a minority stake needs influence, not legal pressure, and paid acquisition is a selling frame while he is the buyer. So he reversed the funnel: people consume his content first.

### POD-0506#11 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:18:58
claim: Business rests on three core skills: build, sell and lead; the learning starts with selling, then building, then leading.
detail: Selling comes first because nobody knows of the product until sold; selling exposes weakness in the product, so you build. Leading others turns a high-paying job into a business.
### POD-0506#12 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: offers, operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:57
claim: To check you provide value, first help people free, then say you will stop and see if they ask you to continue, and ask them to pay.
detail: Early on you will be bad, so promise little and give 110% effort on delivery. Treat unpaid users as customers and learn from their failures. If they ask for more help and then agree to pay, the offer has real value.
