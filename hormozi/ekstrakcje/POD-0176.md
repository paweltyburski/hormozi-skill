### POD-0176#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: The only marketing number that matters is cost per acquired customer, with upfront cash and lifetime value as the two sub-numbers; the further up the funnel, the further from the truth.
detail: Cost per lead, click or impression can mislead: clicks may not be unique, may be bots, or may come from unqualified imagery. The chain he lists: impressions, unique clicks, opt-in, schedule, show, close, upfront revenue, lifetime value.

### POD-0176#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:29
claim: An agency boasted of cutting lead cost from $30 to $20, but once its $2,000 monthly fee was included the lead cost was $45.
detail: The result was worse than before. Always load the service fee into cost per lead.
numbers: Reported $20/lead became $45/lead with a $2,000/month agency fee.

### POD-0176#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: paid-ads | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:58
claim: As a rule of thumb, lifetime value, measured on gross margin, should be 3 to 1 or higher against acquisition cost; he says "our jobs" (possibly gyms) average about 5.5 to 1 on the front end alone.
detail: Example: a $100 meal sale with $60 hard cost leaves $40; four purchases give LTV $160, not $400. The ratio of three comes off that figure. Gym gross margin means spreading rent and trainer cost across classes.
numbers: At least 3:1 LTV:CAC; about 5.5:1 front-end average (stated as "our jobs", possibly gyms); $100 sale, $60 cost, 4 purchases = $160 LTV.
### POD-0176#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:28
claim: Cheap CAC is not enough when the cash arrives later: compare a $10 acquisition paid back over a year with a $500 acquisition that returns $1,000-$2,000 up front.
detail: The first needs capital to float for a year; the second, e.g. a personal-training customer, gives immediate cash even at higher cost.
numbers: $10 CAC for $40 over a year vs $500 CAC with $1,000-$2,000 upfront.

### POD-0176#05 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: sales-process | fw: Basic Sales KPIs | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:38
claim: Measure revenue per show and cost per show for appointment-based services, since pre-opt-in content changes lead-to-show throughput by 3-5x.
detail: Data across many gyms shows food or glamour images sent to short squeeze pages bring cheaper leads that show less; an explanatory page lets weak prospects opt out. Half as many opt-ins that are three times as likely to show wins. He retracts his earlier advice to use food ads with short pages. Show rates also vary by region, e.g. New York below Midwest.
numbers: Lead-to-show throughput can differ 3-5x; 50% of opt-ins at 3x show rate beats the clickbait version.
applies_to: Appointment-based service businesses.

### POD-0176#06 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: paid-ads | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:02
claim: When someone cites a conversion or cost stat, ask what the offer and the traffic were and ask for context.
detail: Hormozi says to measure as close to the sale as possible, using revenue per show, which accounts for close rate. Promotions can close at different percentages, which affects whether you judge a promotion as good.
