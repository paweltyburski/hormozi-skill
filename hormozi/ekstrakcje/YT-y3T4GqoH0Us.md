### YT-y3T4GqoH0Us#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: retention-ltv | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:09
claim: To solve a problem you cannot answer, gather the proven performers in a community, interview them, and look at what they all share, treating the practices all of them use as probably important.
detail: Hormozi polled thousands of Gym Launch gyms for owners with churn under 3% for six straight months and invited them to a call. He went person by person asking what, how and why, wrote about four pages of notes, regrouped them into buckets, and treated practices common to all as probably important and one-offs as probably not important.
numbers: 20 qualifiers out of roughly 1,000 gyms in the community (about the top 2%); about 4 pages of notes.
### YT-y3T4GqoH0Us#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:18
claim: Gym industry monthly churn averages about 9%, while 3% is top-1% performance.
detail: At 9% monthly churn a gym loses about three quarters of its members in a year, forcing constant new acquisition and making the business hard to sell.
numbers: 9% monthly churn average; 3% monthly churn top 1% of gyms.
applies_to: Gyms and other recurring-revenue service businesses.

### YT-y3T4GqoH0Us#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:33
claim: Over 12 months, 3% monthly churn keeps about 69% of customers while 9% keeps only about 32%.
detail: Retention compounds as 0.97 or 0.91 raised to the 12th power. Even 3% still loses nearly a third of customers yearly.
numbers: 3% monthly churn gives 69% retained after 12 months (31% lost); 9% gives 32% retained (68% lost).

### YT-y3T4GqoH0Us#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:04
claim: At a $100 monthly price, 3% churn gives about $3,300 lifetime revenue against $1,100 at 9% churn.
detail: Lifetime revenue is price divided by monthly churn (33 months versus 11), so low-churn gyms earned three times the revenue per member and far more profit.
numbers: $100/month price: $3,300 at 3% churn, $1,100 at 9% churn.

### YT-y3T4GqoH0Us#05 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:01
claim: All 20 low-churn gyms tracked churn as a metric and ran several separate anti-churn tactics.
detail: Hormozi says 95% of operators do not track the metric at all. Tracking and multiple tactics were the first common trait.
numbers: 95% of operators do not track churn (his claim).

### YT-y3T4GqoH0Us#06 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: Activation Points | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:49
claim: Escalate anyone who stops using the service within a few days and keep trying to rebook them.
detail: Example: a member missing Monday and Tuesday was escalated by Wednesday; owners varied between Tuesday and Friday. The shared point was accountability for consumption, with repeated contact.
numbers: Escalation after 2 missed days (by Wednesday); range Tuesday to Friday across gyms.

### YT-y3T4GqoH0Us#07 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: Fee or Feedback | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:16
claim: Make leavers do an exit interview; it gives data and resells over half of them into a higher package.
detail: Venting customers often reveal a solvable pain, such as "no one contacted me", and are moved from a do-it-yourself plan to a coaching level. Turns a cancellation into an ascension.
numbers: Over half of exit-interview leavers were resold to a higher level (his statement).

### YT-y3T4GqoH0Us#08 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:02
claim: Run member events (quarterly cadence usually suffices) and send personalised handwritten cards, sometimes with a gift card to bring a friend.
detail: The card must mention a specific, recent improvement of that person, such as moving to a lighter resistance band, never a generic thank-you. This also reminds them of wins and generates referrals.
numbers: Events quarterly.

### YT-y3T4GqoH0Us#09 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:51
claim: Contact customers personally every two to three weeks with a non-service check-in.
detail: A short human text asking how life is going makes customers feel cared for, which is what they pay attention for.
numbers: Interval about 14 to 21 days.
