### POD-0525#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:07:11
claim: Hormozi's personal three-step process for leaving a job: he would save about six months of expenses, grow the side income until it matches the salary, then sustain that level for six months before quitting.
detail: Keep stacking savings throughout so a bad month can be weathered. Cut overhead hard (live at home or with roommates, take part-time work that frees most of the week) so that fewer hours go to work you do not want long term.
numbers: Six months of living costs saved; side income equal to job income; sustained six months.
### POD-0525#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:36
claim: If a few hours a week of side work already earns a large share of a full-time wage, extrapolating to full time can justify jumping before matching income.
detail: Example: four hours a week bringing half of a 40-hour salary. Ask whether the other 36 hours could plausibly match the full salary. If logic says yes, quitting is defensible, but the safe route is the three steps. He calls job to full self-employment the hardest jump in entrepreneurship.

### POD-0525#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:28
claim: Do the job to the required standard, then give all remaining waking hours to the new business for a limited season, while being truly present with family in the time that remains.
detail: Hormozi frames it as a season of sacrifice (12-24 months) before a season of reaping, admitting the time has to come from somewhere. Aim: first secure the family financially by living below the job's income, and bank or reinvest side income.

### POD-0525#04 | CASE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:22
claim: His own switch: friends paid him for programming instead of a charity, which sustained a salary-level income before he left his job.
detail: He saved from a consulting job over two years while living far below his salary. As a side project he got about 20 friends to pay $200 a month for fitness and nutrition programming, donating it all. After six months of sustained income he asked them to pay him instead, and all agreed, which was the real risk test. That income (around 2013) paid his living costs when he opened his gym, where early rent was nothing.
numbers: Saved $50,000 over two years on a $50,000 a year job; 20 friends x $200 = ~$4,000 a month; six months sustained; online income replaced the $50,000 a year job.

### POD-0525#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:11
claim: Be more cautious about quitting if you have no fallback; famous founders could return to high-paying jobs at once.
detail: Hormozi notes a hedge-fund veteran and a top-school dropout could each get a job quickly if the venture failed. Without such prospects, respect the risk.
