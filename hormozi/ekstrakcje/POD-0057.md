### POD-0057#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: operator-mindset | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: In a book he read, givers sat at both the top and the bottom of success, split by how much they trusted others.
detail: Takers and matchers clustered in the middle. Among givers, those who rated their trust in people at about 8 out of 10 earned the most; 9 or 10 meant too little discernment and a higher chance of being exploited.
numbers: Best-performing givers: trust about 8 on a 0 to 10 scale.

### POD-0057#02 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:08
claim: To collect reviews, post many reviews for peers first instead of asking for them or offering one-for-one swaps.
detail: Gym owners in his community traded reviews, since more five-star reviews bring in more customers. He ranks the approaches: asking is weakest, a swap offer is middle, and going through the whole member list and reviewing every gym is best. Non-reciprocators are still indebted, others remember who gave unasked, and you gain influence.

### POD-0057#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:20
claim: Give your time and effort first, because people remember it and it earns influence and leadership.
detail: Examples: handing out gum as a child gained goodwill even among those who got none; taking the late shifts during a fraternity pledge process led to being voted president. Remembered contribution decides who gets chosen.

### POD-0057#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:37
claim: He won a member-of-the-year award in a high-level marketers' mastermind by offering help to everyone on joining.
detail: On entering the group he messaged each member to ask how he could help, took hour-long calls, and sent reminders and notes. The result was deep relationships and favours he could call in later.
