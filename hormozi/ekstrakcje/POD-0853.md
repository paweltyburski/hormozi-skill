### POD-0853#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:12:11
claim: Do not launch a new offer while the current one is mid-growth; focus is the number of excellent ideas you decline.
detail: A travel-coaching firm planned a second offer (a B2B concierge service at 50,000 for firms spending 500,000 or more on travel) while still growing from 6 to 15. Hormozi: there is always money left on the table. If one product line can go 6 to 15 to 30 to 60, hitting those numbers with two lines would be no better, and one line makes it likelier.
numbers: Growth path 6 to 15 to 30 to 60 (units as stated); B2B offer 50,000 for clients spending 500,000+ on travel.

### POD-0853#02 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Buy vs Build | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:14:53
claim: A holding company buying very small businesses lacks operating leverage; either raise capital to buy real businesses or choose one platform and bolt on.
detail: A venture studio held a SaaS firm, an early education business and a 3.5M gourmet e-commerce brand, short of talent. Hormozi: private equity starts around 5M EBITDA because small firms need building from scratch. Tiny companies are small because the founders are weak, so removing them means buying yourself a job. Options: raise funds for deal-ready firms, or take the best brand and acquire sub-brands under it.
numbers: PE minimum about 5M EBITDA; PE firms hold about 5-8 investments after raising about 500M.

### POD-0853#03 | EVOLUTION | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:17:11
claim: His own early acquisition strategy of many small minority deals was a mistake.
detail: In the first 24 months of Acquisition.com he did 24 deals with emerging entrepreneurs using his brand. Small businesses are often small because of a mediocre entrepreneur. A roll-up thesis in one industry (for example only plumbing) is more workable than disparate holdings.
numbers: 24 deals in the first 24 months.

### POD-0853#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: lead-getters | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:15
claim: Leila's frame, relayed by Hormozi: choose a channel by asking which problem you would rather have, since every route has one.
detail: Case: a physician-founder launching a niche glasses brand, choosing between doctors as distribution and direct-to-consumer. The affiliate route's problems are recruiting, motivating, training and quality control; solving them can be worth 50-100M. Direct-to-consumer is ad and conversion heavy, competing with brands like Snow teeth whitening.
numbers: Value of solving the affiliate problem: 50-100M (as stated).
### POD-0853#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: lead-getters | fw: Prep for Sale | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:25
claim: A referral network from physicians is likelier to be sellable than a direct-response business, which is volatile before a sale.
detail: Direct-response sales swing, and any dip in the year before a sale hurts the price. Solve the one hard problem (the affiliate network) and you have the business. Hormozi would choose it given the founder's medical background, but would say direct response if he were a hardcore media buyer.

### POD-0853#06 | CASE | topic: sales-team | also: verticals | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:24:04
claim: Chiropractors at a gym chain who dislike selling should be selected for the trait, then drilled by role-play.
detail: In-gym chiropractic at 16M this year, goal 32M; front-end conversion to the eight-week plan is weak; some doctors have three times the LTV of others; many say it feels too salesy. Hormozi: teams lose in the draft, not the playoffs, so pick practitioners with the right beliefs; keep the model that works. Role-play (three times a week) beats all other training.
numbers: 16M to 32M; best doctors about 3x LTV; role-play three times weekly.

### POD-0853#07 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:26:25
claim: Reframe selling for doctors as making offers available to people who need them, and use patient stories of undersold patients.
detail: Have doctors read testimonials from patients who were not offered care and suffered. Reps lose themselves in medical jargon, so keep it plain. Alternative structure: a patient care coordinator makes the sale while the doctor stays in the back, as the gym manager works as a sales manager.

### POD-0853#08 | SCRIPT | topic: sales-process | also: product-delivery | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:28:06
claim: Put the call script on a clipboard or tablet with check marks so the clinician cannot skip steps.
detail: Sequence: ask what brought them in and where it hurts; rate pain 1 to 10; list what the pain stops them doing and what they wish they could do; ask what they have tried; reflect their likes and dislikes; ask permission to explain; then present three options.
applies_to: In-person clinicians and consultants.

### POD-0853#09 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: hiring-talent, sales-team | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:29:01
claim: A solar company with unlimited setters but too few leaders must train leadership as intensively as it trains sales.
detail: Revenue 20M, goal 160M, independent-contractor setters, leaders developed internally over five years. Their leadership programme pulled reps off selling and cut volume. Hormozi: standardise leadership behaviours the way you standardise sales; the company is really a recruiting and training business.
numbers: 20M to 160M; five years to build the current leaders.

### POD-0853#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:31:45
claim: Promote middle-of-pack reps everyone loves into sales manager roles; leave the best closers to close.
detail: In his businesses, the best closers earn more than the sales managers. Those who worked from 3 out of 10 up to 6 or 7 he has found to be exceptional managers, like coaches who were not star players. Promoting top closers might mean losing a seller and gaining no good leader.
numbers: Reps who grew from about 3/10 to 6-7/10.
### POD-0853#11 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:32:48
claim: Train leaders as militantly as salespeople: many hours, shadowing, full days, not one monthly session.
detail: A monthly one-hour training is forgotten between sessions. Example: one day a week for a month or two alongside an existing manager. People know what to do but lose the how. Even with 15 operationalised skills, the other 85 matter.
numbers: Illustration of 100 behaviours, 15 systematised.

### POD-0853#12 | BENCHMARK | topic: sales-team | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:33:34
claim: A player-coach works for small teams; past about six reports leading is a full-time job.
detail: Shared risk keeps the leader credible, and half days of selling are a compromise. With three reps one player-coach is right.
numbers: About 6 reports per full-time manager.

### POD-0853#13 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:37:32
claim: Group lessons beat private ones on margin: four students at 50 each bring 200 against a 40-50 teacher hour, about 80%.
detail: At 60 a session the margin is about 84%. Rule of thumb for brick-and-mortar services: at least 80%, ideally above 90, and he refuses lower. He gave this to a music-teaching business that was supply constrained.
numbers: 4 x 50 = 200 revenue, teacher cost 40-50, gross margin about 80%; at 60 a session about 84%; minimum 80%.
applies_to: Time-based services.

### POD-0853#14 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery, offers | fw: Create Flow Monetize Add Friction | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:41:12
claim: For a supply-constrained owner the first lever is price, then group formats, then delegation; sell private lessons only at a special-snowflake price.
detail: Price is fastest because nothing changes but the number. Sell mainly group, offer one-to-one at a premium, and design a six-week beginner challenge with a concrete outcome (a recognisable song) or a before and after survey. Upfront package guessed at 600-2,000.
numbers: Package price guess 600-2,000; six-week challenge.
