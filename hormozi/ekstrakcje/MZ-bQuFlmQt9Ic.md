### MZ-bQuFlmQt9Ic#03 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:11
claim: The co-host describes Musk holding five to fifteen minute meetings in which each person is asked for their number one issue, then helping solve it.
detail: Two effects: bottlenecks are removed at speed, and people are forced to prioritise their single problem. The same speaker adds that Musk is a player-coach, working long weeks in the work itself, and that brand is used to recruit scarce top engineers.
numbers: 5-15 minute meetings, one issue each, once a week.
applies_to: Leaders of technical teams

### MZ-bQuFlmQt9Ic#04 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:07:28
claim: The "algorithm" has five steps in order: question the requirements, delete unnecessary requirements, optimise, speed up, automate.
detail: Hormozi uses it to fix departments: ask why each task exists, drop the dumb ones (in his media-team illustration, 28 tasks), move the best people to the highest-return work, then shorten timelines, then automate. Questioning the requirements is one of the hardest steps, because people add steps easily but fear removing them and defend a "mythology of process".
numbers: Illustrative example: 28 tasks dropped from a media department's routine.
### MZ-bQuFlmQt9Ic#05 | CASE | topic: product-delivery | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:47
claim: A $500,000 recording studio did not deliver the benefit it was built for, because the lights were shared and needed two hours of setup each time.
detail: He had been told the studio would let him walk across the hall and record. Asking why it took two hours, he learned the lights were reused with another studio and cost about $2,000. Lead time on videos was also two weeks, partly due to large files from expensive cameras. He replaced the cameras with phones and posted within 24 hours.
numbers: $500,000 studio; about $2,000 lights; two hours setup; two-week lead time cut to 24 hours.

### MZ-bQuFlmQt9Ic#07 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:56
claim: Ask "what stops us from doing ten times the current number?" and keep asking why until the real constraint appears.
detail: Each stated reason is tested: if solvable, why is it not solved? One example he gives is that the thread ends at a small gap, such as HR not having posted a role. He calls this pulling the thread and thinks part of why many people miss it is that they lack the business context to see which gap is the tip of the iceberg.

claim: One in-house recruiter would take two quarters to fill the sales roles that outside recruiters could fill in about seven days, so the delay cost far more than the fees.
