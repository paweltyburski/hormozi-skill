### OC-08#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: objections-closing | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Guarantees can make or break a business — a compelling guarantee can double to quadruple sales — because an offer can fail to make money simply by being too risky for the prospect.
detail: Prospects may not believe it will work for them; removing that risk is a major selling lever. Free reverses risk on the front end, a guarantee on the back end.
numbers: Compelling guarantee can lift sales 2–4×; free offers convert ~9× better than paid (Hormozi's figure).

### OC-08#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: offers | also: objections-closing, pricing | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:41
claim: There are four kinds of guarantee: unconditional, conditional, anti-guarantee ("all sales are final") and implied guarantees through performance-based pricing.
detail: 1. Unconditional: money back for any reason. 2. Conditional: terms and many variations, often strongest for closing. 3. Anti-guarantee: all sales final, with a stated reason. 4. Implied: customer pays only if the outcome happens. Unconditional, conditional and performance can be stacked; name guarantees; be bold and own the position.

### OC-08#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: offers, objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:27

### OC-08#04 | SCRIPT | topic: offers | also: objections-closing | fw: Anti-Guarantee | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:15
claim: An "all sales are final" policy works if you own it and give a reason — either a filter for committed people or the fact that proprietary information cannot be unseen.
detail: (1) Identity filter: "if you need a guarantee, this isn't for you; we want A-players." (2) Nature of product: proprietary back-end information is valuable the moment you see it and cannot be unseen. Giving the reason is a damaging admission and the second framing implies information that shortcuts time and effort. Cannot be stacked with other guarantees.

### OC-08#05 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: offers, sales-process | fw: Unconditional Guarantee | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:54
claim: Jason Fladlien's unconditional-guarantee script reframes the purchase as the only way to make a fully informed decision, so delaying the decision requires buying.
detail: Adapted from Jason Fladlien (webinars, usable on calls): (1) you are not asked to decide yes or no, but to decide fully informed; (2) that is only possible from inside, like a house; (3) get inside, keep it if valuable, no hard feelings if not; (4) whether 29 minutes or 29 days later, unhappy means money back; (5) simple refund mechanism; (6) close with confidence, since you only guarantee the real deal. The first lines carry the power: they tie "not deciding yet" to buying. Feature the guarantee prominently.
quote: I'm not asking you to decide today.

### OC-08#06 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: Unconditional Guarantee | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:30
claim: Hormozi framed his unconditional guarantee as a trial with "two no-risk options", only one of which can get the prospect what they want.
detail: (1) "Option one: walk out the door. Option two: buy today and on the last day, if it wasn't worth it, you got it all free. Fair enough?" (2) If they hesitate: both carry zero risk, but option one is guaranteed not to get you there and option two very likely does. (3) Confidence close: he can only guarantee because they are good; if you hit your goal you will not ask for a refund; you cannot decide now anyway, so start.

### OC-08#08 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: Unconditional Guarantee | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:18
claim: If you are nervous about offering a guarantee to everyone, keep it in reserve and use it only to close prospects who are on the fence.
detail: In phone or in-person selling, do not announce it every time; bring it in when someone hesitates. That is often how he used it.

### OC-08#09 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: offers | fw: Unconditional Guarantee | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:12
claim: Guarantee math: even if a guarantee doubles the refund rate, the extra sales usually leave you with more net sales — in the illustration, 117 versus 95, a 23% increase.
detail: Without a guarantee: 100 sales, 5 refunds (5%) = 95 net. With one: 130 sales, 13 refunds (10%) = 117 net, about 23% more. Run your own numbers; you know your data better than customers do.
numbers: 100 − 5 = 95 net; 130 − 13 = 117 net; +23%.

### OC-08#10 | DEFINITION | topic: offers | also: - | fw: Conditional Guarantee | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:57
claim: A conditional guarantee takes the form "If you don't achieve X in Y time, we will Z" — and it must state the condition and the consequence explicitly to have teeth.
detail: Common mistake: not stating the condition or the "or what" ("100 sales guaranteed" over what period?). Model: Domino's "30 minutes or it's free". Some ad platforms and laws limit what you can state; say it when legally possible. Conditions must be realistic or the guarantee is unbelievable.
quote: You have to tell the condition to give it teeth.

### OC-08#11 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: offers | fw: Activation Points | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:29
claim: Build guarantee conditions around activation points — the early actions that predict a customer will stay and succeed — so the guarantee steers customers into the behaviour that makes them successful.
detail: Find them by asking what best customers did early that failures did not; make those the conditions, with basics like paying on time, attending every session and doing homework (e.g. marriage guarantee). Build onboarding to drive those actions, yielding early wins, engagement, referrals, testimonials.

### OC-08#12 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: verticals | fw: Activation Points | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:29
claim: Activation points in Hormozi's businesses: a gym client making a $2,000+ sale in the first seven days is very likely to stay a year; ALAN has three — live and showing stats, first lead shows up, and realising performance beats retainer.
detail: Gym Launch: a client making a $2,000+ sale within 7 days very likely stays a year. ALAN (agency software): (1) system live and stats as expected; (2) first person shows up for the client's own client; (3) client realises performance earns more than retainer. Teams target each point and guarantee conditions are built around them.
numbers: $2,000+ in sales in first 7 days → very high one-year retention.

### OC-08#13 | FRAMEWORK | topic: offers | also: objections-closing | fw: Conditional Guarantee | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:29
claim: Hormozi's ten conditional-guarantee structures: outsized refund, service, modified service, credit-based, personal service, hotel and airfare, wage payment, release of service, delayed second payment, and first outcome.
detail: 1. Outsized refund: if they did everything and missed X, refund a multiple, e.g. 3×. 2. Service: keep working free until X, if conditions met. 3. Modified service: reduced version after the period. 4. Credit-based: 100% credited toward another program. 5. Personal service: owner works with them one-on-one. 6. Hotel and airfare: refund travel and hotel for events. 7. Wage payment: pay for time spent. 8. Release of service: released from remaining contract if X not met by Y. 9. Delayed second payment: next payment waits until first outcome. 10. First outcome: no payment until first outcome. Template: "If you don't achieve X in Y time, we will Z." He says he loves #2, likes #4, #7 and #9 a lot, and likes all of them; calls the list not exhaustive.

### OC-08#14 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: lead-getters, unit-economics | fw: Conditional Guarantee | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:56
claim: Pair a service guarantee with required preferred vendors: if you must keep serving someone for free, the vendor commissions still cover your cost.
detail: The service guarantee is among the strongest; its conditions decide it. One condition: use your recommended vendors, since you know they work. Affiliate revenue keeps paying you while you serve free, lowering the risk.

### OC-08#16 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: offers | fw: Conditional Guarantee | novelty: canon | ts: 00:14:44
claim: For events, guarantee not only the ticket but also hotel and airfare — with the condition that the attendee decides and leaves by the end of day one.
detail: Learned from Frank Kern. Travel, hotel and flights are the real risk, so cover them if the event was not worth it, but in a two-day workshop they must claim by end of day one and leave halfway, which limits claims and is easy to justify.
applies_to: Paid workshops and live events.

### OC-08#17 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: product-delivery | fw: Conditional Guarantee | novelty: canon | ts: 00:15:37
claim: A wage-payment guarantee pays customers for the hours they spent on the program if it does not work, calculated from tracked consumption and their prior-year income.
detail: Needs fulfilment data (e.g. 2 hours on calls + 6 hours of materials = 8 hours). Use last year's tax return for an hourly rate and pay those hours. Most will not claim, but it is distinctive.

### OC-08#18 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: retention-ltv | fw: Conditional Guarantee | novelty: canon | ts: 00:16:03
claim: A release-of-service guarantee lets a contracted client exit if you do not hit X by Y — but avoid it where results inherently take time.
detail: With a fixed contract (e.g. 12 months): if X is not reached by Y, they owe nothing further. Where results are slow (e.g. SEO) it can backfire; align wording with when you can deliver value.

### OC-08#19 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: retention-ltv, money-models | fw: Conditional Guarantee | novelty: canon | ts: 00:16:03
claim: Delayed-second-payment and first-outcome guarantees tie payment to the first activation point: no next payment, or no payment at all, until the first small result arrives.
detail: Delayed second payment: pay today, but the next monthly payment waits until the first outcome (first 5 pounds, first sale, first lead), then billing continues. First outcome: pay nothing until it. Both tie to activation points, since a precursor result makes customers stay.

### OC-08#20 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: offers, money-models | fw: Implied Guarantee | novelty: canon | ts: 00:18:24
claim: Performance or value-based pricing is an implied guarantee and one of Hormozi's preferred methods — uncapped upside for you, no downside risk for the customer — but it requires highly qualified prospects and a consistent track record of achieving the result.
detail: If the outcome is not achieved, the customer owes nothing. He believes high performers want no cap on their income: if he can take a company from $5M to $30M a year in revenue and from $3M to $50M in enterprise value, charging a flat $50,000 makes little sense. His portfolio relationships are value-based; his software and supplement businesses purely performance-based. Requirements: (1) qualify prospects highly, since you front a lot of time; (2) be good at achieving the result consistently, otherwise do not do performance pricing, at least not in the beginning.
numbers: $5M → $30M revenue; $3M → $50M enterprise value vs $50K flat fee.

### OC-08#21 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:20:00
claim: Be exceptional at what you sell or do not sell it — and expect your understanding of what "excellent" means to keep rising as you grow.
detail: Be exceptional before selling. Your sense of excellence changes as you improve: a $250,000-a-year employee versus a $30,000 one showed different value levels, and $3M, $30M and $100M entrepreneurs see value differently.

### OC-08#22 | FRAMEWORK | topic: pricing | also: money-models, verticals | fw: Implied Guarantee | novelty: canon | ts: 00:21:08
claim: Performance pricing can be structured per unit, as a percentage of revenue, as a percentage of growth over a baseline, or as a share of profit or gross profit.
detail: (1) Per unit: B2B per sale or show; B2C per pound lost. (2) Percentage of top line, e.g. 10% of revenue (e-commerce agencies). (3) Percentage of growth above a baseline, e.g. 25%. (4) Profit share, e.g. 20%. (5) Percentage of gross profit on incremental sales. All imply you are good.
numbers: 10% of top line; 20% profit share; 25% of growth over baseline.

### OC-08#23 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: content-brand | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:22:18
claim: Name your guarantee something memorable rather than the forgettable vanilla "30-day money-back satisfaction guarantee".
detail: Better options: "If in 30 days you wouldn't leap into shark-infested water to retrieve our product, you get all your money refunded", "Love it or leave it", or the "club a baby seal guarantee". Vivid names increase perceived value and set you apart.

### OC-08#24 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: objections-closing, retention-ltv | fw: Stacking Guarantees | novelty: canon | ts: 00:20:49
claim: Stack guarantees — typically a short unconditional guarantee followed by a longer conditional one — and tie the terms to the actions you want customers to take.
detail: Example: unconditional first 30 days ("just give it a shot"), plus a conditional 60 days to six months: if you attended all sessions, did the homework and still missed 100 sales / 20 pounds / fixing your marriage in 90 days, get 2× the price back. You can stack unconditional + conditional (+ performance pricing), two conditionals (60-day and six-month), first and second outcome (30 and 60 days), or outcome tiers; never an anti-guarantee. With an unconditional one, place part of the value after the window. Advanced and a calculated risk; do the math.

### OC-08#25 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: offers | fw: Stacking Guarantees | novelty: canon | ts: 00:24:01
claim: A long-duration conditional guarantee future-paces the customer to stay six months or more and attracts people who want long-term rather than quick results — who are better, stickier customers.
detail: A six-month guarantee plants long-term thinking; attracting long-term-minded prospects brings better customers, a better stick rate and easier work.
