### POD-0148#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:35
claim: When someone does not do what you want, there are three causes: they do not know how, do not want to, or do not know what you want.
detail: The first two come from Andy Grove's operations book; Hormozi adds communication as the third and says it is far more frequent than expected. Check clarity first, then train, then motivate. If both training and motivation are in place and performance is still lacking, the person is wrong for the team.

### POD-0148#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: hiring-talent | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:09
claim: Keep staff by incentivizing with money, motivating with opportunity and inspiring with recognition.
detail: Credit goes to Peter Parks. It maps to before, during and after: money brings them in, the visible future makes them reach, public recognition follows achievement. Simple but boring, which is why owners skip it.

### POD-0148#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:35
claim: Money mainly attracts; opportunity and recognition retain, because the emotional lift from a raise fades fast.
detail: He says the feeling from more money lasts about two weeks. Pay is the bar that gets people in the door, so you can protect profit by supplying growth and recognition.
numbers: raise effect lasts about 14 days (his claim).

### POD-0148#04 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:45
claim: Run a fixed culture rhythm: monthly vision and values meeting, weekly departmental star recognition, and a daily wins huddle.
detail: Monthly: review company vision, who is served, the plan, the values and an employee of the month who exemplifies them. Weekly department meetings state the direction and name a star. Each morning staff share wins. Coaches who joined internally said seeing this was worth more than his teaching.

### POD-0148#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: After you can market and sell, the next bottleneck in a service business is how you service clients through your people.
detail: In services the product is the person, who can leave with the skill and start a rival; services are easy to start and hard to scale. People are the problem at any scale, and managing them gets harder with headcount. Software and retail differ only in revenue per employee.

### POD-0148#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:24
claim: Owners who check out after modest success lose staff, and the warning signs appear months before a trainer leaves with clients.
detail: Treat the team as a sports team with a coach: meetings are practice and one-on-ones are skill development. People play sports unpaid for recognition and growth. A trainer leaving with 70 clients was preceded by three or four months in which staff felt the owner stopped caring.
numbers: trainer left with 70 clients; signs 3-4 months earlier.

### POD-0148#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:32
claim: Once communication and training are fine and the person is still not motivated, decide whether they are the right person for the team; if they are the wrong person, get them out.
detail: Nothing demotivates a team of strong performers more than a weak member who stays.
