### POD-230819-04#01 | CASE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: paid-ads | fw: Lead Magnet Steps | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:12
claim: His first webinar ads spent $450 over three days for 80 leads and no sales, because nobody wanted the webinar itself.
detail: In 2016, after paying $25,000 to join an advertising group whose mentor told everyone to run webinars, he spent weeks building the pieces and then launched ads at $150 a day. A peer in the same group meanwhile posted a $32,000 webinar. He shut his campaign off, and later concluded the problem was that people did not want the webinar, not that he lacked polish. He adds that webinars still work for people with the skills.
numbers: $150 per day ad spend; 3 days = $450; 80 leads; 0 sales.

### POD-230819-04#07 | TACTIC | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: content-brand | fw: Three Lead Magnet Types | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:44
claim: Samples and trials suit recurring problems, and the strongest sample should come first.
detail: Cap by uses or time. Consumables are natural fits, and he cites Costco's food samples; a free posture adjustment or a trial of a faster server also fit. His drug-dealer analogy is to give the best hit first, since a weak sample drives people elsewhere and the next one must be paid for.

### POD-230819-04#08 | EXAMPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: product-delivery | fw: Three Lead Magnet Types | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:15:12
claim: A multi-step magnet gives one step free and sells the rest: sealing a garage door with three coats, or free finance tools that reveal how heavy the work is.
detail: In the sealant case the first coat is free, you explain it only partly protects, and the other two coats are bundled for sale. ### POD-230819-04#14 | FRAMEWORK | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: promotions-launches | fw: Fraternity Party Planner | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:25:33
claim: A call to action needs two parts: a clear, direct instruction and a reason to act now.
detail: Use plain commands such as "call now"; be clear, not clever. Good reasons work better than bad ones, and any reason, even a bad one, tends to work better than none; he cites a Harvard line-cutting experiment where giving a reason made people more likely to let someone cut. Scarcity and urgency are among his favorite reasons, and making up a reason is another favorite of his.
### POD-230819-04#15 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: leads-fundamentals, paid-ads | fw: CAC | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:29:31
claim: Worked math: advertising a free magnet that costs $25 to deliver cut cost per customer from $3,000 to $1,000 and raised the return to 10:1.
detail: Base case: $10,000 profit on the core offer, $1,000 of ads per booked call, one in three calls closes, so $3,000 per customer. With the magnet: ad cost per call falls to $75, delivery adds $25, so $100 per call; one in ten magnet takers buys, so 10 x $100 = $1,000 per customer. Same ad budget then triples customer count. A delivery budget lets you provide more value than a zero-cost giveaway.
numbers: $3,000 to $1,000 customer cost; $10,000 profit per customer; $25 magnet delivery; $75 ads per call; 1 in 3 vs 1 in 10 close; 10:1 return.
