### MZ-YMm6kETqdqs#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A salon owner (3 salons, ~$1M, $500K, $500K; coaching business ~$700K, he said about $2M total) coaches salon owners to attract clients through an online retail campaign and then builds the store for them.
detail: His method: run shopping ads for a popular hair brand, retarget buyers on Meta and Google, put a gift card in each box so nearby buyers become salon clients. Owners found it too hard, so he added done-for-you Shopify, SEO and site builds. Selling happens in DMs with no sales team.
numbers: Salons about $1M, $500K, $500K; coaching about $700K; had 5 salons, sold 2.

### MZ-YMm6kETqdqs#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:07
claim: Clients of the done-for-you store build keep paying at about 80% annual logo retention.
detail: Hormozi: with 80% logo retention in an SMB audience that contains zillions of firms, the offer is very interesting. If churn were higher he would have told him to stay with the high-margin information product.
numbers: About 8 of 10 clients stay per year.

### MZ-YMm6kETqdqs#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: verticals | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:28
claim: Education acquires customers easily with high gross margin but is not sticky by design; pair it with a sticky service or physical product as the monetisation.
detail: His salon coach's mechanism includes real service delivery and physical products, giving a moat that pure information lacks, since information is easy to copy and dilute. Remaining task: tune pricing so gross margin is right.

### MZ-YMm6kETqdqs#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:14
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: if clear this is the direction, get rid of the three salons and focus on the coaching and store business.
detail: His test question: if you sold the salons, could this business replace their net income, and how fast? The owner said about six months. He said the story could be: I had five locations, sold them, and here is the model I used. Do not obsess over the valuation.
numbers: Replace salon income in about 6 months (owner's estimate).
### MZ-YMm6kETqdqs#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:52
claim: Hormozi once fire-sold six gyms within 90 days, for about the price of one and a half, to focus on the licensing business.
detail: The advice is not normal, but fits when the new offer is proven and the owner has the expertise. He says that within six months the new business hit about a million a month (partly unclear in audio).
numbers: Six gyms sold in 90 days for roughly 1.5 gyms' value; about $1M a month within 6 months (unclear in audio).

### MZ-YMm6kETqdqs#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: sales-process | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:07
claim: Customers care about the destination, not the vehicle, so the unique mechanism needs only about 30 seconds of the pitch.
detail: Analogy: they want Hawaii, not the plane. Lead with the full package, not coaching with an upsell.

### MZ-YMm6kETqdqs#07 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:57
claim: Sell the build as a fixed-fee, fixed-length project (for example $10K over four months), then continue as paid management of SEO, store and products.
detail: Keep leverage on the front end; group coaching is fine. Since selling is not the issue and he is about to scale, do not add unscalable options now.
numbers: Example $10K for 16 weeks/4 months, then continuity.
