### YT-HsQeQM1jUeg#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: outreach, lead-getters | fw: Warm Outreach Steps | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:47
claim: Hormozi's first online income came from a four-stage ladder that took about a year before the first public offer.
detail: Fitness coaching side project while employed as a consultant. Stage 1: texted friends, about six agreed to free training in exchange for before/after photos. Stage 2: asked them for referrals (6-10 more came, but did nothing because it was free). Stage 3: "free training project", where referrals donated to charity. Stage 4: paid 12-week "Skinny to Swole" programme, sold to the charity group and then through one public post.
numbers: Six months to collect the first proof; about 6 homies, 6-10 referred friends, 20 paying clients at $200 a month for 12 weeks (about $4,000 a month, roughly $12,000 over the programme).
applies_to: Solo service starter with no audience

### YT-HsQeQM1jUeg#02 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:05:50
claim: The "Rule of 300" breaks warm outreach under the Rule of 100 into three daily steps for someone with no audience: post, comment, then message the people who reacted.
detail: Step 1: publish one post on one platform. Step 2: leave 100 thoughtful comments on other people's posts, ideally inside a group or community full of your ideal customers. Step 3: apply the Rule of 100 outreach to a new lukewarm list made of everyone who engaged with your post or comments. It was being tested with students in his school community when recorded; results were promised later, not given.

### YT-HsQeQM1jUeg#03 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:42
claim: Comment in a group instead of posting your own content, because you only compete with the other commenters and borrow the reach of a larger post.
detail: A group is a pool of your ideal customers, so you are judged only against people inside it, and really only against the other comments under one post. A comment of real substance stands out from "cool" or "awesome" replies. Existing posts work as prompts, so comments are easier to produce than original content. Never pitch in the comment: it gets you blocked and looks bad. Pick posts that already attract the buyers you want (dog buyers: comment on dog posts).
numbers: Expect 2-4 hours a day for the whole routine.

### YT-HsQeQM1jUeg#04 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Engaged Leads | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:44
claim: Four sources make up the lukewarm list to message: post likers, post commenters, people who liked your comments, and people who replied to your comments.
detail: Each has taken an action, so they are engaged leads, not strangers. Replies to your comments are the warmest because they already read and answered you. Open the message with a question about their action (what drew them in, are they looking to get in shape), not a bare thank-you, since a thank-you does not start a conversation.

### YT-HsQeQM1jUeg#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: outreach | also: content-brand | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:24
claim: A rough yield for the Rule of 300 routine: 100 thoughtful comments produce at least 100 likes or replies to follow up with.
detail: Reasoning: the author of the post you commented on tends to like substantial comments to encourage more of them. A single own post with 25 likes already gives 25 outreach targets, and a few commenters add to that. Together these cover the 100 daily outreaches without needing an audience.
numbers: 100 comments yield at least 100 likes or responses (his estimate); a post with 25 likes gives 25 targets.

### YT-HsQeQM1jUeg#07 | EXAMPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:11:02
claim: Referred friends of free clients did not act, because getting something free gave them no stake; a required charity donation fixed it.
detail: He asked his first six homies for friends who had seen their results; 6-10 came, but since training was free, none showed commitment. He then asked each referral to donate to a charity of their choice, giving them skin in the game without paying him. Being public about the charity angle also brought supportive attention.

### YT-HsQeQM1jUeg#09 | EXAMPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: content-brand, offers | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:16:55
claim: His first "open for business" post worked because it carried a year of visible proof plus a simple call to reply; he later listed how he would improve it.
detail: Structure: admit a weakness for humour, say he has run a free project for nearly a year, announce a website with transformations, say a few slots are open, ask people to message him. Improvements he named: cut the preamble; lead with transformation images in a carousel so people click; add a clear CTA (DM or comment); text the post link to everyone; add an offer with bonuses and risk reversal; add a time limit only if it is true.

### YT-HsQeQM1jUeg#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:34
claim: Large proof plus a poor offer beats an excellent offer with no proof, but the proof must be similar enough to the buyer.
detail: He started with free clients specifically to get before/afters. By accident the first testimonials covered several avatars: a man who lost 50 pounds, a woman who lost 50 pounds and a recomposition (same weight, very different look). That spread let different prospects see someone like themselves.

### YT-HsQeQM1jUeg#11 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: offers | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:05
claim: Be the biggest visible example of what you sell, so the first obvious objection is answered before it is raised.
detail: A seller of weight loss who is overweight, or of investing who is poor, meets the obvious objection immediately. Having results yourself and for others made people believe he could do it for them, even before he understood guarantees, scarcity or value. If you must choose between promise and proof, choose proof.

### YT-HsQeQM1jUeg#14 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:46

detail: He follows a "full price or free" rule. If conversion is too low he, as owner, may choose to lower the standard price strategically; what he refuses is lowering it for one buyer to win the sale. Alternative to a discount: offer a free trade of actions, or full price with the full service.

### YT-HsQeQM1jUeg#16 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery | fw: Riches in the Niches | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:26:41
claim: Niching lowers delivery cost and raises value at once, because a common starting point and goal make solutions templatable and deeper.
detail: He drifted into "skinny to swole" men because they responded to someone who looked like him, and because he preferred that work. Same goals and starting position mean more of the service can be templated and the solution can go deeper than a generic personal plan at low price. Without niching you would have to charge much more to give each person that attention.

### YT-HsQeQM1jUeg#17 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: Ascension Path | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:27:48
claim: He sold a 12-week programme, did not offer anything next, and had to find 20 new clients; the fix is to sell more of what they just bought.
detail: Customers who got swole still want more; nobody stops after adding weight to their bench. He had no maintenance or follow-on offer ready. His advice: always have a second offer, and if you cannot think of one, offer more of the same result.
