### YT-GDqmlwY_uj4#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:47
claim: Judge an opportunity on three variables: potential units sellable at scale, potential profit per unit at scale, and supply-demand economics in the market.
detail: Units is total addressable market maxed out. Profit per unit depends on value delivered, price sensitivity and cost of goods at scale; early on profit per unit may be low, and in his factory example the contribution margin per extra unit might still be very high despite big upfront costs. Hormozi uses this when choosing companies to invest in.
### YT-GDqmlwY_uj4#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:34
claim: Supply-demand economics are the third factor: a huge market with entrenched supply is a poor entry even if units and profit look good.
detail: Example: competing with a large mobile carrier has vast market and profit per unit, but supply is existing and entrenched. A newer or growing market where demand outpaces suppliers favours a new entrant. A narrow sliver with better supply-demand can also justify short-term niching.
numbers: Illustration: demand growing 20% a year against competitor count growing 10% a year still leaves economics in the entrant's favour.

### YT-GDqmlwY_uj4#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:09
claim: The founder of Blackstone, as Hormozi recalls, requires a big potential, 10-out-of-10 talent to run it, and sensible economics.
detail: Hormozi adapted these three tests for a smaller operator, replacing the first with market size and economics with profit per unit and supply-demand. The talent test matches his aim of opportunities others can run better than he can.

### YT-GDqmlwY_uj4#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Each step up in the leverage of the opportunity vehicle added about one zero to his monthly income.
detail: His path went from four figures a month to five, six and seven, with eight figures a month the current aim.
numbers: About 10x monthly income per vehicle step (four to seven figures per month so far).

### YT-GDqmlwY_uj4#05 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:39
claim: A skilled entrepreneur stuck at low income kept reinventing the model, but the weak link was the opportunity itself.
detail: Hormozi's diagnosis: small owners often stay stubbornly persistent when the vehicle's market, profit per unit or supply-demand cap their income.

### YT-GDqmlwY_uj4#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:46
claim: Focus exclusively on one path; if after one, two or five years you still see no significant progress, use discernment to reassess whether the vehicle fits you.
detail: Hormozi calls knowing when to quit the hardest entrepreneurial decision and says intelligent people change their minds as data accumulates. Do not build a bridge while running both, since neither will work; switch in time. Staying long still compounds, with the second five years probably outperforming the first.
numbers: Review point after roughly 1 to 5 years of one step forward, two back.
### YT-GDqmlwY_uj4#07 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:36
claim: Ask whether the current vehicle is the best use of your time, skills, resources, beliefs and access.
detail: He thinks the difference between rich and poor is simply how they value their time, allocate resources and what they say no to. Example: Elon Musk is probably not pursuing dry cleaning, though it might be a good vehicle for someone else.
