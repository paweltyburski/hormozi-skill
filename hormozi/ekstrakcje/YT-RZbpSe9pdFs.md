### YT-RZbpSe9pdFs#01 | CASE | topic: offers | also: product-delivery | fw: Create Flow Monetize Add Friction | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A founder with no sales yet was already worried about being buried in fulfillment; Hormozi told him to pile on value and stop optimising.
detail: A high-school friend priced a programme at $4,800 for 10 weeks and sent a value ladder and offer-stack graph for feedback, fearing he was offering too much. Hormozi's reply: with no sale made, the overload is an imaginary problem. Give so much that saying no feels foolish, close one deal, then another, and deal with real overwhelm only when it arrives, using the sale revenue as resources.
numbers: $4,800 price for a 10-week programme (unit: total price per client).
applies_to: Stage 0-1 founders with no paying client yet.

### YT-RZbpSe9pdFs#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: product-delivery | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Create Flow Monetize Add Friction | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:41
claim: Even unscalable delivery (your own time, one-to-one, travelling to the client) is acceptable if it gets the first yeses.
detail: Do not think about scale or profit optimisation before the first sale exists. Hormozi compares it to a gym beginner worrying about getting too big: there are a million steps between where you are and that problem. Start by getting any sale, then deliver, and only afterwards optimise.

### YT-RZbpSe9pdFs#03 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: offers | fw: Value vs Willingness-to-Pay Matrix | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:18
claim: Over-stack first, then learn from real delivery which promised pieces to peel back.
detail: Early on you cannot know which items are valuable in the sale but easy to deliver, and which are barely valued by clients yet consume your time. After several overloaded sales you will see this and can remove the time-heavy, low-value parts, ending with an offer that is both profitable and attractive.

### YT-RZbpSe9pdFs#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:46
claim: Clients do not remember the contents of the stack; they remember how they felt and how well you understood their problems.
detail: What sells and sticks is the feeling of being understood, plus a solution that seems unique, easy to grasp and believable for them. So agonising over exact inclusions in the early offer is wasted effort.

### YT-RZbpSe9pdFs#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: objections-closing | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:18
claim: At 23 Hormozi paid $3,000 for a weekend conference and learned from its coach that a good enough offer makes sales skill unnecessary.
detail: The coach, a business coach claiming a million-dollar year, told him the sales secret: make an offer so good people feel stupid saying no. Hormozi's takeaway: if someone says no, keep adding value rather than cutting price, and be willing to scale the unscalable to start demand.
numbers: $3,000 conference ticket, paid when he had no spare money.
