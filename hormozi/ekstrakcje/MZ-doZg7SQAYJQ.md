### MZ-doZg7SQAYJQ#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: sales-process | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Hormozi writes his own persuasion as four statements: more good or less bad if you do the thing, and less good or more bad if you don't.
detail: He names the grid as plus-plus, plus-minus, minus-plus and minus-minus. Copy ping-pongs between these elements. Example for a fitness programme: if you do not sign up, you lose time with your kids (less good), you get more health problems (more bad), and the people whose approval you want will not give it.
### MZ-doZg7SQAYJQ#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:04
claim: To change behaviour in a group, change its incentive structure; announcements do not.
detail: Asking a team of hundreds to adopt AI fails if their incentives reward doing their existing job. He links this to the Innovator's Dilemma: people act inside their incentives.

### MZ-doZg7SQAYJQ#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:30
claim: Do not create demand or attention; channel what already exists and be the most convenient option, which makes "who do I serve" a high-leverage choice.
detail: Quoting copywriter Gary Halbert's idea of carving a channel from an existing river, he says attention is already being spent. What is attractive to one avatar may not be to another, so choosing the audience is among the highest-leverage decisions in a business.
