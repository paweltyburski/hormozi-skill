### POD-0771#01 | DEFINITION | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:46
claim: Strategy is the prioritisation of limited resources against unlimited possible actions, and everyone in a company is a strategist with a different resource pool.
detail: The owner allocates all company resources and personal network (time, effort, money, people, relationships). A marketing director allocates the team's time and an ad budget. An individual contributor allocates only their own time. Each level's choices should be aligned with the one above it, top to bottom.

### POD-0771#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Impact vs Investment Matrix (RICE) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:08
claim: RICE scores a task on Reach, Impact, Confidence and Expense, which amounts to a risk-adjusted return on a bet.
detail: Reach is how many people a decision can affect. Impact is how much it affects them. Confidence is the likelihood it achieves the goal. Expense is the time and money risked. Every decision is a bet, even high-confidence ones like texting a spouse; people who advance fastest simply get better returns on their bets.

### POD-0771#03 | DEFINITION | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:45
claim: Work is volume times leverage, judged by output and not by effort.
detail: Volume is repetitions; leverage is the yield per repetition. Leverage shows up at three levels: some platforms yield more than others, some people yield more than others (put the best people on the best platforms), and some tasks yield more than others within one job.

### POD-0771#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: content-brand | fw: Impact vs Investment Matrix (RICE) | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:41
claim: Of the roughly twenty steps in making a video, fixing the hook or big idea moves reach far more than polishing steps like colour grading.
detail: People tend to start with the easy tasks instead of the high-return ones. Run RICE on the checklist before planning the day; prioritising means doing the highest-impact items first, not the quickest. He credits this habit with disproportionate returns on time, money and energy.
numbers: About 20 tasks per video, unequally weighted (his illustration).

### POD-0771#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:06:46
claim: Good strategists question the premise that the existing list of options is complete and look for moves "off the board" with higher risk-adjusted returns.
detail: School trains convergent choosing between two paths. Order-of-magnitude gains come from divergent thinking, as in the brick exercise: most people list two or three uses of a brick because they assume one type of brick. Vary the assumptions (material, size, shape) to find other paths. He calls this looking for the third door.

### POD-0771#06 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:32
claim: Ask what you would need to do to guarantee the outcome, which pushes the plan toward lower risk.
detail: A question that massively decreases the risk looks for moves off the board rather than choosing among the existing options.
### POD-0771#07 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:36
claim: Set a more extreme goal than the incremental one, and you will still solve it, usually better than a 10% improvement.
detail: A friend, a successful entrepreneur, asked him how to make five million dollars more profit in a year without doing more work. His takeaway: asking "how do I do 10 or 20% more" produces small answers; asking how to add 10 million and not work more still makes the brain answer, and the answer is better. Ask questions you really want answered, since bad questions create bad binary choices. Other examples: reach the goal in one year instead of three; 10x this year; double profit and work less.
numbers: Example goal: $5 million extra annual profit with no extra work; he suggests testing $10 million.

### POD-0771#08 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:50
claim: Ask each employee which single task, if it were the only one done, would most raise their throughput, and check that they can say how their job makes the company money.
detail: If they cannot answer, they should ask their supervisor. Owners should be able to translate every role into business outcomes. He suggests using "do you know how what you do makes us more money?" as an interview question: someone who cannot articulate it will not achieve it by accident.

### POD-0771#09 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:29
claim: Every role links to one of three outcomes: more customers, customers worth more, or less risk to those two continuing.
detail: Examples: HR affects cost of talent and revenue per head; support cuts churn and lifts repeat purchase and ratings, which raises LTV; compliance reduces the chance of regulator action, and paying people keeps functions running. A business grows only by acquiring more customers or making each worth more; risk reduction is the third bucket.
### POD-0771#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:42
claim: At manager level, allocate the more skilled people to the tasks where they earn the best return.
detail: The manager's resources are people with different skill, so matching skill to the highest-return task is the allocation decision. He remarks "duh" and says that no one does it.
### POD-0771#11 | EXAMPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:14:53
claim: At the owner level, put attention where the company's constraint is, even when other improvements are possible.
detail: At Acquisition.com, deal flow is not the constraint, so he would not spend his attention on conversion tests, more emails, ads or more content, although each could help. As the likely highest-output person, the owner should go where the bang for the buck is highest.

### POD-0771#12 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:45
claim: To move up, understand the job one level above yours and act as though you had it.
detail: As an individual contributor, ask how you would allocate the team's work in your boss's place, and begin acting on that. Output of team and self rises, and you become visible as a star. Politics can suppress rising people, so multiple communication cycles across departments help adjacent leaders see and promote them.
