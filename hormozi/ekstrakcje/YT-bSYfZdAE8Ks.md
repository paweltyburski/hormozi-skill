### YT-bSYfZdAE8Ks#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:00
claim: Think on an investor's time horizon (he uses five years) and treat time as an asset that compounds in your favour, not a threat.
detail: Entrepreneurs get caught in weekly and monthly goals and neglect important, non-urgent investments. Choose repeatable actions that make the goal inevitable if kept up long enough. Over longer horizons, comparing yourself to others matters less and monthly swings stop affecting him.
numbers: Five-year horizon; a mentor urges a hundred years.

### YT-bSYfZdAE8Ks#02 | CASE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:47
claim: Early outsized success (after about five hard years, millions a year in profit and nearly $20M in his second year) became a toxic benchmark that made normal businesses feel like failures.
detail: In 2020 profit fell by about half, from roughly $13.4M to about $6M (final books pending), which he says did not trouble him because the pandemic hit his clients' industry and he expected recovery within three years.
numbers: ~$20M profit in year two; $13.4M to ~$6M profit in 2020.

### YT-bSYfZdAE8Ks#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:14
claim: He changed from dismissing inbound marketing (SEO, content that people come to) to wanting a compounding asset that yields rising inbound interest each month.
detail: Founders told him a five-year build now produced about 3,000 applications a month. Alongside manual outbound and paid ads, the third channel is one that, built over five years, would be unreasonable not to have.
numbers: ~3,000 applications a month after ~5 years (as told by others).

### YT-bSYfZdAE8Ks#04 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:40
claim: Do not measure ROI of a long-term initiative for the first year; start it now but judge it next year, which lowers pressure and improves execution.
detail: Example: an inbound-application initiative at Gym Launch. Teams otherwise declare failure after a week.
numbers: Measure after ~12 months.

### YT-bSYfZdAE8Ks#05 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: scaling-constraints | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:08:14
claim: Ask which one non-revenue number, if tripled, would make it unreasonable for the company not to triple; for him it is qualified inbound applications, a leading indicator of growth.
detail: Sales can lag because a new rep has a low close rate, but over time more qualified applications must raise sales. Set a steady monthly goal (e.g. 5%) on applications, which is more consistent than a revenue goal.
numbers: 5% monthly growth in applications as target example.

### YT-bSYfZdAE8Ks#06 | DEFINITION | topic: sales-process | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Engaged Leads | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:59
claim: A sales-accepted lead is one the sales team accepts because it showed up, is the right size of business to serve and has interest.
detail: Counting these gives a cleaner growth indicator than closed sales.

### YT-bSYfZdAE8Ks#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:39
claim: Start modest reliable inflow projects too: something that adds around 20 new clients a month is worth building even if it will not double lead generation.
detail: He moved away from skipping any project that cannot double leads and instead starts the snowball.
numbers: ~20 new clients a month.
