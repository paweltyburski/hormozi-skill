### MZ-HWIxxx5sBOw#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Cost to Acquire a Permanent Customer | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A permanent-makeup trainer should include the machine in a $3,000-$5,000 academy and earn the continuity from consumables, treating education as offset CAC.
detail: The model is the same as the hair extension academies, and he has spoken to about four such founders this month. The machine and its supplies make perceived value higher and create the recurring base. The enterprise value is the number of customers permanently buying supplies, not education.
numbers: academy $3,000-$5,000; supplies $500-$1,000+/month needed.

### MZ-HWIxxx5sBOw#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit | fw: Cost to Acquire a Permanent Customer | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:40
claim: Worked maths: if 1 in 3 graduates switches fully and spends $1,000 a month on supplies, 5,000 permanent customers (15,000 sold over five years) give $5M a month.
detail: The business becomes a game of how many customers it takes to get a permanent one.
numbers: 1 in 3 convert; $1,000/month; 5,000 permanent customers; 15,000 graduates in 5 years; $5M/month.
