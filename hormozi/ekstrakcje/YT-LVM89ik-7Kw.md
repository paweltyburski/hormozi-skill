### YT-LVM89ik-7Kw#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Everything sellable falls into seven categories, each in physical and digital form (14 options): products, services, access, attention, risk, money, and endorsement or brand.
detail: Product (bottle, PDF). Service (lawn mowing, ad creation or media buying). Access (lease a building, subscription library). Attention (billboard, ad platforms). Risk (insurance, cyber cover). Money (banking, charging interest). Endorsement or brand (licensed logo for a share of sales, a paid verification badge).
numbers: 7 categories x 2 formats = 14.

### YT-LVM89ik-7Kw#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:32
claim: A guarantee can itself be sold as a product: selling risk cover to customers for a fee, like paid device warranty.
detail: An established seller of products or services can charge for insuring the outcome, which turns risk into a separate revenue line.

### YT-LVM89ik-7Kw#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:17
claim: Real offers usually combine categories.
detail: A concert sells access to an experience, plus drinks and shirts at the venue, plus recorded digital versions afterward. Look for the extra categories around the core one.

### YT-LVM89ik-7Kw#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:17
claim: Choose what to sell by your resources and the experience you already hold, and count experience absorbed from family or odd jobs.
detail: First-timers say they have none, but what parents or carers did probably gives you knowledge by osmosis (his mother is an eye surgeon, so he knows a lot about eyes). Among the categories ask which you know a little more about, then which problem is biggest that you can solve with your resources.
### YT-LVM89ik-7Kw#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:47
claim: With about $100 to start, most of the time you will be selling your time, often as a service, aimed at the person to whom you provide the most value for the least money.
detail: Little cash would probably preclude most physical products and most software. In his view the sweet spot for the first or next thing to package is probably that person; then use the delivery cube to enhance and scale it.
numbers: Starting budget in the example: $100.
### YT-LVM89ik-7Kw#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: unit-economics | fw: Delivery Cube | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:07
claim: Speed and convenience have two cost models: overstaff so customers never wait, or run staff at full utilisation and let customers wait.
detail: Dimensions are hours per day, days per week and response time (open all day with a 24-hour reply is different from 9-5 with a 3-minute reply). Both models have produced very successful companies. Live and in-person is perceived as most valuable and least scalable; recorded digital is the reverse.
