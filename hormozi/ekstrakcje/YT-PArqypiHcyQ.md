### YT-PArqypiHcyQ#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:36
claim: Treat a signed contract as the start of the obligation to deliver, and see the relationship as week to week.
detail: His team's value "own it" is applied to customers: people sign a 12-month membership and leave after three, so he assumes they stay only while he performs. He says this view gave almost zero churn in the most expensive Gym Launch group.
numbers: Example of customers signing for 12 months and quitting around month 3; near-zero churn in the top-priced group.

### YT-PArqypiHcyQ#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:10
claim: Read high churn as the market saying you are no longer in customers' best interest, not as flaky customers.
detail: Assume people will always act on self-interest, and align their interest with staying and paying, by making the service so good that leaving is unattractive. Complaining that nobody honours commitments is a victim stance.

### YT-PArqypiHcyQ#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:09
claim: Use contracts as protection only; relying on them to keep customers makes the business about contracts.
detail: People do what they want, and customers who feel tricked into an agreement turn hostile. Keep agreements in place but earn retention through delivery.

### YT-PArqypiHcyQ#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:12
claim: Small service failures in a gym (rude staff, late opening, dirty bathroom, stale playlist) end a membership whatever the contract says.
detail: The customer then invents a reason: busy, moving or a doctor's note. The real contract is for today's experience.

### YT-PArqypiHcyQ#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: Price to Value Discrepancy | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:11
claim: Chipotle keeps customers because they pay about $7 and get what feels like a $20 meal; he aims for 10x value at roughly 3x competitors' price.
detail: He is for higher prices as long as value stays far above them, and he never assumes customers will keep paying; he keeps innovating because competitors will copy last month's ideas.
numbers: Meal price about $7 against about $20 perceived value; target of value 10x price, price about 3x the market.

### YT-PArqypiHcyQ#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:21
claim: Before blaming staff, ask how invested you are as a leader; a trainer who leaves with customers was probably badly served.
detail: Make the employee exchange more than fair, with the leader giving more than they get, and meet their needs and growth, especially in service businesses where staff serve the customer.

### YT-PArqypiHcyQ#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:13
claim: People forget what you did for them, so re-earn and re-sell the relationship every day instead of resting on past value.
detail: We recall our own efforts and forget others' efforts, so do more again today instead of lamenting that people forget. Appreciate and resell customers, employees and partners constantly.
