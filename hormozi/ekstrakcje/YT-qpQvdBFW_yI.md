### YT-qpQvdBFW_yI#01 | CASE | topic: lead-getters | also: sales-team | fw: Seven Ways to Ask for Referrals | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:46
claim: A new rep in a portfolio company beat every sales record without a better close rate, simply by asking each buyer for names after the sale.
detail: The rep had the same scripts and close rate as the team, yet booked 30-50% more sales. Leadership found he ended every sale with an open question about who else the buyer knew who might want this, which produced one or two names from about one in three buyers. It was probably already in the team's SOP, but reps skip it for fear of spooking the sale.
numbers: 30-50% more sales than teammates; about 1 in 3 buyers gave one or two names.

### YT-qpQvdBFW_yI#02 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: sales-process | fw: Seven Ways to Ask for Referrals | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:31
claim: Phrase the referral ask as "who else do you know" so the buyer has to think of a person, instead of a yes/no question.
detail: "Do you know anybody?" invites a quick "no". Asking who else they know who would benefit, or who they would want to do this with, makes the customer search memory and name someone.

### YT-qpQvdBFW_yI#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:18
claim: A referred customer gives three advantages over a cold one: higher close rate and spend, a pricier version, and more referrals of their own.
detail: Referred buyers close more often, pay more, choose premium more often and refer again. Because referrals are a percentage of the base, 30% from referrals is 30% growth at $100,000 or at $100 million a year, so the channel scales with the company.
numbers: 30% of new business from referrals is cited as typical across his companies when asked consistently.

### YT-qpQvdBFW_yI#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: lead-getters, unit-economics | fw: Referral Rate minus Churn | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:05
claim: Worked example of net growth: referral rate against churn sets the steady-state growth rate.
detail: A membership business with 1,000 customers and 10% monthly churn needs 100 new customers monthly to stay flat. If 10% of customers each bring one person per month, it holds steady. At 20% referral against 10% churn the base grows 2 to 1, and keeps doing so at any size.
numbers: 1,000 customers, 10% monthly churn = 100 replacements/month; referral 20% vs churn 10% = 2:1 growth.

### YT-qpQvdBFW_yI#05 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: pricing, promotions-launches | fw: Seven Ways to Ask for Referrals | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:56
claim: Gift card play: sell existing customers heavily discounted gift cards that they can only give to others, face value equal to your entry unit of service.
detail: Pick a reason (holiday, birthday, business anniversary). Set face value at the base unit you would give away free to win a customer (a SEO agency's $2,000 monthly service, a garage-door firm's cheapest tier). Discount it 90% or more. Allow only gifts, not self-use. Cap at two per customer so each buys two. Each card is made out to a named person, so you capture the lead and get paid for it.
numbers: 90%+ discount; max two cards per customer; example $200 gym card sold for $20.

### YT-qpQvdBFW_yI#06 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: lead-getters | fw: Seven Ways to Ask for Referrals | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:24
claim: Give the gift card a 30-day expiry and follow up by text using the number captured at purchase; apply the card against a pricier package.
detail: A year-long expiry kills urgency, so use 30 days. Follow up naming the giver. On the visit either give the base free month and take a card on file, or apply the card as credit toward the $2,000 package (10x the card value), which makes it a small discount against a large sale.
numbers: 30-day expiry; $200 card applied toward a $2,000 package.

### YT-qpQvdBFW_yI#07 | EXAMPLE | topic: promotions-launches | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:12
claim: Gift card economics: you lose nothing because there is no acquisition cost, no promo display cost, and the giveaway is something you would give anyway.
detail: With 200 customers, about 30 buy two cards each = 60 cards; roughly 50 of those people come in and a large share close. Three reasons it cannot lose: no CAC paid, the discounted item is something you would hand out as a lead magnet anyway, and you can still upsell the visitor into a far bigger package. Worst case you earn a little on the card sale.
numbers: 200 customers, 30 buyers, 60 cards, about 50 redeemed (his illustrative figures).

### YT-qpQvdBFW_yI#08 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: lead-getters, pricing | fw: Seven Ways to Ask for Referrals | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:00
claim: When a prospect asks for a discount, grant it only in exchange for three friends' names, introduced on the spot.
detail: The buyer asked for something, so they are open to doing something back. Hormozi discounts only when terms change: either what is sold changes or the buyer does work for him, here acting as advertiser. The discount is your paid cost for the leads.
applies_to: Any sale where the buyer pushes for a discount.

### YT-qpQvdBFW_yI#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics, lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:33
claim: Size a referral discount from expected conversion: with three names and one in three converting, discount up to about one CAC.
detail: Reasoning: if one in three referrals buys, three referrals are worth one customer, so you can spend up to one normal CAC on the discount. Start generously so it works, then tune later, for example a flat sum per referral.
numbers: three referrals x one-in-three conversion = about 1x CAC allowed as discount.

### YT-qpQvdBFW_yI#10 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: Bonus Checklist | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:55
claim: Offer extra service instead of money off: it costs you far less than the same discount while looking bigger.
detail: Service priced at $200 costs $40 to deliver. If asked for $200 off, offer $400 of added service in return for the referral: it costs about $80 yet reads as a larger gift. It also nudges the customer to use the product more.
numbers: $200 service, $40 cost; $400 of extra value costs about $80, versus $200 hard cost for a cash discount.

### YT-qpQvdBFW_yI#11 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: retention-ltv | fw: Seven Ways to Ask for Referrals | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:26
claim: After the sale, ask "want to know how to get this free?" and pay a cash bounty per referred buyer.
detail: Used on every promotion; the bounty is paid only after the referred person buys. On a $500 offer he paid $100 cash per buyer. Well-networked people such as nurses and teachers each brought many buyers (one woman brought ten buyers; her stated earnings, $500, do not match $100 each, unclear in audio). List referrals under the customer on the contract so they are credited and the rep remembers to ask.
numbers: $100 cash per referred buyer on a $500 offer; about 30% of each promotion's sales came this way; one referrer brought 10 customers.

### YT-qpQvdBFW_yI#12 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: retention-ltv | fw: Hinge Method | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:23
claim: Ask for the introduction at the moment of success: take a selfie on the customer's phone and text it to a friend as them.
detail: Right after the transformation (house painted, garage door fitted, weight lost) and after getting the testimonial, ask how many other people like them they know. Take a photo together on their phone, then text the friend a three-way message from their phone with the picture. Needs rapport with the customer to work.
applies_to: Local and service businesses with a visible result.

### YT-qpQvdBFW_yI#13 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: lead-getters | fw: Seven Ways to Ask for Referrals | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:02
claim: Quarterly customer-appreciation events with a handwritten invitation whose P.S. lets the customer forward it as a plus-one pass.
detail: Invite customers by handwritten card, with food at low budget. The P.S. says a photo of the card texted to a friend gets them in, and suggests a three-way text so you meet the friend first. At the event, only book follow-up appointments (do not sell), and book them within days, not weeks, since motivation fades.
