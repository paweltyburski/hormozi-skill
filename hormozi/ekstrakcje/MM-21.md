### MM-21#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv, verticals | fw: Rollover Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Hormozi's gym applied Win Your Money Back credit as free months and members quit; a friend kept most paying by rolling winnings into a year.
detail: At $150/month, a $600 win meant four free months after the challenge — nearly half a year before a first payment, and members who didn't feel payments stopped coming. A better-performing gym owner in Las Vegas mentioned getting 70+ (likely 70%+, unclear in audio) to stay and pay: "I just take their winnings and credit them towards a year." It became Hormozi's most-used upsell.
numbers: $150/month; $600 = 4 free months.

### MM-21#02 | DEFINITION | topic: money-models | also: - | fw: Rollover Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:35
claim: A rollover upsell credits some or all of a previous purchase toward the next offer, which makes far more people take it.
detail: Decide the credit, then who to upsell, what to upsell and how to apply the credit — and make sure you still profit after the discount.

### MM-21#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv, lead-getters | fw: Rollover Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:35
claim: Four targets: lapsed customers (win-back), your upset customers, competitors' upset customers, and current customers.
detail: Lapsed = 6+ months since last purchase. Upset customers get a redo instead of a refund. Competitors' unhappy customers are found via reviews. Current customers roll a fresh purchase into a longer commitment.

### MM-21#04 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models, outreach | fw: Win-Back Campaign | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:35
claim: Win back lapsed customers with an "I owe you money" message crediting part of their past spend toward a new, bigger service.
detail: Check what they paid and choose the credit — all of it or a fraction such as a third. "Sandra, you haven't been back in six months and I owe you $500 — I'd like to credit it toward [service] to get you back in." He once recorded personalised videos for ~200 past customers over two days offering credit to return (stated as $4,000) (00:07:15).
numbers: 6+ months lapsed; ~200 videos in ~2 days.

### MM-21#05 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models, product-delivery | fw: Rollover Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:27
claim: When a customer is upset, offer a redo instead of a refund: credit everything they paid toward what you should have sold them.
detail: "No problem — I'll credit your $500 toward what I should have sold you originally, so everything so far was free." Try it before refunding; it saved him many customers and much cash. If they want something different, roll it toward that (00:07:15).

### MM-21#06 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: money-models, outreach | fw: Rollover Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:27
claim: Find competitors' unhappy customers through one-star reviews and credit what they spent there toward your offer — turning haters into ambassadors.
detail: Scrape contacts from negative reviews where available. "How much did you spend with them?" — "$1,200." — "I'll give you $1,200 credit with me." If locked in ("three months left"), credit the remaining payments toward a longer deal, e.g., two years (00:05:21): "Saw your review; I'll credit what you still owe them so you switch now, lose nothing and get the benefits immediately. Fair?"

### MM-21#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Rollover Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:24
claim: Roll credit toward something at least 4× (ideally ~5×) the credit, so the discount is at most 20–25%.
detail: Upsell more, better or new, but always pricier. He first suggests ~5× ($500 → $2,000–2,500+), then sets 4× (max 25% off) as the floor he wouldn't go below (00:08:06). Bigger discounts mean less profit per sale but more sales.

### MM-21#08 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing, retention-ltv | fw: Rollover Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:24
claim: Credit only as much as gets a response — up front or spread over time — and for long-tenure customers only their last purchase(s).
detail: Apply credit up front, spread it, or invent a fee and waive it. Crediting entire lifetime spend ruins businesses: for $5,000 over three years on a $2,000/year service, credit the last few purchases or "the last six months, since we must have let you down". Short tenure plus big packages: credit everything; long tenure plus small tickets: credit the last one. Test the minimum that moves people, e.g., $200 for someone spending $2,000/year, or $500 vs $300 (00:08:06).

### MM-21#09 | SCRIPT | topic: retention-ltv | also: verticals | fw: Win-Back Campaign | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:25
claim: Chiropractor win-back call: "I wanted to give you your money back", check on their pain, then offer $500 credit toward staying pain-free.
detail: Who: patients 6 months lapsed; what: a new plan; how: credit up front. "Hi Mrs. Banks, I wanted to give you your money back — got a minute?" → ask about the back pain → empathise → "As a thank-you, I'd like to give you $500 back as credit toward staying pain-free for good. Interested?" → book them.

### MM-21#10 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: verticals, retention-ltv | fw: Rollover Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:25
claim: Dentist rescue: a patient disappointed that a $200 cleaning didn't whiten their teeth gets the $200 credited toward a whitening package.
detail: Package: several sessions, an at-home kit, deep cleanings. "That's on me — miscommunication. Everything you paid goes toward what you actually wanted." Upset customers still want the result; sell them the higher package.

### MM-21#11 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Rollover Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:26
claim: Roll a current customer's just-bought block of service into a 12-month membership at the moment of purchase.
detail: Spread the first purchase over the year. Rollovers work anytime, but he prefers doing them immediately.

### MM-21#12 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: objections-closing | fw: Rollover Upsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:15
claim: Optionally (his stated preference) make the rollover credit one-time only, valid just when presented.
detail: He calls it optional and up to you; he adds urgency this way so they cannot sleep on it. Surprise and delight them with the credit; take it now or pay full price later.

### MM-21#13 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: lead-getters, promotions-launches | fw: Gift Card Play | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:07
claim: Gift-card play: sell $200 gift cards for $20, usable only by someone else, then roll each redeemed card into an offer worth at least 5× its value.
detail: Run at Black Friday or Christmas (or for birthday gifts), limit two per person. Ask buyers who it's for and to make an introduction; when the friend arrives, roll the $200 into the main offer. You're not giving 90% off your product — you're paid $20 for a hot referral, then rolling $200 into a $2,000 offer (perhaps marked up $200 or with an added high-margin item). Keep the card at ≤20% of the next offer's price. Unredeemed cards are pure cash.
numbers: $200 card for $20; next offer ≥ $1,000, e.g., $2,000.
