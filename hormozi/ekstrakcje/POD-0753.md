### POD-0753#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:00
claim: Do deals with friends as if they were enemies so they stay friends.
detail: Being fooled even once shows you do not control the variables, so a good hit rate does not protect you, since the misses can erase the gains. Structuring every deal as if with a stranger means anything extra is upside. A relationship is judged over a longer period, but is still an exchange.

### POD-0753#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:54
claim: Transactional relationships work on three levels: the single exchange, the relationship of deal-making, and your reputation for dealing.
detail: A strong history lets people extend grace through a bad stretch because they expect a return to baseline. Reputation stands in for that history with strangers, though it counts less than what they experience themselves. Good partners value future deals with you more than the gain from any one deal. He prefers the top level.

### POD-0753#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:00
claim: Character traits are not binary; ask to what stake and under what conditions a person stays honest.
detail: A trait such as honest bundles around thirty behaviours. Honest to $100, $1,000 or a million? Values only show when stakes exist, and business supplies far higher stakes than social friendship. Persistence of behaviour under pressure is the thing to assess.

### POD-0753#04 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: hiring-talent | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:36
claim: When someone is called honest or generous, ask what the stakes were and whether anyone was watching.
detail: Honesty or generosity shown with an audience may be signalling. He prefers people who act from a code about who they are, even when it costs them standing.

### POD-0753#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:27
claim: Open a new engagement by saying the deal must make sense even if you two hated each other, because deals exist for the downside.
detail: Emotions and news swing over time. Terms are for the bad periods; if news were always good, no terms would be needed.

### POD-0753#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:17
claim: Treat a refusal to write terms down, or a statement that someone does not do contracts, as a red flag.
detail: Not every contingency can be imagined in conversation; writing forces you to think through each, like coats of paint. A written contract does not prevent bad behaviour but removes unclear expectations. Handshake deals fit maybe one or two relationships built over about a decade of deals.
numbers: about 1-2 people he would shake hands with; known for about 10 years.

### POD-0753#07 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:29
claim: With a trusted partner, do your irreversible part of the deal first, then sign a short contract that records it.
detail: Both sides are already exposed, so the paperwork only memorialises legal and tax details. A handshake deal means both always put the relationship ahead of one gain.

### POD-0753#08 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:27
claim: For hard conversations, say you are seeking clarity, repeat the other side's words back, and walk through who pays and how.
detail: Sequence: (1) frame the question as seeking to understand; (2) restate their answer to confirm you duplicated it; (3) state the consequence bluntly ("if we drop the ball, we pay") and confirm; (4) settle the mechanics: wire or invoice, and payment timing so service is uninterrupted.

### POD-0753#09 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:01
claim: Signal a role change with a business hat versus a friend hat, and propose ideas as a best bad idea to remove your ego from it.
detail: Putting on or taking off an actual hat is a visual cue, as are coach or CEO hats. The best-bad-idea phrasing invites the team to attack it.

### POD-0753#10 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:11:23
claim: Cure fear of confrontation by role-playing someone freaking out in front of you.
detail: His team used this on sales reps afraid to state the price in person, staging an angry reaction so they saw nothing bad happened. His rule: everything you want sits on the other side of a few hard conversations.
