### YT-f_ceDZqhKbw#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: unit-economics | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:54
claim: Analyse a business from six numbers: new sales per month, current revenue, price, churn, lifetime value and gross profit, and be able to do the maths in your head.
detail: Owners usually know sales, revenue and price but not churn and LTV. They can be derived live in a conversation from cancellations per week and active clients. With the numbers he extrapolates where the business will cap out and where the weak points are.
### YT-f_ceDZqhKbw#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:34
claim: Worked case: 380 clients, 13 cancelling a week (about 50 a month) is 13% monthly churn; a $1,000 price gives about $7,700 LTV and a ceiling near $770,000 a month.
detail: Current revenue was about $400K with 100 new sales a month, so it would grow fast and then flatten along a curve towards the cap; churn eats a larger share of new business as the base grows. The audio garbles the ceiling as "7K" but later states 770K.
numbers: 100 new sales/month; 380 clients; $1,000 price; 13 cancels/week ~50/month; churn 13%; LTV ~$7,700; cap ~$770K/month.

### YT-f_ceDZqhKbw#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:14
claim: With fulfilment costing under $100 on a $1,000-a-month client, gross margin is over 90% and lifetime gross profit is about $7,000; as long as acquisition cost stays below that, the business is profitable for the most part.
detail: Method: ask what it costs to fulfil one customer and multiply LTV by the margin.
numbers: <$100 cost per $1,000/month client; >90% gross margin; LTGP ~$7,000.
### YT-f_ceDZqhKbw#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:03
claim: Cutting that churn from 13% to 3% lifts LTV to about $33,000 and the ceiling to $3.3M a month, mostly margin, which is easier than 10x acquisition for a $7.7M ceiling.
detail: A business can only grow by getting more customers or making each worth more. Hormozi asks which is easier: ten times the acquisition or dropping churn ten points. He refers to a separate video listing eight methods to reduce churn.
numbers: 13% to 3% churn; LTV $7,700 to ~$33,000; ceiling $770K to $3.3M a month; 10x acquisition would be $7.7M.

### YT-f_ceDZqhKbw#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:50
claim: Most private equity firms want to see about 80% yearly customer retention for a business to have enterprise value; at 13% monthly churn he says you're probably keeping less than 20% of customers by year end.
detail: Starting a year with 1,000 clients, 80% retention means 800 at the end.
numbers: 80% annual retention that most private equity firms want; 13% monthly churn probably keeps under 20% per year.
### YT-f_ceDZqhKbw#06 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:06:45
claim: Any potential buyer looks at three main variables: growth, stickiness of revenue and profit, which you could pretty much multiply together.
detail: Fast growth, sticky revenue and high profit make a very valuable business; medium growth, low stickiness and low profit make a low-value one. Acquisition.com's aim is to move businesses from one to the other.
