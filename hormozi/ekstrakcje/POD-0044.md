### POD-0044#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Six months into his first gym, he copied a nearby owner who claimed to be crushing it, and it did not work.
detail: He concluded most businesses do not know what works and that people claiming success often are not profitable. Copying their prices (for example CrossFit price-matching) just imports cheap prices from unprofitable peers.
numbers: Take-home about $10,000 to $12,000 at the time (unclear in audio).

### POD-0044#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:18
claim: Most competitors never research or test, so a business that tests properly will find real winners others miss.
detail: Copying a copy gives a diluted version that does not even work, and looks like everyone else's.

### POD-0044#03 | CASE | topic: product-delivery | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:50
claim: Gym Launch built full-time support because he asked what he would need as a gym owner, not what other gurus offered.
detail: Typical guru businesses run a couple of events a year, a Facebook group and a monthly call. Starting from the customer's problem produced a new delivery model.
numbers: 11 full-time US support staff.

### POD-0044#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:36
claim: Funnel hacking teaches you the copy but not the reasons, so you cannot fix it when it breaks and it narrows your ability to innovate.
detail: When you build your own, you know why each part exists, test it and improve.

### POD-0044#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: promotions-launches | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:40
claim: Innovators enjoy the whole revenue curve of a new method, while followers arrive late and find it stopped working.
detail: Example: companies running "huge opportunity" ads six years earlier. The process is to look at a problem, solve it, then make the solution repeatable.

### POD-0044#06 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:24
claim: Build a product that reliably produces a result: list every way it can fail, fix each, and take ownership of every customer who does not succeed.
detail: He contrasts this with selling an info course first and filming webinars week by week.
numbers: 95% of Gym Launch gyms get strong results against 5% to 10% of customers in typical programmes (his claim).

### POD-0044#07 | EXAMPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:14
claim: His own funnel is poor (never split-tested, a mislabelled survey page, two broken videos) but works because the acquisition system and ticket are strong.
detail: A funnel hacker read the flaws as deliberate psychology; copiers copy what does not matter.
