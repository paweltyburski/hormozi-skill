### YT-ug5N9qmFVKQ#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A neighbour who sold a trash-collection business planned to start another in the same field and resell it to the same buyers instead of learning something new.
detail: His logic: he already knew the business and starting another would mean relearning from zero. Hormozi's lesson: growth is not novelty, it is the character to repeat the same thing. Real-estate owners rarely open restaurants when bored.

### YT-ug5N9qmFVKQ#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:03
claim: Before changing business because you feel bored, ask whether you are loading your own needs for status, connection or challenge onto a company that exists to serve the customer.
detail: Three marketing owners (about $10M-$20M a year, roughly $3M-$5M profit) each told him they would start a software company for more challenge, entering a field with entrenched players and starting from zero. Probably for the first four years he tried to make his business fix his personal issues.
numbers: Owners at roughly $10M-$20M revenue and $3M-$5M annual profit.
applies_to: owners at $1M-$20M
### YT-ug5N9qmFVKQ#03 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: promotions-launches | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:00
claim: Channel the urge for novelty into new front ends, hooks, creative and ad ideas or back ends, not new businesses.
detail: More variety at the front of the funnel attracts different avatars and grows the business, while starting more businesses spreads attention and leaves you with many companies and little money.

### YT-ug5N9qmFVKQ#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:10
claim: Growing only at the market rate for decades compounds into great wealth; chasing outsized change often breaks what works.
detail: Example: $100,000 a year growing 10% a year for 40 years. For his portfolio, doubling about every seven years at 10% takes the roughly $85M portfolio to $1B revenue in about 28 years, which he thinks he can beat.
numbers: 10% a year; doubling about every 7 years; $85M to $1B revenue in roughly 28 years; 40-year horizon on $100,000.

### YT-ug5N9qmFVKQ#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:05:06
claim: Use multiple success metrics and constraints, such as time, health and relationships, to keep a mature game interesting.
detail: Once extra money has little marginal utility, he sets goals like building a $1B-revenue portfolio working two to three days a week while training one to two hours a day. Sacrificing everything is the first level, learned early; constraints are the later level that keeps the machine intact.
numbers: Target $1B annual revenue, 2-3 working days a week, 1-2 hours of training daily.
