### POD-0463#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:29
claim: Asking "how do I make X per year" ties income to your time; ask what value you can create for someone and take a share of it.
detail: Example: if a conversion-rate fix earns a client $5M and takes 10 minutes, charging $1M is fair, and the goal becomes doing as many as possible. Money without starting capital requires skill, not cash.
numbers: $5M client gain, $1M fee, 10 minutes of work (illustrative).

### POD-0463#02 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:47
claim: Zero-down property deal: contract an asset below its value, have the seller finance a slice, and get the bank to lend the rest.
detail: Approach many owners, since one in a thousand will accept a low price. Example: a $100,000 house under contract at $80,000; offer $70,000 with the seller financing $10,000 over five years; bank lends 80%.
numbers: 1 willing seller per about 1,000 owners contacted; 80% bank loan; seller finance over 5 years.

### POD-0463#03 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:19
claim: Buy an option or contract to purchase an undervalued asset at a fixed price, then sell that contract to someone who values the asset more.
detail: Pay a token fee for a 30-day right to buy; sell the paper for a price between your price and the true value. Unlike flipping, you never own the asset.
numbers: Example: right to buy at $2 for a $0.01 fee, sold at $20 when the item is worth $30.

### POD-0463#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:15:43
claim: Listed options typically cost about 1% of the underlying purchase value, which is how a small stake captures a price rise.
detail: 100 shares at $40 is $4,000; the option costs about $40; if the price reaches $50, the gain is about $1,000.
numbers: Option cost about 1% of purchase value; $40 outlay to $1,000 gain in example.

### POD-0463#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: sales-process | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:22
claim: Commission on very large items pays a million on one deal without owning the asset.
detail: Selling a $100M skyscraper pays about 3-4%; a $20M home pays a broker about 5%. Skill is the entry price.
numbers: 3-4% of $100M = $3-4M; about 5% of $20M = $1M.

### POD-0463#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:08
claim: Price balloons where demand from a large audience meets a scarce asset, so buying early from someone with an audience works.
detail: Same logic as NFTs and pre-IPO shares. Attaching an audience to a small supply (five items, a million interested people) lets you charge far more.

### POD-0463#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:08
claim: $1M a year is about $20,000 a week, reachable by many unit and price mixes.
detail: Options: 20 sales of $1,000, two of $10,000, or one $80,000 sale every four weeks. A $100-a-month membership kept 10 months on average is $1,000 LTV, so 20 sales a week stack up to a $1M cap.
numbers: $20,000 x 52 = about $1.04M; $100/month x 10 months = $1,000 LTV.

### POD-0463#08 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: unit-economics | fw: Crazy Eight | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:54

### POD-0463#09 | FRAMEWORK | topic: unit-economics | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:18:16
claim: Sales velocity (units sold per month) times customer lifetime value predicts the level at which revenue levels off.
detail: He uses it to analyze every business. Revenue climbs quickly, then slows near the equilibrium; how long it takes depends on how long customers take to accrue their LTV, and he first said about a year, then noted it may really be about two years. Example: 20 new customers a month at $1,000 LTV gives $20,000 a month at the top.
numbers: 20 units x $1,000 = $20,000/month (his example).
### POD-0463#10 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:19:39
claim: A business stuck at 10 sales a month with $1,500 LTV is capped at $15,000 a month and needs about five to six times more of either.
detail: To reach about $1M a year, raise units by 5-6x up front, or raise value per customer 5-6x through pricier offers, longer stays, upsells and cross-sells.
numbers: 10 x $1,500 = $15,000/month; target about $83,000/month.

### POD-0463#11 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:41
claim: A friend combined four information businesses with no cash by promising their owners the income they would have earned for two years.
detail: Each made about $1M a year; combined: about $4M revenue, $1.5M profit, worth maybe $5M. Seller financing around 2x earnings (about $2M) leaves roughly $3M margin on a flip. Small owners want out, so terms favor the buyer; a run rate shows stability.
numbers: 4 x about $1M revenue; $4M top line, $1.5M profit; sale about $5M; debt about $2M.

### POD-0463#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:10
claim: Roll-ups earn a multiple arbitrage at each tier, since bigger combined businesses sell at higher multiples to larger buyers.
detail: Large private equity will not write checks under about $100M, so small operators bundle for them. Example ladder: $4-5M revenue units, rolled into $25M revenue with $6-7M EBITDA sold for $60-70M, then $125M revenue with $30-40M EBITDA. Retiring owners with no heirs will often give businesses away.
numbers: $25M revenue / $6-7M EBITDA sells for $60-70M; minimum check about $100M (large firm).

### POD-0463#13 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:17
claim: A commercial real estate investor sold tax depreciation to high-income investors who paid the down payment, making the building almost free to them.
detail: Seller financed half the down payment so investors put only 10% down. A $400,000 tax bill on $1M income became a write-off into a building. He leased buildings back to old owners who wanted cash, then flipped to investors, earning the spread on each.
numbers: 10% down; example tax bill $400,000; one deal made about $4M.

### POD-0463#16 | DEFINITION | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:52
claim: Arbitrage is selling the same thing across two markets priced differently, or lending money between two rates.
detail: Works at any size. Examples: a coin at $19,000 in one market and $19,100 in another; borrowing at 1% and lending at 6%; a product at $10 on one retailer and $14 on another.
numbers: Lending spread 1% to 6% = 5 points.
