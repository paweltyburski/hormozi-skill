### POD-0784#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:45
claim: Reallocate team time in order of return, and cut stagnant and merely medium accounts, not only the losers.
detail: He restacked the team by company effectiveness, removed people from projects and cadences with squeaky wheels, and also dropped medium businesses that paid distributions but did not drive returns. What remained: companies with good returns for almost no effort and companies with exceptional returns for high effort. Portfolio then grew faster, more profitably, with spare team capacity.

detail: Most funds write checks with little strategic help. Private equity firms typically hold only 8-10 companies, and managers learn bigger deals earn more than more deals. Counter-model: Constellation (Canada) buys many small companies.

### POD-0784#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:30
claim: The key metric he now screens for is revenue retention: work done this year should still earn credit next year.
detail: Without it, growth depends only on acquiring more customers, and acquisition channels can be disrupted: a platform ban for content or paid ads, a key leader leaving with half a door-knocking sales force. A company with retained customers survives losing all marketing tomorrow and has time to find the next channel.

### POD-0784#09 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:21
claim: Two companies that both sell to 100, 200, 300 customers over three years differ hugely if one retains customers and the other does not.
detail: Company A loses every customer, so its customer count never builds; Company B keeps everyone and sells a flat 100 a year, reaching 300 by year three. B has paid to acquire 300 customers while A paid for 600, and that difference falls straight to the bottom line. Retained customers also become cheaper to maintain, so margins can expand.
numbers: Company A acquires 100, 200, 300 customers (600 total); Company B acquires 100 per year (300 total).

### POD-0784#10 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: retention-ltv | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:04
claim: Before investing, ask whether there is already proof of revenue retention, a retained subset of customers, or a clear missing path to retention.
detail: A subset that retains well while others do not is a silver lining, because they can probably double down on it. Acquiring and activating a customer is the most expensive step in the business; once they keep buying, you are just making money, and margins can expand. Revenue tends to be more of the horse; the jockey is the founder.
