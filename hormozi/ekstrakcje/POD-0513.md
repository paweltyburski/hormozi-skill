### POD-0513#01 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Minimum steps to be able to transact: set up an entity, open a bank account, sign up for a payment processor, then sell something.
detail: An LLC is a five-second, roughly $100 task (LegalZoom, about two weeks for paperwork); taking it to the bank gives the account, checks and card; then add a payment processor. Calling "setting up the LLC" six months of progress is stalling; the real step is selling something to someone. Prompted by dinner with eight college seniors who had done nothing in six months.
numbers: Entity about $100 and about 2 weeks; audience poll: 76% want to start a business but have not.

### POD-0513#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:44
claim: There are only six things you can sell, each in a digital and physical form: product, service, access, risk, money, media.
detail: Product (PDF or parcel), service (massage or SaaS), access (apartment, concert ticket, online room), risk (insurance, cyber protection), money (lending, crypto) and media (books, newspapers, online content). Use it to pick what to sell: look at your own past and parents' industries for things you know "by osmosis" and problems around you you could solve (his parents were eye doctors).

### POD-0513#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:19
claim: Solve the first 5-20 people's problems for free, then charge once requests exceed your time.
detail: Reasons: you are probably bad at first, free lowers the ask, you learn the skill (biggest benefit), and you get testimonials, paying customers or friend referrals. Trade free work for a testimonial and a friend's name; if refused, ask what it would take, then do it. When you have too many free customers, supply is below demand and you can set a price. Refusals reveal hidden costs.
numbers: Free for first 5, 10 or even 20 people.
applies_to: first-time founders with no revenue

### POD-0513#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:09
claim: Start in an industry you know directly or adjacent, to pay down "ignorance debt".
detail: Y Combinator prefers founders with industry experience. He entered fitness knowing fitness but not business, so half was solved. The first business is not the last; lessons carry to later ones.

### POD-0513#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:05
claim: Intelligence is speed of learning, and learning means the same condition producing a new behaviour.
detail: Content consumed without changed behaviour is not learning. Shorten the gap between wanting to do something and doing it. You learn more from the first hundred sales conversations (doable in a month) than from a hundred sales books; books make sense only after you have experience to attach them to. He read no sales book until he had closed 4,000 deals.
numbers: 100 sales conversations vs 100 books; 4,000 deals closed before first sales book.

### POD-0513#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:32
claim: The usual blocker is fear of an imagined judge plus weak impulse control, not ignorance of how to build the business.
detail: Identify whose voice you fear (often just one or two relatives) and ask whether you are living their dream or yours. Consuming business content is easier than starting (wantrepreneur); he read business and self-help books for almost a year before acting.

### POD-0513#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:10
claim: Decide whether your art is the craft or the business; if demand exceeds your time, either raise the price or build a business.
detail: Wallet maker example: stay craftsman and raise price, or hire, mechanise, add low-grade and luxury tiers and upsells. Both work; choose what you enjoy, and if unsure start with the cheaper-to-start option because tastes change.
