### MM-23#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: objections-closing | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Hormozi learned payment-plan downsells in his first gym month, when a prospect who refused every split agreed to pay the full amount on her next payday.
detail: One month's rent left and few sales. The prospect said no to buying, no to half down, no to a third down over three payments. Asked "what can you do?", she said nothing today, but the whole amount on the 1st when she got paid. Rent was due on the 5th, so he took $0 today and scheduled the full charge for the 1st; it went through and rent was paid. Lesson: ask what they can do and time the charge to their pay date.

### MM-23#02 | DEFINITION | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:08
claim: A payment-plan downsell sells the same thing at the same (or higher) total on different payment terms; it works at any price point but is a gamble on completion.
detail: It is not a cheaper product. It makes money when customers finish paying. It loses money when people cancel before you have profited, or when people who would have paid in full take the plan instead and then cancel early.

### MM-23#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:27
claim: People reject offers that cost too much today, not necessarily ones that cost too much overall — a "no" often calls for different terms, not a lower price.
detail: Salespeople and owners misread refusals as mispricing and reach for cheaper products. Usually the objection is the amount due right now. Payment plans get the most buyers to pay the highest price: less in the moment, full price or more over time.

### MM-23#04 | FRAMEWORK | topic: money-models | also: objections-closing, sales-process | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:27
claim: The seven-step payment-plan downsell is a fixed sequence; you move to the next step only when the customer says no to the current one.
detail: 1) Present the full payment-plan price, then reward paying in full with a discount. 2) Offer third-party financing, credit card or layaway. 3) Half now, half on their next payday. 4) Check they still want it (1–10 scale); below 8, exit to a feature downsell. 5) Split into three payments (paydays or monthly). 6) Spread payments evenly over the whole service term. 7) Free trial in exchange for card on file — trial with penalty (next lesson). Purpose: compress cash into the first 30 days for customer-financed acquisition. Recap at 00:16:10.

### MM-23#05 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: money-models, objections-closing | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:19
claim: Reward paying in full instead of punishing paying over time: anchor on the payment-plan price, then offer a prepay saving.
detail: You carry risk when you deliver before being paid, so the plan price is higher — but customers hate visible interest. Pitch the plan price first; when they hesitate, "Want to save some money? Prepay and it's X." If they can't manage it, "No problem, you don't have to pay it all up front" — the plan now looks like no added interest. The reverse (quote 10, then "can't afford it? pay me 12") feels punitive. A 10–20% markup rarely moves a buyer into a different purchase tier; a 2x gap would.
numbers: Example $10k paid in full vs $12k payment plan; typical plan markup 10–20%.

### MM-23#06 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: - | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:25
claim: Use the payment-plan anchor as a price test: if buyers keep accepting it without needing the prepay discount, nudge the price up until people start balking.
detail: The anchored plan price gives a soft market signal of whether a higher price point is sustainable, so you can raise it incrementally.

### MM-23#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:35
claim: Most times, adding third-party financing raises revenue by about 35% on average (Hormozi's figure) — typically worth the setup work.
detail: A finance company pays the business today and the customer repays the finance company over time, as car dealers do. Getting it set up takes effort but typically pays off.
numbers: +35% revenue on average from adding third-party financing.

### MM-23#08 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: money-models | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:35
claim: Credit-card reframe: ask whether they want you to decide the payment terms or decide them themselves — then point them to their credit card.
detail: Structure: (1) "Would you rather I decide your payment terms, or you decide?" (2) They choose themselves; reply that a credit card lets them pick whatever plan suits them. (3) If they ask for the alternative, explain the in-house plan means you set the dates, usually a much shorter timeframe than a card allows. Hormozi learned it from a master salesman; works especially in consumer sales.

### MM-23#09 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Layaway | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:08
claim: Layaway — the customer pays off the product before receiving it — is Hormozi's favourite plan: the customer carries the risk and is motivated to pay fast.
detail: Old-store model: pay every Monday for four Mondays, then take it home. Paying before receiving builds anticipation like saving for a vacation; paying after already getting the value breeds resentment with each charge. Terms can be as flexible as you like, anyone can be closed, and the incentives align: they want to finish paying to get the thing, whereas when value comes first they want to stretch payments. Only works where the start can be delayed (product or service).

### MM-23#10 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Layaway | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:47
claim: At Allen (software), agency owners who wanted to pay a $20,000 onboarding at $800 every other week paid it off within a month once told they could fly in only after paying.
detail: Allen sold enterprise white-label access with an in-person onboarding (in-person onboarding gave higher stick rates and feedback). Asked about payment plans, Hormozi said they could split it however they wanted; they proposed $800 biweekly and to fly out next week. Told they could come only once paid in full, they chose half now and half in a month. Hormozi still owns 25% of Allen.
numbers: $20,000 price; proposed $800 every two weeks; actual payoff ~1 month.

### MM-23#11 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: money-models | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:18
claim: Step 3: ask when they next get paid, offer half today and the rest then; if that fails, ask "what's the most you can put down today?"
detail: Consumer: "When's the next time you get paid?" B2B: "When's your next inflow of cash — any big contracts hitting?" Then: half down today, the rest on that date. If no: "What's the most you can put down today?" Take whatever they name now and the rest when they get paid (e.g., 25% now, 75% on payday). The aim is to pull cash forward into the first 30 days.

### MM-23#12 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:05
claim: Schedule payments on paycheck dates (typically every two weeks) rather than monthly — more cash lands in 30 days and fewer charges decline.
detail: Biweekly paycheck timing can fit three payments into 30 days (e.g., now, two weeks, four weeks). Billing when they get paid also cuts declines and insufficient-funds failures (00:15:24).

### MM-23#13 | DECISION_RULE | topic: objections-closing | also: money-models | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:52
claim: Step 4: when someone keeps saying no, check desire on a 1–10 scale — 8 or above, keep working payment plans; 7 or below, pivot to a different product.
detail: No payment plan satisfies someone who doesn't want the thing, so confirm desire before more effort. Say: "Money's tight, got it — on a scale of 1 to 10, how bad do you want to do this?" 8+: "Don't worry, we'll find something that works for you." 7 or below: "Why not a 10?", then "I think we may have something that's a better fit" — the exit ramp into feature downsells.

### MM-23#14 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics, verticals | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:39
claim: Steps 5–6: drop to a third down with two later payments, then spread payments evenly — but still front-load, since you already paid to acquire and onboard the customer.
detail: Step 5: one third now and one third on each of the next two paychecks, or over the next two months. Step 6: spread evenly — yet a six-month service can be paid over three months. Gym Launch (16-week programme) at its most spread charged weekly, 16 times. Its gym-owner buyers were often broke, so most deals ended at prepay, financing or the evenly spread plan.
numbers: Gym Launch 16 weekly charges for a 16-week programme.

### MM-23#15 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:34
claim: Map the full network of downsell steps first, then tighten it over time to the stop points your avatar most often says yes to.
detail: Start with the complete sequence, observe where customers actually convert, and simplify the script around those highest-likelihood stops. Step 7, the free trial, is normally the end of the ladder (you can hardly go lower than free).

### MM-23#16 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: money-models | fw: Seesaw Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:20
claim: Seesaw downsell: treat the monthly payment as what must be shrunk, so more money down today means smaller payments.
detail: Moves: (1) "Would you rather have giant monthly payments or tiny ones?" — "Tiny." (2) "Usually it costs X; if you prepay today, you get a large discount and no monthly payments — does that work?" (3) If not possible: "Totally get it; the more you put down now, the lower your monthly payments — let's tweak the down payment until the monthly figure suits you." (4) If still a no, confirm they still want it; if so, move your chair to their side of the table and go through the options together.

### MM-23#17 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: sales-team | fw: Seesaw Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:13:07
claim: In Hormozi's opinion, seesaw downselling suits small owner-operated businesses, while the fixed seven-step ladder scales better with a large sales team or finance department.
detail: Seesaw is flexible but produces odd custom plans that create back-end headaches. Fixed step-downs give a known set of plans recorded and tracked in the CRM, so they scale better.

### MM-23#18 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: pricing, retention-ltv | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:13:54
claim: Payment plans have a built-in upsell: keep the paid-in-full discount open for a set window (e.g., 30 days) and remind them to pay off the balance before it expires.
detail: After they decline the prepay and you have taken the card, while explaining next steps: "You may not fully trust us yet — start with a third down, and I'll honour the prepay discount for 30 days." At weeks two and three, and a week before expiry, offer to settle the balance with the discount. Once they know you and you've delivered, many take it. Works especially in B2B businesses that help clients make money.

### MM-23#19 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:15:24
claim: After adding payment plans, check that close rate rose and paid-in-full count did not fall — if PIFs drop, plans are cannibalising buyers who would have paid upfront.
detail: Payment plans only grow the business if they bring more customers and those customers actually pay. They can combine the reach of a discount with full-price profit, but only under those conditions.

### MM-23#20 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-team | also: money-models | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:16:58
claim: Pay salespeople commission on cash collected today, not on total contract value of payment plans.
detail: It demands more salesmanship and aligns the rep with the business goal of getting paid now. Whether plan payments keep coming depends mostly on onboarding and delivery, so reserve any extra commission for the back-end people who secure those payments.

### MM-23#21 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: canon | ts: 00:16:58
claim: In recurring-revenue businesses, quote the annual contract as the price and offer a discount for prepaying the year, then fall back to the monthly rate.
detail: Example: a $3,000/month service is presented as $36,000 a year, or $30,000 if prepaid; if not, monthly at $3,000. Recurring offers may need only two step-downs rather than seven, which suit upfront or fixed-term offers.
numbers: $3,000/month = $36,000/year list vs $30,000 prepaid.
