### MZ-13UIEi0Xz50#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: "Figured out why" is usually an accepted narrative; track observable behaviour instead.
detail: Behaviours predict your own and others' conduct; too many variables to isolate causes.

### MZ-13UIEi0Xz50#02 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:45
claim: After a relationship, list behaviours that changed and keep those that help.
detail: Ask which changes to keep, not why they happened.

### MZ-13UIEi0Xz50#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:57
claim: A tennis coach changing your grip would not spend the session on why you held it that way.
detail: Used to explain scepticism of therapy that digs for causes.

### MZ-13UIEi0Xz50#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:52
claim: To do goal-serving behaviours more, add more good stuff and less bad stuff.
detail: Past events matter only if usable as a lever.

### MZ-13UIEi0Xz50#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:43
claim: When the aim is changing behavior, Hormozi prefers to judge things by whether they raise or lower the likelihood of reaching a goal.
detail: The why is not always knowable, since you cannot relive the same circumstances; he doesn't decry exploring it if someone wants to spend their time that way.
