### POD-0131#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:33
claim: Profit is only the ticket to the next game: employee, self-employed, business owner, investor.
detail: He uses the four quadrants of wealth: the first two trade your own time for money, the third trades other people's time, the fourth trades money for more money. Making a profit does not mean you win; it buys a ticket to the next game, and he says someone at that stage is between step two and step three. He defines freedom for himself as control of time, location and spending, and says that with the fourth quadrant, money making money, you can actually be free.
### POD-0131#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:14
claim: Many owners aim to make money rather than to have it; once profit appears they do not know what to do next.
detail: After one or two profitable months gym owners ask what to do now, and his answer is to keep producing it. The goal needs to shift to holding capital so it can be invested, which is when the next quadrant opens.

### POD-0131#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:26
claim: Income stalls at the level of your felt requirements, so set a larger, unsatisfiable need such as giving.
detail: Parents and families out-earn singles because their requirements are higher. A single person can create an equivalent pull by committing to give to something bigger than themselves, which produces a demand for money that cannot be met. A business-side version is to tighten margins and renegotiate vendors so there is more to give.

### POD-0131#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:06
claim: Test for limiting beliefs by checking whether you can say "I want money" or "I want power" without discomfort.
detail: He predicts that owners moving to the new hybrid gym model will self-sabotage once money problems are solved. He treats money and power as neutral potential, and says condemning them is often an excuse to avoid trying. Pointing to an owner who declined a model offering about four times more profit and kept the high overhead, he calls that actively choosing less money.
numbers: Hybrid model claimed to remove about three quarters of gym risk and give up to 4x profit (his claim).
