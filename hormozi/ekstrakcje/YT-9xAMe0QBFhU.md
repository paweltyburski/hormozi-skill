### YT-9xAMe0QBFhU#01 | DEFINITION | topic: unit-economics | also: product-delivery | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: In a service business, cost of goods sold is the incremental cost of delivering one more unit, such as delivery payroll hours, excluding front desk, HR and rent.
detail: Gross margin is revenue minus that cost, divided by revenue. Net margin is what is left after everything. Gross margin creates net margin, so it is the number he fixes first.

### YT-9xAMe0QBFhU#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:42
claim: Lifting gross margin from 66% to 80% can turn a typical 12% net margin into 26%, about 2.2 times the take-home.
detail: Example: a $60-an-hour service with $20 cost has a gross margin of about 66%. Most brick-and-mortar businesses net around 12%. Adding 14 points at the gross level adds 14 points at the net level.
numbers: 66% to 80% gross; 12% to 26% net (2.2x).

### YT-9xAMe0QBFhU#03 | TACTIC | topic: unit-economics | also: pricing | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:07
claim: Two levers reach 80% gross margin: cut the cost of delivering, or raise the price.
detail: On a $60 price, cost must drop from $20 to $12 (leaving $48), or at a $20 cost the price must be $100.
numbers: $60 price at $12 cost = 80%; $20 cost needs $100 price.

### YT-9xAMe0QBFhU#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:34
claim: Each step of 80, 90, 95% gross margin halves delivery cost and doubles profit per dollar of delivery, letting you serve twice as many for the same cost.
detail: Largest businesses run near 99%. Low-priced founders lack room for marketing, overhead and profit; get over the mental barrier on charging.
numbers: 80% to 90%: cost $20 to $10; marketing budget example 20% of $100 = $16 left.
