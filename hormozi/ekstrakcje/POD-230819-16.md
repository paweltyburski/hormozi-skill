### POD-230819-16#08 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: Document Demonstrate Duplicate | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:01
claim: Build the first checklist by recording yourself doing the job, then test it by following only the checklist.
detail: Record yourself doing the task in several ways and across several shifts, and watch the recordings instead of pausing to take notes, or have trusted observers write down what you do. On your next work block follow only the written steps. If you still get an A-plus result, you have a first draft. Each time the trainee stops or slows down, revise the checklist again.
applies_to: Before training the first lead-getting employee.

### POD-230819-16#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: Document Demonstrate Duplicate | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:20
claim: If a trainee errs, needs a long explanation or repeated demonstrations, treat the checklist as broken.
detail: A step that needs explaining is too complicated, usually because several steps were packed into one. Forcing a weak checklist to work makes training a nightmare when someone else takes over the training. The test of clarity: could a stranger get your results by following the checklist alone?

### POD-230819-16#11 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: Document Demonstrate Duplicate | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:20
claim: Judge trainees on following directions rather than on the result, so a bad result points at the directions.
detail: If they are trained to follow directions, they will. When they follow them exactly and still get the wrong result, you know it's the directions, which you have a lot more control over. Still praise them for following the directions, then correct the checklist on the spot.
### POD-230819-16#12 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:30
claim: Give training feedback fast, one piece at a time, with praise and no penalties.
detail: Praise each step done right. When they goof, pause, step back and let them try again rather than taking over. No punishment of any kind for errors in training, since learning is punishing enough. Give a single piece of feedback and practise until it is right before moving to the next step, because fixing several things at once is hard for a beginner. Reward what you want repeated.

### POD-230819-16#16 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: sales-process | also: unit-economics, hiring-talent | fw: CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:15
claim: One question tells you whether to focus on advertising staff or sales staff when CAC is high.
detail: Ask whether the engaged leads actually have the problem you solve and the money to pay for it. If not, they are unqualified, which is an advertising problem. If yes and they buy but there are too few of them, it is also an advertising (volume) problem. If they are qualified but do not buy, it is a sales problem. Do not fire the sales person over an advertising problem, or advertising staff over a sales problem.
applies_to: CAC above roughly 3x the industry average.
