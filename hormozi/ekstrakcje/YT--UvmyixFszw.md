claim: If a service business cannot afford to pay $100-$200 to get someone in the door, it has a business problem, not a marketing problem.
detail: The cure is how much cash each new customer produces up front.
numbers: $100-$200 per acquired customer.

### YT--UvmyixFszw#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Money Model | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:38
claim: Cash-per-customer quadrant: (1) bolt-on product, (2) add-on services via a menu close, (3) prepay or financing, (4) continuity.
detail: It applies whatever the front end is: free, discounted low-barrier offer, or full value. In his math example, one $2,500 prepay among the customers lifts average cash per show from $500 to $1,000.
numbers: Example only: average rises from $500 to $1,000 per show.
### YT--UvmyixFszw#03 | TACTIC | topic: verticals | also: money-models | fw: Classic Upsell | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:38
claim: Pick the physical product that naturally goes with the service: supplements for gyms, orthotics, pillows or braces for chiropractors, masks and serums for beauty.
detail: A high-margin product sold on the first visit (about $100-$200) can cover the cost of acquiring the customer even if the service came free. A med-spa sequence adds a take-home kit, a menu of other treatments, a package, then continuity.
numbers: $100-$200 product per person.

### YT--UvmyixFszw#04 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:26
claim: Ask people to "prepay" (not "pay in full") with about 10% off, and get a financing company to carry larger packages.
detail: In healthcare, CareCredit-style financing can cover half or more of package purchases.
numbers: ~10% prepay discount.

### YT--UvmyixFszw#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:58
claim: One prepaid $2,500 sale among buyers averaging $500 lifts the average ticket from about $500 to about $1,000.
detail: In his example 10 walk in, 5 buy and one prepays. The exact average of 4 x $500 and $2,500 across 5 buyers is $900, so his figure is rounded.
numbers: $500 normal ticket; $2,500 prepay.

### YT--UvmyixFszw#06 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Money Model | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:04
claim: Worked build to $2,400 per person who walks in on a $19 offer: half buy a $2,000 package, product $200, two in five add $1,000, two in five join continuity at $200 a month.
detail: Running totals he gives: $1,000, then $1,200, $1,600, and $2,400 (continuity at $200 a month with 10% churn is $2,000 per member). His product step looks rough.
numbers: $19 front; 50% close; $2,000 package; $200 product; $1,000 add-on to 40%; continuity $200/month, 10% churn; $2,400 per show.

### YT--UvmyixFszw#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: unit-economics | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:58
claim: Revenue ceiling per month is roughly new customers a month times lifetime value per customer; a stalled business has too little back end or too little inflow.
detail: Example: 20 sales a month at $2,000 lifetime value caps at about $40,000 a month.
numbers: 20 x $2,000 = $40,000/month.

### YT--UvmyixFszw#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:33
claim: Take more cash at the start, because onboarding costs more than maintenance, then offer the same service recurring at a discount.
detail: This locks in recurring cash and extends lifetime value.
