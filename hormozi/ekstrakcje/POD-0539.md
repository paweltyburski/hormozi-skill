### POD-0539#02 | DEFINITION | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:10
claim: Acquisition.com model: minority stakes of roughly 20-33% in companies doing $3M-$100M a year, hands-on help in exchange for a valuation discount.
detail: Investments are either for cash flow or to reinvest and sell later. In more than half of deals no money goes in; the stake pays for the value he adds. His pitch to a founder: if you can grow 10x, is giving up a third worth it? Preference is to hold forever; a founder who wants to sell is first readied.
numbers: Portfolio of about 16 companies and about $200M a year combined; more than half of deals involve no cash invested; sale prep about 24 months plus about 12 months to go to market.

### POD-0539#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:03
claim: He does not endorse his portfolio companies; one reason is that brand is his biggest risk, and if he did, he would likely want majority or at least 50/50 control most times, though he'd be open to special cases like a massive company.
detail: An endorsement bets that nobody at the company does something really dumb. Also, if he drives a large share of a company's sales, it becomes more difficult to sell because a buyer must deal with him as well as the company. The goal is to make companies independently valuable, not dependent on him.

### POD-0539#06 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:12:57
claim: A buyer buys future profit, so value rises with sustainability and falls with risk: multiple acquisition sources, long-lasting customers and a unique mechanism raise it.
detail: Danger signs: highly transactional sales, founder-led with the founder as the face, a single acquisition source. Unique mechanism can be in acquisition, not only product. Brand is one example of a cushion against copycats, which is why Buffett talks about buying big brands.
### POD-0539#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:47
claim: Strategic buyers pay more than financial buyers, but deals are slower and tied to performance.
detail: A strategic buyer can cross-sell, so it earns more. Expect integration, a one- or two-year consulting-back period and earn-outs or kickers. Valuations also move with the cost of capital: cheaper borrowing lifts what companies sell for, and the same business sells differently in different years.

### POD-0539#08 | CASE | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:16:32
claim: Raising money makes sense when unit economics already work and cash only speeds up acquisition.
detail: Software at $100 a month, customers staying about 40 months, CAC $500: LTV $4,000, an 8:1 ratio, but about five months to recover CAC from gross margin. Bad reasons to raise: covering losses to cut prices artificially (a price-led market share can disappear if a cheaper rival appears).
numbers: $100/month x 40 months = $4,000 LTV; CAC $500; ratio 8:1; payback about 5 months.

### POD-0539#09 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:07
claim: Instead of selling equity, with a good LTV:CAC add an onboarding or sign-up fee or a front-end product so acquisition cost is recovered by day 1 or day 30, with a short revolving credit line as backup.
detail: His belief: constraint drives innovation, and asking the right questions solves growth profitably without dilution.
numbers: Target net zero on acquisition cost at day 1 or day 30.

### POD-0539#19 | DECISION_RULE | topic: offers | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:39:34
claim: In Hormozi's opinion, fake-guru problems come down to deception about legitimacy and about expectations; he says to be honest about your credentials and what you promise.
detail: College promises only a diploma if you get the grades, and he notes they fulfill that promise. A $1,000 course promising $10,000 in 30 days breeds haters when it is not met even if the content is excellent. He thinks that with truthful credentials and honest expectations you're completely in the clear.
numbers: Example: $1,000 course promises $10,000 in 30 days; college costs about $200,000 over 4 years.
