### MZ-rMCfjYM3LwA#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:59
claim: Whatever happens right after a behavior is what the person learns, whether or not you intended it.
detail: When a bad mood stopped only after he turned stern, he learned that being mean works. Reward given late teaches the link to whatever just happened. Ask what the person associates with the reward.

### MZ-rMCfjYM3LwA#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:51
claim: Punishing a teenager on return from sneaking out teaches the teenager not to come home.
detail: The punishment lands on coming back, not on leaving, so the unwanted outcome is the avoidance of home.

### MZ-rMCfjYM3LwA#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:10
claim: Punishment teaches avoiding that person or situation, not dropping the behavior; the lesson is highly specific.
detail: If someone cuts you off or calls you stupid, you learn not to speak in front of them. Only after punishment in many contexts does the lesson generalize.

### MZ-rMCfjYM3LwA#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:50
claim: Punishment makes people look for many workarounds, while reward focuses effort on one behavior.
detail: Punished people still want what they were chasing and invent other routes. With a carrot everyone goes for the carrot; hit it and it could do a hundred things. Knowing the game does not weaken it, it speeds understanding.

### MZ-rMCfjYM3LwA#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:10
claim: Reward and punishment from the same source sit on separate scales and do not cancel out.
detail: A parent who gave both great memories and terrible ones causes a pull toward the good and fear of the bad, felt at full strength together, not as zero.

### MZ-rMCfjYM3LwA#06 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:05
claim: Reward desired behavior right after it happens, and audit what you accidentally punish.
detail: Look for side effects of your presence that punish the other person, then stop them. Call it encouragement if "training" sounds taboo. Explain behavior by what follows it, not by who someone is.
