### POD-0403#01 | CASE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:15:03
claim: Alex and Layla split the CEO role: he owns product, vision and the external face, she leads people and the internal team, and her sister as COO owns process.
detail: They share the CEO title and each works part of what a CEO normally does. Alex handled marketing and the face of the company; Layla began in operations but, being a people person, hired someone who knew systems. They map the roles to a people, process, product model from another author's book: Layla people, the COO process, Alex product.
applies_to: Founder pairs at Gym Launch stage (2018).

### POD-0403#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:45
claim: When there is more work than one person can dent, stop doing it yourself and delegate or teach it.
detail: They stopped working after dinner once the workload exceeded what any single person could clear. Work is never finished, so the fix is to delegate and teach skills, not to extend hours. Cited as comparison: a very large company founder does not run his companies himself.

### POD-0403#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:03
claim: Similar partners can still divide a business by role: pick the strength you both share (vision) and hire for the gaps rather than hunt for an opposite.
detail: Though alike, they split roles over time because sharing the same tasks did not work. Layla hired people for operations rather than learning it herself.
