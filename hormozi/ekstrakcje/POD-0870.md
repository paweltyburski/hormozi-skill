### POD-0870#08 | CASE | topic: sales-team | also: hiring-talent | fw: Try Before You Buy | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:53

### POD-0870#09 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: hiring-talent | fw: Try Before You Buy | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:38
claim: Hire BDRs as 30-day contractors who must hit KPIs, or pay full base with a 30-day proof window.
detail: Risk is small: someone paid $50K a year costs about $1,000 a week, so two weeks of a bad hire costs $2,000. Pipeline typically builds in 14-30 days for transactional sales. Accept a margin hit to attract better people, as good BDRs expect some base while they build pipeline.
numbers: $50K/year = about $1,000/week; 2 weeks wasted = about $2,000; pipeline in 14-30 days.

### POD-0870#12 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: Activation Points | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:34
claim: Find activation actions by M12 retention: take customers who stayed 12 months, look backwards at actions and demographics, rank by correlation.
detail: Illustrative: getting the site live makes month-12 retention 4x likelier, adding a custom URL 8x. Then redesign onboarding to raise the odds of each step and cut friction. This is a data and engineering task before it is a money one; if tracking is missing, install it. Growth in SaaS looks like many signups with a stable group who never leave.
numbers: Example multipliers: site live 4x, custom URL 8x (illustrative).

### POD-0870#13 | DECISION_RULE | topic: retention-ltv | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:23:41
claim: To reduce churn from "I did not make money", sell the product as a tool for building something, not as a money promise.
detail: If you promise income, users who earn nothing believe the software failed. Framing as a way to build a community (even a free one with 20 friends) counts that as success.

### POD-0870#16 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:34:00
claim: Barbell investing: put cash into your own business first, and only the surplus into truly passive assets.
detail: Highest return is reinvesting in what you control, including things like buying the neighbouring building for the team. Whatever is left goes somewhere needing zero of your time; if it cannot be forgotten for a year or two it is not passive, and expect a lower return there.
