### POD-0995#01 | DEFINITION | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Culture is the set of rules, mostly unspoken, that govern what gets rewarded and what gets punished in a business.
detail: People learn the rules by experiencing consequences first-hand and observing them second-hand. If a new hire sees lateness ignored, that is the rule; if another is scolded, the rule differs by boss. Systemic behaviour that follows is what gets called culture.

### POD-0995#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:44
claim: Build culture by modelling the behaviour you want, rewarding it, and punishing its opposite, then cascade the same through leaders because it happens in teams you do not see.
detail: The owner acts as chief reinforcement officer. Reinforcement happens all the time, which is why leaders must repeat the same behaviour down the chain.

### POD-0995#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: A Players | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:44
claim: Who you fire is the strongest culture signal, stronger than promotions, and the bar is set by the worst person you tolerate.
detail: Raise the minimum bar within each function, not demand executive-level skill from frontline staff. Teams are glad when mediocre people leave, and slow firing discourages winners.

### POD-0995#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:31
claim: The real drag on a business is the mediocre middle, not the obvious failures, because they are slow and their work gets redone by winners.
detail: True losers get removed because they hurt; clock-in, clock-out staff persist. Create rewards for managers who let mediocre people go and lower tolerance: give a few chances, not nine.

### POD-0995#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: sales-team | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:12
claim: In his own experience, Hormozi says he knows within 14 days (seven, though he says 14 is generous) whether a salesperson who has been trained is exceptional.
detail: In a ten-person team where four are petering along, he says that if you want only A players, you have to get rid of the B players. If training is in place, you can see the output.

### POD-0995#08 | EVOLUTION | topic: hiring-talent | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:48
claim: The biggest change in 15 years is who he counts as good enough; as you win, standards must rise and people must grow or leave.
detail: Early on he could not attract better talent. Ask whether team quality has improved, then pull the standard you will have in five years into the present. He says the wealthy entrepreneurs he knows show growth correlated with how little mediocrity they tolerate.
### POD-0995#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:56
claim: Removing the weakest member lifts the team's quality more than adding another star.
detail: With three people at 140 IQ and one at 80, removing the 80 beats hiring a fourth 140. Winners are demotivated by working with losers.
numbers: example: three at IQ 140 plus one at 80.

### POD-0995#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:41
claim: Choose global empathy over local empathy: take the pain of letting one person go so the rest avoid it.
detail: Keeping someone who lacks the horsepower is a disservice to everyone else. Shrink the delay between knowing what to do and doing it; top entrepreneurs act almost at once. Treat the person with respect, but make the call.
