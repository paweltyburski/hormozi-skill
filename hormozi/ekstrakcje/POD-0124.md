### POD-0124#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:13
claim: Being copied has never reduced his revenue, and copying is a sign that you are worth copying.
detail: He says the more people copied his company, the more it earned, and the biggest copying came when it was earning most. The imitators took roughly two years to copy something that did not require innovation.
numbers: About 2 years for copycats to catch up.

### POD-0124#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:20
claim: For most owners, suing copycats is usually not worth it: it eats time and attention that would earn a higher return in the business, and he says the business will be worse off nine times out of ten.
detail: He sued once; it took a year and the attention cost outweighed the result. His firm uses lawyers for compliance and attacks when needed, but for most owners he says it will hurt the business nine times out of ten.
numbers: One lawsuit lasted about 1 year; "nine times out of ten" business worse off (his claim).
### POD-0124#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:53
claim: Your bottleneck is inside the business (you, infrastructure, capacity to serve), not a competitor stealing customers.
detail: If all the copycats' clients came to him, it would overwhelm his team. Growth limits are leadership, detail management and consistency.

### POD-0124#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:32
claim: Copiers lack the skill to create, so they usually fail and vanish.
detail: Customer service receives about two messages a week from new competitors claiming to have reinvented the wheel; none are heard from again after about two months.
numbers: About 2 inbound claims per week; gone after about 2 months.
