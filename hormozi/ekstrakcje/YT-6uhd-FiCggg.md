### YT-6uhd-FiCggg#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Passion usually appears after skill, so "follow your passion" gets cause and effect backwards.
detail: Successful people who say it are describing their present state, like telling someone to fly private in order to get rich. Those who seem born with a passion found the activity early: a chance encounter, some deliberate practice, praise from others, a skill identity, then passion. Others, himself included, must try many things to let one take root.

### YT-6uhd-FiCggg#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:00
claim: Skills are easier to compare than preferences, so choose a path by what you are better at and start a feedback loop.
detail: Pizza versus tacos is a coin flip; English versus maths is an answerable question. Bill Gates tried law, mathematics and programming before settling. The real goal of "developing passion" is to begin a feedback loop through action, which Hormozi lacked before starting (he drifted between frozen yoghurt, fitness and test prep).

### YT-6uhd-FiCggg#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: verticals | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:03
claim: Loving the subject is a small part of running a business in it.
detail: Many gym owners adore fitness yet stay broke, because understanding fitness is only a sliver of the fitness business; the same holds for loving cooking and opening a restaurant. The passion has little to do with monetising the skill. Buffett's "circle of competence" is the safer anchor: focus on what you understand best.
numbers: understanding the craft is about 5% of the business (his rough figure).

### YT-6uhd-FiCggg#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:35
claim: Deliberate practice is, by the research he cites, not enjoyable, and skill is built in the dull middle.
detail: He liked ping pong but declined the drills of hundreds of repeated forehands and backhands needed to go from hobby to elite. Repeating a sales script or the same shot is tedious iteration on feedback, like sanding with finer paper each pass. Most people cheer a start and the finish, but nobody watches the long middle. His line: real work starts when the excitement ends.

### YT-6uhd-FiCggg#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: sales-process | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:48
claim: He applied the Rule of 100 to a launch presentation by running it about 90 times before going live.
detail: Because a full run is long, he did not do 100 a day but three full runs daily, watched each recording and fixed stumbles or weak visuals. He notes that practice becomes motivating once you associate it with winning.
numbers: about 90 rehearsals; 3 runs a day; 90 minutes each, so about 4.5 hours of deliberate practice per day.

### YT-6uhd-FiCggg#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:19
claim: Even good work is mostly overhead, so expect only a fraction of your day to be enjoyable.
detail: Citing Bezos ("everything is overhead"), acting careers contain more waiting, rejection and administration than creative work. If roughly 30% of the day is work you love and the rest enables it, you are doing well. He also says passion has to be built over time spent not enjoying it, since enjoyment comes with competence.
numbers: 10-30% of the day enjoyable is a good outcome (his range).

### YT-6uhd-FiCggg#07 | EXAMPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:05
claim: How you approach the work changes the work: a waiter who got a "do better" note improved his service and his tips.
detail: Two stories: a Japanese floor sweeper who imagined being paid a million dollars a day to sweep, and a waiter who received a $0 tip with "do better", then learned names and smiled, after which tips and opportunities rose. The three stonecutters parable (cutting stone, earning a living, building a cathedral) shows the same job as chore, career or calling depending on purpose.

### YT-6uhd-FiCggg#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:13
claim: Popular passions are supply-crowded, so keep them separate from the way you earn.
detail: If many people love singing, acting or painting, supply is large and income hard. A mentor asked what stops you from painting when you want and earning when you want. Gym Launch's early mission, in his words, was to stop being broke after losing everything twice; the larger industry mission came later.

### YT-6uhd-FiCggg#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:51
claim: Work satisfaction depends on pay, coworkers, recognition, security and purpose, and company often matters more than the task.
detail: Passion misses these. Two comparisons: a job you like doing but with bad people and a product you dislike is painful, while a job with one disliked component but everything else good is more fulfilling. He quotes the mentor line (destination in your 20s, journey in your 30s, company in your 40s) and warns against demanding that life be fulfilling, which creates a gap you cannot close.

### YT-6uhd-FiCggg#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:54
claim: Do not build everything around one passion because both you and the market will change.
detail: Interests differ at 12, 22, 32 and 52, and the world values different things (the MySpace example). Committing to one passion forever is too heavy a decision; shrink it to "interested for now" to make the first step easy. This sits beside his rule of committing to the deliberate process, not to one fixed interest.

### YT-6uhd-FiCggg#11 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:16:21
claim: Serving others raises your pain tolerance, so tie your work to a purpose beyond yourself.
detail: He calls this the martyrdom effect and describes an electric-shock study in which people tolerate much more when told a loved one would be spared (he cannot recall the source). He says he endures more for his wife than for himself, and that struggling with motivation often means being motivated only for yourself.
numbers: tolerance said to rise roughly tenfold in the study as he recalls it (source unclear).

### YT-6uhd-FiCggg#12 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:19:40
claim: Replace "follow your passion" with nine steps: curiosity, learning, existing strengths, commitment, realistic expectations, money, environment, evolving interests, serving others.
detail: Start with mild curiosity and build competence first, since mastery precedes passion. Build on natural advantages. Burn the boats on the process. Expect tedious parts. Treat money seriously; when two paths are about even, follow the higher income. Change the environment to one full of people pursuing the same thing, which supplies feedback. Allow interests to change. Link work to others.

### YT-6uhd-FiCggg#13 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:02
claim: Money keeps adding happiness if you learn to spend it for intangible returns.
detail: His theory is that spending is a skill; people stop getting happier at some level because they never learn to spend well, not because of a ceiling. Money cannot buy happiness but helps avoid pain.
