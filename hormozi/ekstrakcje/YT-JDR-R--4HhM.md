### YT-JDR-R--4HhM#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: paid-ads | also: content-brand | fw: Seven Headline Characteristics | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: The headline is the biggest lever on ad results because a click-through lift multiplies how many people see everything after it.
detail: Quoting Ogilvy, once the headline is written, 80 cents of the advertising dollar is spent. Lifting click-through from 1% to 3% triples the people who see the rest. Sometimes split tests give 20-50% lifts, which he calls a monster test, while a killer headline can outperform the control by 2, 3, sometimes 5x. The best advertisers obsess over the hook; with a winning hook they keep using it and try to beat it.
numbers: CTR 1% to 3% equals 3x; killer headline can beat control by 2-3x, sometimes 5x; 20-50% lifts are sometimes seen and count as a monster split test.
### YT-JDR-R--4HhM#02 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: content-brand | fw: Seven Headline Characteristics | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:39
claim: Headline notes: curiosity is king, different is ideal, never run an ad without one; it is only new wrapping for the same offer.
detail: Every ad has a headline, good or bad. To know what works beforehand, study the first three seconds (visual and spoken) of organic content and others' ads, and borrow from other industries such as travel or weight loss.

### YT-JDR-R--4HhM#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:52
claim: Say what only you can say: competitors can copy your offer but not your proof.
detail: He states his group spends tens of millions a year on ads in its own portfolio companies, not clients'. You choose the pond in which you are one of a kind (only triple black belt locally, only coach for vegan powerlifter mothers). As AI spreads, verified real stories matter more.
numbers: Ad spend: tens of millions of dollars per year across portfolio companies.

### YT-JDR-R--4HhM#05 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: sales-process | fw: Call-Outs (Labels, Yes Questions, If-Then, Ridiculous Results) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:17
claim: Call out who you are not looking for too; the exclusion is stronger than the inclusion.
detail: For example, "under $250,000, $500,000 or $1M a year, this isn't for you, use my free content", or "only if you have over 30 pounds to lose". Narrowing still leaves enough buyers (a few hundred customers can suffice), polarises the right ones, and the ad speaks to one person.

### YT-JDR-R--4HhM#06 | FRAMEWORK | topic: paid-ads | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:06:12
claim: Match the hook to awareness level: unaware needs curiosity, then problem, solution, product and fully aware (make an offer).
detail: Problem aware: "Do you get up at night to pee more than twice?" Solution aware: "tried supplements that did not deliver?" Product aware: compares types or agencies. Most aware already know you, so go straight to the offer.

### YT-JDR-R--4HhM#07 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:23
claim: Replace marketing-speak labels with the concrete moments people live through.
detail: "Are you overweight" becomes trouble climbing stairs or a rash while walking. People remember moments, not vague words, and a moment triggers emotion and lowers the action threshold. Everyone feels like the centre of their own universe, so specific wording feels personal.

### YT-JDR-R--4HhM#08 | CASE | topic: paid-ads | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:31
claim: A bar-consulting guest used generic copy; the fix was to name the moment of looking behind the bar and seeing chaos.
detail: Something like "does it look like a bomb went off behind your bar" lands because the owner likely saw it in the last seven days. Fresh pain makes the next action likely.
numbers: Pain recency: within the last 7 days.

### YT-JDR-R--4HhM#09 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: promotions-launches | fw: Fraternity Party Planner | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:32
claim: Any reason works: the word "because" lifts compliance even with an irrelevant reason.
detail: In the line-cutting study (Cialdini), people let others ahead even with an unrelated reason. Examples: the dog's birth month, a business anniversary, last month's birthday. Bonus if the reason ties to urgency or scarcity.

### YT-JDR-R--4HhM#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:32
claim: Every line of copy should sell only the next line, and the headline of each page only the next step.
detail: Chain: line sells line, lines sell the click, the page headline sells the opt-in. People cannot take nine steps. Confusion stops both clicks and purchases.

### YT-JDR-R--4HhM#11 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:12:13
claim: Damaging admissions: state your flaws up front and you gain trust, because trust is worth more than promise.
detail: Research shows people believe someone more after he admits his bias. Referrals need little promise since trust is borrowed. Eminem's battle strategy: list your own flaws so rivals have nothing left.

### YT-JDR-R--4HhM#12 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:50
claim: Put the damaging statement first and the benefit after "but", because the second half is what is believed.
detail: "These markers smell terrible, but they write four times longer" persuades; reversed it stresses the smell. Same for "our websites are ugly but they convert".

### YT-JDR-R--4HhM#13 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:31
claim: List everything customers complain about, claim it first, and add humour if possible.
detail: The prospect is already thinking it. Example: a gym admitting poor parking and flaky air-conditioning but the most fun sessions. Plain claims like "most fun" are unbelievable.

### YT-JDR-R--4HhM#14 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:38

detail: Instead of "get more sales", picture running out of order forms as customers read cards too fast, a gym full with a line around the corner. Describing future problems proves you have seen them.

### YT-JDR-R--4HhM#15 | EXAMPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: sales-process | fw: What-Who-When | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:18:17

### YT-JDR-R--4HhM#16 | DECISION_RULE | topic: offers | also: promotions-launches | fw: Scarcity | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:08
claim: Keep urgency and scarcity real and refuse sales after the deadline or the cap.
detail: Saying no keeps reputation, so the unlucky buyer is first in line next time ("dig the well before you're thirsty"). He prefers to undersell demand; otherwise you keep chasing the next sale and everyone knows you will not sell out, so the scarcity loses power. Scarcity is quantity, urgency is time (offers book pp. 104, 112).

detail: Examples: helped 5,000-6,000 gyms in seven years; in business 40 years; combined 20 years across founders; a local "best of" list used as a conversation opener even if few know it.
numbers: 5,000-6,000 gyms in 7 years (his companies).

### YT-JDR-R--4HhM#19 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:25:20

detail: Click the button, enter your information, pick a time. When the stated outcome follows the instruction, say-do correspondence builds influence (the Martha Stewart recipe effect). He named his company acquisition.com so the name itself is the call to action.
