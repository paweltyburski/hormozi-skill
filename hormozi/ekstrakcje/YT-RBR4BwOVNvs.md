### YT-RBR4BwOVNvs#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:21:12
claim: A single unifying principle turns recurring choices into a decision algorithm, which saves mental bandwidth for new decisions.
detail: His principle for buying things is function over form, meaning net utility to his life. Entrepreneurs make thousands of decisions a day; writing "if this, then that" rules for repeating ones means they are never remade. He plans a separate video on his larger list of such algorithms.

### YT-RBR4BwOVNvs#02 | EVOLUTION | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: updates | ts: 00:08:02
claim: Extreme minimalism did not work for him either; he thinks the ideal is a few well-stocked home bases, not owning everything or nothing.
detail: After about nine months of renting high-end places while living out of bags, he missed familiar people, his own set-up and stable social circles. He thinks the ideal is two to three, maybe four primary homes stocked with his things, so he can pick up and leave while keeping a home base.
numbers: Rentals cost roughly $50,000-$100,000 a month; he suggests two to three, maybe four primary homes.
### YT-RBR4BwOVNvs#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:48
claim: He works in 3-5 year "seasons" and treats each shift of focus as a new season, here moving from operating to investing in service and e-learning businesses.
detail: Past seasons: college and consulting, running gyms, licensing gyms and building three bigger companies. The current season is full-time investing in service-based or e-learning businesses with about $1M-$10M profit a year. Material things had begun to weigh on his attention before the sale.
numbers: Target investee profit $1M-$10M a year; portfolio about $85M annual revenue (as stated).

### YT-RBR4BwOVNvs#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:24
claim: In his own view, spending is best judged relative to income, and private flights make sense to him because they turn travel from a negative into a positive experience.
detail: His example is private flights: using a hypothetical $10M a year, about 200 times a $50,000 average family income, a $10,000 item costs the equivalent of $50, and the flight removes recovery time, document prep and wasted days, so he is more likely to travel. Status purchases did not pay for him: a new Bentley brought better service but felt tainted and he judged it not worth the money.
numbers: $10M a year (his hypothetical) is about 200x a $50,000 average family income; $10,000 relative cost is about $50.
