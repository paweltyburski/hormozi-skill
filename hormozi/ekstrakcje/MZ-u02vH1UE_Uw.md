### MZ-u02vH1UE_Uw#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: When gym trainers confronted him for raises, he pointed out that owners are paid for risk employees never take.
detail: He had rewarded no one and was rarely on site. His reply: staff bank accounts only go up; his can fall, including a year with no pay. The business could not afford raises then.

### MZ-u02vH1UE_Uw#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: unit-economics | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Small changes in revenue can swing a thin-margin business into loss: in his example, seven $10K-a-month clients at 20% margin lose money after two leave.
detail: Revenue falls from $70K to $50K while costs stay near $56K, so the owner works full time at a loss of about $7K a month by his figure. In his example of a $1M-profit year, he says the financials his team looks at show about four negative months a year.
numbers: 7 clients x $10K = $70K per month; after two leave $50K; 20% margin; in his example, about 4 negative months per year in a $5M revenue, $1M profit business.
### MZ-u02vH1UE_Uw#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:59
claim: Owners feel secure only with months of costs in the bank, and with a 10% margin 90% of revenue is committed cost every month.
detail: A $5M business has about $400K monthly revenue and roughly $320K of cost before first profit, so three months of $80K profit do not cover one month of no sales. This is why raises must be weighed against volatility.
numbers: $5M revenue; about $400K per month; $320K monthly cost; $80K monthly profit.
