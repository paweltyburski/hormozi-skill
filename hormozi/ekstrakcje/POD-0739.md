### POD-0739#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: If you like a potential partner but cannot agree on the deal structure, do not partner.
detail: A partnership needs both the right person and the right structure. Conceding on structure to make it work causes problems later. Only partner with someone who values your contribution as you do and whose contribution you value as they do. Disagreeing on value is fine; just walk away from the deal.

### POD-0739#02 | DEFINITION | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:57
claim: A bad partner is one who deceives you to take your share; wanting a bigger share openly is normal.
detail: He separates a bad structure with decent people (his earlier partners) from a bad person. Wanting more of the pie is fine; deceit is the line.

### POD-0739#03 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:03
claim: After selling five of six gyms, he partnered with a gym owner on new locations and lost his proceeds when the partner took half the cash.
detail: He had sold the gyms to employees, customers and a competitor, using cash, seller-financed and debt deals. A gym owner offered to staff each location after Hormozi launched it, but terms shifted: Hormozi guaranteed the lease, fronted the capital and ran everything. He launched with 370 new members, put all the cash in the new account, and found it emptied by a withdrawal to the partner, who called it his half. The partner then wired money abroad and filed for bankruptcy.
numbers: 370 new members in about 6 weeks; 5 gyms sold in 90-120 days.

### POD-0739#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: verticals | also: unit-economics | fw: Sales to Fulfillment Continuum | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:03
claim: His earlier solo model was a cash-light fill-up service with no employees and no fulfilment.
detail: He flew to a gym, ran a launch and sold twelve hours a day; costs were only airfare, hotel and ad spend. The average gym owner earns far less for far more work.
numbers: About $100,000 of sales in roughly 3 weeks with $1,000-$3,000 of ad spend; average gym owner about $36,000 per year at roughly 80 hours a week.

### POD-0739#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:43
claim: Red flags are real: when someone shows you who they are, believe them the first time.
detail: Flags he ignored: a prior fraud indictment dismissed as a misunderstanding, a personal guarantee demanded of him, capital fronted by him, and the partner not delivering his promised part (running the gyms). He no longer gives benefit of the doubt, because many other partners are available.

### POD-0739#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Many-Sided Die | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:50
claim: Entrepreneurship means leaving money on the table; saying no lets you grow the one pile you chose.
detail: Before the loss he had a simple model with no employees and no fulfilment, selling twelve hours a day. A gym owner pointed out the uncollected money in owning locations, and his greed and wish to say he had 12 gyms led him astray. The average gym owner earns far less for far more work.
numbers: Average gym owner about $36,000 per year at roughly 80 hours a week, versus $100,000 in 21 days for him.

### POD-0739#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:09:46
claim: When things are hard you must decide between push (continue) and pivot (your assumptions were wrong), and doing neither is the worst choice.
detail: There is no formula; both errors have victims (the gold miner who quit just before a find; the person who stayed nine years on a losing idea). You cannot replay the game, so use judgment.

### POD-0739#08 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:42
claim: He coasted for years in a defense-consulting job after learning what he could in six to nine months; the right response was to ask for challenge or quit.
detail: His boss eventually wanted him gone. He read business books all day. Advice: if you feel deadened and clock-watching, leave. If unsure, work at a small business of about ten employees to see all departments and be paid to learn; real-time feedback beats books.
numbers: Learning plateau after 6 to 9 months; target employer about 10 staff.
