### POD-0820#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: management-ops | fw: Impact vs Investment Matrix (RICE) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:49
claim: Every proposed action should be tied to one of three goals: more customers, more lifetime gross profit per customer, or less risk to those two continuing.
detail: Teach the filter to your team. When someone proposes using limited resources, ask which of the three it moves; if they cannot draw a clear line, you should probably not do it. This is how he sets priorities in quarterly meetings and annual planning.
### POD-0820#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: management-ops | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Impact vs Investment Matrix (RICE) | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:43
claim: Evaluating a website redesign: ask the cost, time, people and expected lift, then ask whether the same resources could do better elsewhere.
detail: Hypothetical: redesign costs $25,000, takes eight weeks, uses two people full time, expected lift about 10% in opt-in rate. If anything else for that money and time returns more than 10%, drop the redesign. The question is not whether an idea works but how much. The best entrepreneurs get the highest return per unit put in.
numbers: $25,000; 8 weeks; 2 people; +10% opt-in rate.

### POD-0820#11 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: Revenue Churn | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:03

### POD-0820#13 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Big Head Long Tail | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:26:24
claim: Put the ascension inside the first sale: take a large first payment and monthly payments right after, rather than relying on a second sale.
detail: A second sale for ascension loses a conversion percentage. Example: roughly $3,500 up front and $500 a month afterwards (about $5,000 over four payments) instead of a $5,000 front-end plus an upsell. His highest-ticket programme following this shape showed 95% year-on-year retention, partly because after $3,500 a $600 monthly fee seems small.
numbers: $3,500 then $500 x 3; 95% retention year over year at the top programme.

### POD-0820#20 | SCRIPT | topic: pricing | also: promotions-launches | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:39:22

### POD-0820#22 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:40:09
claim: A business that has not raised prices in four years is making 30-40% less profit per unit.
detail: Costs inflate, so a frozen price shrinks margin on every unit. Fear of rejection makes pricing dogmatic; if a change fails, change it back the next week, so testing costs less than standing still. Raising price is the easiest operational move and should be exhausted before others.
numbers: 4 years without a price rise means 30-40% less profit per unit.
