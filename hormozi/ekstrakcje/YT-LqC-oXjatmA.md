### YT-LqC-oXjatmA#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: - | fw: Four Market Variables | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Pick the market (not shrinking) where you can provide the most value given your own skills, and use the four-variable checklist when you lack that edge.
detail: Asked to choose between dentists, hearing-aid specialists and pharmacists, his first rule of thumb is whether each market is not dying (normal or growing), then where the person can add most value. If you have experience, especially personal experience, he would usually recommend that market first, because you know their pains and build a better product. Without experience, these are the four things he looks for.
### YT-LqC-oXjatmA#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: - | fw: Four Market Variables | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:48
claim: Keep the same service but move it to a better market: relationship coaching for seniors beats coaching for college students.
detail: The core promise (help people find love) stays the same. Seniors feel strong pain from being alone, the group keeps growing as people age, they are easy to reach and have more money than college students, so all four variables are met.

### YT-LqC-oXjatmA#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Four Market Variables | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:59
claim: A market that is hard to find fails even if it is rich: ads aimed at plastic surgeons that mostly reach nurses cannot deliver the offer.
detail: Prefer markets with associations, groups or channels where members congregate, so you can target them cheaply. If you cannot find prospects, they cannot find you.

### YT-LqC-oXjatmA#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:47
claim: When investing in a company he checks first whether the founder has experience in the market, then whether the problem meets the four market variables.
detail: Only then does he call it a good opportunity. This mirrors his advice to pick a market the person already knows they can serve.
