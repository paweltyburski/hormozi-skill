### POD-0755#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:34
claim: The pay you offer mainly shortens how long it takes to find someone; it does not by itself get you a better person.
detail: At $40,000 a year a perfect person exists, but you may need 100 times the interviews to find one who accepts. Raising pay cuts interview volume. Early on you overcompensate with effort (more candidates), but you never lower the bar. He started by hiring anyone with a pulse and later saw that higher-cost people are often, not always, better.

### POD-0755#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: A Players | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:33
claim: Always hire for the smallest skill deficit: soft skills first for junior roles, hard skills first for senior ones.
detail: Attitude and kindness are bundles of small behaviours, but teaching them takes longer than teaching a task like working leads. So for lower-level roles hire the soft skills and train the few hard skills; as roles become senior, hire the hard skills and train the soft. He sees this as a dichotomy to manage.

### POD-0755#04 | EVOLUTION | topic: hiring-talent | also: - | fw: A Players | novelty: updates | ts: 00:06:29
claim: A second layer on top of skills: intelligence is the rate of skill acquisition, so he leans toward raw intelligence plus work ethic over experience, a shift he describes as large.
detail: This revises his earlier habit of buying been-there-done-that experience. Big technology companies recruit raw talent; skills are the output of intelligence, so a fast learner gives a higher return on each dollar invested. His claim: experience is good, but intelligence is better.
### POD-0755#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: - | fw: A Players | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:57
claim: A zero-skill fast learner overtakes a five-skill slower hire in roughly six or seven months.
detail: Role needs 10 skills. Person A has 5 and learns one per quarter, so reaches proficiency in about 15 months. Person B has 0 but learns one per month. Around month 6 they are near level (B about 6 skills, A about 7), and B is ahead afterwards and can also move up by learning the next job faster.
numbers: Person A: 5 of 10 skills, +1 per quarter (about 15 months to full). Person B: 0 of 10, +1 per month (about 10 months).

### POD-0755#06 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:41
claim: To move up, start doing the job above yours until the title change is a formality.
detail: He and his partner advocate proving the next-level work before asking for the promotion, rather than asking first and proving later.

### POD-0755#07 | DEFINITION | topic: hiring-talent | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:10:26
claim: Experience means provable accomplishments you personally produced, not years in a title (tenure).
detail: Someone can have a lot of experience in a short time. Hormozi cares zero about tenure, and values experience only for how fast it gets the desired result in the role. Example from his team: they have personally scaled software, physical products, brick and mortar, and national services.

### POD-0755#08 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: pricing | fw: A Players | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:26
claim: Hormozi says he now cares far more about ability to learn, and he models big companies that pay intelligent people with no experience more to start.
detail: Reason: few employers screen for this except the largest firms, so such people can be below market, and Facebook or Google style recruiting shows the pattern. He has seen better returns from able, less technically experienced hires. Hard part: measuring intelligence at screening.
### POD-0755#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:56
claim: Most professional skills are learned quickly, so train a smart hire your way instead of hiring someone who pastes in what worked elsewhere.
detail: Cold calling can be learned in about three days; adding qualification and closing takes a few months. Experienced hires who copied their old playbook into his business, he found within a year or two, built something he would not have built from zero.
