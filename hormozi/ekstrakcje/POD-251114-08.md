### POD-251114-08#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: unit-economics | also: - | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Three levers drive CFA: more customers (lower CAC), more value per customer (higher LTGP), and speed (shorter payback period).
detail: Growth means getting more customers and making each worth more, and he adds speed as a third variable because how fast the business grows matters. Lower CAC and higher gross profit shorten the time to break even.

### POD-251114-08#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: - | fw: CAC | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:48
claim: CAC example: a $10,000 monthly content producer who brings 10 new customers gives a CAC of $1,000.
detail: CAC includes spend on advertising, sales and their supporting activities, divided by customers gained. How much you can spend depends on what each customer is worth.
numbers: $10,000 per month / 10 customers = $1,000 CAC.

### POD-251114-08#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: product-delivery | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:01
claim: Gross profit examples: a $100 widget costing $20 yields $80; ten $1,000 service packages with a $2,000 employee yield $800 each.
detail: Gross profit is price minus fulfilment cost. For the service, $10,000 sold minus $2,000 of cost leaves $8,000, spread over 10 customers.
numbers: Product: $100 - $20 = $80. Service: $10,000 - $2,000 = $8,000, or $800 per customer.

### POD-251114-08#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Payback Period | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:50
claim: A customer who pays back in one day lets you buy a new one daily; at 30 days you can reinvest once a month, so speed multiplies growth.
detail: He frames it as going 30 times faster. Payback period is when gross profit from a customer passes the acquisition cost.
numbers: Payback of 1 day versus 30 days: about 30x faster reinvestment.
