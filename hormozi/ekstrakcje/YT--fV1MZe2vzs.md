### YT--fV1MZe2vzs#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Some business tensions are dichotomies to manage, not problems to solve: delegation against micromanagement is the example.
detail: Early on, after reading a book on delegation he handed everything off and left; results slipped, so he swung to micromanaging, which stifled initiative and made him the bottleneck. The answer is a managed middle ground. The same applies to variety against consistency in a marriage; recognising these patterns stops wasted effort on unsolvable issues.

### YT--fV1MZe2vzs#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:37
claim: When someone starts talking fast to give context, Hormozi usually pauses them with "what problem are we solving?"; he highly advises people with direct reports to do the same.
detail: Sometimes you might even have to ask "is that a problem?" For example, customer service complaints may be reducible through process over time, not a threat to survival, so they should not stop everything. New businesses should expect an imperfect product and improve it.
### YT--fV1MZe2vzs#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:17
claim: Replace yes/no verdicts with a continuum: ask to what extent it failed and where the drop-off sits.
detail: Brains store binary labels as shorthand, which makes decisions less accurate. "Facebook does not work for me" should become "how far did it get and where does it fall off". A diet example: being 10% over yesterday means aim 10% under today, not abandon the diet. Credited to a biochemist friend.
