### YT-1UhvBSQFy6A#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: sales-process | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: The diagnostic sale has seven steps that end in a price tied to how the customer gets from their current state to their desired state.
detail: 1. Pre-sale questionnaire. 2. Collect contact details and the card. 3. Ask the current state. 4. Ask the desired state. 5. Ask the obstacle. 6. Present the vehicle that overcomes the obstacle and tie the price to getting them from current to desired. 7. Offer an incentive to prepay.
applies_to: Brick-and-mortar service chains with one-off transactional sales
### YT-1UhvBSQFy6A#02 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:28
claim: After an acquired clinic chain rewrote its sales process (four hours of work with the sales director), revenue per customer rose from $200 to $800 and recurring revenue grew more than tenfold.
detail: The chain had good lead generation and a good product, but packaging was weak: sales were one-off services (filler-type treatments) with a "call when you want it again" ending. Core services were untouched; only presentation changed. Locations grew from 14 to 32 over about 18 months.
numbers: Revenue per customer $200 to $800; recurring share of this service line 9% to 60%; memberships 0 to 30%; visits per customer from about 2 to 4-6; line revenue about $20,000 to over $250,000 per month.
applies_to: Multi-location medical-aesthetic or service chain

### YT-1UhvBSQFy6A#03 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:51
claim: Secret-shop your own sales process, and any business you plan to buy, using a short checklist.
detail: You will likely be horrified by what reps say. Checklist used: did they send a pre-sale question? Did they ask for card or details before service? Did they have the shopper pick current state and desired state? In this chain the shopper picked a current state only and was then sold a single service with no package or membership. For a chain, a sales director could shop locations before the deal closed.

### YT-1UhvBSQFy6A#04 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:39
claim: The pre-sale questionnaire asks the same question several ways to agitate the problem.
detail: Sample questions: what brought you in, what problem, how long, what has it cost financially, how much worse would it be in five years. It serves the salesperson with information and raises the prospect's own awareness of the problem and its importance. Works in person or as an online application.

### YT-1UhvBSQFy6A#05 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: Menu Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:25
claim: Take the card as "completing your customer profile" before anything is sold, so no payment ask is needed later.
detail: After the questionnaire the prospect feels they need the thing; the profile step is framed as standard procedure. If they hesitate, say it is how the system completes profiles; most give it. Later the card is already on file for the sale and for any follow-up.

### YT-1UhvBSQFy6A#07 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:40
claim: Let the customer place themselves on a chart and choose the goal; the price then feels like their own decision.
detail: In weight loss the scale, not the seller, names the starting point; with fillers, a chart of faces lets them self-identify and pick a target look. ### YT-1UhvBSQFy6A#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:21
claim: Move a transactional sale toward a custom one without changing the product: the customer feels it is personalised, operations stay standard.
detail: A transactional sale fits the customer to the product, by listening to wants or by educating them that they are wrong. It is fast and cheap to run. The gain comes from keeping the same deliverable (gyms still give workouts, nutrition, accountability) while naming it as the three steps that close the customer's own gap. Price is presented only after current, desired and obstacle are known.

### YT-1UhvBSQFy6A#09 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:26
claim: "Kill zombies": anticipate objections before the price, because after the price the prospect sees a salesperson.
detail: Be the trusted expert first. Ask with childlike curiosity (head tilted, "what changed between then and now, just so I understand") so the question feels non-threatening. If you win the argument you lose the sale; you must be willing to lose being right.

### YT-1UhvBSQFy6A#10 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:38
claim: With low-skill staff, build the sales steps into the point of sale so they cannot proceed without the checkbox, then drill until it is automatic.
detail: Forced steps ensure questions are asked and the card is requested. Repeat the process until reps say "want to use the card on file?" without thinking.

### YT-1UhvBSQFy6A#11 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Anchor Upsell | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:19:40
claim: The chain's upsell was a roughly $20 product for customers worth about $200, which is why it barely moved revenue.
detail: Upsell should be about five times the current price, because the top 20% of customers spend about five times more. With a $1,000 base, a $5,000 upsell taken by 20% adds $1,000 per customer; a $100 upsell taken by 20% adds $20. Staff used the product for commissions, a different aim.
numbers: $1,000 base + 20% x $5,000 = $2,000 average; $100 upsell adds $20.

### YT-1UhvBSQFy6A#12 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:22:29
claim: A friend's $300-a-month membership averaged three months; selling it as a $10,000 program with 36-month interest-free financing raised average tenure to eight months.
detail: Product unchanged. The commitment framing and plan made people stay.
numbers: $300/month, 3 months = $900; after the change, 8 months x $300 = $2,400.

### YT-1UhvBSQFy6A#13 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:23:50
claim: End a payment plan with an automatic recurring charge at the same amount, so the plan flows into continuity.
detail: An agency friend used this: customers on a plan churn less than on monthly billing, and what it is called matters less than regular payments. Put the auto-recurring at the end of the program.

### YT-1UhvBSQFy6A#14 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:25:27
claim: Quote the full price first, then show the prepayment discount subtracting from it, instead of quoting the low price and adding interest.
detail: Worked example on $4,500: prepay in full minus 20% is $3,600; half down with 10% off is $4,050; rest on a weekly plan at the full rate. The anchor stays high and you are the one offering a benefit, not penalising someone who cannot pay.
numbers: $4,500 full; $3,600 (20% off) prepay; $4,050 (10% off) half down.

### YT-1UhvBSQFy6A#15 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:27:48
claim: Say "prepay" with a discount, not "paid in full", and give the team the mantra "everyone buys something".
detail: "Paid in full" benefits the seller; "prepay" names a customer benefit. Step-down order: prepay, half down with smaller discount, third-party financing, partial with a small discount, payment plan matched to delivery dates, and finally just rebook the next visit.
