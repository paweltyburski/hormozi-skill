### YT-OVhNSzFSoZs#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:20
claim: Look for opportunity in the industries where your family or past jobs already gave you knowledge, and move up the ownership ladder.
detail: Past experience in an industry is a criterion accelerators look for. His own example is Smoothie King: blender tender gives no leverage, managing little, owning a location gives leverage over labour, owning the franchise gives much more. Research is done by doing; without a baseline you cannot spot the ideal opportunity.
applies_to: someone starting out

### YT-OVhNSzFSoZs#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:19
claim: Old, large businesses signal high-margin opportunity because only high profits let a firm survive downturns.
detail: Companies that have lasted generations (a major bank, insurers) must earn large margins. Insurance is profitable because customers pay for something that may never happen while the insurer prices risk. The biggest firms sell a base unit with very high gross margin (oil for Exxon, attention for ad platforms). Cut cost but stay long-term greedy so you do not add risk.

### YT-OVhNSzFSoZs#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:50
claim: A millionaire in ten years is nearly guaranteed for those who accept not being one for nine; skills, sales above all, compound.
detail: People who want it in 90 days keep chasing shiny objects. Sales skill alone can earn $200,000-$300,000 a year for someone with entrepreneurial tendencies, and it transfers to selling investors, hires and teams. Take early jobs as skill-building; his rule is to be either learning or earning at any job. Business ownership is the best route to the most wealth but not the fastest.
numbers: $200,000-$300,000 a year from sales skill (as stated).
