### MZ-sSX5ts5gQEU#01 | CASE | topic: sales-process | also: pricing, objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A mastermind for HR chiefs closed about 80% in person but lost sales once groups went virtual with in-person pricing.
detail: The Dallas-based hybrid group did well pre-pandemic. Prospects now ask who is in their city. Hormozi saw two options: raise the quality of virtual or the price, or restore the in-person feel.
numbers: In-person close rate about 80% (own estimate).

### MZ-sSX5ts5gQEU#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: objections-closing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:25
claim: If he had to test things, he would pick learning to overcome the objection over the risk of opening lots of locations (advice to this caller).
detail: He would test having people fly in to the central hub (Dallas, direct flights everywhere), because he thinks people like a change of pace; the alternative is remote, where you'd probably have to drop the price.
### MZ-sSX5ts5gQEU#03 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:29
claim: Reframe the absence of local members as an advantage: no competitors, no shared secrets, no local politics, a diverse safe space.
detail: Fly-in can be an optional upsell for those who want it.

### MZ-sSX5ts5gQEU#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:15
claim: If you accept a prospect's objection after hearing it once or twice, you are being sold by them; believe in your offer more than they believe the objection.
detail: List all reasons the model is great and sell them; many masterminds have no local element.
