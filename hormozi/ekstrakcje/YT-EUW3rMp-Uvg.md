### YT-EUW3rMp-Uvg#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: unit-economics, paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:30
claim: Three eventualities compress margins in any mass-market business as it scales: ad costs per impression rise, you reach colder audiences with lower conversion, and infrastructure costs add fixed overhead.
detail: Revenue keeps rising while margin shrinks. Applies to mass-market consumer products, business-opportunity offers and services sold to the mass market. A business can still scale because the small, warm audience earned enough to justify the colder one, but a cold-enough audience eventually will not buy.

### YT-EUW3rMp-Uvg#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:34
claim: In physical-product businesses, volume discounts diminish: most of the savings come from the first two orders-of-magnitude increases in purchase size.
detail: After that, he says the savings from buying more quantity are razor thin.
numbers: First two 10x steps in order size give most savings.
### YT-EUW3rMp-Uvg#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:42
claim: Illustrative margin path: about 40% at $1M, 30% at $10M, and 10% around $10-30M of revenue as the three costs build.
detail: Hormozi gave these figures as examples of the shape, not measured data; the risk is a business with high revenue, no margin and liability for payroll that feels like a large nonprofit. He prompted this from a conversation with a business-opportunity company at $50-60M a year with paper-thin margins.
numbers: Illustrative margins 40%, 30%, 10-20%; the owner he spoke to made $50-60M a year.

### YT-EUW3rMp-Uvg#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:42
claim: At roughly $1-5M a year, pause doing more of the same and invest in customer experience and word of mouth, a channel that does not behave like the three eventualities.
detail: Word of mouth is quadratic in nature (one tells two, two tell four), so it lowers cost of acquisition at scale and warms colder audiences. Going slow to go fast is sometimes needed.
numbers: Pause point $1M to $5M per year.
### YT-EUW3rMp-Uvg#05 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: management-ops | fw: NPS | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:32
claim: Add a customer success lead who tracks time to value, NPS and customer health scores, since referrals are what matter most.
detail: The aim is to move word of mouth from neutral or negative to positive.

### YT-EUW3rMp-Uvg#06 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: retention-ltv | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:32
claim: Test for negative word of mouth: if your cost of acquisition rises faster than cost per impression, it is working against you.
detail: Example: cost per impression up 10% while acquisition cost is up 40%. A negative experience is told to about ten times as many people as a positive one.
numbers: CPM +10% vs CAC +40%; negative tellers reach ~10x.
### YT-EUW3rMp-Uvg#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: offers, retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:31
claim: Set modest expectations and over-deliver, since a gap between expectation and delivery drives word of mouth; raising expectations to win a short-term sale creates a negative gap.
detail: Movie analogy: a film hyped to the skies feels average, while one you expected to be bad feels great. Cut time to value so customers are surprised.

### YT-EUW3rMp-Uvg#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:29
claim: A brand is what people say about you behind your back, and a good one gives pricing power: a 10-20% price rise with no demand loss can double, triple or quadruple profit in a mass-market business.
detail: Without a brand you are only arbitraging media, and the three eventualities will eat margin. Build it by keeping or beating your promises and improving quality.
numbers: 10-20% price increase.
