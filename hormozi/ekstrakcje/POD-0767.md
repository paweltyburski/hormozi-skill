### POD-0767#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: product-delivery, verticals | fw: Delivery Cube | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A service founder can scale along three paths: fractionalise access to yourself, add bodies who each do part of the work, or build a firm hierarchy of juniors and sign-off seniors.
detail: Path one raises the ratio of you to customers (1:1, 1:4, 1:10, 1:100, 1:1000) and can change medium (in person vs remote). Path two breaks your expertise into if-then behaviours and drills other people on parts. Path three is many-to-one: juniors do the legwork and a senior signs off and risks the firm's reputation, as in McKinsey, Accenture, Bain, big accounting firms and law firms. He notes consulting firms prove services can scale.
numbers: Services are about 78% of businesses (as stated).
applies_to: Knowledge-based service businesses

### POD-0767#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: finance-exit | fw: Prep for Sale | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:41
claim: In his opinion, the choice depends on working backwards from your goal: fractionalise access if you will keep working and want income; if you want to sell the business, go the many-to-one route as an intermediary.
detail: Fractionalising gives many customers with light headcount, higher profit in general, and cash to reinvest. For a sellable asset he suggests the many-to-one route in between: chunk the work out and give pieces of it to other people, rather than hiring people and teaching them everything.
### POD-0767#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:49
claim: Fractionalising access forces your skill up at each ratio step, and he expects that to be the biggest driver of value, at the cost of key-man risk.
detail: Each jump (1:4, 1:10, 1:100, 1:1000) requires more varied and deeper skills. Tony Robbins is the example of one person scaling to events of 10,000 to 50,000 while staying the star. The downside is that if you leave the business stops working.

### POD-0767#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:07
claim: Hiring people and teaching them everything in a week or two rarely scales, because it sells a diluted version of the expertise.
detail: If it took you years to become good, a short onboarding misses the ancillary skills that make you the expert. He prefers a smaller serving of the real thing (fractionalised) to a bigger one watered down. Customers might buy but will not stay.

### POD-0767#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:59
claim: The firm path needs a brand that attracts smart people and a career ladder with incentives that make them stay for years.
detail: Intelligent recruits think five years ahead, so "coach" with a roster and a week of onboarding is not enough; seniority titles help but are weak. Layers developed over years, not onboarding weeks, produce partners.
