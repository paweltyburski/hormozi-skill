claim: In a local market, reaching the whole population once is cheap, so offers fatigue faster, but one impression is not fatigue.
detail: On most platforms about 1,000 people can be reached for about $20, so 200,000 people costs about $10,000 for a single pass. Most people do not even notice an offer the first time, so refresh the creative, hooks, stories and copy around the same offer. You can use an offer for a long time; after years of use, not months, offers can eventually fatigue.
numbers: ~$20 per 1,000 reach; 200,000 people = ~$10,000 for one pass.
applies_to: Local businesses.

### POD-230819-13#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: offers | also: promotions-launches | fw: MAGIC Naming Formula | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:06
claim: Skip a stated time interval in the name when the platform treats the claim as an implied guarantee.
detail: Quantifiable claims, such as income or weight loss, with a duration are often rejected by ad platforms. Use the interval where compliance is not an issue or where the goal is not a claim per se, for example "make your first sale in 10 days" rather than "$10,000 in 10 days".
applies_to: Advertisers on rule-bound ad platforms.

### POD-230819-13#05 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: content-brand | fw: MAGIC Naming Formula | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:08
claim: Make the container word rhyme or alliterate when it comes naturally.
detail: Container words signal a bundle that cannot be compared with a commodity (challenge, bootcamp, sprint, reset, masterclass). Rhymes and repeated starting sounds stick in memory; a rhyming dictionary helps, but forcing it is not required. Examples: six-pack fast track, debt detox, real estate reset.
numbers: Use 3 to 5 components in a name.

claim: Test two or three names in the campaign, keep the winner as the control and pit new names against it.
detail: He admits he does not know why some names win, so he treats it unemotionally and keeps trying. The formula also works for naming each bonus and sub-item in the stack, which raises their perceived value.

### POD-230819-13#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: promotions-launches | also: management-ops | fw: Promotion Refresh Order | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:20
claim: Change the lightest item on the refresh list first, because each step further down adds operational drag.
detail: Once an offer is monetized, change it rarely; entrepreneurs' urge to change creates inefficiency. Rotating between several proven offers is the strongest position if it adds no operational load. Wrapper example: a six-week stress release challenge becomes a 42-day relaxing holidays challenge for a massage centre.
numbers: Six-week challenge renamed as 42 days; duration might shift to 28 days or eight weeks.
