### YT-s3tH9Sz1fEc#01 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Gym Launch earned about $17M profit when Hormozi was the business, and about $8M a year later when he was involved only about 90 minutes a week.
detail: At the $17M peak he was the face of marketing, the product, the sales calls and the CEO, so nobody could buy it. Afterwards it was an enterprise with a management team. Profit halved but his dollars per hour rose, and the enterprise was worth more because it generated revenue without him.
numbers: profit ~$17M in 2018 (quality of earnings; he first says 2019, then 2018) versus ~$8M "this year" (2021); 90 minutes per week of his time.

### YT-s3tH9Sz1fEc#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:20
claim: Do not leave the CEO seat too soon; he sees people jump out too soon, and he stepped back only after reaching the wealth he wanted.
detail: Owners at $1-3M ask him whether to outsource everything. His answer is that he stopped pushing growth for its own sake and switched to other success metrics (time with wife, gym, meeting people). Entrepreneurs also find it tempting to turn passive assets active to squeeze return.
applies_to: Owners at roughly $1M-$3M asking about outsourcing.
### YT-s3tH9Sz1fEc#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:52
claim: Delegating only pays if the freed hours are refilled with higher-value work.
detail: Entrepreneurship is repeated time arbitrage: pay $10-15 an hour for work that frees ten hours a week, then use those ten hours on a higher-value skill (marketing, a new channel, the product). People who outsource and do not refill the time are choosing lifestyle, not growth.
numbers: $10-$15/hour example; 10 hours/week freed.

### YT-s3tH9Sz1fEc#04 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:17
claim: Jump two steps forward: hire someone for your current job and another for the next role you would have moved into, then step back and own.
detail: One step only moves you to the next job. Taking two means the business grows at the same pace with others doing both of your roles, and you can see the weaknesses and the market from outside. You then act as chairman, coaching the CEO on direct reports and vision, which rarely changes.

### YT-s3tH9Sz1fEc#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:01
claim: Split owner income into pay for your work and return on what you own; replacing yourself may cut take-home but raises net worth.
detail: Example: a business netting $3M a year where five high-level leaders would cost about $1.5M is really a $1.5M business plus $1.5M of salary you pay yourself. Once you quit and hire the leaders, income falls (maybe by half), dollars per hour and enterprise value rise because it is sellable. Once it runs without you, ask how to raise revenue without your involvement.
numbers: $3M profit minus ~$1.5M leadership cost = ~$1.5M true business profit; 4-5 high-level leaders.
