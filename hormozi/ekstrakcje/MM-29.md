### MM-29#01 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals, retention-ltv | fw: Waived Fee Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A consultant running a ~$5M/year no-employee lifestyle business keeps almost every client on payment plans by waiving a $25,000 onboarding fee for those who commit to a year.
detail: Offer: $25,000 to start then $4,000/month month-to-month, or $4,000/month with the fee waived on a 12-month commitment. Nearly everyone takes the year and initials that leaving early means paying the $25,000. When someone calls to quit: "No worries — just wire the onboarding fee and I'll cancel you right away." That usually keeps them, and carries them through the emotional ups and downs of business. He says he barely needs to market because he loses almost no one.
numbers: $25,000 fee; $4,000/month; 12-month commitment; ~$5M/year.

### MM-29#02 | DEFINITION | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv, pricing | fw: Waived Fee Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:17
claim: A waived-fee offer charges month-to-month customers a startup fee of 3–5x the monthly rate and waives it for those who commit to a long term — unless they cancel early.
detail: Commitment at least six months, ideally 12+. It is a cancellation term sold as a front-end price: the fee makes commitment attractive at signup and later hangs over the decision to quit — if they didn't want to pay it at the start, they won't want to in month six. By month four to six, leaving costs as much as staying, so it carries them past the early churn point. Risk is shared: month-to-month, the fee covers your risk; committed, they carry it. Fairness: once the term is complete, they can cancel with no fee.
numbers: Fee 3–5x monthly (e.g., $5,000 on $1,000/month); term ≥6, ideally 12+ months.

### MM-29#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: Waived Fee Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:18
claim: Customers stay when leaving costs more than staying — a large exit fee shrinks whatever reason they had to quit.
detail: People quit for countless reasons, but an extra cost to cancel makes the original reason look small next to the value of avoiding the fee. The same motive that got them to start (avoiding the fee) keeps them in (00:06:55).

### MM-29#04 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: Waived Fee Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:18
claim: Explain the fee as the true cost of onboarding: a customer wanting short-term flexibility covers their own setup costs, whereas one who commits long term has them absorbed by you.
detail: Explanation: new customers consume more resources than established ones, so a one-off fee ensures they get the best start; it is never charged again and serves their benefit. Call it anything — onboarding, setup, activation, enrollment, initiation, processing, commencement — followed by "fee". If challenged: "Getting you started costs us money. If you just want to try it, you cover that; if you commit for longer, I'll take it on" (00:06:55).

### MM-29#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Waived Fee Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:30
claim: Hormozi's favourite version — fee with commitment — on a $1,000/month service with a $5,000 fee and a 12-month term.
detail: Option A: $5,000 plus the first $1,000 now, then $1,000/month, cancel any time. Option B: fee waived with a 12-month commitment at $1,000/month; the $5,000 is owed only if they break it early. Variant: keep the month-to-month rate higher than the committed rate; anyone breaking the commitment pays the savings accrued so far, so the exit cost grows the longer they stay.
numbers: $1,000/month, $5,000 fee, 12 months.

### MM-29#06 | FRAMEWORK | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Waived Fee Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:15
claim: The waived-fee offer has four levers — commitment length, committed rate, month-to-month rate and the fee — that can be mixed freely; default to a fee of 5x the monthly price.
detail: Hormozi's draft had nine variants; he cut them to avoid confusion. A proven starting model: monthly price × 5 = the fee, waived on commitment, hanging over the whole term.
numbers: Default fee = 5x monthly price.

### MM-29#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: Waived Fee Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:01
claim: The ethical case: many services involve emotional ups and downs, and a fee acts as a guardrail that keeps customers on track through the rough months to success.
detail: Hormozi says he genuinely believes this, not as wordsmithing — the price structure builds incentives around the customer's own volatility.

### MM-29#08 | DECISION_RULE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: Waived Fee Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:55
claim: If more than 5% of committed customers want to cancel early, investigate the product — fees should nudge people, not handcuff them to something they hate.
detail: Pricing can encourage sticking but cannot and should not overcome a terrible product; trapped customers simply come to hate you.
numbers: >5% early-cancel requests = warning sign.

### MM-29#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: pricing, unit-economics | fw: Waived Fee Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:41
claim: Size the fee by your goal: a larger fee pushes more people to commit; a smaller one (1.5–3x monthly) pushes more to month-to-month and brings more cash up front.
detail: Same logic as the continuity bonus price ratio. Hormozi's guess: a fee about 33% above the comparison point would split buyers roughly 50/50 (unclear in audio).
numbers: Cash-focused fee 1.5–3x monthly; commitment-focused 3–5x.

### MM-29#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Waived Fee Offer | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:27
claim: Waived fees work best with commitments of a year or more and services whose results take a long time, such as SEO, investing or weight loss.
detail: The longer the commitment, the better it works; it holds people in when they get emotional before results arrive. Drop the fee once the commitment is fulfilled — that is what keeps it fair.
