### POD-0428#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: content-brand | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:49
claim: Five phases of content: post once, post on a fixed cadence, post at least weekly on every platform where your avatar is, max out each platform, then create and capture across all.
detail: Phases 1-2 serve nurture; phases 3-5 aim at new customers. A platform without your avatar can be skipped. Munger on consistency: hard to fail if you are consistent at anything.
numbers: Phase 3 minimum: once a week per platform. Phase 4 maximizing: short-form platforms can sometimes take 5-10 posts a day; feed platforms are usually capped at 1-2 a day.
### POD-0428#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:22
claim: When other channels bring leads, the job of organic content is to prove in advance that paying you means getting more of the same value, which raises perceived likelihood of achievement.
detail: Prospects arriving from affiliates, ads or outreach check your profile; it only has to look recent and legitimate.

### POD-0428#03 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: content-brand | fw: Greatest Hits | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:22
claim: Have the sales reps themselves build the master sheet of top converting content and call FAQs, and send items before or between calls ("many people liked this video on our insurance setup").
detail: The same sheet feeds new content topics. Winning hooks (saved, shared, commented) carry over to paid ads and the first lines of cold emails, tested against the current control.

### POD-0428#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: content-brand | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:49
claim: Deliberately undersell your demand: sell to only part of the line, close the door for now ("back in a month") and keep giving far more than you ask.
detail: Many oversell their small demand and never grow the audience. Over-index on giving with the occasional ask, and you always have more people at the door than seats.

### POD-0428#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:40
claim: Pay vendors for speed first, learn their methods, then hire in-house staff, who you pay for time and control, and scale volume there.
detail: A small business owner can do just the first two phases to look like a legitimate, open business.
numbers: Vendors about 5x faster ramp.
### POD-0428#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:58
claim: Data-driven vendors push virality; ignore a format that gets views but draws the wrong audience (a food-eating contest for a plumber).
detail: Easy to quantify does not mean important; values, message and target outweigh counts. A smaller audience of the right type is acceptable.

### POD-0428#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: content-brand | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:36
claim: His content for two people costs about $70,000 a month for roughly 160 pieces a week, versus about $2 million a month of equivalent ad spend.
detail: Content as lead source took about 18 months before deal flow appeared, even with video experience and a track record.
numbers: $70,000/month; 160 pieces/week; about $2M/month ad equivalent; about 18 months to first deal flow.

### POD-0428#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: content-brand | fw: Perceived Hidden Costs | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:12
claim: The biggest cost of a free service is not price but the other frictions around it, and the same holds for free content, which costs attention.
detail: If nobody takes your free offer, find the friction and fix it; if no one pays attention for your content, it is not yet worth their time.

### POD-0428#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: operator-mindset | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:56
claim: Rather than "fake it till you make it", build evidence; evidence makes you beyond reproach and insults only hurt where they touch a truth.
detail: A negative remark ("scam") hurts only if you believe it. Stack proof of what you have done, then make frameworks and teach, with strength to ride out hard periods.
