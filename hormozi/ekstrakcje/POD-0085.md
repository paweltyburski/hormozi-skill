### POD-0085#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:50
claim: In a Zen Planner industry report, profitable gyms charged about $172 a month against an average of about $132; his own client data averaged $118.
detail: His own minimum recommendation is $167 a month. The difference between leader and laggard prices is roughly 30%.
numbers: Leaders ~$172/month list; average ~$132 (his data $118); recommended floor $167/month.
### POD-0085#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:25
claim: Leaders barely discount: their average member paid about $142 against a $172 list, while laggards' members averaged about $72 against $132.
detail: Lifetime "founder" rates for early members drag the average billing down for good. Either fill the gym first and then raise rates for existing members because of capacity, or raise them immediately; he has done both.
numbers: Leaders: $142 paid vs $172 list; laggards: $72 paid vs $132 list.

### POD-0085#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:25
claim: One early discount tells the buyer everything is negotiable and makes the business look like a mom-and-pop shop; owners must follow their own no-discount rule.
detail: Discounting also signals a lack of conviction in the product and the seller. The first discount makes the customer feel they own you. Sticking to the stated price earns respect and fewer difficult customers. Owners who tell staff not to discount but make side deals break the standard.

### POD-0085#04 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:12
claim: Leaders use about half the floor space per customer; choose space-light exercises and partner people up to double capacity.
detail: For a general-population gym he drops movements that need lots of space or a steep learning curve. In partner work one person lifts while the other cheers, spots and rests. A gym serving 16 per hour could move to about 30 per session, then cut session times and halve overhead.
numbers: Space per customer: leaders about half of laggards; capacity 16 to about 30 per session; overhead potentially halved.

### POD-0085#05 | SCRIPT | topic: sales-process | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:55
claim: Sell partner workouts to members as safety, motivation and exercise science.
detail: Three angles: safety (a spotter), motivation (cheering), and a work-rest rationale: hard effort for 45 seconds then rest for 45 seconds, rather than hard for 60 minutes straight.
numbers: 45 seconds hard, 45 seconds rest.

### POD-0085#06 | BENCHMARK | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:03
claim: Laggard gyms spend about half of gross revenue on payroll; leaders spend about a quarter.
detail: Partly it is because higher prices make the payroll share smaller, partly pay design.
numbers: Payroll: laggards ~50% of gross revenue; leaders ~25%.

### POD-0085#07 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:03
claim: Pay trainers a lower base plus commissions on semi-privates, other products, front-end sales and internal promotions.
detail: Leaders paid trainers more in total, with about twice the commissions of laggards, but kept fixed cost lower, so a slow month hurts less.
numbers: Commissions at leaders ~2x laggards.

### POD-0085#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:21
claim: Market leaders were in the most crowded areas, so learn from the fiercest competitive markets, not from isolated ones.
detail: He moved to Southern California, the hotbed of fitness, on the same logic: if you can make it there you can make it anywhere. Owners in less saturated markets should copy leader tactics from hotbeds.
