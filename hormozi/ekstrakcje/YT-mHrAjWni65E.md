### YT-mHrAjWni65E#01 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A loss leader sells a product whose market price everyone knows below that price to bring buyers in, then earns the profit on items whose prices they do not know.
detail: Diapers.com is the model: diapers at the lowest price, profit on ancillary purchases such as baby bottles. For a holiday sale, list the components of your offer (facility, accountability, workouts, nutrition), splinter one off and discount it about 90%. Buyers then want the rest too. Holiday timing helps because businesses sell most in the last month, both from heavier advertising and from heavier consumer spending.
numbers: example discount on the splintered component: 90%.

### YT-mHrAjWni65E#02 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: promotions-launches | fw: Hinge Method | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:38
claim: Sell gift cards worth one full unit of service at 80-90% off, limited to two per person and only as gifts, so existing clients pay you to bring new customers.
detail: Steps: 1) card value equals one unit of service ($200); 2) market only to current customers; 3) limit two each, priced at 90% off ($20) or 70% off for a $400 double; 4) restrict to friends and family. Recipients are referrals, who buy and stay at higher rates, and the buyer helps onboard them. At sale, ask who the card is for and capture their contact details, then do a three-way introduction if unredeemed.
numbers: $200 card sold at $20 (90% off); two units worth $400 at 70% off, which he stated as $60 (30% of $400 would be $120; unclear in audio).

### YT-mHrAjWni65E#03 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: sales-process | fw: Rollover Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:26
claim: When the gift-card recipient comes in, hold a sales consult and credit the card toward a larger purchase.
detail: Credit the $200 card against a $1,000 offer so the recipient pays $800. The full chain: unit-sized card, discount, two per client, gift-only, contact capture, three-way intro, consult and upsell. It brings in many new customers at a profit in a period when it is hard to be noticed.
numbers: $1,000 offer minus $200 card = $800 sale.

### YT-mHrAjWni65E#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:38
claim: Gift cards are very profitable because many are never redeemed and the cash arrives first.
detail: His example is Best Buy cards with a low redemption rate: effectively a loan from the customer, held on the balance sheet, and after expiry the business keeps the money without delivering goods.

### YT-mHrAjWni65E#05 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: pricing | fw: Scarcity | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:58
claim: A holiday high-ticket drop: offer a 10-100x version of your normal price to only 5% of customers, announce the launch moment and count down units left.
detail: Normal $200 offering becomes a $2,000-$20,000 package, limited to 5-10% of customers; use 5% the first time so it sells out, then raise to 6-7% next year. Rationale: customers are fractal, with 80/20 inside the 20%, so about 5% sit two standard deviations above the rest. Build hype before the holidays, tell people to have cards ready, then post "four left, three left, sold out" for FOMO.
numbers: 100 customers x 5 sold at $5,000 = $25,000, which can double a $10,000-$20,000 month; limit 5% (first time), 6-7% later.

### YT-mHrAjWni65E#06 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: promotions-launches | fw: Buy X Get Y Free | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:50
claim: A Nashville boot shop tripled the single-pair price and advertised "buy one, get two free", with long-term success.
detail: Boots that normally cost about $200 were $600 a pair with two free pairs, fitting bachelorette-party groups. Make the free amount larger than the paid amount. Shirts: $5 each normally, sold as $10 with two free, which is about a 30% discount in reality but looks far better. Holidays are a good time to launch a new service level since customers have no price comparison.
numbers: boots $200 normal, $600 for three pairs; shirts $5 normal, $10 for three.

### YT-mHrAjWni65E#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: Buy X Get Y Free | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:21
claim: For recurring offers, buy-X-get-Y-free front-loads cash; require full prepayment and cap the quantity.
detail: A $100/month membership takes a year to yield $1,200, but a year sold for $600 now brings cash immediately. No payment plan on the discounted version. Limiting units limits downside from people "taking advantage". He passed on investing in businesses too dependent on this tactic.
numbers: $100/month membership: $1,200 over a year vs. $600 prepaid.

claim: Use buy-six-get-six at a raised price and with one or two cheap extras to cash-flow a grand opening without being unfair to regular clients.
