### POD-0481#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: verticals | also: money-models, strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:36
claim: The rainmaker model: a solo operator brings a compelling offer, runs the ads and sells the leads for a local brick-and-mortar facility that fulfils at close to cost, and keeps the spread instead of a commission.
detail: Three skills are all it needs: build a strong offer, run a basic lead ad, close the leads. The operator carries the capital and time risk, so the economics are the sale price minus what the facility is paid to fulfil, minus ad spend. It works in any local market and industry that has expensive services, and was Hormozi's fallback each time he was broke.
applies_to: Solo starter with little money; local service businesses with spare capacity.

### POD-0481#02 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: paid-ads, money-models | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:36
claim: His own gym funnel: ads for a six-week challenge, opt-in, booking on the thank-you page, sold in person at the gym for $500-$600 with a lose-the-weight-or-money-back promise plus a satisfaction guarantee.
detail: Leads who booked were phoned to confirm; those who did not book were phoned to schedule. The first sales became the ad budget for the rest of the month, so no start-up capital was needed.
numbers: About $10 per lead, 1 in 5 leads bought the $600 challenge, so roughly 12:1 on ad spend ($5,000 in, about $60,000 out). Gym Launch gyms still reach similar numbers (as stated in 2023).

### POD-0481#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:14
claim: Launching and filling a gym earned more than running it full, because revenue per client per week halved after the move to the back-end membership.
detail: A client on the six-week front end paid about $100 a week; the same client on the ongoing membership paid about $50 a week. This is how he stumbled into selling launches rather than operating gyms.
numbers: Front end $600 for 6 weeks (about $100 per week) versus back end about $200 per month (about $50 per week).

### POD-0481#05 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: money-models | fw: Upsell Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:30
claim: The facility deal: the operator flies out at his own cost, markets, works the leads, books and sells, runs a nutrition orientation, and the facility only delivers the six weeks and then keeps the converted members.
detail: The supplement sale of about $100-$150 per head at the orientation covered his own cost to acquire a customer. The facility took zero risk; he took what he collected during the first six weeks. Offer it to owners as "customers for free, no risk" in local business groups.
numbers: Supplements about $100-$150 per head at the nutrition orientation.

### POD-0481#06 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:23
claim: When negotiating with a facility, ask what the absolute lowest price is at which it would fulfil the customers you bring, and push hard because they gain lifetime customers at no acquisition cost.
detail: It works best for services with low marginal cost, where an extra customer costs the business little. Ideally they deliver near free for a set period; afterwards they keep everything. Framing: even at break-even they get customers for life while you carry the risk and labor.
applies_to: Low-marginal-cost services such as chiropractors, dentists.

### POD-0481#07 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: unit-economics | fw: Attraction Offers | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:16
claim: Chiropractor version of the model: a promoted $20 consult (normally $200) with assessment and X-ray, then a follow-up where a roughly $2,500 six-month treatment plan is sold, with the doctor paid about $500 to fulfil it.
detail: The operator runs the ads, calls leads, makes sure they show, and recommends the package after the doctor's team does the X-ray. Longer, pricier packages are better where possible.
numbers: Plan $2,500 less $500 fulfilment cost = $2,000 spread before ad cost.

### POD-0481#08 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:30
claim: Worked monthly math of the chiropractor model: 48 sales a month leaves about $84,000 profit for one person with no staff.
detail: Assumptions: 2 sales a day for 24 working days. Lead cost $50 with 1 in 5 buying gives $250 per sale; $400 a day of ads. Revenue is not profit, so ad spend and the doctor's fee are subtracted.
numbers: 48 sales x $2,500 = $120,000 revenue; ads $12,000 (30 days x $400); doctor 48 x $500 = $24,000; leaves about $84,000.

### POD-0481#09 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Upsell Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:18
claim: Pre-negotiate that you keep all retail product sales (creams, supplements, oils, orthotics) made to the same leads in a second meeting.
detail: This added about $500 profit per head, which covered the doctor's fee. Living costs on the road (extended-stay motel, food, gas) ran about $3,000 a month, and the total net was around $105,000 a month with roughly $1,000 to start.
numbers: About $500 per head x 48 = $24,000; total about $108,000 less $3,000 living = about $105,000 per month.

### POD-0481#11 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:18
claim: Mistakes 1 and 2 in his own hindsight: he would spend far more time planning with the partner how they would fulfil the extra customers, and he probably would have looked for good partners, not just anyone who would let him sell.

### POD-0481#12 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:50
claim: Mistakes 3 and 4: disclose up front your own costs (travel, lodging, ad spend) and the revenue and profit you plan to make, so the owner is not shocked by your results.
detail: Owners were surprised and half of them tried to make him stop selling before he hit his target. He should have said how many sales just cover his costs and how many more he needs for his margin. Fear of scaring partners off made him skip it, which hurt him later.
numbers: Example framing: about 40 sales to break even, another 40 to reach a 50% margin (illustrative, as stated).

### POD-0481#13 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:27
claim: Mistake 5 and 7: put a written agreement in place, with a separate-signed clause that if the partner steers a customer to refund and re-sign directly, the partner owes twice what the customer paid.
detail: A couple of bad actors, including an owner who told customers to refund because it was not his money, drove the business down. Example: customer paid $2,500 to the operator, refunds, and re-buys from the doctor at half price. The contract also fixes each side's accountability.

### POD-0481#15 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:59
claim: Mistake 10: test about $250 of ads in each market before agreeing to work there, then only take partners in the markets with the cheapest leads.
detail: About four markets cost roughly $1,000 to compare. Some markets give a third of the lead cost of others, so work goes where the leverage per hour is best. Separately, capacity of the facility is the only limit of the model, so a solo operator can rotate three or four facilities, one a month.
numbers: $250 test per market; roughly a $1,000 test across four markets.

### POD-0481#16 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: sales-process | fw: Penny Gap | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:06
claim: Mistake 11: sell thousands, not hundreds: advertise a $0-$99 lead magnet, then upsell it into a multi-thousand-dollar service; charging $20-$50 for the first step cuts no-shows.
detail: The idea fits insurance, pest control, solar, gyms, chiropractors, dentists, wherever customers buy expensive things locally. Charging a small amount improves lead quality and lowers no-shows and reschedules.
numbers: Front-end price $0-$99; back end in the thousands; small booking fee $20-$50 reduces no-shows.

### POD-0481#17 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:13
claim: Minimal tech stack: a Facebook lead ad feeds a Google Sheet through Zapier, with a Zapier SMS alert on every new lead, and no landing page.
detail: The sheet works as a makeshift CRM; the alert lets you call and text each lead at once. The giveaway plus a strong guarantee gets leads; the skill to build is upselling them. He says it can be learned in under an hour.

### POD-0481#18 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:47
claim: To find which offer works, sit at the front desk of a local business that sells expensive things, test offers and tune the price until you reach 10:1 or better, then copy it to other markets.
detail: Learn on someone else's money and accept low pay at first (or work for free). Once ratios of 10:1, 20:1 or sometimes 30:1 are repeatable, scale and negotiate a much stronger split.
numbers: Target 10:1 or better; sometimes 20:1 to 30:1.

### POD-0481#20 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:52
claim: Selling over the phone earns more per hour than in person, even at a lower close rate, because there are no wasted waits for no-shows.
detail: You talk to about twice as many prospects, as you skip the travel and appointment step and can sell the lead at once or move to the next one.
