### YT-3P1XjUvo1b4#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:46
claim: The route to a fortune is to sell things people do not stop buying; tactics for leads and pricing make money but not a fortune.
detail: Success came less from inventing technology than from improving something until customers keep paying. Structure the offer so that value far exceeds price, to the point that customers call you when their card fails.

### YT-3P1XjUvo1b4#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:49
claim: Thought experiment: count every customer you have ever sold, multiply by your current monthly price, and ask what you could spend on acquisition if none had left.
detail: The result is probably a lot, maybe a lot more than current revenue. It shows how much churn costs and how confident you would be spending on growth if you never lost a customer.
### YT-3P1XjUvo1b4#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:37
claim: Sell a model rather than a method: a method is used once, a model can keep evolving and be needed continually.
detail: E-learning that sells a method gets bought, then not needed. Franchises sell models; the marketing channel changes over time (Groupon, email, Facebook, YouTube) but the model stays.

### YT-3P1XjUvo1b4#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: retention-ltv | fw: Create Flow Monetize Add Friction | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:06:40
claim: Do promotion first to get customers (usually about $0 to $1M), then work on the product until people do not leave ($1M to $10M), then marketing compounds.
detail: Learnt from a former early HubSpot executive. In the first phase price high enough that people care but do not maximise profit; the aim is to get customers, not to maximise sales. Keep testing the product and stay in that phase until retention is solved. Without churn, traffic sticks like glue and revenue grows every year.
numbers: Promotion phase usually $0-$1M; product phase $1M-$10M.
### YT-3P1XjUvo1b4#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:25
claim: Aim for monthly churn of 1% to 2% at most, against the 10% to 20% typical of most businesses.
detail: Reach it by interviewing and surveying customers, studying usage and consumption patterns, and adding collateral to the community, repeated until churn falls.
numbers: Target monthly churn 1-2%; typical 10-20%.

### YT-3P1XjUvo1b4#06 | CASE | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:50
claim: A consulting client sold the same number of customers as Hormozi's company but had far lower monthly revenue and about 1/100 of the profit.
detail: They made $300,000 a month versus his $4 million a month at the same unit sales, because of better monetization and a better product. He adds that 100 B2B sales a month should build a $100M business if customers are not lost.
numbers: $300,000/month vs $4M/month; ~100x profit gap; 100 B2B clients a month.

### YT-3P1XjUvo1b4#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: Price to Value Discrepancy | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:41
claim: "High ticket" is the wrong target; aim for high value, where price is far below what the customer gets.
detail: Even a low price can feel expensive if the value is unclear, and a large price can feel cheap if the value is obvious. Fortunes come from delivering the value at a small fraction of its price.
