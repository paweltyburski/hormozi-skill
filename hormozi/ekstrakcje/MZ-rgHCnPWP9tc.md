### MZ-rgHCnPWP9tc#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Every major decision (content cadence, book launch, programmes, features) starts as a written memo that records the thinking process.
detail: As a company grows, shared thinking is hard to carry through two, three or four levels of leadership away from ownership, where people form mini cultures. Memos force slow thought on costs and benefits, require defending the idea to smart people, and make you pre-solve naysayers' objections. New hires read past memos to learn decisions and context.
### MZ-rgHCnPWP9tc#02 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:36
claim: Run each memo through an AI with a memo prompt, a data source and your best past memo to tidy it and surface blind spots.
detail: Output might be 20 questions: roughly 10 irrelevant, 5 obvious, and 5 worth weaving back in. Goal: a "one-hit KO" memo that has considered every way the initiative could fail, written like a sales letter to the team with execution detail.
numbers: ~20 questions, ~5 useful.

### MZ-rgHCnPWP9tc#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:11
claim: If your team cannot write memos explaining why a decision is right, they probably should not be making decisions.
detail: Adopting memos reveals team capability gaps quickly.

### MZ-rgHCnPWP9tc#04 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:31
claim: Send memos before the meeting and collect comments in them, so the meeting only handles the comments.
detail: Difference from the Amazon method, where everyone reads the memo at the start of the meeting because many do not read it ahead. His teams do read in advance.
