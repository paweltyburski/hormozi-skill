### MZ-BS422kNTSro#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints, verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A 10-location dietitian group wants to jump from $3.5M to $100M but keeps chasing adjacent models.
detail: Referral-driven: about 400 referrals a month from 18 practitioners. Tempted by licensing, franchising, a gym and a training university while VC-funded virtual competitors chase quantity. Hormozi's answer: improve the current vehicle, buy smaller practices, license later.
numbers: Revenue $3.5M (2025); 35-50% yearly growth for three years; 30 dietitians; about $500k per location (350-500k) at about 15% margin ($75k); 60% of clients virtual; goal $10M in three years.

### MZ-BS422kNTSro#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:46
claim: The owner recalls that Alex and Leila asked what would break if referrals doubled tomorrow. His answer was everything across front, middle and back, and he then went to fix operations.
detail: The owner spent two years fixing operations after this question, and only then went to double referrals.
### MZ-BS422kNTSro#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:38
claim: Weigh a pivot by upside, downside, likelihood and whether you can live with the downside.
detail: Credited to Keith Cunningham. If the downside is not livable, skip it. Hormozi personally likes to assume competitors are not morons, especially well-funded ones, though he notes they might actually have a bad business.
### MZ-BS422kNTSro#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:01
claim: Franchising is the most expensive form of capital and makes sense only for capital-intensive businesses.
detail: It gives up almost all the business for a royalty. Licensing at 6-8% of top line on a $500k site beats his 15% margin ($75k), because the fee comes off the top.
numbers: Licence fee 6-8% of top line; site revenue $500k; profit $75k.

### MZ-BS422kNTSro#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:47
claim: When price is fixed by insurers, grow profit through cost efficiency: ten margin points on 15% is a 67% bottom-line gain.
detail: That equals opening about six more sites and can be done in months. Reinvest in smart people for tasks he hates.
numbers: Margin 15% to 25% = 67% profit increase.

### MZ-BS422kNTSro#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:19
claim: Start licensing only once a location can open every quarter with a team that excludes you.
detail: Acquiring small practices and retraining them takes about six months using internal trainings, against a year when he does it himself.
numbers: One new location per quarter; retrain acquired practice in six months.

### MZ-BS422kNTSro#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:07
claim: Patience compounds: a chain founder built $3.7B one location at a time over 45 years, and every large company looks the same at the top.
detail: At scale you manage seven to ten leaders (sales, marketing, product, IT, legal, finance) whatever the product, and the grass is greener elsewhere because it's fertilised.
numbers: Example: $3.7B top line, 27% net margin, 45 years.
