### POD-0038#01 | CASE | topic: operator-mindset | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:47
claim: A gym owner who had added about $150,000 and tripled recurring billing asked what to do next; the answer was to keep repeating it.
detail: At the Gym Lord Summit (late January 2018) Jason Skisik had used the system for roughly four to five months. Hormozi treats the question as typical of entrepreneurs who solve the equation and then look for the next thing; the money comes from repeating the boring parts.
numbers: about $150,000 extra revenue in 4-5 months; recurring (EFT) billing tripled.
applies_to: Gym owners who have a working acquisition system.

### POD-0038#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:08
claim: Owners cycle between scarcity and drift: they work the proven activity when cash is short, then chase something new once safe.
detail: This cycle repeats until they stop treating the working routine as optional. The routine includes service, leading the team, class quality, calling leads and checking in on customers. He admits to losing a lot of money by changing what already worked.

### POD-0038#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:10
claim: Most businesses need better delivery of the same promise, not a different promise.
detail: He credits Gym Launch's growth to doing what it said it would, with revenue several times larger than any other coaching company in the space, using no novel model. Chipotle is the parallel: standard burritos done better. College entrepreneurship classes teach inventing something new, yet he never wrote a business plan.
numbers: Gym Launch revenue about 3x the next company in its space (his claim, March 2018).

### POD-0038#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: verticals | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:52
claim: Gym Launch sold only two things, one front end and one back end, and taught gyms the same pattern.
detail: The front end acquired customers from profit; the back end was long-term recurring billing, Gym Legacy for himself. Client gyms run two front ends and two back ends, the second levels being taught later.
numbers: 2 offers: one acquisition, one recurring.

### POD-0038#05 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:49
claim: Keep checking whether the sales team works leads fast and properly, even months in.
detail: The follow-up question for a bored owner is whether reps are reaching out quickly. Boredom is the cost of consistency. He keeps the free case-study front end although he would like something new, because it works.
