### POD-251114-25#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:46
claim: Work splits into manager time and maker time, and each needs a different calendar.
detail: Managers use 30 to 60 minute blocks of varied meeting-based work and see an empty slot as a lost opportunity. Makers often make something different each time and, depending on what they make, need half-day or full-day blocks. A maker's output is variable and the workday ends when quality per unit time drops, not at a set time.
numbers: Manager: 10-20 blocks per day. Maker: may only have 1-2 blocks per day, maybe 10-14 per week.
### POD-251114-25#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: Open to Goal | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:17
claim: Much of a maker's real work is invisible thinking, because solving the problem is the work.
detail: It can mean 45 minutes stuck then a sudden answer, or dry spells of days. He cites up to six months just to settle the table of contents of his second book.
numbers: Up to 6 months for one book outline.

### POD-251114-25#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:41
claim: One short meeting costs a maker about ten times what it costs a manager.
detail: A 10 a.m. meeting of 30 minutes splits a morning into fragments of roughly 2 hours and 1.5 hours, too short for deep work, and uses one of the maker's weekly blocks. Managers wrongly assume the maker's time is as cheap as theirs.
numbers: Cost ratio about 10x; about 10 maker blocks per week.

### POD-251114-25#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:02
claim: A meeting later in the day still spoils the maker's morning because open loops occupy attention.
detail: By the Zeigarnik effect, pending items are remembered better than finished ones. The maker watches the clock and rehearses the meeting, so immersion never starts, and ends up working early mornings and late evenings, which inflates hours without adding output.

### POD-251114-25#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:28
claim: Managers who pull makers into meetings damage the output they check on, creating a lose-lose loop.
detail: The maker either declines and risks the relationship, or accepts and works late to compensate. As performance falls, the manager monitors more, which lowers performance further.

### POD-251114-25#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:19
claim: A manager should count both costs of a request (coordinating the time and burning a whole block) and treat a decline as protecting a commitment.
detail: Meet makers when it is worth it, but price each request. A refusal is an effort to keep a larger promise, not an insult.

### POD-251114-25#07 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:10
claim: If a meeting ruins a maker block anyway, fill that whole block with meetings and admin.
detail: This uses the lost chunk for feedback and quick wins and protects other blocks from being broken later.

### POD-251114-25#08 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:58
claim: Keep fixed meeting blocks you defend, and schedule meetings from the end of the day backwards.
detail: Few meetings are truly urgent, so push them into pre-set blocks open to regular contacts and ad hoc requests. His first half of the day is maker work, with meetings open after 1 p.m. Backward scheduling puts any booking as late as possible, so the maker block stays long.
numbers: Meetings open after 1 p.m.

### POD-251114-25#09 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:58
claim: Solo operators can protect maker time by working before interruptions start.
detail: With fewer meetings, he did maker work from 4 a.m. to 10 a.m. and handled daily business fires later, giving nine blocks a week (one per weekday, two per weekend day).
numbers: 4 a.m. to 10 a.m.; 9 blocks per week.

### POD-251114-25#10 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:59
claim: Announce slow response times for maker blocks in advance, then really produce in them.
detail: People adapt when told ahead and get upset only when expectations are missed. A maker who wastes protected time damages trust in all makers.

### POD-251114-25#11 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:18
claim: Organizations should impose quiet chunks with no messaging or meetings for maker roles only.
detail: Quiet chunks can be part of each day or whole days of the week. They apply to engineers, developers, copywriters, builders, presenters and editors, not the entire company.
