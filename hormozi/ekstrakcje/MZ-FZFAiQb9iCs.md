### MZ-FZFAiQb9iCs#01 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: An Australian short-term rental manager (55 managed properties, 5 owned, about $2M earned for owners last year, profit near $240K to her) wants $2M profit but sits in a small seasonal beach town.
detail: She takes about a 20% management cut. She can buy the book of a departing manager with 18 high-end properties (about $1M more owner revenue, around $120K a year to her for about $90K). Hormozi: take such deals, since it is her core business.
numbers: 55 properties; fee about 20%; profit about $240K; acquisition about $90K for about $120K a year.

### MZ-FZFAiQb9iCs#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:45
claim: Keep winning the local market until diminishing returns, then enter an adjacent market where you have the advantage of systems.
detail: She and the one other large manager (120 properties) already hold most of the market, so growth is limited, but a few hundred unmanaged homes remain. Work in the existing market is probably about the same as in a new one. In a new area, pitch being the established second-biggest manager.
numbers: Competitor has 120 properties; a few hundred unmanaged homes remain.
### MZ-FZFAiQb9iCs#03 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:31
claim: Build a hyper-targeted list of about 200 owners (platform listings plus ownership lookups, which AI can compile in minutes) and contact them by call, text and mail.
detail: Use every channel because the list is small. Send a regular pulse afterwards so you are present the day a tenant wrecks something.

### MZ-FZFAiQb9iCs#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:31
claim: Given roughly $4,000 a year per property and, he guesses, sticky clients, he says to this caller she'd be willing to spend, call it, up to about $1,000 to win a property, and he thinks that is reasonable; he back-of-napkin puts LTV around $40K.
detail: Retention was about 97% excluding the clients she fired (her figure); counting the ~11 fired he said roughly 8%, something like that, as the real figure; with 10% churn the life is 10 years. He says she'd probably win about 20% of the 200, taking 55 to about 95, plus the 18 gives 120, level with the competitor. He calls LTV:CAC the most basic unit of monetary arbitrage.
numbers: Profit about $4K per property per year; spend, call it, up to about $1,000; retention 97% (roughly 8% rough figure with fired, per his estimate); LTV about $40K back of napkin; probably 20% win rate on 200 leads (about 40).
### MZ-FZFAiQb9iCs#05 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:08:40
claim: The $1-3M "swamp": at $2M revenue with 25% margin, profit is $500K; reserve $100K and $400K remains, so hiring two good people erases it, and escaping needs more risk than entering.
detail: He says that typically at this size you've reached roughly the edges of your current business model's ability, and some owners are probably feeling stretched. This is why so many businesses get stuck between one and three million.
numbers: $2M revenue, 25% margin = $500K; $100K reserve; $400K spendable.
