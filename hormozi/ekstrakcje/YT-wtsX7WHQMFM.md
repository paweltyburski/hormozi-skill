### YT-wtsX7WHQMFM#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:51
claim: Top earners combine three skill groups (build, sell, lead), each a lever on money, and the whole is multiplied by a further factor he labels R.
detail: Build means making things such as software or bridges, sell means selling stuff, lead means getting people to act on your behalf. Each skill is a degree, not a yes/no, and each lets one person collect the output of many. Typical order: build and sell early, leadership develops later because early on there is nobody to lead. Many of the wealthiest people have all three skills highly developed.
### YT-wtsX7WHQMFM#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: sales-process, hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:15:18
claim: Build, sell and lead form a tripod; a business missing any one leg falls over, and each missing leg has a recognisable failure.
detail: Selling without a good product is lying at scale (his Wolf of Wall Street example). Building and leading without selling leaves a product no one knows about. Leading with no one who can build or sell produces an organised group doing nothing. Leadership is a meta skill because it gives access to other people's skills.

### YT-wtsX7WHQMFM#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:18
claim: Every new hire must either build, sell, or directly support building or selling; otherwise they are waste.
detail: A hire is a guaranteed cost but not a guaranteed return. The test for any role: does it sell to more people, or make each customer worth more by improving the product? He cites Steve Jobs as someone who built the team that built the product.

### YT-wtsX7WHQMFM#04 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:17:57
claim: Keeping equity is risk-taking that multiplies wealth; Jobs and Gates would have been far richer had they not sold or diversified.
detail: Jobs held 15% at Apple's IPO, left, and returned to a much smaller stake. Gates held about 49% of Microsoft at IPO and later diversified. Hormozi's point is that selling shares lowers both risk and upside. Treat the dollar figures as his estimates of what the held stakes would be worth today.
numbers: Jobs 15% at IPO, worth about $465 billion if kept; Gates about 49% at IPO, about $1.5 trillion if kept (his estimates).

### YT-wtsX7WHQMFM#05 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:21:03
claim: When someone brings a people problem, ask what behaviour they want started or stopped; communication that changes no behaviour is waste.
detail: A director arrived to "bridge the gap" between departments but could not name a behavioural change. His response: what do you want to happen, which behaviour should stop, which should begin? Ask the same before any meeting. If the answer is nothing, the conversation need not occur. He calls waste the biggest threat to large organisations.

### YT-wtsX7WHQMFM#06 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:37
claim: Audit a role by asking the person to explain what they do, then what each activity changes; activities that change nothing get dropped.
detail: He asked an employee to explain their job without intending to intimidate. The research they did had never changed what the company did, so it was cut. The role shrank to the few actions that drive economic activity, and the question became how to do more of those faster and better. If you cannot follow someone's explanation, assume nobody knows, and seek clarity over agreement.

### YT-wtsX7WHQMFM#07 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:25:11
claim: From scratch, start with selling: find something to sell, then learn to sell it, and skip branding, logo and website until money moves.
detail: No economic activity happens without something to exchange and someone aware of it. His first business ran on his email and phone number, sending personalised nutrition and workout plans, with no CRM or website. Later the business is limited by product, selling or capacity to deliver (needing more people); identify which is the constraint and invest time there.

### YT-wtsX7WHQMFM#08 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:51
claim: To find what to say in ads, ask customers, and for an advanced step read competitors' user reviews to find the complaints and speak to those pains.
detail: A more specific call-out converts a higher percentage of an audience, so start with the narrowest group and widen as you grow. He gives the early Facebook sequence (college students, then high school, then adults).

### YT-wtsX7WHQMFM#09 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: - | fw: Problem-Solution Cycle | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:15
claim: A psychological solution can beat a logical one: mirrors in slow elevators ended the complaints without replacing the elevator.
detail: Cited from Rory Sutherland. Customers are not purely logical, so ask whether the problem can be solved by changing perception rather than by changing the mechanism.

### YT-wtsX7WHQMFM#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:40
claim: Over many years a good builder grows an ecosystem of products that solve successive customer problems, as Apple did over decades.
detail: Each solved problem exposes the next, so the product line expands. He warns that Apple's closed ecosystem took around 50 years, so it is a long-term outcome and not a starting point.
numbers: About 50 years for Apple's ecosystem (his rough figure).

### YT-wtsX7WHQMFM#11 | PRINCIPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:30
claim: Selling leverage rises from one-to-one, to one-to-many through ads and a personal brand, to having others sell for you.
detail: Technology applies to each leg: tools to build faster (coding assistants reportedly make coders about 10x faster), platforms for one-to-many selling, and project-management or tracking systems to lift output per employee. He admits he has been hesitant with technology and calls that a limiter; he sees possible use in predictive screening of recruits.
numbers: Coders about 10x faster with Copilot (his claim).

### YT-wtsX7WHQMFM#12 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:30:17
claim: Profit has three fates: wasted, stored, or multiplied; the wealthy multiply it by lending or buying income-producing assets.
detail: Storing (gold) only waits for a higher price; buying farmland or companies yields cash each year. Lending earns interest but risks non-repayment; buying a stake takes a share of other people's build-sell-lead. In his firm, cash either returns to a company or buys another one, compounding as a percentage. Percentage growth on a larger base gives larger absolute gains.
numbers: 10% growth equals $100,000 on $1M and $100 million on $1B.

### YT-wtsX7WHQMFM#13 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:34:13
claim: Investing in yourself is the lowest-risk of three uses of money (buy a stake, hire people, buy skills); spend as much as possible on the skill that constrains you.
detail: Buying a skill means paying someone to give feedback on your performance until you can reproduce their result without them. Find which of build, sell or lead limits you most and put cash into it. The limit is the time to learn. He calls the choice to pay people ahead of him the best-returning decision of his life.
