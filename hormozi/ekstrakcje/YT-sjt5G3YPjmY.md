### YT-sjt5G3YPjmY#01 | DEFINITION | topic: finance-exit | also: management-ops | fw: Prep for Sale | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Key person risk exists when one individual is vital to a function, so that losing them would materially cut income or end the business; without redundancy you own a well-paid job, not an asset.
detail: The opposite is redundancy: several people can do each function. It counts double when the key person is the owner who leaves at a sale, because buyers will not buy a production that depends on a cog they are removing. If key people remain after a sale, the buyer can pay them to stay. Employees can be key too.

### YT-sjt5G3YPjmY#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:43
claim: Key person risk shows up in four places: marketing, sales or conversion, the product or service itself, and operations (the glue connecting people to functions).
detail: One person can be the key in several places at once, and each additional place raises the risk. A key leader with wide influence over the team is an operations key person even without owning marketing, sales or product.

### YT-sjt5G3YPjmY#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: pricing | fw: Crazy Eight | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:36
claim: For a maxed-out small service business, Hormozi says that if he had to sequence the options he would first raise prices, then raise the service ratio, then hire others to do the work.
detail: Price rise needs the least work. Raising the ratio (for example one-to-five or one-to-ten, or a four-person team serving 40 clients instead of 20) dilutes service while value still exceeds price. Only the third step removes key person risk; the first two add money. Some roles cannot be transferred (a performer), so there align incentives or create liquidity.
numbers: Example: four-person team serving 20 clients moves to 40 clients.

### YT-sjt5G3YPjmY#23 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:31:45
claim: When locking a big customer into a three to seven year term, add a breakup fee of roughly 10% to 20% of the contract value, and a notice period of 6 to 12 months.
detail: A fee discourages exit without a lawsuit. Variants: pay back the whole discount received over the term, or pay one year on a five-year deal. Sweeten the term with dedicated reps or a discount.

### YT-sjt5G3YPjmY#29 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:41:19
claim: Four defenses against key vendor risk: redundancy, mutually assured dependence, contract terms, and acquiring the vendor.
detail: Redundancy across acquisition, conversion, product, processing and banking is insurance that looks like waste until needed, and gives negotiating leverage. Dependence: grow to a large share of their revenue (small vendors are probably the likelier ones to do shady things). Terms: notice period, breakup fee, and fines for missing service levels as a hedge. Acquisition or acqui-hire if you outgrow them.
numbers: Notice 6 to 12 months.
