### MZ-z8_mWrBQHXc#01 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:55
claim: The pain-recap step exists to raise the priority of the problem in the short term, and a pitch after a good recap can be under about 320 words.
detail: Expanding the prospect's deprivation around what they want raises motivation to act now. If you describe their situation accurately, listing three problems and what each fix unlocks, they confirm it and the pitch becomes easy. Keep the pitch to three points and keep a short story ready for each.
numbers: Pitch under 320 words; three points per pitch.

### MZ-z8_mWrBQHXc#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:30
claim: Each of the five concerns is a decision fallacy with its own reframe: timing is really priorities, money is really value, and the decision maker issue is a need for support, not permission.
detail: For the decision maker, use past agreements ("does your husband want you to stay as you are?"). For a stall, help the person decide, since you hold the conversation far more often than they do (a business seller has four or five a day, a buyer 5-10 a year).
numbers: Buyers have 5-10 such conversations a year; sellers 4-5 a day.

### MZ-z8_mWrBQHXc#03 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:14
claim: For a preference objection ("can I get the result my way?"), say that changing the variables changes the outcome, then ask which matters more.
detail: Steps: ask if they like the outcome; if so, they should not change the method; then ask whether keeping their current habit matters more than the result they want. If yes, nothing will change until they do, which opens the real talk.

### MZ-z8_mWrBQHXc#04 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:31
claim: After every explanation of a concern, ask whether it settled that point, and then ask if they are ready to move on.
detail: Check each concern separately before asking for the sale again.

### MZ-z8_mWrBQHXc#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:52
claim: If you have a family and a mortgage, he recommends keeping the job while building a side business, and making the jump when side income exceeds full-time income; he would say after about six months of that.
detail: The two prime blocks are 5-9 a.m. and 5-9 p.m., a full work day. He calls himself relatively risk averse and says burning the boats has its time and place, but with a family and mortgage he would keep the job. The six months allow for volatility in the business.
numbers: 8 hours a day (two 4-hour blocks); six months above full-time income.
### MZ-z8_mWrBQHXc#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: unit-economics | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:43
claim: Volatility in a small business is a symptom of insufficient volume, and effort per sale gives a clear input-output equation.
detail: Example: 100 outreach units a day yielding one sale a week means 700 units per sale; 700 a day would yield one sale a day. He says the difference between small and large businesses is mostly how far apart the volumes are, and that most small owners do not know how much more volume leaders produce.
numbers: 100 actions a day, one sale a week = about 700 actions per sale.
