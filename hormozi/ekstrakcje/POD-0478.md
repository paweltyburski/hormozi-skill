### POD-0478#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:58
claim: Money in a bank account is a loan to the bank; interest is the price of risk and of illiquidity, so a bank paying 0.1% to 1% is a poor deal.
detail: The bank lends it on, for example to the government or on mortgages, and keeps the spread. Two things matter in any loan: the rate and the likelihood of getting repaid.
numbers: Big-bank savings rates 0.1%-1% a year (late 2022).

### POD-0478#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:52
claim: He moved nearly all cash into US Treasury bills yielding about 4%, roughly 40 times a bank account's 0.1%, with equal liquidity.
detail: His reasoning: the government is less likely to fail than a bank; T-bills can be sold almost instantly or borrowed against (about 80% of value at a low secured rate). A bank's own bond paid about 4.7%, the same bet with more yield. Figures stated for late 2022; treat as his claims, not advice.
numbers: About 4% on Treasuries versus 0.1% bank; loan against T-bills up to 80%.

### POD-0478#03 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:07
claim: Ladder the bonds by maturity, buying 6, 12, 18 and 24 month bonds so cash plus interest returns every six months and is rolled into another 24-month bond.
detail: He treats it as a cash recycling machine and does not trade price moves, since he just waits for maturity and can still borrow against the bonds.
numbers: Maturities 6/12/18/24 months; cash returns about every 6 months.

### POD-0478#04 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:16:42
claim: Three-stage investing order: first invest in your own skills, then park excess in index funds while stockpiling a war chest, then, once you are good enough at your main game, deploy that stockpile into deals.
detail: Stage one was his highest-return investment ever (courses, coaching, access to people). Stage two applies while you still do not know what you are doing. Stage three comes once your main business earns so much that more of it is less risky than the stock market. Skills learned earlier get levered with capital later.
### POD-0478#05 | EVOLUTION | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:58
claim: After selling three companies he first adopted a "barbell" of stocks, real estate and his own private companies, then dropped real estate for Treasuries plus deals he knows.
detail: Earlier he put all spare cash into the S&P 500 because Buffett advised it. Now: divest the small real estate share, keep ETFs, put the rest in Treasuries, and when a big deal appears, draw on the war chest and refill it afterwards.

### POD-0478#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:45
claim: Write big cheques only into an industry you know, with an operator you trust and a shared plan, because risk lies in not knowing the game.
detail: He cites Buffett that diversification hedges against ignorance and that risk comes from not knowing what you are doing. His private deal was in a familiar sector, with an experienced operator short of capital; he knew where the money would go and how to add value. He avoids real estate because he is not expert in it.
numbers: Expected return on the private deal about 100%-500% a year (his estimate, high risk for outsiders).

### POD-0478#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:18
claim: Buying a profitable professional-practice business with seller financing can beat index returns: a dental office at $1M revenue and $200,000 profit might cost about two times profit with half financed by the seller.
detail: Napkin maths: $200,000 down, $200,000 financed. Year one profit repays the cash, year two pays off the debt, year three is free return, about 33% a year. If you run it better and double profit, you recover everything in year one and double your money in two years, while owning two practices whose equity grew tax-free. Only do it once you are good at the trade.
numbers: Price about $400,000 (2x profit), $200,000 seller-financed; return about 33% a year, or about 100% if profit doubles.
applies_to: Skilled owner-operators of professional services (dentists) with surplus cash.
