### YT-VgEvIIpDYG8#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Choose a niche you have experience in, since a business does not need to be new, only better.
detail: Y Combinator looks for prior experience in the space. He says he doesn't even do anything new, only tries to do it better (dry cleaning or lawn care done better can still win), by applying the value equation. After about three to five years in an industry you understand the lingo, the avatar and the problems, and can see opportunities if you have an entrepreneurial hat on.
numbers: 3-5 years of experience in a space.
### YT-VgEvIIpDYG8#02 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:39
claim: An agency owner whose clients were not getting results, apart from one, was told to close the agency and build a real estate business himself.
detail: For nine months at a software company (name unclear in audio) the agency owner made no progress; the one client who made money did so because of himself, not the agency. Advice: get a realtor's licence, generate your own leads, succeed, and only then teach others if you want. Once good, the real choice is to teach or to scale the thing itself (e.g. a team of a hundred realtors).
numbers: 9 months without progress; example team size 100 realtors.

### YT-VgEvIIpDYG8#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:31
claim: Business is finding a problem, how much they think it is one, and whether you can solve and charge for it; solving it well drives referral growth.
detail: He says the books sold 100,000 copies in six months with no promotion because the offers problem was solved well. He argues you cannot teach a niche you have never operated in; goals like going from $10,000 to $100,000 a month without testimonials point to not being good at the delivery.
numbers: 100,000 copies in 6 months (earlier video says 200,000+ in first year).

### YT-VgEvIIpDYG8#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:21
claim: Start at the bottom for trench knowledge; career is a pyramid whose height depends on foundation depth.
detail: Success feels like struggle for years; progress is getting better. Passion follows competence. A third of questions he gets come from a very short horizon ("why am I not a millionaire after 90 days").
numbers: 90 days versus ~5 years; he started at 23.

### YT-VgEvIIpDYG8#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:48
claim: Every entrepreneur he knows put in about five years before anything really happened; accept that when picking a niche.
detail: The list to learn is long: leads, appointments, closing, banking, billing, payroll, fulfilment, customer journey, ascension, churn, recruiting. Compare to getting a chemistry degree in four years; some players have played for 40 years.
numbers: ~5 years; 40-year veterans.

### YT-VgEvIIpDYG8#06 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:54
claim: Start selling before reading books on sales or entrepreneurship.
detail: Books make sense only after real calls; a business starts with a bank account, an LLC and something to sell. If you do not know what to sell, you are probably not good at the thing yet.

### YT-VgEvIIpDYG8#07 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:08
claim: A future gym owner was refused loans by about ten banks for lacking industry experience, so he worked at a big gym chain for 3-4 years and then opened his own without needing the loan.
detail: He rose to personal-training manager, saved money and learned the business; he later said it was the best advice he had received.
numbers: ~10 banks declined; 3-4 years of experience.
