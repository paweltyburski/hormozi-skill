### OC-11#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Hormozi collects customer success stories by asking for a 60-second form submission and offering two incentives: being featured each month and a monthly random draw for a two-day visit to his headquarters.
detail: At the end of the free courses he asks viewers to fill in a short form describing where they started, what results they got from the material, and where they are now. Incentives: (1) each month some stories are featured on the site; (2) each month one submitter is drawn at random and invited to the Las Vegas headquarters for two days. He frames the ask around benefits beyond himself: feedback on what actually works, and proof that gives earlier-stage entrepreneurs the conviction that the material is worth their time. The pattern — low-effort ask, a lottery-style high-value reward, public recognition, and an appeal to helping others — is a simple way to gather testimonials and results at scale.
numbers: Form takes about 60 seconds; one random winner per month; two-day visit.

### OC-11#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:49
claim: Relative to the audience his media team reaches, there are few formal ways to hear results, and feedback sent as DMs sometimes gets lost, which is why he asks for a form.
detail: Hormozi explains that his public content is produced by a media team separate from his portfolio companies. The portfolio sees wins directly as companies grow, but the media side, relative to the number of people it reaches, has few formal ways to hear about results — messages in DMs get lost. A dedicated submission form turns scattered feedback into usable proof and insight into what is working.
