### POD-230819-07#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: operator-mindset | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:45
claim: Offer building is divergent thinking: many possible answers, one far better than the rest, and the work is paid because it is done once for something that lasts years.
detail: Convergent problems are binary right or wrong, what school trains; real business has unknown variables and shifting conditions. The first step is to generate many versions without judging.

### POD-230819-07#08 | CASE | topic: objections-closing | also: offers, sales-process | fw: Problem-Solution List | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:24:09
claim: One unsolved objection can block a sale: a rule that clients prepare all meals at home lost sales until an executive who ate out daily got a custom eating-out guide.
detail: He needed the sale for rent, so he offered to build a guide for eating at restaurants while still hitting the goal. She bought. ### POD-230819-07#10 | EXAMPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: offers | fw: Delivery Cube | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:19:55
claim: One problem (buying healthy food) was solved at three group sizes: one-to-one shopping trips, small-group lists and orientation, and one-to-many tours and tools.
detail: One-to-one: in-store coaching, a personal list, full shopping, a call during shopping. Small group: group shopping, weekly list-making session, delivery. claim: Cut in this order: highest cost with lowest value first, then low cost with low value; keep low cost with high value and high cost with high value.
detail: If a valuable idea costs more than you will bear, step back one level at a time to a version you can live with, or raise the price enough to justify it. Save one-to-one for big value adds; he still works one-to-one with portfolio CEOs scaling past $30M.
