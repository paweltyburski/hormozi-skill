### YT-Jc2UW3nlNBA#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:47
claim: Before sitting down to negotiate, build your best alternative to a negotiated agreement (BATNA); he says a strong one is probably the greatest source of psychological power.
detail: Negotiators who know their alternatives set higher aspirations, open more aggressively and get better outcomes (a London Business School study). Only accept deals better than your alternative. Hormozi's examples: staying in his current home instead of buying a new one, and keeping his companies rather than needing to sell them. Without an alternative, tactics amount to bluffing at poker.
applies_to: Employees, vendors and partners (M&A).
### YT-Jc2UW3nlNBA#02 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-team, finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:07
claim: Build the alternative differently for each side: employees get another offer, buyers collect several bids, sellers hold more demand than supply.
detail: An employee negotiates with a competing offer in hand, but cannot repeat it many times without losing goodwill. As a buyer of services, getting four to six bids teaches you terms to reuse. As a vendor, a queue of other customers means you can let one walk. In a business sale, collect several buying offers; when buying, keep other targets.
numbers: 4-6 vendor bids as a learning set.

### YT-Jc2UW3nlNBA#03 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:45
claim: Hold a rival offer either openly ("beat it or no worries") or silently as a floor that lets you ask for far more.
detail: Showing the offer tends to get a marginal beat. With a quiet fallback you can aim much higher. Example: with a $10 offer for inventory you will sell anyway, ask $11, $12 or even $15 instead of edging up by cents.
numbers: Rival offer $10 per unit; targets $11-$15.

### YT-Jc2UW3nlNBA#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:22
claim: Name the first number yourself and make it high, because the first number is the anchor even if you say you are not anchoring.
detail: Counter-anchoring exists but is typically not as effective as anchoring. People give too much weight to initial information and adjust too little (a Nobel-winning finding). A high anchor also changes the increments in which people think: after a $100,000 quote, the other side bargains in steps of $20,000. A lot of people avoid anchoring for fear of shorting themselves.
numbers: Example: a $100,000 quote leads to counters like $80,000.
### YT-Jc2UW3nlNBA#05 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:08
claim: If the other side accepts your price fast, say you would not have gone higher, so they feel at ease about leaving money on the table.
detail: Hormozi bought a penthouse where the seller said he would not have sold for a penny less; it felt classy and settled him, whether or not true.

### YT-Jc2UW3nlNBA#06 | CASE | topic: objections-closing | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:12
claim: In a live home purchase he countered a large seller concession with a tiny raise and then sweetened non-price terms.
detail: The house was listed at $25M, cut to $20M, and he offered $15M; the seller countered at $16.9M. He answered $15.25M, signalling small movement while the seller moved about $3M. Then he added all cash and asked for the $4M of furnishings, which technically made the second offer worse in terms, while the number rose. Lesson: anchor high, counter low, and focus on terms, not only price.
numbers: List $25M to $20M; offer $15M; counter $16.9M; reply $15.25M; furnishings $4M.

### YT-Jc2UW3nlNBA#07 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing, product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:31
claim: When a vendor has caused a defect, ask what fixing it would cost them, then use that figure as the discount.
detail: Example from a gym: a custom front desk arrived with a nicked corner. His partner asked the builder what a replacement would cost; the builder said about $1,500, and the partner took that as a fair starting discount. The vendor cannot easily retreat from his own stated cost.
numbers: Replacement cost quoted by the vendor: $1,500.

### YT-Jc2UW3nlNBA#08 | FRAMEWORK | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing, offers | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:11:17
claim: Present multiple equivalent simultaneous offers (two or three packages with different prices and terms that all work for you) to learn the other side's priorities.
detail: Their choice reveals what they value, so you can trade what matters to them but not to you. It makes negotiation positive-sum and behaves like a reverse assumed close. Example set: low monthly fee with long commitment; higher fee with premium support; pay-as-you-go at slightly higher rates for flexibility.

### YT-Jc2UW3nlNBA#09 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:51
claim: When handed several offers, ask for the best parts of each combined as a new option, or ask the other side for two or three versions of their deal.
detail: This shows you listened and may win items that cost them little. The tip came from a friend with a large deal history.

### YT-Jc2UW3nlNBA#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:40
claim: In his view reciprocity only matters in cultures where it is the norm; he wants to trade concessions in a way that stays advantageous to him, and personally splits his offer into as many pieces as possible so he can trade more times.
detail: A favour such as carrying a lunch order looks like reciprocity but has a very different value from a large ask. Break terms into many parts such as cash versus financed, 90-day versus 30-day close, and furniture included.
### YT-Jc2UW3nlNBA#11 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:22
claim: Hold many negotiable variables (speed, risk, ease, price) so concessions can be traded without moving the price.
detail: Hormozi keeps a deal sheet of about 80 changeable items. Example: hold at $15M while adding ease, then risk, then speed, as the other side moves from $17M to $16M to $15.5M. The other party often sees only two variables.
numbers: ~80 deal variables; example $17M to $16M to $15.5M versus $15M.

### YT-Jc2UW3nlNBA#12 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:01
claim: Frame yourself or your product as an investment with return, and reframe the other side's offer as cost or overhead.
detail: An employee presents hiring as a return-generating investment, like a commission, not a cost centre. A vendor quotes by return: $5,000 spent against $15,000 of maintenance savings. Many people accept the frame you give them.
numbers: $5,000 cost vs $15,000 savings (example).

### YT-Jc2UW3nlNBA#13 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:19:46
claim: Support the framing with comparable data: for home upgrades, show resale value gained.
detail: For a pool, awning or patio, ask whether the seller has data on resale uplift in that neighbourhood. If a $100,000 pool adds $200,000, the pool is effectively free; if it adds $50,000, present it as half off. Cite comparable recent sales.
numbers: Example: $100,000 pool adds $200,000, or $50,000.
applies_to: Home services businesses.
