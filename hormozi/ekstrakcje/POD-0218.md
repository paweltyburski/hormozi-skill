### POD-0218#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:49
claim: At a smoothie shop running about 15% margins, a 20% group discount wiped out the whole profit on a big order.
detail: A teenage Hormozi, working for a store owner, gave a busload of customers 20% off for volume. The owner said the business runs on 15% margin, so the discount erased all the profit from the work. He notes service businesses treat their own and staff time as if it were free.
numbers: 20% discount vs 15% margin = zero profit.

### POD-0218#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:30
claim: Answer price pushback with your margin, not a concession: with 15% margin on $100, a 15% discount means working for free.
detail: When someone says they cannot afford it, he says they can, they just don't see enough value, so work on selling instead of changing the price. Reject the fixed-cost mindset that extra revenue is all profit. His example: with a 20% margin on $100, going to $120 doubles the profit.
numbers: Margin 20% at $100 a month: going to $120 doubles profit; margin 15% at $100: $15 is all you earn.
### POD-0218#03 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:48
claim: Price payment plans above half-and-half to reflect the default risk, such as $250 twice for a $400 product.
detail: When a customer offers $200 twice for a $400 offer, bake in the chance the second payment never arrives. Those losses are known costs of doing business.
numbers: $400 offered as 2 x $250.

### POD-0218#04 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:58
claim: To lift margin by about 10 points without more overhead: sell better, use a payment plan that covers backend losses, and add fees such as activation or enrollment.
detail: He suggests studying rental car and telecom companies, which bolt fees and add-ons onto fixed costs. His example target is lifting margin from 30% to 40% by adding about 10% to price (his percentages are loosely stated).
numbers: Margin 30% to 40%.

### POD-0218#05 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:57
claim: Multiply stated price by customer count and compare it with actual revenue to expose grandfathered and backdoor discounts.
detail: Example from sales calls: a gym says $189 a month with about 100 customers (or 150), implying $18,900 a month, but revenue is about $10-11K because most members pay about $60-100 and about 10 pay full price. A study of top gyms found the best had no discounts and no special deals.
numbers: Claimed $189 x 100 = $18,900 vs actual $10-11K a month.

### POD-0218#06 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:11:33
claim: Buyer-side trick: after work is done, point out a small flaw and ask what a fix would cost; use their inflated number as the starting point for a discount.
detail: A mentor used it with contractors: the contractor lists effort to redo the work, and you accept a discount instead. Small business owners who do not protect margin will often also agree to add extras for the same price, so ask what else they can include.
numbers: Example: contractor quotes at least $500 to fix a minor flaw.
