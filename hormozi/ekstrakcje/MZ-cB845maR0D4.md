### MZ-cB845maR0D4#01 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: verticals, strategy-positioning | fw: More Better New | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: An executive career-coaching firm wanted B2B because it seems scalable; Hormozi asked why and told it to exhaust B2C first.
detail: Founder said it cannot get a first enterprise deal and the big competitor is far larger. Hormozi probed what stops tripling the consumer side (sales capacity, fulfilment).
numbers: $7.2M revenue and about $2M profit last year; ad spend $3,000 to $5,000 per day, could reach $50,000 to $100,000 per day.

### MZ-cB845maR0D4#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:20
claim: Enterprise B2B raises leverage but needs many touch points and stakeholders on both sides, cutting deals per rep.
detail: Conversion and stickiness rise with the number of people involved on both sides.
numbers: About 10 to 15 touch points per deal.

### MZ-cB845maR0D4#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:54
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: Hormozi would have this company max out its consumer (B2C) market before pursuing B2B, and if it wants variety, add a second channel rather than a new customer type or business.
detail: He said a new business is the riskiest move because everything is new (offering, acquisition and so on). If the caller has the itch for something new, a second channel with the same product is the option, though he thinks they are not near that point.
