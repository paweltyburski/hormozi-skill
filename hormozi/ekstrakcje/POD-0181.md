### POD-0181#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:02
claim: Meditation and a sedative drug share the same outcome, but the drug scores far higher on likelihood, delay and effort.
detail: Meditation: pleasant outcome, low likelihood for distracted beginners, weeks before skill arrives, effort and feeling stupid. The pill: near-certain, minutes, almost no effort. That gap explains why one is a giant pharmaceutical category and the other has no dominant players. Fitness likewise scores well only on outcome.
numbers: Meditation delay about 30-60 days; drug effect in 10-30 minutes.

### POD-0181#02 | EVOLUTION | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:26
claim: Hormozi says most people, himself included, focus on the top of the value equation; right now his focus is on the bottom (shrinking the timeline and the effort).
detail: He observes that billion-dollar companies focus on removing friction. Anything divided by zero is infinite, while pushing the top to infinity is very hard, so cutting obstacles is a tangible way to add value. For a gym: how little can the client do, can meals be handled, can they avoid thinking when they arrive.
numbers: Share of career spent on the top of the equation: ~70-75% (his estimate).
### POD-0181#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:47
claim: Netflix and Amazon created value by cutting delay and effort without changing the product.
detail: Netflix offered the same shows instantly from a phone, without a store trip. Amazon made buying one-click. Neither changed what people consumed.

### POD-0181#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: product-delivery | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:16
claim: A lead-working software tool delivers value by removing the interruption cost of handling leads.
detail: Working one lead takes about an hour end to end, over 30 to 60 days if done fully, and a new lead interrupts everything. Switching between two tasks costs far more than the sum, per research he cites: two 10-unit tasks taking 70 or 90 units (as stated, likely exaggerated). A person hired to do it is hard to manage and idle when no campaign runs. Hormozi is an investor, not the owner.
numbers: ~1 hour per lead end to end over 30-60 days.

### POD-0181#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: Activation Points | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:14:35
claim: For that tool, users who last 14 days stay, so onboarding was streamlined to remove friction in the first two weeks.
detail: The ramp-up takes 14 days; after that users do not want to go back to working leads manually. Many variables affect show rates and were standardised to make onboarding seamless.
numbers: Retention threshold: 14 days.

### POD-0181#06 | CASE | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:16:18
claim: A competitor selling to gym owners had the same CAC but about one fifth the customer lifetime value.
detail: The competitor made about $300,000 a month and admitted his product was weaker. Gym Launch earned more per customer because it kept customers longer and kept them earning longer, by shrinking the time to a win and the number of tasks needed.
numbers: Competitor LTV about $8,300 versus Gym Launch about $42,000; equal CAC.

### POD-0181#07 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:22
claim: Make the customer portal shorter, not longer, since most buyers use only a quarter of the content.
detail: About 90% do not watch all the videos, and a quarter consumed and acted on is enough to win and stay. Each update removes videos, reducing friction and speeding the time to money.
numbers: ~90% do not finish the content; ~25% used is enough.

### POD-0181#08 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: sales-process | fw: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:29
