### YT-WRW7eY6K4Jo#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:54
claim: There are four ways to change behaviour: add a positive, remove a negative (both reward), add a negative, or remove a positive (both punishment).
detail: Using a child's chores as the base case: a cookie for the chore (add positive), skipping the bus ride (remove negative), a penalty for not doing it (add negative), taking away games or dessert (remove positive). He says the example can be extrapolated to whatever you want someone to do. Punishments get people to avoid the behaviour but not necessarily to do what you want, whereas incentives are easier for getting the action.
### YT-WRW7eY6K4Jo#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:32
claim: Punishment teaches people to avoid getting caught or to avoid the whole area, not to do the wanted thing; incentives direct them toward it.
detail: A child punished for sneaking out learns to hide it. Nagging a spouse produces avoidance of the nagging. Citing Jim Collins' research, he says constant critical feedback degrades performance and mostly positive feedback improves it.

### YT-WRW7eY6K4Jo#03 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:05:16
claim: Break an activity into many small steps and reinforce each step, usually with status and feedback rather than money.
detail: Praise specific actions the day after ("that thing you did yesterday was great") so the person repeats it. Measure circumstances, incentive inserted and outcome (did the action happen yes or no), rather than guessing inner states such as inspiration.
