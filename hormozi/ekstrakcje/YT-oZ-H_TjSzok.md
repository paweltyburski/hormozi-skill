### YT-oZ-H_TjSzok#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Passive versus active income is a spectrum; judge every activity by return on time, meaning money per hour.
detail: Everyone trades hours for money, only the payout is not labelled in hours. Hormozi lost money chasing low-effort income while neglecting the work that paid. Put money into multiplying the active work that already earns, and do more hours of anything paying well above your average hourly rate.

### YT-oZ-H_TjSzok#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:10
claim: Divide last year's income by 2,000 for pay per working hour, or by about 8,000 for pay per waking hour.
detail: The second figure is the real hourly value of your time. An activity paying above it is probably worth doing. He once found one activity paid 15 times his average hourly earnings, and said he should do as many hours of it as possible until all his hours are like that.
numbers: 2,000 working hours per year; about 8,000 waking hours per year; one activity at 15x his average hourly rate.
### YT-oZ-H_TjSzok#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:28
claim: Convert prices into hours of after-tax work; if you would not work those hours first to get the item, do not buy it.
detail: As a smoothie shop worker on $6.75 an hour (about $6 after tax), a $13 lunch cost two hours of a six-hour shift and a $30 shirt a full shift. Feeling that cost changed his buying. Rule of thumb: if you cannot afford it two to five times over, skip it. Saving does not make money, but it lets you take bigger risks.
numbers: $6.75 per hour pre-tax; $13 lunch about 2 hours of work; $30 shirt about 1 shift.
