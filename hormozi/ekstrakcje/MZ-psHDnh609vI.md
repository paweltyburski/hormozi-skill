### MZ-psHDnh609vI#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: verticals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Agencies reach $1M, $3M and even $10M a year easily in almost any model; beyond that talent becomes the limit.
detail: The firms that reach $10M mostly serve small and very small businesses. To study an industry, look at what the biggest players do differently: the largest agencies serve the Fortune 100 on long contracts with reliable payers.
numbers: $1M-$10M easy range; five-year contracts at $5M a year for the biggest.

### MZ-psHDnh609vI#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: 80/20 Customer Analysis | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:52
claim: Agency scaling is scaling the avatar: choose clients who can keep their word and know how to sell, because your service is "more of" what they already do.
detail: Telltale sign of too low an avatar: you start doing their closing or sales for them. One client set at 10+ headcount saw 1% churn at 50 staff, so build for that group.
numbers: churn ~1% at 50 staff (as stated).

### MZ-psHDnh609vI#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:27
claim: With the right avatar, price elasticity changes: you may charge 2x and make about 5x profit, accepting slower revenue growth.
detail: Selling to wrong-fit clients to pay bills brings operational drag, churn and team morale costs. Choose which debt (talent, tech, financial or reputational) is easiest to repay. Counter-intuitive moves, like selling to fewer, better-qualified buyers, are what most people avoid.
numbers: 2x price gives about 5x profit; sales velocity of 3-4 a month cut to 1 acceptable.
