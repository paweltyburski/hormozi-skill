### MZ-D_E4Be4Or_E#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:19
claim: Misery is a constant whether the business grows, plateaus or he does nothing.
detail: He found a year off after the sale as miserable as growing or plateauing, so he sees no alternative he enjoys more than business and keeps playing.

### MZ-D_E4Be4Or_E#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:06
claim: Aim to be useful rather than happy, because usefulness forces service and learning.
detail: At 19 he dropped happiness as a goal. Being useful to someone requires learning, which gives a task, and continuing to play improves play.

### MZ-D_E4Be4Or_E#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:58
claim: He ranks years to keep hard times relative.
detail: Ranking the last 13 years, the current one is the fifth worst overall but the worst of the last eight. Losing everything twice in one year ranked second worst; the first business year was the worst.

### MZ-D_E4Be4Or_E#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:00
claim: Suffering over a loss is optional: ask what actually changed in daily life.
detail: After a bad decision cost $17M in cash, he noted that food, spouse, job, team, building and city were unchanged; only a number was gone. Then ask what he will do next, which is almost always to learn and improve.
numbers: $17M cash lost on one decision.

### MZ-D_E4Be4Or_E#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:58
claim: Not raising your lifestyle with success makes you resilient to swings.
detail: If the baseline stays low, upward volatility changes nothing and downturns bite less. He compares it to anchoring 100 feet below the waves instead of riding the surface.

### MZ-D_E4Be4Or_E#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:40
claim: People adapt to suffering like to pleasure, so business keeps varying the type of pain.
detail: Repeating a problem (a bad employee, a lawsuit) hurts less the second time. Staying in one vein dulls pain but also forfeits the novelty of growth, so you lose both joy and pain.
