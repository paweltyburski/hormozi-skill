### YT-q6SdmgIji30#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Take jobs to learn, not to earn, in your 20s; do not haggle over small pay differences while you are still acquiring skills.
detail: His example: among about a thousand applicants for a video-editor job, one candidate asked for $3,000 more a year on a good offer, missing that he was in a learning season. A few extra thousand a year is trivial against the value of the skills; the premium employers pay is for what you already know.
numbers: About 1,000 applicants; request of $3,000 a year extra; extra $10,000-$25,000 a year judged inconsequential.
applies_to: people early in their careers

### YT-q6SdmgIji30#02 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:09
claim: Fix the learning budget first and automate it; make spending manual and inconvenient.
detail: Take the education money off the top into a separate account, let it pile up, and deploy when you spot something worth learning; do not ask what is left at month end. Split money into accounts (rent, investing in yourself, food and car) so only the spendable portion reaches your main account, and prefer cash, which hurts more than cards.

### YT-q6SdmgIji30#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:09
claim: Shop from a pre-approved list only, and buy takeout once your hourly earnings pass about $15 because it saves time worth more than the cost difference.
detail: Aimless shopping equals wasted money. Eating out costs roughly $10 a meal, twice a day, about $600 a month; groceries are perhaps $100 a week against $150 a week for eating out, so the net $50 is less than the value of cooking and cleaning time.
numbers: Break-even at more than $15/hour; $10 a meal, $20/day, $600/month; $100 vs $150 a week.

### YT-q6SdmgIji30#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:03
claim: Choose roommates and a micro-community that share your goals, and pay for convenience that saves time.
detail: The worst roommates distract; the next worst are responsible people who lack your goals and undercut you. Never listen to people whose goals for your life are smaller than yours. Overpay for being near a gym, a highway and cheap food, and live with more people in a better area. He paid $400 a month in a house of seven.
numbers: $400/month in a seven-person house.

### YT-q6SdmgIji30#05 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:12
claim: Measure prices in hours worked, and compete on the amount saved each month, not on income.
detail: At $20 an hour with about 20% payroll tax, a $100 shirt costs about six hours of work. As a teenager earning $7.25 an hour, a six-hour shift netted about $36 after tax, and lunch next door took $15 of it. Budget toward an investment target (such as $2,500 saved of $5,000) and chase personal records on savings. Check the account every morning.
numbers: $100 item = about 6 hours at $20/hour; $7.25/hour shift of 6 hours = $43 gross, about $36 net.

### YT-q6SdmgIji30#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:15
claim: Model the right season of someone's life: copy what a successful person did before they were rich, not what they do now.
detail: Reputation is built from what you have done and what you said you would do, both coming true. A private plane does not make anyone rich; sequence matters.
