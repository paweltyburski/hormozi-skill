### MZ-eJV27iH3jB4#01 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A luxury landscaping and maintenance firm in Aspen is advised to simply raise prices instead of hunting for more clients.
detail: The owner does $7M and wants $30M; he thinks pricing and local talent are the blockers. His clients are wealthy and spend other people's idea of "expensive" easily. Hormozi calls this the best problem to have: say a higher number and change the process, then watch churn.
numbers: $7M revenue now, $30M goal; 70% of revenue is recurring maintenance, the rest one-time landscaping.
applies_to: Premium home services selling to affluent clients.

### MZ-eJV27iH3jB4#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:46
claim: Keep raising price until you start to see more churn; that is the point he treats as the top limit.
detail: Raise the price and watch for more churn; when it appears, he treats that level as the top limit.
### MZ-eJV27iH3jB4#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:46
claim: With thin margins, a modest price rise moves profit disproportionately.
detail: At an 11% net margin, a 20% price increase with unchanged costs roughly triples profit; Hormozi says the business could probably sustain it without changing much.
numbers: 11% net margin last year; +20% price is about 3x profit.
### MZ-eJV27iH3jB4#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: Price to Value Discrepancy | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:31
claim: A recurring fee that follows a large one-off project is judged against that project, so it feels small and can rise at the back end.
detail: Clients who just spent $80,000 to $180,000 on landscaping see $500 to $1,000 a month as insurance on that investment, so the maintenance price has plenty of room.
numbers: Install spend $80,000-$180,000 versus maintenance $500-$1,000 per month.

### MZ-eJV27iH3jB4#05 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:17
claim: In a small, isolated market, recruit nationally and pay for relocation.
detail: Advertise roles beyond the local area and offer a negotiable moving package that works like a signing bonus. The owner already does this.
