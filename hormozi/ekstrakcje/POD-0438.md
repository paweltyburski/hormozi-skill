### POD-0438#01 | EVOLUTION | topic: outreach | also: lead-getters | fw: Core Four | novelty: updates | ts: 00:00:50
claim: He used to think cold outbound mostly suited higher-ticket B2B, or recruiting affiliates for consumer goods, but now sees it can work for direct-to-consumer too.
detail: The belief broke when he worked out the maths of street product sampling. His conclusion: any of the core four can work for any business if the unit economics allow, whether door knocking, handing out samples, calls, email, message or mail.
### POD-0438#02 | CASE | topic: outreach | also: unit-economics | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:36
claim: Two people handing out 1,000 energy drinks a day cost about $500, or 50 cents per can, and pay back strongly if enough recipients become buyers.
detail: Labour was six hours at about $20 an hour each (roughly $250-$270), product about $0.25 a can ($250). A subscriber taking 30 cans a month at about $90 leaves roughly $67 gross profit after cans and shipping. Over a five-month life that is about $335. If 4% of recipients subscribe, 40 buyers give about $12,000 against $500.
numbers: $500 daily cost; $0.50 per can handed out; $67 monthly gross profit; $335 LTV (5 months); 4% conversion gives ~$12,000. Cost figures are his estimates.

### POD-0438#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: retention-ltv | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:11
claim: A bar-products business gets 63% of first-order customers to buy again, very high for a consumable.
detail: Repeat purchase rate decides whether sampling or other free-product outreach pays off, which is why a good product matters.
numbers: 63% repeat rate on a first order.

### POD-0438#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:02
claim: Marketing always works; the question is how efficiently, so work out break-even conversion from your LTV and cost per contact.
detail: Even at $100 LTV and only 1% conversion, 1,000 samples give 10 customers worth $1,000 against $500 spent. Use LTV in gross profit terms, not revenue.
numbers: $100 LTV x 10 customers per 1,000 = $1,000 vs $500 cost.

### POD-0438#05 | TACTIC | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:39
claim: Fund acquisition with a credit card: if 30-day net free cash exceeds CAC you repay it and the customer cost nothing.
detail: Net free cash means revenue minus fulfilment cost in the first 30 days. Example: $1,000 of ads that returns $1,000 revenue with $500 fulfilment leaves you $500 short that month. Fix pricing or the offer until that gap is closed. The card limit then rises as you repay.
numbers: Example: $1,000 ad spend, $500 net cash back, $500 shortfall.
