### YT-5cOwh-8scu8#01 | CASE | topic: content-brand | also: lead-getters | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A podcaster whose business jumped after one ask concluded that more asks meant more money, then saw returns and listenership fall.
detail: His sales came almost entirely from listeners. Each extra ask earned less than the previous one and listeners left because goodwill was spent.

### YT-5cOwh-8scu8#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Big media shows the ask ceiling: TV carries about 13 ad minutes per 47 of content, Facebook about one ad per four posts.
detail: These companies have studied how much they can ask without losing audience, so Hormozi treats the TV ratio as the max for asking; he personally prefers about 98% give and 2% ask.
numbers: TV 13 minutes ads to 47 minutes content; Facebook 1 ad per 4 posts, which he calls almost a three to one ratio.
### YT-5cOwh-8scu8#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:38
claim: The audience is the asset, not any single piece of content.
detail: A post vanishes from feeds in days but leaves new people who stay, provided you do not ask too often. Keep giving and eventually inbound demand replaces public asking.
numbers: One example piece added about 300 people to the audience.

### YT-5cOwh-8scu8#04 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: sales-process | fw: Integrated vs Intermittent Asks | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:43
claim: Cap asks at a few percent: integrated asks inside long content, and in short-form one offer post per roughly ten value posts.
detail: Even a five-minute pitch inside long content must keep at least 3:1 give to ask; Hormozi prefers about 98% give. With several short posts a day, ask in at most about 3% of them, once or twice a month.
numbers: Integrated about 98:2; intermittent at most 3% of posts, 1-2 offer posts a month.

### YT-5cOwh-8scu8#05 | DEFINITION | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:29
claim: The cost of an ask is goodwill, not money, so judge each ask by how much audience it loses.
detail: Treat an ask as a commercial interrupting your own content; always under-ask.

### YT-5cOwh-8scu8#06 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:29
claim: Launch a new business with a short "declaration of business" post to your network, offering something free that helps them.
detail: Say you may be known from school, work or life, say what you now do, offer a free benefit, invite them to follow. With no prior content, show what you have done first. Hormozi's post offered free training to people who donated $500-$1,000 to a charity of their choice, so they valued it and gave testimonials.
numbers: That post led to his first 20 paying clients.

### YT-5cOwh-8scu8#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:31
claim: Holding back a big ask usually pays, because audience and leverage compound and the same ask later is worth far more.
detail: A tequila founder refused a celebrity's bid for two thirds of the business; the celebrity later built his own brand worth about $6B. Hormozi likewise declined early mentoring and endorsement requests. Operationalised patience means deciding what to do meanwhile.
numbers: An ask can be 10x-100x bigger 12-24 months later.

### YT-5cOwh-8scu8#08 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:36
claim: Content raises demand while you control supply, so keep supply below demand to push price and profit up.
detail: Pricing power comes from supply and demand; content shifts the demand curve in your favour.
