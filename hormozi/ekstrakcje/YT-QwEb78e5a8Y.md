### YT-QwEb78e5a8Y#02 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: BAMFAM | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:27
claim: Group-session programme losing half its members because almost nobody attends is told to onboard with one-to-one sessions before the group, book the next session at the end of each one, and add reminders.
business: coaching-education — back-end programme of semi-private/group live sessions (eight a week) sold by closers; $195/month or $595 per four months; ~7% of members attend
detail: Owner traces churn to non-attendance. Hormozi, first the logistics: the closer should book a specific onboarding call (BAMFAM), not just point to the calendar; add four to six one-to-one sessions (start with two; adjust price) before members join the group; in the last minutes of each session have everyone book the next; send automated and, ideally, manual reminders. Sales: offer an either/or — start in the group or one-to-one with a named coach; if they choose group, gift one or two private sessions. See #04.
questions: what does onboarding look like?
numbers: $195/month; $595 per 4 months; 50% churn on each (owner's "30% weighted", unclear); ~7% attendance; 8 sessions/week; 4-6 onboarding sessions (start with 2)
speakers: owner: business facts; Hormozi: advice and narration to camera
applies_to: group coaching, classes or gyms with weak attendance and churn

### YT-QwEb78e5a8Y#03 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: GROWTH Onboarding | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:53
claim: Hormozi: do not drop new members straight into a group; run a few one-to-one sessions first, so the group becomes something they graduate into.
detail: He calls this a gym onboarding process (ideally ~six private sessions) that lets newcomers settle in and learn the culture. Putting the group on a pedestal makes it a prize they earned, which raises buy-in.

### YT-QwEb78e5a8Y#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:27
claim: Hormozi: when you do not know what to do, collect data on what precedes the problem first; usually the solution then becomes obvious.
detail: Low consumption typically precedes churn, so track consumption before fixing renewals. He says nine in ten owners with a problem collect no data on its precursors and waste time debating hypothetical fixes.

claim: Bleacher-rental company whose leaders do not delegate is told to hire for the smallest skill gap and to train by naming specific behaviours to stop, start and keep.

### YT-QwEb78e5a8Y#11 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:41
claim: Hormozi: hire for the smallest skill deficiency; if one gap takes days to train (sales) and another months (kindness), hire the person who already has the slow one.
detail: In his view a trait is just a bundle of skills. A candidate with a large useful skill set and a small gap can be hired if the gap is named up front as something to fix.

### YT-QwEb78e5a8Y#16 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: pricing, scaling-constraints | fw: Internal Core Four (Recruiting Mirror) | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:25:09
claim: Physical therapy and training business with surplus demand but no practitioners is told it is really in the recruiting business, and to fund hiring in proportion to each therapist's gross profit.
business: health-medical — physical therapy and personal training for 35-45-year-old athletes, two locations, ~$3M+ revenue, good margins, more demand than capacity; raised prices; started an e-commerce side line
detail: Owner asks: keep raising prices toward concierge, or crack recruiting. Hormozi: a supply-constrained business; don't break a working model. Story: a friend's cleaning company had $25 CAC but could not hire reliable cleaners. Design an attractive career path; recruit via the Core Four plus headhunters and staff referrals; outreach likely beats ads for therapists. Since a therapist yields ~$350K gross profit a year, a $500 referral bonus is weak — offer ~$20K (contingent if needed), and paying $50-100K could be rational.
questions: any trouble getting customers? / gross profit per therapist per year?
numbers: ~$3M+ revenue; ~16,000 open PT positions (owner); ~$350K gross profit per therapist/year; referral bonus $500 vs ~$20K; up to $50-100K justified (his view); friend's cleaning CAC ~$25, maids at ~$15/hour
speakers: owner: business facts; Hormozi: diagnosis, advice, narration
applies_to: service businesses limited by scarce licensed practitioners
