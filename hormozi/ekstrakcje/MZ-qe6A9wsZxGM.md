### MZ-qe6A9wsZxGM#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Simple writing reads as smarter; imitators who use complex words and sentences miss why his sounds good.
detail: Simplicity takes more effort, and readers perceive the result as more intelligent.

### MZ-qe6A9wsZxGM#02 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Write in present tense and active voice, and use simple tenses.
detail: Mixed tenses confuse. Prefer actions with a clear doer: "we beat iron", "we carried the body".

### MZ-qe6A9wsZxGM#03 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Cut adverbs, because they usually stand in for a weak verb.
detail: Replace "shut the door really hard" with "slammed the door".

### MZ-qe6A9wsZxGM#04 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Keep sentences short, almost always at most one comma; split or rework sentences with two.
detail: A clause pile between commas can usually be removed or turned into a second sentence.
numbers: maximum one comma per sentence.

### MZ-qe6A9wsZxGM#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:01
claim: His vocabulary is under third grade, ideally fifth grade at the most.
detail: He says his vocab is under third grade, and ideally fifth grade is the top limit; he frames these as how he writes.
numbers: under third grade typical; fifth grade top limit (ideally).
### MZ-qe6A9wsZxGM#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:01
claim: Use positive language: tell people what to do, not what to avoid.
detail: Negatives are hard to picture (the pink elephant problem). "Stay inside" is clearer than "do not leave the facility"; only very common phrases like "do not stop" work as shorthand.
