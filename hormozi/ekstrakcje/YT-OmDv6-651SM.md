### YT-OmDv6-651SM#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: sales-process | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Hormozi thinks five P's need to be in alignment before the sale: promotion, prospect, price, product and process.
detail: Hormozi checks the whole pipeline against five P's: the right promotion to the right prospect, at the right price, for the right product, through the right process. Only when these line up does it make sense to blame how the script is delivered on the call itself.
### YT-OmDv6-651SM#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:30
claim: A proven script can fail when a rep delivers it with different tone and emphasis than the person who wrote it.
detail: Two delivery components matter: tone and cadence (pauses, stress on particular words). The same words change meaning with emphasis, so a script that works for the founder may flop for a rep who says identical words differently. Check delivery before rewriting the script.

### YT-OmDv6-651SM#03 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: sales-process | fw: Game Tape Review (Five A's) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:30
claim: Have reps replay recordings of calls where they were on a hot streak, to copy their own tone and pauses.
detail: Tell reps to find calls from days when they felt unstoppable and study them repeatedly: the micro differences in pauses and slowing down on key points are what separate a master from an average closer. Beginners without such tapes should study other people's hot calls. Great closers recreate success on demand instead of going cold.
numbers: Analogy: a .250 against a .300 baseball hitter is about one extra hit every two or three games, a small gap that defines mastery.

### YT-OmDv6-651SM#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: sales-team | also: retention-ltv | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:55
claim: Reps closed their highest rate on days when cohort graduates had just shared success stories.
detail: At his gym-coaching company, graduation days stacked many client interviews in one day. Sales reps listened to them and then went onto calls visibly more convinced, and close rates peaked. Fresh proof heard by the rep raises conviction, which the buyer can feel.

### YT-OmDv6-651SM#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:41
claim: You cannot promise a perfect product, but you can have perfect intentions and be the best shot the buyer has.
detail: Owners know every flaw, which erodes conviction. His fix: separate product (never perfect) from intentions (fixable and checkable), then ask whether this prospect is better served by you than by anyone else. Where the buyer must do part of the work (weight loss, coaching, business), you cannot guarantee the outcome, but you can honestly claim the best chance.
applies_to: Education, coaching and skill-based services where the customer does half the work.

### YT-OmDv6-651SM#06 | EXAMPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:07
claim: Early in gym weight-loss sales he knew half of sign-ups would stop attending, and learned to sell anyway from the best-shot stance.
detail: He knew the dropout statistic but could not tell prospects not to join. He reframed: they still have to do their part, but he is their best option, so he could speak with eye-contact conviction.
numbers: About half of new gym sign-ups were no longer attending after six months.
