### POD-0095#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: product-delivery | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Limit large-group training to three sessions a week because space and capacity make daily training unprofitable.
detail: With daily sessions a gym reaches full capacity before it earns the profit owners signed up for. Hormozi sets this as pure math, not a belief about training.
numbers: A full gym on daily sessions may only make $2,000-$3,000 a month profit.
applies_to: Large-group boot camp and micro gyms

### POD-0095#02 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:53
claim: Use a full-body workout every session so members can attend on any days.
detail: Most clients miss sessions in a push-pull-legs split and keep hitting the same muscle group; with full body every visit, irregular attendance is fine. Hormozi trained full body for years and set state records.

### POD-0095#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: product-delivery | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:45
claim: Most clients' goals (look and feel better) can be reached with three 45-minute sessions a week plus conditioning.
detail: He says most people get about 80% of their muscular gains in the first 9 to 12 months, and that he himself has probably not gained any real muscle in roughly the last 10 years.
numbers: 3 sessions a week x 45 minutes; most people ~80% of gains within the first 9-12 months.
### POD-0095#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:22
claim: Do not train clients the way you train yourself; mix modalities so different personalities all find something addictive.
detail: Different personality types gravitate to different exercise styles (powerlifters, runners, yoga). Including cardio gives a stronger endorphin hit for some members. Design sessions to maximise adherence and limit risk, since clients are not seeking a heavier deadlift.

### POD-0095#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: product-delivery | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:51
claim: Optimise sessions for the maximum good for the maximum number of people so you can have a business.
detail: As a hypothetical, he says sessions of two hours (or an hour and a half) with mobility work would not make a profitable business. Training people individually is fine, but it is not a scalable model.
