### POD-0117#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: content-brand | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:21
claim: A trainer with 8,000 followers earned about $200 per month from them after hours of daily effort, roughly $3 per hour.
detail: Case used in the episode title: two years of building the account, two customers from it, and two to three hours a day thinking about posts, refreshing and replying. At 60 hours a month for about $200 the time return is trivial.
numbers: 8,000 followers; 2 customers; about $200 per month; 60 to 90 hours per month; about $3 per hour.

### POD-0117#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: content-brand | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:54
claim: Ask a trainer who makes too much content what money, hours and return per hour Instagram has produced.
detail: Questions: how much money has it made, how long have you worked on it, how many hours in total, what came back, what is the return per hour. Hormozi says such a trainer should be focused on learning to sell, making better ads or teaching a better class rather than details that do not matter.
### POD-0117#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:15
claim: With limited time, attention and money, pick the one or two actions to take, and in Hormozi's opinion, for the average gym owner he talks to, working leads or making more ads would probably beat making content.
detail: Of the thousand things that could help, choose by what each costs in time and attention. Content cost includes hidden items: choosing the right picture, the angle, and feeling bad about a flop. He says replacing it with working leads, nurturing, selling and delivery, or making ten more ads per week would probably make more money.
numbers: 10 more ads a week as the alternative use of time.
### POD-0117#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:54
claim: Chasing followers before doing anything influential is the sign of a beginner entrepreneur.
detail: Large audiences of billionaires came after value and money, as a byproduct. Accounts of 60,000 followers can still fail to pay rent when the owner never learned business. He also notes rivals with hundreds of thousands of followers earning less than Gym Launch, which helps more gyms in a month than they do in a year.
numbers: 60,000 followers yet unable to pay rent (example).

### POD-0117#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:42
claim: Gym Launch spent its time on a fourth full rebuild instead of speaking events and visuals.
detail: The team re-recorded all videos, polished them and refreshed words, software and scripts using proven best practices. His view: improving the product beats collecting footage for social posts, and influence grows as a result of helping many customers.
