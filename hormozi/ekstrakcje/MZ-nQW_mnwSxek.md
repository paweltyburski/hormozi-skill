### MZ-nQW_mnwSxek#01 | DEFINITION | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A business exists once you have an LLC, a bank account, a payment processor and a stranger who agreed to pay you for something.
detail: He lists these four steps as doable in one day, so waiting years is a distraction problem, not a barrier problem.
numbers: 4 steps.

### MZ-nQW_mnwSxek#02 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: lead-getters | fw: Warm Outreach Steps | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:31
claim: Message your contacts personally (video and text), ask them to introduce you rather than buy, and show consistency so they risk less social capital.
detail: Mention when you met, say what problem you solve, and ask who they know. Referrers fear recommending someone fly-by-night, so consistency raises referrals. Add risk reversal: the first ten are free to gather reviews and learn.
numbers: Example base of 400 phone contacts and 300 friends; 90% of responders offer themselves as the first customer for free (his claim).

### MZ-nQW_mnwSxek#03 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Free with Consumption | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:53
claim: "10 by 10": give ten people ten hours of free one-to-one work each (or five by five), since even basic labour has a visible value.
detail: Do the work in front of them (build the site live, meet several times weekly) to get real-time feedback and finish the hours faster. No tech needed, you just text to schedule. You learn the delivery cycle, so you are not afraid of a hundred clients.
numbers: 10 people x 10 hours; alternative 5 x 5.

### MZ-nQW_mnwSxek#04 | SCRIPT | topic: sales-process | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:06
claim: Pre-frame the free hours: on call one ask the problem and permission to describe what you do at the end if it was useful.
detail: Sequence: clarify the problem, ask permission up front, deliver, then at the end recap progress, check they are happy, and propose a paid engagement for problems one to three (for example six months). Accumulated reciprocity makes yes easy, and the hours reveal the offer you should sell.

### MZ-nQW_mnwSxek#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: objections-closing | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:52
claim: Refusing to work free is entitlement and often hidden insecurity; free work comes with terms such as feedback and reviews in several formats.
detail: Terms: feedback through the project, a review in written and video form at the end. Business owners pay to get testimonials, so a glowing review beats a paid job with no review. Starter flow: free, then limit your time and charge, bump price in steps.
numbers: Price rise of 20% every five clients; a testimonial worth more than $500-$1,000 (his example).

### MZ-nQW_mnwSxek#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-process | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: BANT | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:10
claim: Free time should go only to prospects who pass BANT; trust for a higher-ticket sale needs hours.
detail: He describes BANT (budget, authority, need, timing) as old IBM logic. The reason timeshares use six-hour blocks is that trust takes time; you spend free hours on the right people to prove competence.
numbers: Six-hour timeshare blocks as the analogy.
