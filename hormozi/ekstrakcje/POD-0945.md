### POD-0945#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:40
claim: Diagnostic question "why can't we do more of what works?", routed through five blockers: metrics, market, model, money, manpower.
detail: Start by asking why you cannot do more of what already works; if the answer is "never thought of it", do it. If you cannot say what to do more of, get metrics first (but incomplete data is normal; decide directionally or from first principles, and gather a bit more data around irreversible one-way doors). Then check, in order: market too small, model wrong for your goal, money, manpower. Called out as taking about 10,000 calls to figure out.
numbers: ~10,000 calls behind the model.
### POD-0945#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:58
claim: In a mature business the control usually beats the variant, so changes should be small; more of what works is the best risk-adjusted move.
detail: A business is like bricks that already form a standing structure, so a random change is more likely to hurt than a gap-filler. Early on, try anything since nothing is working; once past hundreds of prior changes (about 100 worked, 500 did not in his example), changes must be small and strategic.
numbers: Illustration: 100 changes that worked, 500 that did not.

### POD-0945#03 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:01
claim: A lounge in a commune of 140 people earning $1,000/month asked about marketing; the market, not marketing, was the limit.
detail: Extreme example of a market-size constraint; he told them to just text everyone. Usually (9 in 10 or 99 in 100) the market is not the limit; a 20,000-person town for a brick-and-mortar can be, solved with another location.
numbers: 140 people; $1,000/month revenue.

### POD-0945#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:02
claim: If the model seems capped, almost any business can reach $100M via more units, franchising or licensing.
detail: Question is whether the vehicle fits the goal (dry cleaning will probably not reach a trillion). A national or internet business can pretty much reach $100M; brick-and-mortar needs more locations, franchise or licence.
numbers: $100M baseline goal.
### POD-0945#05 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: unit-economics | also: paid-ads | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:48
claim: When "money" blocks growth, separate: leads too expensive, conversion too low, or lifetime gross profit per customer too low.
detail: Benchmarks across industries help locate it: for a mass-market consumer weight-loss webinar, $20 per lead is probably too high; at $2 per lead the issue is probably the conversion process or pricing. Low LTGP example: a $100 or $100/month offer where customers leave after 3 months while competitors keep them 12; he says you have to fix that hole or nothing will work.
numbers: $20 vs $2 cost per lead (consumer weight-loss webinar); churn 3 months vs 12 months competitors.
### POD-0945#06 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:40
claim: Home services and construction are often capped by cash flow when customers pay in 90-180 days; fix with financing, offer structure and pulling cash forward.
detail: Metrics and market fine, but receivables of net 90 to 180 days (or 120 days) cause cash crunch. Solutions: financing relationships and offer changes (see canon on payment terms).
numbers: Payment terms 90-180 days.
applies_to: HVAC, plumbing, roofing, construction

### POD-0945#07 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: pricing, scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:39
claim: Manpower constraint: measure how you attract talent, widen supply (relocation, cross-training), and fix commodity pricing that blocks above-market pay.
detail: Chain for skilled services (accounting, legal, healthcare, trades): cannot hire because cannot pay above market; cannot pay because priced as commodity; priced as commodity because offer and sales motion are weak. Unlocking needs about three steps in sequence.

### POD-0945#08 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:12:26
claim: Each business type has a "shape" of difficulty; know which difficulty is a feature of the model, not a bug.
detail: E-commerce and physical products: stepwise, inventory then distribution until supply chain breaks. Services: steady but slower. Information or media: a few million fast, tens of millions hard. Software: hard to start, then scales hugely after product-market fit. Home cleaning: constraint is reliable low-skilled labour (speaks English, honest, punctual), so the edge is a unique talent proposition; fitness: talent is easy (people love it), customers are hard, so it is a marketing and sales business.
