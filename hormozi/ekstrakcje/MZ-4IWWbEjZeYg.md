claim: A third-generation firefighter runs a $1.7M wellness program for first responders, aims to help 100,000 of them, and cannot decide whether to leave the fire service.
detail: The job is part of his identity; the business earns far more than the salary (the ratio is garbled in audio). He feared they would not see him as legitimate if he left.

### MZ-4IWWbEjZeYg#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:35
claim: Questions like push versus pivot, or spend versus save, are dichotomies to manage, not problems to solve, and there is no correct answer.
detail: The question is what life you want at 85: contentment day-to-day or maximal impact. He holds a meaning and impact skew; Steve Jobs had impact without apparent happiness.

### MZ-4IWWbEjZeYg#04 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:29
claim: For big decisions, write out the decision framework so you can reread your reasoning when doubt returns; it eventually becomes a shortcut.
detail: Giving yourself permission to keep the current path lowers the angst. Also look for ways to reduce time at the firehouse while keeping identity and friends.

### MZ-4IWWbEjZeYg#05 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:06:34
claim: Layla's frame: ask which problem you would prefer, since you will have one whichever way you choose.
detail: Leaving costs relationships and not seeing the lives you save; staying costs the feeling of unused potential. Pick the trade and make the cost smaller where possible.
