### YT-BO_59sGxztY#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: There are four routes to ultra wealth, defined by whose money goes into whose business.
detail: 1) Other people's money into your business (venture-backed founder). 2) Your money into your business (bootstrapper). 3) Your money into other people's businesses (investor). 4) Other people's money into other people's businesses (fund manager). He started studying this after reaching $100M net worth at 31, looking for the way from $100M to $1B. Routes 1 and 4 are the top-100 wealth routes; ordinary investing is the safe way to get rich, not the richest.
numbers: $100M net worth by age 31.

### YT-BO_59sGxztY#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:22
claim: Every business takes on some debt from day one; the choice is which kind (financial, talent or technology debt).
detail: A bootstrapped business cannot afford the best people or tech systems, so it carries talent debt and technology debt. Founders take financial debt (equity capital) in exchange when the business needs huge capital to start, or is a winner-take-all market where speed secures a monopoly-like position (social networks).

### YT-BO_59sGxztY#05 | DEFINITION | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:54
claim: Dilution and primary versus secondary: new money buys a share of the company, or part of it can be paid to the founder personally.
detail: Example: a founder who owns 80% of a company after raising $20M owns less of a bigger pie. A "$500M valuation" raise means shares were sold at that price: selling 2% at $500M brings $10M in cash. Secondary means part of the investor's money goes to the founder, so a founder with a billion-dollar asset and little cash can decide without panic. His stated split: a $10M company plus $2M new money becomes $12M with the investor at 20% (arithmetic is loose in the audio; 2/12 is about 17%).
numbers: 2% sold at $500M valuation = $10M; $10M pre-money plus $2M investment.

### YT-BO_59sGxztY#10 | FRAMEWORK | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:19:12
claim: Build a compounding vehicle: customers who never stop buying, or a distribution base that never stops selling.
detail: Two companies each sell to 100 new customers a year; one loses prior customers while the other keeps them, so the second has steadily growing base without having to sell faster. Type 1: repeat-purchase or subscription (Coca-Cola, Starbucks, Netflix). Type 2, for businesses with little repeat buying (home services, solar, roofing, one-time products): a growing network of sellers, like Prestige Labs' roughly 1,000 locations, which acts as chunked-up recurring revenue even if end customers cycle.
numbers: Starbucks average customer spends $14,000 over time (his claim).

### YT-BO_59sGxztY#15 | FRAMEWORK | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:31:32
claim: Acquisition.com's investment thesis has four tests: high cash flow, clear growth path, a focused founder, and the jockey above all.
detail: 1) Profitable, cash-generating businesses paying monthly distributions, which return principal and give fast feedback. 2) Growth through more customers or higher lifetime gross profit per customer, with a path they can see. 3) A founder focused on one avatar who can say no (avoid "niche slap" for everyone). 4) Jockey over everything, in markets not about to die. Among qualifying companies they attack each constraint in turn; lawn care example: 10x ads needs 10x salespeople, then more mowers.

### YT-BO_59sGxztY#20 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:47:51
claim: A fund needs proprietary deal flow and an edge, and each maps to LTV:CAC for deals.
detail: Proprietary (off-market) deal flow means one-on-one negotiation, so you pay less than in an auction (as a house seller wants many bidders; a buyer doesn't). Edge means industry knowledge, such as funds specialised in manufacturing, consumer goods, software, services or media. CAC for deals is the purchase price and terms; lifetime value is the value after you accelerate growth. Better buy price plus better exit value creates the spread over the market.
