### POD-0133#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:53
claim: Run the company on four numbers: increase opportunities, increase closing percentage, keep churn below a ceiling, and increase profit.
detail: Opportunities means inflow; closing percentage covers nurture and sales; churn ceiling for gyms is below 3% (period not stated; likely monthly, unclear in audio); profit means cutting cost that does not return value. If one lags, focus everyone on it and maintain the others.
numbers: gym churn ceiling 3%.
applies_to: Gyms; adapt the numbers to your business.

### POD-0133#02 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:53
claim: Ask every role how they can move each metric from their angle, and repeat the four numbers often.
detail: Trainers, front desk, billing and managers can all name ways to add opportunities or cut churn; sales staff know referrals but need prompting for more. Staff rarely know owner KPIs; poor performance usually comes from not knowing what is wanted, not malice.

### POD-0133#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:31
claim: Cross off any goal that cannot be shown to move one of the four metrics, then ask what else the same time and money could buy.
detail: Follow-ups: what will it cost in time and attention, and what alternative use could raise the metric more? Putting a concrete budget on it, such as $5,000 and 40 hours of someone's time, pushes creative thinking. "Bet your child's life" is his image for picking the single best lever.
numbers: example budget $5,000 plus 40 hours.

### POD-0133#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:31
claim: Cut effort that returns less than it costs, such as one dollar of time and attention yielding fifty cents.
detail: Many activities add some value but at a disproportionately low return. Standing on the sidewalk with a sign will not hurt but is a poor use of the same attention.

### POD-0133#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:02
claim: For most people, most of the time, prioritising profit over growth is a good idea for a small business; his own case differed once he had enough money and reinvested in top-line growth.
detail: For his first seven years he focused fully on profit. At the time of recording, top line mattered more because he reinvested. He defines small business as under 100 employees.
numbers: 7 years profit first; small business under 100 employees.
