### POD-0821#01 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: An owner of many auto shops on thin margins served a niche whose vehicles cost $10,000-$20,000 a day off the road, yet charged them the same as everyone; Hormozi told him to add a speed pass.
detail: Most customers would not care if the return took a week, while this niche is not price sensitive. Diagnostic questions: are they price sensitive, do you price them differently, would most customers be fine waiting.
numbers: $10,000-$20,000 per day lost income for the niche.

### POD-0821#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:21
claim: Selling speed by reordering the delivery queue is close to 100% margin, because the work, staff and time are the same whichever customer goes first.
detail: If you build for customers A, B and C in sequence, moving C first costs nothing, yet C may pay twice as much. E-commerce sells fast shipping routinely while service businesses rarely sell speed.
numbers: Customer may pay about 2x for faster delivery.

### POD-0821#03 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:31
claim: For surge delivery, ask suppliers "what would it take" to rush, and charge two to three times the normal price; even if your costs rise 20-50%, margin in dollars and percent both go up.
detail: Example: a kitchen remodel normally taking three months delivered in one for a customer paying 2-3x. Faster delivery also brings word of mouth. Do not sell from your own wallet: you know how to do the job, your customer does not, so the value of speed to them may be huge.
numbers: Price 2-3x; extra cost assumed +20% to +50%; 3 months compressed to 1.

### POD-0821#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:05
claim: Service firms vary price by features but rarely by speed or risk, though those are two of the four value elements (outcome, risk, speed, ease).
detail: He says he has found no element of value outside those four. Each can be traded in or out of a package and priced separately.

### POD-0821#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:37
claim: Spotify beat free downloads on speed, and a long free-burrito line made Hormozi wish he could pay $30 to skip it: fast beats free.
detail: Delay is what people hate most; Amazon and Netflix also won on speed and convenience.

### POD-0821#06 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:37
claim: A restaurant with fuller weekends can print a weekend menu priced 10-20% higher; at 10% margins and half its volume on weekend days, that adds 50-100% to profit.
detail: Math: a 10% premium on 50% of revenue lifts revenue by 5% of total, which on 10% margin is a 50% profit gain; 20% doubles profit. Rule: when demand passes supply on a price curve, shift price up.
numbers: Weekend days = about 50% of volume; margin 10%; +10% price = +50% profit; +20% price = about 2x profit.

### POD-0821#07 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:58
claim: A gym can sell peak-hour and off-peak memberships, shifting flexible members out of the crowded 5-6 pm classes and serving more people overall.
detail: Example: 30 bikes in an oversubscribed 6 pm class where 40 could fit; people who could come at 7 am but lack an incentive crowd it, and the ten who cannot come at other times go to another gym.
numbers: 30 bikes vs potential 40 attendees.

### POD-0821#08 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:15:32
claim: A hair salon's Friday and Saturday cuts and colours should cost about 20% more than midweek; demand falls on those days and fewer customers are turned away.
detail: Facebook ads in Q4 and Uber on Friday nights show the same surge logic. The ideal of charging each person their maximum is impractical, so use data to find high-demand pockets.
numbers: Salon surcharge about 20% on peak days.

### POD-0821#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:24
claim: In his view, if you have more demand than supply at certain times, it means you were mispriced then; consider raising prices for those times, and if you are a chicken about it, change prices for new customers only.
detail: Old customers cycle out over time. If you fear saying you decided to charge more, you can cite cost increases, inflation or rent.
