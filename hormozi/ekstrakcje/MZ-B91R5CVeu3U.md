### MZ-B91R5CVeu3U#01 | CASE | topic: unit-economics | also: scaling-constraints, verticals | fw: Payback Period | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A financial planning firm for people 55+ is cash-constrained because each new advisor takes six to eight months to break even.
detail: Leads come from organic YouTube and podcasts; the firm turns away leads for lack of bandwidth. Fully used, the current team yields top line of about $45-50M and EBITDA just over half.
numbers: Revenue $12M, goal $100M in four years; team 30 to 64; about 1,000 clients; fee starting at 1% of assets; advisor cap about 125 clients, about $2.5M revenue each (as stated); payback 6-8 months per advisor.

### MZ-B91R5CVeu3U#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:28
claim: To grow faster than cash flow allows, either collect an upfront plan credited to year one or raise equity.
detail: Example: a $10,000 plan credited toward first-year services pulls cash forward. Otherwise growth is pinned to new customers onboarded and assets activated.
numbers: Example upfront plan $10,000.

### MZ-B91R5CVeu3U#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: retention-ltv, operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:52
claim: The biggest risk of growing too fast is reputational: service quality falls and lead flow dries up.
detail: Bootstrapped firms can't go below zero cash; each margin point given up is recaptured as growth but raises risk. The opportunity door stays open if proof grows, but poor service can close it.
