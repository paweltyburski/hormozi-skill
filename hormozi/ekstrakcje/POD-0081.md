### POD-0081#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:49
claim: Consistency is a product the market pays for, so aim for a boring, wildly profitable business.
detail: Examples: a fast-food chain whose food is mediocre but identical everywhere, and a burrito chain that added only two menu items in 20 years yet grew from a few sites to over 2,000. A founder who is moody also makes the team and customers feel threatened.
numbers: Burrito chain: 2 menu additions in 20 years; 2,000+ locations.

### POD-0081#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:26
claim: Usually urge owners of several gyms to downsize step by step (three to two, two to one) and make the remaining single site hugely profitable.
detail: A second location often lifts revenue but leaves profit flat or lower, since owner time spreads and liability doubles. Hormozi advises three to two and two to one, then chasing profit from the single site. He says he is not impressed by location counts.
numbers: Clients above $100,000 a month, aiming for $200,000 a month from one location (profit, as stated).
### POD-0081#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:36
claim: Point your innovation at making the current offer better, not at new programmes; customers never stay satisfied.
detail: Expectations reset upward, like a four-minute mile becoming the daily norm. That gives endless things to improve, so never resent customer complaints. Best ideas come from customers and from other industries.

### POD-0081#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: promotions-launches | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:45
claim: In marketing "new" and "free" are the most powerful words, but in operations better beats new.
detail: Improvements do not catch attention the way new things do, so the marketing wrapper may be new while the operation changes by small steady improvement.

### POD-0081#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:58
claim: Eliminate the bad experiences: even one in ten negative experiences can sink a business.
detail: One bad class can lose a member, so fix the exceptions, not the average (planes on time 90% still leaves 10%). Disney's rule of thumb is cited as 39 magic moments to offset one tragic one (as he states it). A burrito chain fixed double-meat mistakes with a simple physical marker.
numbers: 10% negative experiences described as enough to drown a business.

### POD-0081#06 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:33
claim: Audit every customer touchpoint for gaps, such as no regular outreach and no follow-up after missed attendance.
detail: Example: track attendance and follow up if a member has not appeared in three days.
numbers: Follow up after 3 days of absence.

### POD-0081#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:27
claim: At an event of CEOs running $10M+ revenue companies, Gym Launch's net profit was nine times the next highest.
detail: Hormozi credits focus; most others ran three or four businesses. What took a founder from zero to one is not what takes the business from one to two.
numbers: 9x the net profit of the second highest company.
