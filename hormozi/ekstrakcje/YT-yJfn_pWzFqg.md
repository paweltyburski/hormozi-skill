### YT-yJfn_pWzFqg#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: He had nine failed partnerships, started almost simultaneously because he believed he needed good partners, and has had no partner for four years while making all his money after.
detail: He says he may be the common factor. The failures came from not knowing how to structure roles and agreements.
numbers: 9 failed partnerships; no partner for 4 years at the time.

### YT-yJfn_pWzFqg#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:48
claim: The only reason to take a partner is that they bring money or skills you lack, and two people with the same strengths almost always fail.
detail: Starting with a buddy often meets an emotional need for company rather than a business need. If both are promoters, one is not required.

### YT-yJfn_pWzFqg#03 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:48
claim: Draw up a written partnership agreement with a lawyer first, because the process shows how the person acts under stress.
detail: Walk through scenarios: one wants to work and the other does not, one leaves, one no longer wants to continue. Ideally two lawyers; if money is short, use a free template and pay a lawyer for an hour. Most partnerships end from unspoken expectations.
numbers: Lawyer for 1 hour as a budget option.

### YT-yJfn_pWzFqg#04 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:57
claim: Every business has three pillars: acquisition (the rainmaker), product (fulfilment) and operations (payroll, taxes, legal, IT, finance, HR).
detail: They are not necessarily equally weighted. Operators are easier to find and often paid without equity, whereas acquisition and product skills are scarcer. A partner or hire should fill one of these. A lot of marketers with no fulfilment or organisation leave customers unanswered.
### YT-yJfn_pWzFqg#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: management-ops | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:43
claim: At Gym Launch he held acquisition and product while his wife ran all operations, giving three legs of the stool.
detail: Most of his audience are promoters, so the first hire should be a strong operational second.

### YT-yJfn_pWzFqg#06 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:09
claim: Divide partner roles with a time study: log your activity every 15 minutes for a week, put all activities on a board, and assign each to one owner.
detail: If everyone owns everything, no one owns anything. Divide by skill; if skills are identical, either specialise or do not partner.
numbers: Time log interval: 15 minutes, for one week.

### YT-yJfn_pWzFqg#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:42
claim: Giving half the equity away at the start is the biggest dilution of what you will earn if the business grows.
detail: Also settle working hours, vacation, weekends and expected responsiveness before money is involved.

### YT-yJfn_pWzFqg#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:55
claim: Married couples starting a business often succeed because one is the front-end hunter and the other the organised back-end operator.
detail: They also provide each other an emotional sounding board.
