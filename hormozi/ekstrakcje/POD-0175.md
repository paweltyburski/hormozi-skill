### POD-0175#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:48
claim: To get people to change, describe the pain they will stop suffering, not the gain they will receive.
detail: Hormozi spent weeks telling gym owners that full availability would triple their income, with no response. When he said they were wasting two thirds of ad spend because leads could not book, the response was strong. People weigh avoiding pain more than chasing upside, so say what they will no longer have to do.
numbers: Cost framing: paying about 3x what is needed to bring a customer in.

### POD-0175#02 | CASE | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:48
claim: A gym spending $9,000 a month on ads could spend about $3,000 with the same arrivals if it fixed appointment availability.
detail: Saving $6,000 a month goes straight to the bottom line. Against a typical gym margin it is a large share of profit.
numbers: $9,000 to $3,000 a month in ads; $6,000 a month, about $72,000 a year, saved; average gym ~12% EBITDA.

### POD-0175#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: sales-process | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:54
claim: Opening availability can lift schedule rate and show rate sharply; he says he has data to back this up.
detail: Defaulting to full availability and blocking only the times you know you cannot meet would take schedule rate from 40 to 60 percent, he says. Tracked across the whole funnel from click to close. Stacked over months, if a gym went from 10 to 30 new clients a month by opening availability, it compounds.
numbers: Schedule rate 40% to 60% (a 50% increase in throughput); show rate 40% to 60%; leads showing 15% up to 35-40%; hypothetical of 10 to 30 new clients a month.
applies_to: Appointment-based local services
### POD-0175#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:04
claim: Treat extra profit and avoided cost as equal in dollars when effort and complexity are the same, and decide on the math.
detail: Owners jump at a $3,000 rent cut but ignore an equal $3,000 gain, such as an affiliate arrangement with no extra operations. Compare options by the monthly swing in dollars, not by whether it feels like growth or saving.
numbers: Example: $3,000 a month saved on rent equals $3,000 a month added profit.

### POD-0175#05 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:30
claim: Be available to sell at all times, like a convenience store, and hire part-time help if needed to cover hours.
detail: A business does not need customers every minute but must be open for them. Many gym owners offer only morning hours while clients work nine to five. Short-term personal commitment for about six months can double or triple growth, then pay someone to take the hours.
numbers: Example: a six-month push aimed at 2x to 3x walk-ins.
