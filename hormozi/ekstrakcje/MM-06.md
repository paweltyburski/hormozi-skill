### MM-06#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models, paid-ads | fw: Three Levels of Advertising | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Advertising has three levels: lifetime gross profit above CAC, 30-day gross profit above CAC, and 30-day gross profit above twice CAC.
detail: Level 1 (LTGP > CAC, ideally ≥3×): the minimum to be a business that can make money and grow. Level 2 (30-day GP > CAC): you never spend your own money to grow. Level 3 (30-day GP > 2× CAC): you are never limited by money at all — the goal of the whole course.
numbers: L1 LTGP ≥ 3× CAC ideal; L2 30-day GP > 1× CAC; L3 30-day GP > 2× CAC.

### MM-06#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:45
claim: The 30-day window matters because nearly every business can borrow interest-free for 30 days through credit cards or credit lines.
detail: Some banks extend interest-free lines for 60 or 90 days. If 30-day gross profit beats CAC, you put acquisition on the card, repay within the month, and recycle the cash — growing without ever carrying debt. Hormozi says he grew all his businesses this way, owning everything, without selling equity or taking on debt.
numbers: 30 days interest-free standard; sometimes 60–90 days from banks.

### MM-06#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: - | fw: Three Levels of Advertising | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:01
claim: At level two — e.g. $160 CAC and $180 gross profit in 30 days — customers are effectively free, but growth is capped by your credit limit.
detail: You can only acquire as many customers per cycle as your credit limit buys before repayment, unless you reinvest profits beyond the 30 days into marketing instead of taking them out.
numbers: CAC $160 vs 30-day GP $180.

### MM-06#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: product-delivery | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:49
claim: Once acquisition funds itself, operations usually become the bottleneck before advertising does.
detail: You will typically be unable to handle the customers you can bring in, and that — not finding customers — throttles your advertising.

### MM-06#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Three Levels of Advertising | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:03
claim: At level three each customer repays their own acquisition cost and funds the next one, so acquisition compounds per customer rather than per month.
detail: Using "house money", every new customer arrives with two more pre-loaded. Doubling can happen as fast as you can turn customers over — even daily. On a simple 30-day reinvestment loop it goes 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 new customers a month, ending year one around 4,000 total customers if all gross profit is reinvested. 2× is the minimum; 3×, 5× or 10× multiplies faster. Hormozi says this is how Skool is growing now.
numbers: 30-day loop doubling → ~4,000 customers after 12 months.

### MM-06#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:15
claim: "I need money to make money" is backwards — people with fewer skills need more money; money-model skill removes money as the growth limit.
detail: Once reinvested advertising profits cover new customers, advertising stops being the bottleneck.

### MM-06#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: money-models | also: verticals, scaling-constraints | fw: Three Levels of Advertising | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:52
claim: In Gym Launch's first year Hormozi got a 100:1 return on advertising — about $100K spent for $10M.
detail: The business was then (and still is) limited by market size and the operational capacity to scale service, not by profitable acquisition. He reports reaching this level in virtually every business since his first gyms, thanks to a better money model.
numbers: $100K ad spend → $10M; 100:1.

### MM-06#08 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Three Levels of Advertising | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:13
claim: Do not move on from the money model too soon — keep improving it until acquisition effectively pays for itself.
detail: He says most people move on from this part too soon. Keep making the money model better until it works for free (30-day gross profit covering CAC, with twice CAC as the level-three goal), and only then move on.
