### YT-CojS0DwflXc#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:00
claim: A seller only needs three kinds of statements: questions, restatements of what the prospect said, and short stories used for the pitch.
detail: Questions lead the prospect to conclude for themselves that they should work with you. A restatement (what I hear is the issue, what you tried, why it failed; right?) shows you listened. Nothing is a declarative lesson, so the call coaches and does not teach.

### YT-CojS0DwflXc#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: Three Core Beliefs | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:10
claim: Teaching tactics on a sales call does not work because buyers act only after their beliefs are broken.
detail: In early Gym Launch he gave owners pricing advice; they took notes, did not buy and ignored it. A friend's rule: everyone benefits from breaking beliefs but only about 10% use tactics, and those are likely not buyers.
numbers: ~10% of people benefit from tactics (his friend's estimate).

### YT-CojS0DwflXc#03 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:43
claim: Deliver the pitch as pre-packaged mini stories and analogies that win agreement on what must happen, ending each with "does that sound fair?".
detail: Sequence: restate their goal, state what they must change (e.g. learn to market, work out, change eating), ask agreement, then give an analogy (teach to fish, like exercise more than TV, foods they like). Skip the mechanics such as programming or tools. Keep several stories ready.
applies_to: Complex or coaching-type sales

### YT-CojS0DwflXc#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:43
claim: Hold the pitch back until you expect a yes; keep asking and restating until the prospect asks how to work with you.
detail: He compares it to a lawyer who avoids questions with unknown answers, or someone proposing marriage already sure of the reply. If they demand an early explanation, say you need more context first, which retakes the frame. Otherwise offer only to explain how it works if they want to hear.

### YT-CojS0DwflXc#05 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:38
claim: If the first 5 to 10 minutes show you will not close them, shift the frame with a challenging question.
detail: Example: say that it sounds like they are out of luck and there is no way to succeed; a contrarian prospect then argues themselves into the possibility. Closers ask hard questions that challenge the prospect's paradigm.
numbers: Signal appears within the first 5-10 minutes.

### YT-CojS0DwflXc#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-team | also: sales-process | fw: Game Tape Review (Five A's) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: The more complex the deal, the more business knowledge the rep needs; transactional B2C sales can be highly scripted.
detail: Reps selling to business owners are really consultants. Owners and small teams tend to slip into teaching, found while reviewing recorded calls.

### YT-CojS0DwflXc#07 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: sales-team | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:32
claim: Look at talk time in call recordings: engagement drops where a rep talks for five minutes straight at the belief-breaking part of the pitch.
detail: Per Hormozi, the thing to avoid there is technobabbling about the program. Question order comes naturally from really caring, and scripts exist to mimic a caring conversation.
