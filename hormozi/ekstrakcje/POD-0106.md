### POD-0106#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: money-models | also: content-brand | fw: Goodwill (Price-Value-Cost) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Income tracks goodwill multiplied by the number of offers, but each offer you make lowers goodwill.
detail: He credits the first formula to Frank Kern. More offers add conversion points but erode goodwill, so there is a balance. From the customer's side an offer is just an act of providing value.

### POD-0106#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:32
claim: Avoid nickel-and-diming: every offer costs goodwill, but revenue varies widely between offers, so Hormozi says most times a lot of things people would charge for should be offered only as goodwill, and you should pick the select offers with the most upside.
detail: Example: an agency charging an extra 10% each for creative, then copy. Small monetisable offers still cost goodwill without matching return. Members events or an internal challenge can be goodwill.
### POD-0106#03 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:47
claim: Free additions still need selling: explain their value to the team and to customers as if you would charge, then say "since I love you, here it is".
detail: Without that framing customers do not see the value and you do not bank the goodwill. Selling it to staff makes them pass on the enthusiasm.

### POD-0106#04 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:34
claim: Fill the base level first and build up goodwill, so a higher-tier offer later fills almost on its own.
detail: Model: with 200 to 300 members, an exclusive higher tier fills without effort. He compares it to pumping air, then releasing the valve. Constant small upsells damage the brand and cap profit.
numbers: example base: 200-300 members.
