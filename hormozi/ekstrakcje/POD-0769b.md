### POD-0769b#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: sales-process | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Congruence means keeping the same promise from ad to landing page, thank-you page, setter or closer call and onboarding, restating the original benefit at each handoff.
detail: Each handoff between people, departments or platforms is a leak in a pipeline; at each one remind the prospect what they did and why. Level zero is no handoff at all: leads that never get a reply, where most businesses sit.

### POD-0769b#02 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: sales-process | fw: CRO | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:33
claim: Match the landing page headline to the ad, and test headlines for the main benefit while varying only the ad hook.
detail: Headline tests need less volume than ad tests because ads vary more. Test the benefit phrase on the page, then keep that benefit as the core of every ad and change just the hook. The thank-you page repeats the benefit with the next step.

### POD-0769b#03 | SCRIPT | topic: sales-process | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:43
claim: Open every setting, closing and onboarding call by recapping what the person did and the benefit they came for.
detail: Sequence: name the original reason for opting in, show they are in the right place, then proceed. Follow-ups and onboarding repeat the same benefit rather than a new one. Assume prospects forget; they get thousands of messages and have other crises.

### POD-0769b#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:16
claim: The prospect moved first, which gives you a lasting advantage: remind them of what they already did, using commitment and consistency.
detail: Any engagement counts: opening a cold email, a like, a comment, a click, an opt-in, a booking. Saying "you asked for this" keeps you leading, unlike pushing a product they never asked for.

### POD-0769b#05 | TACTIC | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:06
claim: Audit your funnel yourself: click your own ad, opt in, and read every follow-up email and text in the order a prospect sees them.
detail: People change three or four things in the process and do not review the whole path from the prospect's eyes for six months or more. He says this is among the easiest uplifts when he buys companies.
numbers: audit cadence implied: more often than every six months.

### POD-0769b#06 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:06
claim: Small lifts at about five funnel steps combine to roughly a 28% revenue gain, which at about a 28% margin can double profit.
detail: Steps: click, opt-in, schedule, show, close. Revenue rises with no added cost, so profit grows more than revenue. Usually audits find 15-25% at several points, sometimes doubling or tripling.
numbers: about 28% revenue lift; 28% margin example; 15-25% per spot found (his estimate).
