detail: His own gym tactics would differ from what he teaches, because advice must work for people who are not him. Offers and sales processes are built so that anyone can run them, even if a few operators could do better a different way. He compares it to a weight-loss program that helps most people well.

### YT-3ktgTBNlg7M#02 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: finance-exit | fw: Sales to Fulfillment Continuum | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:49
claim: Hypothetically he would flip six staffed gyms to a fully remote accountability model during COVID.
detail: Cut or renegotiate rent, move recurring members to nutrition and accountability, keep the two best trainers per site, drop other staff, and spend the owner's time on sales. He argues that remote fulfilment has better margins and that the forced change is a permission to make a move he had already argued for eight months before.
numbers: See next atom.

### YT-3ktgTBNlg7M#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:49
claim: His worked math leaves roughly $100,000 a month in profit from six gyms moved to remote.
detail: Each gym has $30,000 a month in recurring membership payments only (no supplements or other cash), so $180,000 a month in total. He assumes about 25% drop off because staff, unlike owners, do not nurture members, leaving about $135,000 (he states roughly $135,000-140,000). Twelve trainers at $3,000 a month cost $36,000.
numbers: 6 gyms x $30,000 = $180,000; 25% churn; ~$135,000 recurring; 12 trainers x $3,000 = $36,000; ~$100,000 left (before other costs).
applies_to: Multi-site gym owners

### YT-3ktgTBNlg7M#07 | TACTIC | topic: sales-team | also: outreach | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:45
claim: Trainers spend the first two hours daily on 100 cold reach-outs and book interested people onto the owner's calendar to close.
detail: Daily quota of reach-outs, with at least one appointment per day expected. The rest of the day: contact existing members, and record one workout per day. Role split: trainers set, the best salesperson (the owner) closes.
numbers: 100 reach-outs per day, 2 hours; at least 1 booking daily.

### YT-3ktgTBNlg7M#08 | SCRIPT | topic: outreach | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:45
claim: Open cold messages by asking whether they know anyone who wants help, not by pitching.
detail: Ask if they know someone trying to stay in shape (with a light, humorous goal). The recipient sees you are not fishing, goodwill toward small businesses pushes replies, and many answer that they want it themselves; then book a call.

### YT-3ktgTBNlg7M#11 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:05
claim: In a stressed market, spend the first 15 minutes of a sales call making the prospect feel hopeful.
detail: Prospects start below neutral (quarantined, anxious, bloated), so resistance is higher. Convey hope and confidence that you will endure; people buy more of that feeling, and the weight-loss vehicle is secondary. Train staff to do the same.
numbers: First 15 minutes of the call.
