### YT-kGfzLjPNsBU#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: paid-ads | also: promotions-launches | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:46
claim: Ad headlines for fixed-length promotions follow duration, then benefit or result, then a power word.
detail: Examples: 28-day, six-week, 42-day, then a result (six pack, bikini, booty, detox, "skinny friend"), then a word such as challenge, blueprint, accelerator, intensive, program or transformation. It goes on the ad banner. He says an ad lacking it converts much less. The duration shows the commitment and when the result arrives, without being a guarantee.
applies_to: Fitness and other time-boxed programmes

### YT-kGfzLjPNsBU#02 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:41
claim: Show the benefit as a status or a concrete visible change, such as "skinny friend", a little black dress or two belt notches.
detail: The "skinny friend" angle frames the prospect through other people's eyes. Banner imagery (jeans and belt) can reinforce the result.

### YT-kGfzLjPNsBU#03 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:04
claim: The banner headline and the creative need not match, but the subhead, copy and landing page must stay congruent with the promise.
detail: Creative (group workouts, sweaty photos, testimonials, selfie pitch) is there to stop the scroll. After the click the page should match the offer and the result stated in the ad; he calls this unglamorous consistency what separates good marketers from mediocre.

### YT-kGfzLjPNsBU#04 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Call-Outs | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:05
claim: Use the first copy lines to name who the offer is not for, using your own client data to pick the disqualifiers.
detail: Excluding prospects shows selectivity and is as strong as naming the target. Gym Launch's ads limited to gym owners with a lease and over 25 clients, after analysing which clients earned most and stayed longest. On Facebook, describe the audience without addressing the reader directly.
numbers: Example filter: gym owner with 25+ clients and a signed lease.

### YT-kGfzLjPNsBU#05 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:44
claim: Match banner colours and fonts to the audience; avoid hardcore black with yellow or red and stencil lettering for women.
detail: Male-leaning ads work with black plus green or blue. Some fonts suit both, so match palette to the prospect; colours and fonts sometimes matter more than the words.

### YT-kGfzLjPNsBU#06 | SCRIPT | topic: paid-ads | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:36
claim: Structure the ad copy as call-out, lead-in tied to the headline benefit, implied authority, the opening, then a call to action.
detail: Example: the subhead links to the "skinny friend" idea ("it is habits, not genetics"), then credibility ("done with over a thousand people in this area"), then the spots now open in the programme.

### YT-kGfzLjPNsBU#07 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:07
claim: Run separate funnels for the same campaign by demographic, such as men and women, then older and younger women.
detail: Message and page design change with each group, and finer splits mark a better marketer.
