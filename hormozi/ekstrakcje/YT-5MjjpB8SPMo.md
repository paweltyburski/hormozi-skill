### YT-5MjjpB8SPMo#02 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:39

detail: Questions must follow a format or are not answered. ### YT-5MjjpB8SPMo#03 | CASE | topic: management-ops | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:46
claim: To move a community's calls from the founder to a team, step the founder out slowly and train the team's presenting skills.
detail: A participant's team of coders answered members' questions but members still wanted the owner. Hormozi: customers are bloodhounds for value, so if they go to you, you give more value than the team. He cut his daily calls from five days a week down to one, replacing them with others; attendance roughly halved and then held. Training meant drilling answers like phone-sales scripting (play, pause, say it another way, repeat) and building up the team's credibility. Most people refuse this, which is why most businesses are not sellable.
numbers: 5 days/week down to 1; took 12 months; attendance about halved.

### YT-5MjjpB8SPMo#07 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: promotions-launches | fw: Point of Greatest Deprivation | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:20
claim: Running two community events a month made churn rise; locking events behind a 12-month tenure and always announcing the next one fixes it.
detail: A member held monthly in-person events open to all members and saw churn go up. His advice: make the event something earned, so the thought shifts from "I'm missing out" to "I can't wait". He learned this when churn spiked right after a huge event (he says about $2M spent on events) while it fell going in: after the feast, members are satisfied, so open a loop about a better next one. Upsells happen at the point of greatest need, not greatest satisfaction.
numbers: 2 events/month; lock after 6-12 months; ~$2M events spend (his own).

### YT-5MjjpB8SPMo#10 | FRAMEWORK | topic: retention-ltv | also: paid-ads, lead-getters | fw: Epiphany Bridge | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:46
claim: Success-interview grid: internal and external, across before, during and after; the key question is the concern they had before buying and why they bought anyway.
detail: Before: what life was like internally (feelings, fears) and externally (numbers others could see). During: what made them act, the biggest concern, and what resolved it (for example seeing testimonials from gyms like theirs). After: external and internal change. Because the answer comes from a prospect, it addresses the objection for other prospects. One interview yields content, bonus material and, cut to 60-90 seconds, a lifecycle ad.
numbers: 60-90 second ad cut.

### YT-5MjjpB8SPMo#11 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: unit-economics | fw: 80/20 Customer Analysis | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:14:42
claim: A competitor in the gym space had the same sales velocity and similar CAC as Hormozi but customers worth $5,000 against his $42,000, so he made about 70 times more profit.
detail: Hormozi looked at his top 20% of customers, found their commonalities (gym owner, signed lease, at least one employee, 30+ members) and made them the qualification across ads and funnel, saying who it was and was not for. Real gym owners were relieved. To attract better customers you must be willing to turn away worse ones; the offer, bonuses, marketing and funnel all change, otherwise it is only a complaint.
numbers: LTV $5,000 vs $42,000; ~70x profit; 20% top customers; 1 employee and 30 members minimum.

### YT-5MjjpB8SPMo#13 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:28
claim: Separate low-ticket from high-ticket by implementation versus things that are unlimited and have no marginal cost; the non-scalable parts are what you charge more for.
detail: As long as price points are not close, cannibalisation is not a problem; the right customer wants to pay more for support and implementation. Add a higher ticket when you have more team and can bear the reputation risk, since anger at a $7 month is far smaller than at $10,000.

### YT-5MjjpB8SPMo#14 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Activation Points | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:04
claim: Offer the mid or high ticket only to members who hit an activation milestone; another participant (Sam) added that you know it is time once successful students ask for more.
detail: Do not pitch the whole community.
