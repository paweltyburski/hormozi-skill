### MM-08#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: verticals | also: unit-economics, money-models | fw: Low Barrier Offer (LBO) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:13
claim: The old gym "low barrier offer" model — $19–29 trials with nothing else to sell — yields about $600 gross profit per 10 show-ups in 30 days.
detail: Gurus taught cheap trials ($19 month, $21 for 21 days). Of 10 people who show up, about 8 buy the trial; the industry converts ~35% of trials, so about 3 continue. Discount front ends work only if there is something to sell afterwards.
numbers: 10 shows → 8 trials → ~3 members; 30-day GP $600; $60 revenue per show; $75 per trial; trial conversion ~35%.
applies_to: Gyms and similar local membership businesses.

### MM-08#03 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: verticals, unit-economics | fw: Sell to Goal | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:46
claim: Hormozi's gym model — sell to goal, then meals, supplements, a continuity commitment and a prepay, all within 30 days — produced about 16× the revenue per show-up of the trial model.
detail: Fewer closed at first, but they were sold to their goal: one took a $2,000 package, most took ~$500 programs, one took about $1,000. Then meals, then supplements (average $85 gross profit per person), then two people committed to a year after their program and prepaid it at a further discount. That cash let his gyms outspend old-model gyms 25:1; many big franchises that dismissed it faded.
numbers: 30-day GP ~$9,644 (transcript reads "96.44") vs $600; revenue per show $960 vs $60; revenue per close $1,928 in 28 days; supplements $85 GP/person; 25:1 spending advantage.

### MM-08#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:35
claim: Business models and offers are not IP, so monetization innovation is a speed game: exploit it before others catch up, then innovate again.
detail: Anyone can copy a headline or a "free six-week challenge". Leaders capture roughly 80% of the profits because by the time followers arrive the early gains have been harvested. For incumbents, the right move is to copy a better model immediately — as Facebook did with Snapchat's stories — not to posture against it; the gym franchises that mocked his model could not out-execute the math.
numbers: Leaders take ~80% of profits (his estimate).

### MM-08#05 | FRAMEWORK | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Downsell Flow | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:23
claim: Each layer of the gym money model had its own downsell ladder, so prospects who refused the top option were walked down to something they would buy.
detail: Service: sell to goal → smaller package → free trial with penalty → free trial without commitment → free nutrition orientation (non-buyers bought ~$300 of supplements there). Supplements: full bundle → four-pack with extras → four-pack → on continuity → without continuity → extras only. Meals: biggest to smallest. Continuity: to goal → smaller package → month-to-month. Prepayment: whole package → prepay for a discount → prepay next month → simply commit to continuity. Timing: front-end offers within 48 hours; later ones around days 14–21 and 21–28.
numbers: ~$300 supplements sold to people who bought no service.

### MM-08#06 | FRAMEWORK | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Four Types of Offers (Attraction, Upsell, Downsell, Continuity) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:34
claim: A money model combines four offer types, each with one job: attraction offers liquidate CAC, upsells maximize profit, downsells maximize conversion, continuity stabilizes cash flow.
detail: Attraction turns strangers into customers. Upsells, usually right after, get customers to spend more. Downsells get a yes from people who said no, who may be upsold later. Continuity puts them on recurring payments so they keep buying. Together they produce client-financed acquisition. Not every business needs all four — some reach CFA with one offer, and funded companies may not need to break even — but his most successful companies had all four, roughly in this order. Multiple offers can sit in one prong.

### MM-08#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: money-models | also: management-ops, scaling-constraints | fw: Four Types of Offers (Attraction, Upsell, Downsell, Continuity) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:34
claim: Do not launch a full money model at once — add offers in layers, and only as many as you need to scale.
detail: A fully fledged model from day one will break the business. Build toward it like coats of paint. Each extra offer adds operational drag, so prefer the fewest offers that still maximize gross profit in the shortest time.

### MM-08#08 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: Four Types of Offers (Attraction, Upsell, Downsell, Continuity) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:09:48
claim: Three businesses mapped onto the four prongs: Gym Launch, a weight-loss gym, and the rental-car counter.
detail: Gym Launch: free books, lead magnets and podcast → 16-week upfront-cash program → supplements sold through gyms → three-year licensing continuity. Weight loss: free promotion → challenge (win your money back) or sell to goal → supplements → memberships. Rental car: $19/day gets you in → upgrade, insurance, fuel and late-return upsells → rewards program as continuity.
