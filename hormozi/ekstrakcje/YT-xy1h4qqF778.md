### YT-xy1h4qqF778#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: money-models, objections-closing | fw: Menu Upsell | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: After a layover chat with a former classmate who ran two supplement stores at $70,000-$80,000 a month, he bought stock, failed to sell it, then sold on the first try by asking "chocolate or vanilla?"
detail: Earlier pitches full of jargon failed. With a rich-looking client at the end of an orientation, he simply asked which flavour (and kiwi or strawberry for pre-workout) and suggested the card on file. He never explained benefits. The key was asking which, not whether.
numbers: Friend's stores: $70,000-$80,000/month, two locations.

### YT-xy1h4qqF778#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: objections-closing | also: money-models | fw: Menu Upsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:55
claim: Hormozi says that with an assumed-close upsell you can sell about 9 to 9.5 out of 10 people, whereas normally you might close one in three, one in four, or if you're really good one in two.
detail: Reasons: the buyer already knows and trusts you, has already purchased, has data on file, and both options involve buying. He usually knows a high-traffic process closing in the 80%+ range is an upsell.
numbers: Upsell with assumed close: 9 to 9.5 out of 10; normal sale: maybe 1 in 3, 1 in 4, or 1 in 2 if really good.
