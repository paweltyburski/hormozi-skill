### YT-F3NyhOtRpOE#10 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:24:41
claim: Genie game: a business with 30 new clients a month, 33% monthly churn (3-month average life), $100 a month price and 20% net margin ($10,000 revenue, $2,000 profit) is offered one doubling.
detail: The base is 100 clients (30 in, 30 out). Doubling new clients doubles revenue to $20,000 and profit to $4,000. Halving churn (6-month life) gives the same revenue but saves acquisition cost, so profit is a little more than double. Doubling price with no change in delivery adds $10,000 of pure profit: $12,000 profit, six times the original.
numbers: 30 new clients/month; 33% churn; $100 price; 100 active clients; $10,000 revenue; $2,000 profit; price doubled gives $12,000 profit (6x).

### YT-F3NyhOtRpOE#11 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:27:51
claim: Of the three levers (more customers, higher price, longer retention), doubling price is the strongest and doubling customers the weakest, because extra price carries no extra cost.
detail: Owners chase 20% gains when a price change can give a 600% profit increase in the example, which is why many undercharge and under-deliver.
numbers: Price doubling gave +500% profit in the game.
