### YT-Fy8XX8EuEnA#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:26
claim: When an owner says the niche is too small, check whether the business model, not the market, is the cap.
detail: Example he gives clients: a company making about $500,000 a year in a $6 billion industry has not exhausted the niche; its low customer lifetime value means it can only profitably reach a tiny fraction of the market. Fix the model (raise lifetime value) or add acquisition channels. A platform is exhausted when you have reached everyone you can reach profitably, not everyone on it.
numbers: about $500,000/yr revenue in a $6B industry (a quarter of a quarter of a quarter of one percent reached, per his phrasing).

claim: A department of about 20 hired to fix churn left churn unchanged after six months; letting it go changed nothing except raising profit.
detail: Twice in 2020 he removed a team tracked on metrics, and the business then grew. His belief is that only about one in five employees adds value, with work being "cutting grass with scissors". ### YT-Fy8XX8EuEnA#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:46
claim: A founder who has taken out large cash needs a plan for it, because unmanaged money compounds lost and drains attention.
detail: He took $17.4M in profit in 2018 and about $35-36M in direct distributions over 3.5 years. Early on he argued that 20% returns on that sum ($3.4M) were lower than income from the business, but at about $30M the missed compounding exceeds salary. He spent roughly 30% of his year on allocation, and prefers investing inside his own companies where returns are higher and he has control; one supplements business began this way and later paid over $10M in commissions.
numbers: $17.4M profit in 2018; $35-36M distributions in 3.5 years; 20% on $30M is about $6M a year; about 30% of a year spent on allocation.
