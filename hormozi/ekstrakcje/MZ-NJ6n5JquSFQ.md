### MZ-NJ6n5JquSFQ#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: At $1M-$3M the profit you make is about the size of the bet you must take, so escaping the swamp needs more risk than entering it.
detail: Early on you run cheap with one or two helpers and earn well. After crossing $1M you hire to cope, margin drops, and you feel worse off than with fewer people. Most companies pile up in this band because of this structural risk.
numbers: stage: $1M to $3M annual revenue.

### MZ-NJ6n5JquSFQ#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Bet one is hiring a person who will consume most of the margin without certainty that it works.
detail: This is the bet inside the existing model: a senior hire whose cost eats the profit you have, in the hope that growth repays it.

### MZ-NJ6n5JquSFQ#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: 80/20 Customer Analysis | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:01
claim: Bet two is dropping the less valuable customer segments and serving only the valuable one, trusting your capacity covers it.
detail: The risk is losing revenue short term. If the infrastructure already handles the valuable segment, serving only that segment at full volume would break the company out of the swamp.
