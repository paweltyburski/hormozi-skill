detail: About $50,000 of that year's expenses were one-time. Hormozi's reading: roughly $30,000 profit per location. Earlier, with one location run by three owners with group and personal training, the gym made about $40,000 profit a month, so he earns less with more locations.

### YT-_8olk4-VFP8#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: verticals | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:12
claim: Do not license or franchise until unit economics are strong; around $50,000 net per facility interests nobody, and corporate units should run 30% or more margins.
detail: Common failure: three-to-five-location owners who franchise with thin economics; most franchises never pass about 30 locations. First prove a unit with a high return on capital, which is what a franchisee or licensee evaluates.
numbers: ~$50K net per facility is unattractive; target 30%+ margins; many franchises stall below ~30 locations.

### YT-_8olk4-VFP8#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: verticals | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:10
claim: Small-town gyms should earn higher margins because costs and competition are lower; the trade-off is that leads are good at first and then run dry, so referral and retention matter more.
detail: He advised copying the proven small-town model into other small towns instead of entering a metro such as Fresno, where rivals take home $250,000-$400,000 a year.
numbers: Competitors in the metro take home $250K-$400K a year.

### YT-_8olk4-VFP8#05 | TACTIC | topic: verticals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:02
claim: Consolidate: merge one weak location's recurring members into another, accept losing half of them, and spread the same fixed overhead.
detail: Use lease end dates to time it. Then focus on making one gym very profitable and running without the owner, with an incentivised team. That gives an asset that can be sold, licensed, franchised or used to own all locations.
