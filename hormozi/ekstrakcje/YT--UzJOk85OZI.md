### YT--UzJOk85OZI#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:46
claim: Conviction in a sensible plan matters more than finding the perfect plan; waiting for the perfect pick is a trap.
detail: Several paths up the mountain work; none works if you keep hopping between them. Switching means descending, crossing and climbing again, and the path you already walked looks easier only because you know it. Most owners already know roughly what to do; doing only that for three years beats most people. The problem is stuck at the "not knowing" stage, where people change strategy instead of treating figuring it out as the strategy.
numbers: Three years of consistent basics cited as enough to beat most people.

### YT--UzJOk85OZI#02 | DEFINITION | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:05:11
claim: Culture is the set of rules about what gets rewarded and punished, reinforced mainly by who is hired and fired and by which behaviours are left unchanged.
detail: Culture sits on top of hundreds of behaviours. Unrewarded good work teaches people not to repeat it; ignoring lateness teaches that lateness is fine. If staff do not work to your preferences, reinforcement is weak. List the specific behaviours you dislike (sometimes 2-3, often 20-30) and what you would want instead.

### YT--UzJOk85OZI#03 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:47
claim: Give feedback in start, stop, keep form.
detail: Tell the person what to start doing that is missing, what to stop doing, and what to keep doing because it will keep being reinforced. Used to turn a diagnosed non-performance culture into concrete behaviour changes.

### YT--UzJOk85OZI#05 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:08:24
claim: In interviews, ask what candidates are obsessed with and how they spend off-hours, to see if it matches the role.
detail: If someone hiring for video spends spare time studying lighting, audio and sets, they will welcome feedback and likely excel. If their passion is elsewhere (sports, hobbies), the job may become a side activity. Ask the question several ways to see where discretionary effort goes.

### YT--UzJOk85OZI#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:40
claim: Hire for proven performance in senior roles and for potential in junior ones, preferring intelligence and work ethic.
detail: The further up the role, the more weight on actual results; the lower, the more on potential. He would choose a generally smart, hard-working person over a more experienced but less sharp one, expecting the smart one to catch up and pass.

### YT--UzJOk85OZI#07 | CASE | topic: management-ops | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:25
claim: A solar sales company (multi-million) wrote six sales-leadership pillars at an offsite and saw no behaviour change afterwards.
detail: Abstract words like accountability meant different things to different people. Fix: define each as observable actions. For accountability, that became a morning text to the team, an end-of-week report per person, end-of-day recognition of winners, and asking those who missed what they struggled with and drilling the skill.

### YT--UzJOk85OZI#08 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:25
claim: Ask what a value looks like in behaviour and how you would know it happened.
detail: Used to convert values into checkable actions, so feedback becomes "you skipped these four things" instead of an argument over definitions.

### YT--UzJOk85OZI#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:04
claim: When the plan is not executed, name the cause (talent, culture or training) instead of changing strategy.
detail: Talent problem: recruit better. Culture problem: teach leaders to reward and punish behaviours. People arrive but are unproductive: improve training. Keep going long enough to make the mistakes needed to learn it, rather than restarting every month.
