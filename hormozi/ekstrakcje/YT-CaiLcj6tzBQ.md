### YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ#01 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:55
claim: Pitch a service by asking how the prospect finds time for the task, since every service sells the same resource: the buyer's time.
detail: After the opener and a compliment, ask a question that steers toward the problem, for example how they have time to handle the chore you do. Nearly everyone says they wish they had more time, which sets you up as a seller of time. It works for fitness, cleaning, IT or any other service, because each frees time.

### YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ#02 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: lead-getters | fw: Warm Outreach Steps | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:37
claim: Ask a warm contact whether they know anyone with the problem, not whether they want the service, and give a reason such as taking a few case studies.
detail: This is not soliciting business. Explain you are taking five people for free to collect testimonials or reviews. If they already use a provider, ask whether it is free; if not, offer to do it. Either result is acceptable.
numbers: Offer to five (or five to ten) people for free.

### YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: outreach | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:32
claim: Work given free to friendly people pays four ways: they become customers, leave a review, send a friend, or give feedback that makes you better.
detail: Each outcome stands alone; one person can do several. Even the person who says you were not good enough still helps by telling you what to improve, which raises the price you will eventually be worth. It also provides practice with friendlies.

### YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: outreach | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:07
claim: Frame free work as "you are helping me out", never "you owe me one", or resentment will show.
detail: Without a track record or reputation you are not entitled to payment, which is why you work free. Expecting thanks makes you a weirdo to the prospect. The honest frame is that they do you a favour, and you hope to leave them better off.

### YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ#05 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: lead-getters | fw: Hinge Method | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:07
claim: Make the introduction a live three-way text while the contact is still with you, instead of asking them to remember later.
detail: Two gains: you capture the lead on the spot, and a person introduced inside a group message is much less likely to be rude to you. Asking "can you introduce me" leaves the work to their memory.

### YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: outreach | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:27
claim: Warm outreach has two limits, your hours and the number of people who know you; about 1,000 contacts means reaching everyone in about 10 days at 100 a day.
detail: Add up the email list, phone contacts and followers across platforms. After one pass you hit friction, though re-contacting is still right.
numbers: 1,000 known people / 100 contacts a day = 10 days (example).

### YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ#10 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:58
claim: In door-to-door outreach, look the part (work clothes, visible tools) and drop the name of a neighbour you served free as the first line.
detail: Giving one household a free service and citing it at every other door creates instant trust and politeness, because people treat someone who knows someone they know better and give more time. Leverage each free client hard: if one free job yields ten more sales, it paid off. Visual cues also raise the chance they open the door.
numbers: Example: one free job used to approach every other door; ten extra sales would justify it.

### YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ#11 | DECISION_RULE | topic: outreach | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:02
claim: Ask a yes/no question where both answers lead to a prepared next step: "do you have someone doing this" works whether they say yes or no.
detail: If they have no provider, propose doing it. If they do, that is sometimes even better, because someone already paying for the service is more likely to buy from you. Plant an alternative offer for that case.
### YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ#12 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: outreach | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:04
claim: Phrase the ask as a no-based question ("would you be opposed to...?"), which yields slightly more yeses across a large population than a yes-based ask.
detail: People say no more readily, so "no, I would not be opposed" gives you the yes. Hormozi notes yes-based closes also work and he has closed plenty; the difference is small and shows over volume.

### YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ#13 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:53
claim: Once the prospect says yes, stop talking and start the work, because every extra sentence raises the chance they reverse.
detail: Everything said after the yes adds risk. Move to the next step, such as taking the trash to the truck.

### YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ#14 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: outreach | fw: Big Fast Value | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:15:50
claim: Beating an incumbent provider at the door: offer the first month free, then a low monthly rate.
detail: The prospect already has a provider who charges; the pitch asks whether the incumbent also works free this month, and if not, offers free pickups for the first month (three or four pickups) and then a low monthly price. Rapport and humour (pattern interrupt) let the same service win because the buyer likes the seller more.
numbers: Free for the first month (three to four pickups), then $29 a month.
applies_to: Local recurring consumer service

### YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ#15 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:43
claim: To collect payment details on the spot, take their ID first, then swap it for a card, and write down the card number yourself.
detail: Writing the number yourself avoids a customer accidentally or deliberately changing digits so the charge fails. Hold the ID until they hand over the card, then swap back.

### YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ#16 | TACTIC | topic: outreach | also: lead-getters | fw: List Building (Scrape, Buy, Elbow Grease) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:23:44
claim: Purchased or software-built lists get tired because competitors use them too; a list you assemble yourself from free sources stays fresh.
detail: Three routes: list software (paid monthly, export by criteria), brokers (ask how many contacts fit, then negotiate), or scraping Yelp, Google Maps, free online databases, Facebook groups and Reddit forums. Hormozi calls the self-built list the best long-term route.
numbers: Example broker quote: 17,000 contacts matching the criteria.

### YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ#17 | BENCHMARK | topic: paid-ads | also: content-brand | fw: Content Unit (Hook-Retain-Reward) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:33:09
claim: About 80% of viewers decide within the first three seconds not to watch an ad, so the hook is what you test the most.
detail: If the first seconds hold them, you buy time for the rest. An ad is like content (hook, retain, reward) but ends with a call to action. Content may end with a shareable moment instead.
numbers: Roughly 80% leave in the first 3 seconds (speaker's figure).

### YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ#18 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:31:28
claim: Announce "three things we do better than everybody else" in an ad, since the number opens a loop and gives structure to the benefits.
detail: Hormozi used fitness, nutrition and accountability when selling fitness programmes. Each of the three maps to a pain point of the avatar. Use several pain points because the first one named may not be the one the viewer feels.

### YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ#19 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: offers | fw: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:31:28
claim: The guarantee can sit at the start, middle or end of the ad; place it beside the pain point it answers.
detail: The demo ad answered the biggest pain, a missed pickup, with a guarantee of three free months for any missed day, which is safe if you simply never miss. Copy is about detail: smell, chasing the truck, bins piling up, neighbours noticing (status).
numbers: Guarantee example: three free months if one pickup day is missed.

### YT-CaiLcj6tzBQ#20 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Call-Outs (Labels, Yes Questions, If-Then, Ridiculous Results) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:34:00
claim: The people shown in an ad and its setting attract the matching audience, and showing two to four different people can double or triple lead flow.
detail: Put women in the ad to attract women; if all clients are men, it may be because the founder is the only face. A visual or spoken call-out (for example naming a city) filters who responds. Pain yes-questions can bridge to the service (missed practice, then trash), and different hooks can be cut onto the same body clip.
