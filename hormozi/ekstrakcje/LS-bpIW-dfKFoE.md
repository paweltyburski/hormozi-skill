### LS-bpIW-dfKFoE#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:16
claim: His litmus test for degree of change: if you have stagnated and no version of your kind of business has reached your goal, consider changing it dramatically; if you are winning, bigger versions exist and results trend up, it is usually not the time for big change; refine with small tweaks.
detail: The first case "might or should" warrant willingness to dismantle and rebuild (a jackhammer); the second calls for sandpaper and a chisel. His summary: winning, change very little; losing, perhaps change a lot, with losing defined by your own goals. Prompted partly by a company he sold to a group unwilling to take risks in proportion to what the business needed.

### LS-bpIW-dfKFoE#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:28
claim: Ask what the largest existing version of your kind of business looks like; if none exists although many want one, there is probably a reason, something in the model that makes it very hard to scale.
detail: Example: no billion-dollar coaching business exists despite countless people wanting one, while billion-dollar insurers, banks and software firms do. Either you are the innovator who breaks the pattern, or the model resists scale. Aimed at owners stuck for a while at $1M, $3M or $10M.

### LS-bpIW-dfKFoE#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:03
claim: Some owners cannot get past a level because they keep reinventing the business, leaving team and customers in constant whiplash; others hear only the few words said about each change, not the reasoning.
detail: His own version: killing a mosquito with a cannon, overhauling everything when something feels uncomfortable, which he ranks as his second-biggest weakness after lack of focus.

### LS-bpIW-dfKFoE#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:52
claim: Founders who shut working businesses to build larger ones: Alex Becker (information business, then Hyros) and Sam Ovens (Consulting.com at ~$3M a year, then Skool).
detail: Ovens worked about a day a week on Consulting.com (Hormozi's recollection), and even that distracted from Skool, which took off only after the shutdown. Large change usually means a short-term sacrifice of lifestyle and ego. Names corrected from the transcript ("HiRose", "Sam Muffins").
