### POD-0931#01 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: sales-team, scaling-constraints | fw: Game Tape Review (Five A's) | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: UAE brokerage whose founder bills two-thirds of revenue is told to stop haggling over the office, fill desks now and shift from top closer to trainer and recruiter of agents.
business: real-estate — brokerage selling property to wealthy investors in the UAE, $6M revenue, ~$4M profit, founder plus 7 agents
detail: Goal $24M. Constraint: manpower; UAE visas require the office before hiring, so a 40-desk, four-year lease. Diagnosis: nothing is done to recruit agents, and selling skill lives only in the founder's head. Advice: take the office; fill the four free desks immediately; codify what he does, script everything said to clients, review game tape; hard-block about 25% of his time for the team, since one extra deal per agent repays the lost billing. Long term, a big brand attracts already-productive agents.
questions: are agents unskilled? / margins? / what is unique about the market? / office cost per year? / what do you do to recruit agents?
numbers: founder ~$4M (68%); 7 agents ~$2M (~$300K each); goal $24M needs ~80 agents; office ~$350K/year, four-year term; 4 desks free (~50% growth); founder earns ~$500K/month; 25% of his time ~$1M/year (his estimate); 20-30 agents ~$12-15M/year (his estimate)
speakers: owner: facts and numbers; Hormozi: questions, diagnosis, advice
applies_to: founder-led sales firms where the founder is the top producer

### POD-0931#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:19
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: a UAE-based owner of a sales operation haggling over an office lease should stop, because the saving equals only a couple of days of his earnings and the lease gates his growth.
detail: He calls some of his own past negotiations dumb in hindsight. Method for this caller: compare the saving (about $50K a year, taking a roughly $350K office down to $300K) with what the business earns (the caller makes about $500K a month); he puts it at about two and a half days of earnings, and says it hardly matters if extra haggling costs another month.

### POD-0931#03 | CASE | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:33
claim: Founder of a Chinese-language business community wanting $1M a month is told education has one-time value, so recurring revenue needs consumable value, probably after niching down.
business: coaching-education — online community for overseas Chinese entrepreneurs, 75 founding members at $1,000 one-time, ~20K YouTube subscribers
detail: Asks how to reach $1M/month. Diagnosis: once a member knows a skill they stop paying for it. Advice: sell the system once, then sell weekly or monthly inputs; for beginners, teach one to three business models and supply their inputs; for larger owners, offer network, access and group discounts. Members should add value, not dilute it. On partnering with Hormozi: he would need strong proof of his own results.
questions: lifetime, monthly or one-time? / how were the first customers won? / what ongoing value? / who is the audience?
numbers: 75 members x $1,000 = $75K; goal $1M/month; ~20K subscribers
speakers: owner (Max): facts, questions; Hormozi: questions, teaching, advice
applies_to: education communities trying to add continuity

### POD-0931#04 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:32
claim: Sell the machine once at a high price, then sell its inputs on a recurring basis.
detail: Examples: a 3D-printing community uploading weekly blueprints of trending items; a wholesaling community posting pre-vetted local deals each week; Gym Launch handing gyms monthly pre-tested winning ads, which made it media rather than education and kept it sticky. Inputs require a niche: he could not test ads for 100 different business types.

### POD-0931#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:47
claim: The recurring component can stay sticky even if far below the upfront fee, provided its ongoing value usually exceeds its price by about 3-4x.
detail: A large gap between the one-time charge and the small recurring fee is fine, he says; his example: $10,000 up front then $500 a month would probably be relatively sticky. The condition he states is that the ongoing value surpasses the monthly price, usually by three or four times.

### POD-0931#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:20
claim: The messenger is about 80-90% of the context in which a message is received, so a partner or creator needs a proven track record first.
detail: Anyone can repeat his words; his content outperforms because of proof behind it. Authority persuades because following it lowers perceived risk and mental effort. High-agency people should separate message from messenger, but almost nobody does, so he would only partner with someone with a big proof point.
