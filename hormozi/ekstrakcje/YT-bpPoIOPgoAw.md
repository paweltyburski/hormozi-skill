### YT-bpPoIOPgoAw#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:45
claim: After a conference, two people each offered $50,000 for a single day of coaching and Hormozi's wife declined both because the attention would cost more than it gave.
detail: The company had just won an award for over $10 million in sales. Their view: the better you get, the better the opportunities you must refuse; splitting attention across many ventures ensures none works.
numbers: Offer $50,000 per day, two offers; award threshold $10M in sales.

### YT-bpPoIOPgoAw#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:01
claim: A $30 million business and a $100 million business look nearly the same: the bigger one just does more of the same thing.
detail: They keep one customer type and problem rather than adding verticals; for example, going from 5 to 15 sales a day (figures partly unclear in audio).
numbers: Run rate then about $30M; compared with a $100M business.

### YT-bpPoIOPgoAw#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:01
claim: Attention, not time, is the most valuable asset, and side deals always have strings attached to it.
detail: For gym owners he says skip online training, meal prep services and partner side deals; focus on one location and repeat the core loop until bored, then do more of it.
applies_to: Gym owners and small businesses with one working model.
