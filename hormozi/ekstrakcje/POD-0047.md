### POD-0047#01 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: CLOSER | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:26
claim: When told "that's expensive", agree and own it, then explain, restate the value, and offer a choice between the cheap cycle and fixing it for good.
detail: Steps: 1) agree, hopefully we are the most expensive because we are the best; 2) say you chose to be best, not cheapest, which needs more attention, accountability and coaching, and note they already tried the cheap route; 3) restate the full outcome ("if this does what I say") and ask whether they would believe it at a low price; 4) ask: keep running in circles or solve it for good? Then close. Add that ten years of cheap fixes cost more.
numbers: example price $3,000; Gym Launch claim: about $200,000 added first-year revenue for virtually all gyms (his claim).

### POD-0047#02 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:39
claim: Only three objections matter (delay, not the decision maker, money); keep a library of overcomes but use the few that feel natural.
detail: His team trains 10 to 15 overcomes per objection. Dan Kennedy's story: a top vacuum rep memorised 356 overcomes yet used about three. Choose the ones whose wording resonates with you.
numbers: 10-15 overcomes per objection; 356 memorised, 3 used (Kennedy story).

### POD-0047#03 | SCRIPT | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:08
claim: Myron Golden's close for lack of money: ask how long you want not having the amount to be among your life's problems.
detail: Phrased as "can I talk to you like you're my little brother?" He uses it for B2B and personal-service sales. If they say they can barely put forward $1,000, ask what will change unless they change something.

### POD-0047#04 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:20
claim: Normalise fear of the spend: "if you were not afraid to spend this, I would send you out the door."
detail: Being afraid is normal; the lack of money is itself caused by lacking the systems. A high price also signals the promise is credible, since a cheap price would make the claim suspect. He adds that Gym Launch is no guru business but a company dedicated to making clients money.
numbers: Gym Launch staff about 40 (2018).

### POD-0047#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: sales-team | fw: Downsell Offers | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:42
claim: At Six Pack Shortcuts reps were taught that a price with no gasp was set too low, then they downsold from the high anchor.
detail: Mick Elliott was top rep among 75 phone sellers handling about $1M a month in high-volume transactional sales. Hormozi does not recommend that burn-and-turn style for relational gyms but accepts the principle of starting at the top.
numbers: 75 reps; about $1M per month phone sales.
