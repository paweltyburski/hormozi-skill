### YT--NLqtk4F4oY#01 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:13
claim: Solve a hard growth problem by inverting it: list the fastest ways to destroy the business, then flip each item into an action.
detail: People find threats far more easily than opportunities, so "how would I wreck this?" produces a longer and different list than "how do I grow?". Write the destruction moves in order, then reverse each. Examples given: hiding your product from everyone becomes telling as many people as possible; a bad product that earns angry reviews becomes an exceptional product that people recommend and rate well.
applies_to: Any complex business or personal problem.

### YT--NLqtk4F4oY#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:41
claim: When a deal or business model is complicated, opt out and take the simpler path, because complexity at scale becomes impossible.
detail: Munger's most frequent verdict on deals was that they were too hard, which shows humility rather than limited ability. Applied to business models: the simple one still gets complicated at scale, so choose it.

### YT--NLqtk4F4oY#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:49
claim: The Munger-Buffett perfect business is unique, high-margin, sticky and low in capital spending, run by an owner-minded manager.
detail: Unique means a brand or other barrier that is hard to copy. Margin means buying at a penny and selling at a dollar, so gross profit can be reinvested or distributed. Sticky means customers buy again and again. The transcript renders the last element as 'air', which Hormozi explains as low capital expenditure, so the business need not keep pouring in money to stay competitive.
### YT--NLqtk4F4oY#04 | DEFINITION | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:16
claim: Recurring means contractual repeat revenue (insurance); reoccurring means habitual repeat buying (Coca-Cola), and both are valuable to the owner.
detail: A consumer buying a soft drink does not feel subscribed, but the restaurant or gas station buying stock from the parent company does so regularly. Demand becomes more reliable the further up the distribution chain you sell.

### YT--NLqtk4F4oY#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:52
claim: Judge managers by whether they act as if their entire net worth sat in the company's stock and could never be sold.
detail: The test pushes decisions toward the long term. The pairing is a business so good that an average person could not ruin it, plus an exceptional person running it.

### YT--NLqtk4F4oY#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:38
claim: Do only deals that are clearly good, leave some of the pie to the other side, and become the first person people call.
detail: Munger held that a deal is either good and obvious or bad and obvious, so a spreadsheet is not needed to see it. Squeezing the other party for a few more points wins that deal but makes you the last resort in future. Being everyone's first call compounds over a lifetime.

### YT--NLqtk4F4oY#07 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:31
claim: Berkshire's Apple bet and its wholly owned consumer companies show brand being treated as the strategic moat.
detail: Apple was bought because a loyal audience should let it charge above-average prices for average products for years; Hormozi says roughly half of Berkshire was Apple at the time he checked. Owned brands named: Ben Bridge Jewelers, See's Candies, Dairy Queen, Geico. He says this confirmed his own investment in building a personal brand.
numbers: About half of Berkshire's portfolio in Apple (as last checked by him).

### YT--NLqtk4F4oY#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:18
claim: Rule zero of money is to spend less than you earn, and many people chase advanced tactics before balancing a budget.
detail: If you always spend less than you earn you never run short, and investing rules such as not losing money assume that surplus exists in the first place.

### YT--NLqtk4F4oY#09 | BENCHMARK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:43
claim: Hormozi's portfolio added about $100M of revenue organically in three years by simplifying and doing fundamentals everywhere, not by anything complex.
detail: His claim is that doing the basics in every function, all the time, is already as hard as business gets, and that this makes you an advanced operator.
numbers: About $100M added revenue in three years across the portfolio companies, organically (his statement).
