### POD-0451#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: offers | also: promotions-launches | fw: Scarcity | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:52
claim: Three honest ways to add scarcity: a total business cap, a growth-rate cap, and cohorts.
detail: 1) cap total customers (say 100) and keep a waitlist; 2) cap new customers per week or month; 3) enrol fixed classes starting together, for example 40 people, which also simplifies delivery. Only the first needs a true cap; the others create scarcity without limiting size. Sales rise as the cohort deadline nears.
numbers: Examples: 100 total customers; five new clients a week; class of 40.

### POD-0451#04 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: retention-ltv | fw: Scarcity | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:52
claim: With a total cap, tell people who leave they cannot return and raise the cap periodically, using a waitlist.
detail: A full roster also provides social proof. The fear of missing out lowers the purchase threshold and raises lifetime value.

### POD-0451#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:52
claim: Fear of missing out moves buyers more strongly than desire for gain, and people hoard scarce slots.
detail: Positioning a slot as scarce lets you sell to more people at higher prices.

### POD-0451#06 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: promotions-launches | fw: Scarcity | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:12
claim: A high-ticket mastermind with capacity for 25 new members a month announced spots weekly, then planned to raise capacity to 40.
detail: The guest's group targets 275 members and shows its journey openly. Coaches handle 40 clients each, so 40 per month means one new coach per month; matching intake to coach capacity makes onboarding systematic. Scarcity is simply a true statement of capacity.
numbers: Capacity 25 new members a month now; 40 per month goal; 40 clients per coach; 275-member target.

### POD-0451#07 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: sales-process | fw: Urgency | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:59
claim: Start everyone on Mondays so the sign-up deadline is Thursday, creating time-based urgency without a quantity limit.
detail: Missing the cutoff means waiting a week, and some people dislike the delay.

### POD-0451#08 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: pricing | fw: Urgency | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:46
claim: Use month-end named promotions so every month has a true deadline, as large fitness chains do.
detail: The offer can be essentially the same under a different theme (New Year, Valentine's, "sexy by spring", "lean by Halloween"). Sales concentrate in the final week of the month. He says: deadline-driven decisions.

### POD-0451#09 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: objections-closing | fw: Five Upsell Moments | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:55
claim: For renewals, add scarcity and urgency to bonuses and use coach scarcity: tell clients their coach is booked and may not be free later.
detail: Do the re-sign halfway to two thirds through the term, while the loop is still open, and recap what is done and what comes next. Add urgency to the early-signing deal and bonuses.
numbers: Re-sign at one-half to two-thirds of the term.

### POD-0451#10 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:33
claim: Offer a predetermined early-signing incentive by tacking two free months onto the end, framed as roughly 20% off, conditional on on-time payments.
detail: Pricing stays non-negotiable; the incentive is set in advance, and if payment problems occur the bonus is withdrawn. Showing the extra months avoids displaying a discount.
numbers: Two extra months; he equates it to ~20% off.

### POD-0451#11 | DECISION_RULE | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:12
claim: Ask whether price or terms matter more to the other side; he says people in general understand price decently well but not terms.
detail: If they say price, meet their price and win on terms; his illustration was buying a house at 10% above market but financed over 30 years with no money from him. Terms can be adjusted in many ways.
numbers: Illustration: 10% over market, 30-year financing.
### POD-0451#12 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: pricing | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:16:08
claim: A $12,000 12-week programme can step into a one-year offer at half the weekly price, yielding twice the total.
detail: The front end costs $1,000 a week. The follow-up, a year at half price per week with more included, is four times as long, so the customer pays twice as much overall. He calls it downselling the upsell.
numbers: $12,000 for 12 weeks = $1,000/week; year-long at half rate = about 2x total revenue.

### POD-0451#13 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: objections-closing | fw: Conditional Guarantee | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:15
claim: A guarantee's strength is proportional to the risk it reverses, so the "or else" part must have teeth.
detail: A promise whose remedy is continued access to materials does little, since a customer who got no results won't want them. Conditions reduce reversal, so keep few.

### POD-0451#14 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: retention-ltv | fw: Activation Points | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:34
claim: Build guarantee conditions from the first-30-to-90-day behaviours of customers who ascended and renewed.
detail: Example: if clients who send their meals daily for 30 days ascend 90% of the time, guarantee twice the money back provided they do that for the first 30 days. This keeps conditions few and gets the key habit installed.
numbers: Example: 90% ascension for the 30-day daily habit; double money back.

### POD-0451#15 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: product-delivery | fw: Guarantees | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:09
claim: Guarantee something that scares you more than your customers, then fix the business model and delivery that make you afraid.
detail: The guarantee works as a fear driver for improving fulfilment so you can honour it.

### POD-0451#16 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Stacking Guarantees | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:09
claim: Stack milestone guarantees, such as 10 pounds by 60 days and 20 pounds by 90 days, and pay out in credit or extra time rather than cash.
detail: Combine an unconditional window with conditional ones. Money is the least creative remedy; credit or extended service also works.
numbers: Example milestones: 10 lb at 60 days, 20 lb at 90 days.

### POD-0451#17 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: objections-closing | fw: Unconditional Guarantee | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:23
claim: Put an unconditional guarantee on only the first 30 days of a longer programme.
detail: Frame it as an informed decision like a test drive; after day 30 the refund right ends. He expects far more sales at higher prices.
numbers: 30-day unconditional on a six- to twelve-month programme.
