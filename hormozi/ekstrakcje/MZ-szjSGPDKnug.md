### MZ-szjSGPDKnug#01 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Observe experts instead of only asking them, since top performers often cannot say what they do.
detail: What someone explains is filtered twice (their ability to communicate, your ability to understand); observation skips that. Apply it to your own work too: record content and sales calls, and for above-average outcomes ask what was different.

### MZ-szjSGPDKnug#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:57
claim: Mastery is identifying the variables that raise the likelihood of an outcome; every piece of content, call or email is a dice roll.
detail: He says prediction is control, so the more variables you understand the more your forecasts and results improve.

### MZ-szjSGPDKnug#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:49
claim: Before a hard conversation, define the outcome you want and what raises its likelihood; often the best move is to do nothing.
detail: Example: an influencer wanted to confront a creator who copied her. He asked what she wanted (less chance it recurs), then whether yelling achieves it; usually it only invites retaliation. He and a friend call doing nothing the "undefeated champion".

### MZ-szjSGPDKnug#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:45
claim: Play only games you have a strong chance of winning; otherwise learn the players and rules or build an unfair advantage first.
detail: He took this from his father and applies it to deals and markets.
