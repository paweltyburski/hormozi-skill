### YT-t9Vv7rK-4Jc#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: offers | fw: Delivery Cube | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: The cube is meant to find idle capacity: repackage resources you already have into a more valuable product without adding cost.
detail: He compares it to a lumber mill monetizing sawdust as sandpaper or fuel pellets. Place a current service on each of the six sides and ask whether moving one step either way produces a more valuable or cheaper product. Meant for services and consulting, not physical products.
applies_to: Service and consulting businesses

### YT-t9Vv7rK-4Jc#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: verticals | fw: Sales to Fulfillment Continuum | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:33
claim: Each delivery mode has a typical company type: do-it-yourself companies behave like marketing and equity businesses, done-for-you ones are operationally complex but pull customers in.
detail: Do-it-yourself is the hardest to sell (low value, all effort on the buyer), done-with-you adds a learning component, done-for-you is easiest to sell and hardest to run. Billion-dollar companies exist in all three modes.

### YT-t9Vv7rK-4Jc#03 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: pricing | fw: Delivery Cube | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:45
claim: Sell speed by splitting a normal support tier from a VIP tier with a much faster response promise.
detail: Same resources, different expectation: standard support replies within 24 hours, VIP within 60 minutes or less; another variant is delivering results in half the time. Examples of paid speed: Uber, FedEx overnight, Spotify, Amazon Prime.
numbers: 24 hours vs 60 minutes response; target result in half the time.

### YT-t9Vv7rK-4Jc#04 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: Delivery Cube | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:36
claim: Run the "10x" and "one-tenth" questions on an existing offer: ideas from the 10x price may already be affordable inside the current price.
detail: Ask what you would add if the price were ten times higher and which of those items could go into the current offer; then ask how you would add value if it had to sell for one-tenth. Example scale: a $1,000 product asked at $10,000, and a $100 version asked to be more valuable.
numbers: Price multiplier 10x and 1/10.
