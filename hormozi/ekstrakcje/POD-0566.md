### POD-0566#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:05:39
claim: Sort decisions by reversibility; for reversible ones commit to a "best bad guess" and test.
detail: Most of his biggest mistakes have come from delay more than from actual wrongdoings, and decision speed has slowed as the business grew. Irreversible decisions get graded by how irreversible and how big they are (a kid, marriage, lunch). Reversible ones use the phrase "my best bad idea", which lowers the action threshold; at Acquisition.com the single commitment is to test and iterate, so the only choice is which test to run first.
### POD-0566#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:20
claim: He quit consulting by comparing risk: staying guaranteed he would miss his goal, quitting gave a shot, and the twenties were the cheapest time to fail.
detail: Goal was $1M/year without heavy work; the only two paths he could see that paid that were investment banking and finance/consulting, at the cost of his soul. Later in life, more income, family and assets raise the cost of correcting. Downside protection (GMAT done, accountancy master's applied, entrepreneurial experience as a business-school essay); business school cost about $200,000-$250,000 with ~$110-$120k median salary. The decision still took six months of near-daily talks with a friend, and he told nobody he had left.
numbers: Business school ~$200k hard cost, ~$250k with lost income; median post-MBA salary ~$110-120k; decision time 6 months.
### POD-0566#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:14
claim: Character traits are actions taken despite feeling, and they are graded, not binary; bigger goals demand a higher grade.
detail: Loyalty, patience and courage mean acting accordingly despite temptation. His 7/10 got him through Gym Launch, Prestige Labs and Alan; he says he needs a 9 for a billion and 9.5 for ten billion. As the company grew he had to stop reacting to a bad day with strategy changes. At portfolio selection, about 80% of the scorecard is the entrepreneur ("betting on the jockey").
numbers: ~80% of investment scorecard weight on the entrepreneur.

### POD-0566#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: verticals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:22
claim: First gym: break even in month one, then about $5,000, $10,000, $15,000, $20,000 monthly profit; shift to licensing took monthly revenue from $300-400k to $2-4.5M.
detail: He ran the gym alone for about six months, no employees, only rent as a cost, sleeping on the floor, banking all cash. numbers: Profit month 2 $5,000, then 10k, 15k, 20k; turnarounds $300-400k/month vs $2-4.5M/month after licensing; 8 gym launches/month.
