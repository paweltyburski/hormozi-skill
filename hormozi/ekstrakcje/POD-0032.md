### POD-0032#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: retention-ltv | fw: Virtuous Cycle of Price | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:37
claim: Lower prices attract more demanding customers, while higher prices bring less demanding ones.
detail: At very low prices clients ask for extras far out of proportion to what they pay, while the business has less margin, so service per customer falls and results and testimonials fall. After a price rise there are fewer, better clients who ask for little, and any extra service delights them. Hormozi says he has lived both sides.

### POD-0032#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:28
claim: Cheap, under-delivering services give the sales team integrity problems.
detail: Reps must sell certainty, so when they know the business cannot deliver the promise they feel they are lying, and the owner feels it too if they sell. Higher prices fund service and give reps real testimonials and conviction, which raises inflow.

### POD-0032#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: Price to Value Discrepancy | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:14
claim: A service team at a high price can solve problems fully instead of racing the clock.
detail: At low prices staff must cap calls at about five minutes to keep volume. With margin left over they can stay on a problem for two hours. Illustration of perceived value: if a software tool cost $5,000 a month, users would see it as the best ever and learn it thoroughly because they are invested.
numbers: Illustrative price: $5,000 a month for a funnel software; call cap at low prices: about 5 minutes.

### POD-0032#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:52
claim: When raising prices, he says to go up by a significant margin; two memberships at $100 and $120 are basically the same buying decision.
detail: Memberships at $100 and $120 are basically one buying decision, so you might as well put everyone at $120; he says that if you go to $120, the same buying decision would be about $167. In his paid group he says that virtually every time he tells gyms they should probably raise prices, usually by a significant margin.
numbers: Example tiers: $100 vs $120 (same decision); about $167 is also described as the same buying decision.
