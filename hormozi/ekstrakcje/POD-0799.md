### POD-0799#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:21
claim: If a goal's benefit is not enough to start today, it will not be enough in a month, so start immediately or redesign the goal to pull some reward forward.
detail: Hormozi says most people set goals around anniversaries (new year, birthday); when younger he set many goals and did not get started on them. Now he begins the moment something becomes a priority. If starting cannot be made worthwhile, the goal may be just the equivalent of a complaint ("it would be nice").
### POD-0799#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:11
claim: Goals take seconds to set and years to achieve, so you will always hold more goals than you achieve; decide which you will not pursue and which fires you will let burn.
detail: People add goals to a fixed 24-hour calendar without removing anything, planning something like 28 hours of work in 24.

### POD-0799#03 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:59
claim: For each goal write the goal, the time you think it takes, then multiply by three and create a sacrifice worth three times that time.
detail: Three numbers exist: how long you want it to take, how long you think, how long it will really take; the real one is about three times the realistic estimate, and even people who know the rule still overrun. Example: a friend in the fitness industry aimed for a six-pack in a year and was closer but not there at year end, taking far longer than he expected.
numbers: Real duration about 3x the estimate; sacrifice equal to 3x the estimated time.

### POD-0799#04 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:08:02
claim: Treat goals as price versus value on a finite budget: list goals as the shelf, your time and money as the budget, and choose the best risk-adjusted return.
detail: Most people look at value without price and put too much in the basket. First realisation: set your goal budget. He rarely conquers multiple domains in one year; achieving is harder than maintaining.

### POD-0799#05 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:25
claim: Find your real budget from records: card statement (money), screen-time reports and calendar (time).
detail: How you spend shows what you actually care about. List what you are willing to give up and, separately, what you are likely to give up; start with the likely ones.

### POD-0799#06 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:04
claim: Replacing a habit is easier than stopping it, because behaviour is held by a reinforcing stimulus that needs a substitute.
detail: His example: audiobooks before bed instead of TV, which also improved sleep.

### POD-0799#07 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:53
claim: Choose one goal and commit to it fully; its progress partially lifts the others, whereas chasing all three means achieving none.
detail: If you cannot pick, your first goal is learning to pick and commit. Commitment is the elimination of alternatives. Money might help relationships (better dates, better people); a strong body will probably attract better mates and help in business.
numbers: He cites a 0.7 correlation between relationship with a partner and subjective well-being, the strongest he knows (claim not sourced).
### POD-0799#08 | CASE | topic: operator-mindset | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:18:39
claim: When scaling from six gym locations toward ten, with 20+ consults a day, Hormozi gave up Netflix, football and fantasy football as the sacrifice.
detail: A year on he could not remember the shows he had watched; the goal mattered more. He now works more than before and is simply better at it, writing a 20-page document in a day.
numbers: 6 locations toward 10; 20+ consults per day.

### POD-0799#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:14
claim: Eliminate alternatives rather than grit your teeth: remove every disruptor of the one thing, and think of focus as how many things you say no to.
detail: Writers set a clock with only an empty room and a pad; boredom makes them write. Start from zero extras (100% focus) and add back sleep and food; most people are about 5% focused on their goal.
numbers: Many are around 5% focused on the goal (his estimate).

### POD-0799#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:18
claim: Most goals are achievable but not on the person's timeline; big outcomes take years, plural, so annual goals are often the wrong frame.
detail: His personal brand began in 2019 and six years later he still sees the road ahead. We meet successful people after the fact and wrongly assume speed. Many goals are two-to-four-year goals.

### POD-0799#12 | DEFINITION | topic: operator-mindset | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:29:21
claim: The difference between a beginner and an expert is the number of ways they can mark progress; beginners judge only on outcome.
detail: Running ads: a beginner asks if they worked, an expert tracks the hundred steps to profit. So commit to progress and feedback (more closes, lower churn, better content), not to a date.
