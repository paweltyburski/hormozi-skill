### MZ-BjS9rqul4x4#01 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A pre-revenue ERP for rope-access window-cleaning firms needs 938 customers at $735 average monthly revenue each to reach $8.3M a year.
detail: Base $99 a month plus $34.95 per employee; about 20 target firms per city, around $5M revenue each. He debates selling on value or dropping the price and earning on inventory resale.
numbers: 938 customers; $735 per customer per month; $8.3M per year.

### MZ-BjS9rqul4x4#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:27
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: he would probably drop the $99 throwaway fee and charge about $5K one-time to get on, then roughly $35 a seat or something, with a clear price per seat.
detail: He says the $99 feels like a throwaway number since all the juice is in the seats. He first says the caller will learn more from the first 10 conversations than from him.
numbers: One-time fee about $5,000; seat price around $35 ("or something").
### MZ-BjS9rqul4x4#03 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:11
claim: Pitch business owners on two things: money saved and ease of onboarding, e.g. onboarded within 24 hours.
detail: Ask what they pay now and promise to beat it; aggregated purchasing volume lets the software find savings, and per-employee savings should dwarf the seat cost.
