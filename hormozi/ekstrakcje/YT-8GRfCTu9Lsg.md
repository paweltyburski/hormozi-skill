### YT-8GRfCTu9Lsg#01 | CASE | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:32
claim: Dividing roles between two founders took the business from roughly $400-500K a month to millions a month.
detail: Both founders were strong at sales and tag-teamed everything. A mentor listed all tasks on a board and circled about 80% for the wife as operator (fulfilment, flow); Hormozi kept marketing, sales and product. The operator then fulfilled the promises he generated.
numbers: From $400,000-$500,000 a month to millions a month; about 80% of tasks moved to the operator.

### YT-8GRfCTu9Lsg#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:06:34
claim: A partner outside the business plays one of two roles: cheerleader (supportive, gives perspective, never asks you to quit when it matters) or fullback (blocks while you carry, then swap).
detail: Source credited to Gary Vee. A spouse not in the business is fine but must be supportive; the cheerleader helps you detach and see business in context, which prevents emotional, erratic decisions when all your chips are on it. If they do neither, they detract.

### YT-8GRfCTu9Lsg#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:42
claim: Some tensions are dichotomies to manage rather than solve, such as micromanaging against delegating or closeness against distance.
detail: Credited to Esther Perel. Constant closeness makes partners familiar roommates; too much distance makes strangers. Keep the pendulum near the middle. For founders who live and work together: separate offices, opposite sides of the house, not attending the same meetings, and creating space to be missed.

### YT-8GRfCTu9Lsg#04 | EVOLUTION | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:28
claim: After about $30M profit in 24 months, they found their money had been driven by fear of loss and chose to prioritise their marriage in 2020.
detail: Fear shifts over time: first of being poor, later of losing the status money brought. Both admitted they were unhappy with the pace, each had feared the other would say they were happy, and saying so diffused it. Admitting a shared reality let them move forward with truth and integrity first.
numbers: About $30M profit in the 24 months before 2020.
