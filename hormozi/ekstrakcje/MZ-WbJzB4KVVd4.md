### MZ-WbJzB4KVVd4#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals, content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A skincare e-commerce owner relying on AI influencers and many products is told she runs an arbitrage business without a brand; brand needs two or three premium associations.
detail: Free clicks and impressions are reach, not influence. His own trust in a platform he invested in cannot be copied by AI avatars. Less cool people then want to join the cool ones.

### MZ-WbJzB4KVVd4#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:15
claim: Arbitrage is buying clicks at X and selling for Y; a brand needs a narrow avatar, representative faces and no attempt to be for everyone.
detail: A hundred avatars say you are for no one. Pick one avatar with three problems and repeat them in every video. The chain: aspirational hero says best, needs to be cool, needs a good product.

### MZ-WbJzB4KVVd4#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:27
claim: A $300M-a-year performance-marketed weight-loss business is unsellable because it has no brand and its monthly revenue swings with cost per click.
detail: Monthly revenue swings between about $8M and $30M and margins are thin due to acquisition costs; no buyer will touch it.
numbers: $300M/year; monthly swings from $8M to $30M (as stated).

### MZ-WbJzB4KVVd4#04 | DEFINITION | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:05
claim: The north-star metric is revenue retention, not subscription arbitrage; brand value is the distribution of customers who trust you and rebuy.
detail: Subscription arbitrage is bundling to push average basket from one to four lotions; it gets people to buy more, not more times. A large base of repeat buyers lets you launch new products.

### MZ-WbJzB4KVVd4#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:39
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: sell through existing inventory quickly, use the cash to fund legit products and the biggest sponsor possible, then leverage them to bring in lower-tier affiliates.
detail: On rebranding: if premium people say they think you're a scammer, you probably need to rebrand; if they accept it, you're probably fine and the brand will shift. Get to the point where you love the products first.
### MZ-WbJzB4KVVd4#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:24
claim: Brands should be aspirational ideals no one reaches; buyers discount the claim to their own use.
detail: People buy a jacket because it stands on Everest; they know they will only use it in a car park. His $100M launch shows a buyer that he could add an extra million. Use cool associates for whitelisted ads and affiliate recruiting.

### MZ-WbJzB4KVVd4#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: retention-ltv, verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:16
claim: Well-run e-commerce gets about half of revenue from email; this store had about 15% and should aim near 40%.
detail: Build flows per decision path (abandoned cart, etc.) plus two or three value emails a week. Many big brands lose money on the store and take all profit from email.
numbers: ~50% of revenue from email when done well; ~15% here; target ~40%; 2-3 emails/week.
