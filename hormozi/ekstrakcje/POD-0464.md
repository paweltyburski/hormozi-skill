### POD-0464#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:23
claim: A worked example shows ads becoming self-funding once the first sales cash arrives.
detail: $100 a day on ads gives 10 leads at $10, 7 book, 4 show, 2 buy at $600 each, so $1,200 from $100. Because the buyers close about three days after the spend, he is about $400 down before the first $1,200 lands in the bank. After that the campaign is funded by sales, which he calls a negative acquisition cost, so there is no reason to stop until another bottleneck appears.
numbers: $100/day; 10 leads; 7 scheduled; 4 showed; 2 sold at $600 = $1,200; peak cash gap about $400 by day four.

### POD-0464#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:27
claim: When acquisition stops being the limit, operations become the bottleneck, because owners lack leadership and organisation.
detail: More customers expose the weak process rather than solving everything. He lists reach-outs, handwritten cards and a "wow" package as examples of process to build, and adds that if the service were as good as owners claim, people would already know about it.

### POD-0464#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:55
claim: Saying that customer service is your point of difference is usually false, since yours is likely just the expected level.
detail: When he asks salons for their point of difference, most answer service. He treats that as a sign of mediocrity unless the service is truly exceptional.

### POD-0464#04 | CASE | topic: sales-process | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:09:13
claim: For a celebrity-booked hair stylist, he would sell a $100 meeting and then use an assumed close for about $2,000 of extensions.
detail: The $100 appointment positions her as in demand and screens buyers; the sale of the larger service then happens in person. His reply to the owner's doubt that she can get $40 up front is that the answer is sales and an offer that is hard to refuse.
numbers: $100 consult then ~$2,000 close, against the owner's belief that $40 up front is the limit.
applies_to: Salons and appearance services

### POD-0464#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: offers | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:47
claim: If the business needs a brilliant salesperson to work, the offer is broken.
detail: A mentor told him that sales is giving an offer people would feel stupid refusing. Make the offer amazing, then let the model and systems back up why it is great, and use outlandish guarantees to make buyers happy about the purchase.

### POD-0464#06 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Trim and Stack | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:10:57
claim: Splinter stack: peel apart what you already do, name each piece, and present them as bonuses to make a stack of value.
detail: Competing on "best value" with everything included is unclear to buyers. Instead price one core service, then list the parts that were included anyway as separate named bonuses.
applies_to: Service businesses such as chiropractors and salons

### POD-0464#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:12:28
claim: Do not copy competitors' offers and prices, because most of them are barely surviving.
detail: He says copying peers whose heads are just above water for years guarantees the same result. In gyms he cites that about 80% of those that survived were struggling.
numbers: ~80% of surviving gyms barely afloat (his estimate).

### POD-0464#08 | DECISION_RULE | topic: unit-economics | also: management-ops | fw: Crazy Eight | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:43
claim: The three growth moves are more clients, higher prices and more purchases per client, but measure attrition, average ticket and lifetime value first.
detail: Without these three metrics you cannot improve them. His advice is to establish them before anything else, then move them.
