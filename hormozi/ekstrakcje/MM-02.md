### MM-02#01 | CASE | topic: unit-economics | also: strategy-positioning | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Hormozi's meals company looked like 5:1 on revenue but was about 1:1 on gross profit, so he shut it down.
detail: CAC was $100 and lifetime revenue $500, and the team wanted to scale. But gross margin was 20%: customers paid about $165/month for roughly three months, giving $33 gross profit in month one and $100 lifetime — barely covering CAC before staff costs. Making it work at 3:1 would have needed about $1,500 lifetime (he says gross profit; the margin math implies revenue). Always ask for gross margin before trusting a revenue ratio.
numbers: CAC $100; lifetime revenue $500; margin 20%; 30-day GP $33; LTGP $100.

### MM-02#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:19
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: Hormozi shut down his meals company because it produced most of the customer complaints and little of the profit, and he advised himself to stop rather than keep working on it.
detail: Meals caused 80% of customer complaints and 5% of profits; supplements caused 20% of complaints and 95% of profits. Meals was his fourth business while he was CEO of another, Layla pushed him to pick, and a co-packer meant no layoffs, so he closed it.
numbers: Meals 80% of complaints / 5% of profit; supplements 20% / 95%.

### MM-02#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: - | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:19
claim: Lifetime gross profit (not revenue) should be at least three times CAC — 3:1 is the floor, not the goal.
detail: Below 3:1 you are in serious trouble; at 3:1 you are still not in a great position; ideally the ratio is much higher.
numbers: LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1 minimum.

### MM-02#04 | FRAMEWORK | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models | fw: Three Numbers That Matter (CAC, GP, Payback Period) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:05
claim: Three numbers govern acquisition: CAC, gross profit, and payback period — aim for low CAC, high gross profit and short payback.
detail: CAC is what a customer costs to get; gross profit is what they pay you after delivery costs; payback period is how long until you recover the CAC. Cheap customers who pay a lot quickly means more money faster — the whole course is about how to achieve this. He ties these to the "30 day thing" from client-financed acquisition.
