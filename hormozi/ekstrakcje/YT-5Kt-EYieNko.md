### YT-5Kt-EYieNko#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:18
claim: Before taking a bet, ask: what is my upside, what is my downside, and can I live if the downside happens.
detail: Taken from the book The Road Less Stupid. He assumes the downside will occur, including unknown costs, and says no when he cannot live with it. This frees attention for the few bets that matter.

### YT-5Kt-EYieNko#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:07
claim: The "positive expected value, so always bet" argument ignores that a small company has limited capital to place bets.
detail: Bezos-style logic (a 1-in-10 chance at a 100x payoff is worth taking) is correct mathematically but assumes unlimited funds. Illustration: a casino with $10 bets paying $1,000 at 1-in-10 odds, but only $30 in the pocket, gives roughly a one-in-three chance (his estimate) of leaving with anything. Most owners have constrained capital, time and team bandwidth.
numbers: $30 bankroll, $10 bets, 1-in-10 odds, $1,000 payout.

### YT-5Kt-EYieNko#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: management-ops | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:33
claim: He declines marginal tests against a proven control when the loss exceeds the gain.
detail: A new follow-up sequence might add 10% leads, but could cut results 50% against a control already beaten into shape; he cannot live with that downside, so he says no.
numbers: Upside +10%, downside -50% versus a tested control.

### YT-5Kt-EYieNko#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:13
claim: The best bettors bet rarely, and only on near-certain opportunities.
detail: Citing Charlie Munger, he notes that a small number of bets (about 15 over roughly 40 years) produced the bulk of the wealth. In business, choices such as hiring a salesperson first or spending on a campaign are also bets.
numbers: About 15 winning bets over about 40 years.

### YT-5Kt-EYieNko#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:19
claim: Untested changes impose a hidden cost of change and often produce no clear answer, and optimism understates it.
detail: Typical failure: a half-trained team launches a new process, results stay flat, three other things changed in the same period, and nothing is learned. Optimism makes owners overrate the chance of success and underrate the cost. Saying no to small tweaks keeps the team on the fundamentals, so they compound 1% improvements instead of resetting every two weeks.
