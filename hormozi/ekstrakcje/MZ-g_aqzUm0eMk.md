claim: A done-for-you cold-email agency serving B2B small businesses has low LTV and high churn; Hormozi blames the avatar.
detail: Small businesses lack sales processes, mis-price, and turn the agency into their consultant. No massive public marketing agency sells mainly to SMBs.

### MZ-g_aqzUm0eMk#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:36
claim: Structural churn happens when customers' own businesses fail, so consistent delivery does not retain them.
detail: If delivery is consistent and a client still leaves, the avatar is the problem. Serving the masses means winning on cost at scale, perhaps a tenth of the usual price.

### MZ-g_aqzUm0eMk#03 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: retention-ltv | fw: 80/20 Customer Analysis | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:36
claim: List the best customers (say 20%), write messaging for their three or four shared traits, and tell others they are not a fit.
detail: Repeat the cut to move up market.
numbers: Example split 20% great / 80% poor.

### MZ-g_aqzUm0eMk#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:26
claim: Pick a side from a strategy perspective: win on cost at the low end or go upmarket; he describes the middle as a mediocre muck.
detail: Cheap: agencies he has seen at $1.99-$2.99 a month built around servicing customers for $50 or less; with 5% churn, about $4K LTV against about $100 CAC (arithmetic unclear in audio). numbers: $1.99-$2.99 per month; 5% churn; CAC about $100; upmarket about $1M/year per client.
