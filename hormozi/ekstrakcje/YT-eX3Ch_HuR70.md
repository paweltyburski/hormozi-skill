claim: Rihanna priced for her whole audience, not for her luxury partner's customers, and he calls affordable pricing a good, on-brand move for her.
detail: Her partner sells bags for $5,000-$50,000, yet her site lists $3 underwear, $5 G-strings, $10 bras, $15 sports styles and $20 lounge styles. Her audience was everyone, so she made it affordable for everyone. For his own firm, big deals make high price right.
numbers: Prices from about $3 to $20 per item.

### YT-eX3Ch_HuR70#09 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: paid-ads | fw: Content Unit (Hook-Retain-Reward) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:58
claim: Make organic content native to each platform and let demonstration create the association with your brand.
detail: Instagram: polished images, behind-the-scenes only in stories. TikTok: short product clips, trending sounds, creator collaborations, TikTok Shop checkout. YouTube: long try-on hauls, reviews, promoting the membership. In B2B or services, demonstrating expertise does the same job.

### YT-eX3Ch_HuR70#10 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: sales-process | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:23:05
claim: Treat affiliates as another customer tier: they will not promote on their own, so make an offer so good they feel stupid refusing.
detail: Steps she followed: pick affiliates deliberately, make an offer (mostly cash sponsorships with posting cadence and wardrobe), supply banners, links and promotional materials to keep it turnkey and on brand. The ideal deal is brand-accretive: both parties gain goodwill and distribution at no cost (her Emmy-winning show with invited stars).
numbers: His community platform has about 65% affiliate or referral traffic.

### YT-eX3Ch_HuR70#11 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:24:19
claim: Only about 3% of people are ready to buy today, so brand building enlarges the buyer pool, and brand beats direct response over a year while direct response wins in 30 days.
detail: Direct-response marketers compete for the 3% with ever better offers. Brand awareness and association move more of the other 97% along the continuum, say to 15%, which is five times the pool, and extra reach is yours alone. That is where 20x to 100x returns on advertising come from, he says.
numbers: 3% ready to buy, 97% not; illustrative 15% pool = 5x.

### YT-eX3Ch_HuR70#12 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:33:23
claim: A credit-based membership works like a gift card: customers lend you money interest-free and unused credit is profit.
detail: The credit rolls over, so skipped months stack ($60 plus $60), acting as layaway or savings for a special occasion, and a $75 item costs $15 extra at checkout. Credits cannot be redeemed for cash. Because lingerie is bought on special occasions, not monthly, credit plus discount keeps the brand top of mind, and browsing alone is enjoyable.
numbers: $59.95 monthly credit; example: $75 purchase with $60 credit = $15 to pay.
applies_to: Physical products bought irregularly

### YT-eX3Ch_HuR70#13 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: money-models | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:32:32
claim: Personalised monthly recommendations plus a skip option lower the risk of paying for something unwanted.
detail: Recommendations come from past purchases. For the first days of each month members see the basket and can skip billing at no cost, so being a member without buying costs nothing.
numbers: Skip window: first four days of the month (podcast said first to fifth).
