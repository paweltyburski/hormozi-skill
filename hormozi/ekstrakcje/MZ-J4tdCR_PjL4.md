### MZ-J4tdCR_PjL4#01 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: verticals, scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A skincare brand (women 55-65, direct-response funnels plus phone sales) at $4.5M a month wanted $25M a month; the bottleneck had moved from sales staffing to leadership.
detail: Earlier, ad spend outran call capacity and margins fell; a recruiting team fixed sales hiring. Now heads of function are missing, so the owner gets pulled into daily fires. Plan was to copy the recruitment team for leaders. Hormozi asked whether roles were one-time or recurring (one-time), and advised a mix of outside headhunters and personal outreach.
numbers: $4.5M monthly revenue; target $25M monthly; target roles pay $100K-$200K a year.

### MZ-J4tdCR_PjL4#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:44
claim: Use your network for roles you can judge, and pay a headhunter for functions where you lack expertise and network, such as finance, tech lead and HR.
detail: A sales-and-marketing founder can find sales and marketing leaders personally. A good blue-chip search firm can also run the technical interview, leaving the founder to judge culture and fit.

### MZ-J4tdCR_PjL4#03 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: outreach | fw: Internal Core Four (Recruiting Mirror) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:28
claim: Headhunting is an outreach game where the CEO as sender gets higher replies; ask the target who they know who is like them.
detail: Message structure: flatter their experience, describe a great role and its pay, and ask if they know anyone like them. The low-pressure referral ask lets them say yes or name a friend. For big hiring constraints, he and his wife block a six-week sprint of interviews; the founder joins for C-suite roles, not for directors or managers.
numbers: 6-week interview sprint.

### MZ-J4tdCR_PjL4#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:22
claim: Do not replace every leadership role at once; fix the role most on fire first, then the next.
detail: Owner feared performance would drop if he stopped managing six direct reports to recruit full time. Hormozi: order by pain, recruit one, then attack the next worst.

### MZ-J4tdCR_PjL4#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:07
claim: Speed in business depends on pattern recognition for talent: you recognise a hire level only after you have found that type once.
detail: He found his first salesperson, then copied what they looked like; same for sales manager, one level at a time. Where you do not know the pattern, borrow experts (for example, have your attorneys interview an in-house counsel).

### MZ-J4tdCR_PjL4#06 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:59
claim: Reframe recruiter fees as an investment: paying a fee to get a role that earns a multiple is a risk-reward trade.
detail: Recruiters charge 20-25% of annual package, so six or seven roles cost $25-40K each; the owner considered in-house recruiters at $10K a month. Hormozi: spread the hires month by month to soften cash impact and ask whether you would pay $40 to make $400.
numbers: fee 20-25% of annual package = $25K-$40K per role; in-house recruiters $10K a month.
