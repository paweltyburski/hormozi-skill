### SC-01#02 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops, hiring-talent | fw: Three Elements of Scaling | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:31
claim: Scaling needs three elements: functions (what the business must do), people (who does it) and operations (the glue connecting people to functions).
detail: Functions: marketing, sales, finance, customer success, legal and so on. People: directors, managers, individual contributors. Operations: meetings, communication, training. With all three you can grow from $0 and no staff to $100M+ and many people.

### SC-01#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: hiring-talent | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:45
claim: Scaling stages are defined by headcount, not revenue, because org structure and functions are far more consistent across businesses than revenue is.
detail: A restaurant making $2M and a software company making $100M can both have 35 employees and organise similarly — a 50x revenue gap set by the opportunity of the business, not its structure. He adds that a company that reaches about 500 employees will very likely be able to reach $100M a year (a likelihood, not a rule).
numbers: 35 employees: $2M restaurant vs $100M software; ~500 employees: very likely able to reach $100M/year.

### SC-01#04 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:21
claim: The roadmap has 10 headcount stages, each described by a one-word theme, headcount, founder role, leadership structure, share of companies, function-level constraints and a bottom-line objective.
detail: Founder role: how priorities translate into functions and where focus goes. Leadership structure: how many layers of leaders. Prevalence: how many companies reach the stage (US Census / Bureau of Labor Statistics data). For each of eight functions: the constraint at that level and what to do to graduate. Bottom line: the one big constraint and the one action to graduate — the single takeaway if you only keep one (00:06:52).

### SC-01#05 | DEFINITION | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: Eight Business Functions | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:03
claim: The roadmap tracks eight business functions, each defined by its job.
detail: Product: goods, services and results delivered. Marketing: telling people about your stuff. Sales: getting people to give you money for it. Customer service: raising the odds customers rate you highly, buy again and refer. IT: gathering, storing, analysing and displaying information. Recruiting: finding and hiring people. HR: lowering the odds that regulators and employees sue you. Finance: reporting and managing money.

### SC-01#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: - | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:52
claim: Treat the stages as guidelines: if you've skipped steps in a function relative to your headcount, it will likely become a weak link, so consider prioritising it; if you're ahead in a function, it's probably not your constraint.
detail: Every company differs, so use the nearest stage, not an exact match. Look at the stage before (to confirm it's complete) and the stage after (to see what's next) so you don't build something you'll later have to reverse (00:07:41).

### SC-01#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:28
claim: Study only your current stage (the one before and the one after are optional) — he says a lot of companies die of indigestion, not starvation.
detail: Consuming every stage jumbles in priorities that aren't yours yet, and businesses stall by trying to do too much, not too little. Strategy means putting limited resources where they earn the highest return, which usually means the current constraint of the business.
quote: A lot of companies actually die by indigestion, not starvation.
