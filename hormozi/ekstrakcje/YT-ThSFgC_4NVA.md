### YT-ThSFgC_4NVA#01 | CASE | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Hormozi bombed a stage pitch by rushing it and apologising for it, and handled objections with irritation.
detail: At a fitness mastermind he had a three-hour slot and a product built for that audience. He gave value for two and a half hours, then raced through a normally 90-minute deck with no pre-frame and a rushed offer. It was his first sale ever below $16,000 (this one was $2,000). Questions about payment plans and a vegan objection got curt "then don't buy" replies, followed by 30 minutes of irritable Q&A.
numbers: Room was about one third of the promised size (roughly 30 people); product price $2,000; usual deck 90 minutes.
applies_to: Anyone pitching a cheaper product to a new audience

### YT-ThSFgC_4NVA#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-process | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:22
claim: No half measures: either pitch or don't pitch; if you ask, ask hard and mean it.
detail: Walking through the offer fast and casually undermines it. When you decide to ask, ask hard and make it obvious you are asking. He says that asking means drawing on the goodwill you have built with the audience.
### YT-ThSFgC_4NVA#03 | EXAMPLE | topic: sales-process | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:22
claim: Telling an audience they do not need scarcity, guarantees or bonuses removes the very things that help them decide.
detail: One of his gym-business executives pitched to an audience of gym owners and skipped the persuasion elements, saying they already knew them from the training. It failed. Even people who know the techniques still need them applied to the offer in front of them.

### YT-ThSFgC_4NVA#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:39
claim: Humiliating or highly emotional moments are a chance to learn fast, so deliberately pick the lesson to keep.
detail: He explains that intense emotion puts the brain in a heightened learning state, citing conversations with a psychologist (name unclear in audio). His habit after a painful event is to ask what he wants permanently ingrained from it, and then write that lesson down as a rule.

### YT-ThSFgC_4NVA#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: content-brand | also: sales-process | fw: Give:Ask Ratio | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:29
claim: Usually hold the ask back and keep giving; treat every ask as a withdrawal from goodwill, and make it a big one.
detail: He postulates that a longer run of giving than the common "give, give, give, ask" pattern is even more powerful. A longer run of giving, with only occasional asks, makes people reciprocate unprompted, and many good things in his life came that way. Since an ask spends accumulated goodwill, do not make small nickel-and-dime asks; keep over-delivering, then make one clear, committed request.
