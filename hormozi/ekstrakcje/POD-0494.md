### POD-0494#02 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:34
claim: A short-term bridge loan to real-estate wholesalers earned a high return and, after the borrower repaid more than the invoice, a lasting reputation gain.
detail: Wholesalers needed $3M for a distressed building overnight and offered a $100K-a-month return. Hormozi lent against a first lien on the building, personal guarantees, and a new buyer's earnest money. His lawyer under-invoiced; the borrower paid the correct, larger sum against his own lawyer's advice to keep the difference, saying he wanted future deals. Lesson relayed: you get one name, so invest in it.
numbers: Loan $3M against property valued at $5.6M; interest $100K/month (stated as 48% a year, which does not match $100K on $3M); held about 3.5 months; invoiced about $280K, actual about $330K, a $50K shortfall. Borrower made about $2.3M net.

### POD-0494#03 | FRAMEWORK | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:09:06
claim: Hire and partner on three values in order: unimpeachable character, sincere candor, competitive greatness; most candidates fail the first, so the rest rarely need testing.
detail: Character matters first because hardship in business is certain, so test it before urgency. Hormozi likes a Navy SEAL style test he was told about (he says it may or may not be true): would I trust this person with my wife for a long time, or with a huge sum I'd come back for later and find it there 10 years on. Hard questions show how deep character goes.
applies_to: Hires, partners and portfolio-company founders.

### POD-0494#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:16:36
claim: Every business rests on information arbitrage: one side knows how to do something profitably that the other does not.
detail: Even a cattle ranch sells know-how to buyers who cannot raise animals profitably. If buyers had the knowledge, they would do it themselves and absorb the supply chain, which is vertical integration. This is the source of seller-buyer advantage, distinct from how value is communicated, which is persuasion.

### POD-0494#07 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:18:16
claim: Judge an opportunity on three variables: total units you could sell, the value-to-cost discrepancy (profit per unit), and competitive dynamics.
detail: Example: mobile phone service has huge demand and very low cost per added customer but entrenched players, so only two of three. The equation changes over time as you learn how to expand the market.

### POD-0494#09 | FRAMEWORK | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:20:32
claim: Five ways to resize a market: go up-market, down-market, adjacent, broader, or narrower.
detail: Using hair salons: up-market is chains or franchisors (fewer but larger clients); down-market is individual stylists or aspiring ones; adjacent is similar lash or nail salons; broader bundles adjacents under one umbrella such as beauty; narrower adds constraints on who qualifies. Narrowing is usually the first step at portfolio companies.

### POD-0494#10 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: retention-ltv | fw: 80/20 Customer Analysis | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:22:29
claim: Common factor analysis: take the top 20% of customers, find what they share, and sell only to that profile.
detail: Same 100 customers all drawn from that top 20% would, by his estimate, multiply revenue with no extra operating drag.
numbers: Pareto: top 20% of 100 clients give about 80% of revenue; selling only that profile claimed about 5x revenue.
