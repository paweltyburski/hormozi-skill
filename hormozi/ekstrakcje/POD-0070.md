### POD-0070#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: sales-process | also: offers | fw: Perceived Hidden Costs | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:28
claim: In the sale, talk about the outcome and never the method, because describing the work only reminds the buyer of the cost.
detail: The buyer wants the destination (his analogy is a holiday island), not the flight, lines and baggage. Training style, meal plans, macros and trainer certifications all read as cost. He points to a weight-loss franchise (the NIC) whose process does not tell people about the cost.
applies_to: Gyms and transformation services.
### POD-0070#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:48
claim: Large-group training should sit at $150-$225 a month, typically near $200; if the owner is not there, he says they are probably not taking much home because the math does not work out.
detail: $150 is the very bare low end, only for a very high-capacity facility. If an owner charges $400 for semi-private and says it works, charging $600 would earn about 50% more.
numbers: Large group $150-$225/month (low end only with very high capacity); $400 vs $600 is a 50% gap.
applies_to: Brick-and-mortar fitness with a 16+ person capacity.
### POD-0070#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:12
claim: About $600 is the break point for a first-time buy from cold traffic, and a weight-loss franchise with 220 locations followed a $600 front end with a $1,500-$6,000 upsell on day five.
detail: The franchise (name unclear in audio) asked clients only to come once a week for 15 minutes with a food log, so the buyer saw little cost. His view: most people underestimate how much they will pay for a result, and the franchise needed no income segmentation.
numbers: $600 front end; $1,500-$6,000 upsell on day 5; 220 locations.

### POD-0070#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:59
claim: A variant of the value equation: significance of outcome times likelihood of achievement, divided by time and effort, illustrated by a sleeping pill versus meditation lessons.
detail: A sedative has a medium-to-high outcome, near-100% likelihood and zero effort, so it is a giant industry. Meditation offers a similar outcome but lower likelihood, plus learning time and effort, so it is worth less. The same logic means training style is irrelevant; only the outcome and its cost to the buyer matter.

### POD-0070#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: promotions-launches | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:22
claim: Six weeks works for the general population because it is long enough to feel significant and short enough to feel doable; twelve weeks works only for a narrower customer.
detail: At twelve weeks people think they can get a lot but doubt they will finish. He says six-week challenges had run, in effect, untested against alternatives for about six and a half years.
numbers: 6 weeks vs 12 weeks; about 6.5 years of use.

### POD-0070#06 | EVOLUTION | topic: product-delivery | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:47
claim: Fitness purists give beginners what they themselves needed (lifting, macro counting) at the start; instead give a large early psychological win, then teach the detail.
detail: Telling a person 50-100 pounds overweight they will lose a pound and a half a week for two years loses them. Show a large early loss first, then introduce food logging and counting once they are bought in. He admits he held the purist view for a long time. People think almond butter or quinoa is a good protein source, so meet them where they are.
numbers: Example result: 40 pounds in the first eight weeks; realistic rate cited: 1.5 pounds of fat a week.

### POD-0070#07 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:48
claim: Over 24-25 Gym Launch gyms had passed a $1M run rate, across kickboxing, martial arts and small-town studios, which he uses as proof that style and location do not matter.
detail: A $1M run rate is about $83,000 a month. The only caveat is that the owner is willing to offer a fitness transformation. Humans behave the same: women diet about five times a year. His own start: he emailed 40 gym owners to ask to learn, only one replied, and that owner said the semi-private programme was full; Hormozi's answer was to put extra people in the bootcamp with a nutrition plan, believing they would pay $500 for results.
numbers: $1M run rate = ~$83,000/month; 24-25 gyms at that level; 1 reply out of 40 emails; $500 for bootcamp plus nutrition plan.
