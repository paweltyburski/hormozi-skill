### POD-0130#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:19
claim: Leadership sits on a triangle of fear, love and respect, and the leader aims for the centre.
detail: Midpoints produce behaviours: respect plus love gives loyalty, fear plus love gives sacrifice, fear plus respect gives obedience (he says buy-in is the polite word). He compares it to how people relate to a creator: all three emotions, not one. How big an organisation you can lead is proportional to how deep each of the three roots runs.

### POD-0130#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:27:40
claim: When the team will not buy in, ask which of the three is missing: enforcement, sacrifice, or genuine care.
detail: Fear side: did I set expectations and then fail to enforce consequences? Respect side: do I ask others for things I would not do myself? Love side: do people believe I deeply care about them, beyond coddling? Symptoms he maps: no loyalty or people leaving points to a love-respect imbalance; eye-rolling and talking behind your back signal no fear; no consequences means no accountability.

### POD-0130#03 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:32
claim: Set the tone in the first interaction, give respect first, and correct small disrespect immediately.
detail: If a new hire arrives two minutes late, say that you will not be late for them, which raises the bar. Apologise explicitly when you are late to a vendor or employee ("no disrespect meant"), which surprises people and earns respect for your time.

### POD-0130#04 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:25
claim: Earn respect by sacrificing for the group beyond what anyone expects, especially in ways others notice later in private.
detail: Examples: take the late shifts and weekends on a sales team, work late so others can prepare for tests. Respect from things other people say about you exceeds anything you say yourself. He also cites Gym Launch's founders not taking a paycheck to build the business.

### POD-0130#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:21
claim: Owning flaws publicly turns shame into perceived strength.
detail: Hidden shame costs headspace; once discussed openly, people respect the courage and you stop posturing. Live the same in private and public, because the truth always surfaces.

### POD-0130#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:54
claim: To hold others to a high standard, hold yourself to a higher one, because asking more of others than of yourself loses respect.
detail: Hormozi personally feels it is easier to ask something of people who know beyond doubt that he would do five times more. In his view this gives him the high ground in that conversation, though he says there may be other ways.
numbers: leader should be believed to do 5x what they ask (his phrasing).
### POD-0130#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:23:08
claim: Match leadership style to the team: sales teams tolerate more fear, creative teams need less.
detail: Salespeople tend to be more alpha and push boundaries; marketing staff are more creative, and creativity does not survive an environment of fear.
applies_to: Managers of mixed teams.
