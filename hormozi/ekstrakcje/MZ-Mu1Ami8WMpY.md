claim: An owner of two med spas (LTV:CAC 3.9, 30% EBITDA) is tempted by online weight loss.
detail: Run rate $2.5-3M revenue, about $1M EBITDA, a lean model. Med-spa M&A is active; around 10 locations ($5-6M) could sell for about $50M. Online has unknown devils.
numbers: LTV:CAC 3.9; EBITDA 30%; $2.5-3M revenue; ~$1M EBITDA; ~10 locations for exit.

### MZ-Mu1Ami8WMpY#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:31
claim: Discount a new opportunity by the cost of distraction (perhaps 80%), since you cannot do both.
detail: Work backward from the goal and pick the lowest-risk path. Compare year four and five of staying against zero-to-five in year one elsewhere.
numbers: ~80% distraction discount (his example).

### MZ-Mu1Ami8WMpY#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:24
claim: He suggests asking which option you could do for ten years; he framed it as a "you" question for this caller.
detail: Over a ten-year horizon he says it won't matter which works; for a shorter flip, usually the current business is the devil you know while the new one has many unknown devils, and everything always looks more attractive when you're not in it.
