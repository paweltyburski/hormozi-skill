### YT-s59jbVBprn0#01 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A clinic owner at about $550,000 a month wanted to start a licensing business for physicians, and Hormozi pointed him back to growing the existing business.
detail: Clinics went to telehealth in COVID and profit exploded in about two years. Hormozi's objections to the new idea: a saturated market, a value proposition based on a handful of examples, nothing unique, a new acquisition system. Asked if he would still want it if the clinics tripled to $1.5M a month, the owner said no; he had simply felt stuck.
numbers: $550,000/month; $1.5M/month; goal of $5M/month.

### YT-s59jbVBprn0#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:29
claim: He usually starts conversations with an entrepreneur or a portfolio company CEO by taking the current run rate and asking what is stopping the business from being at 5x (for example, $1M a month to $5M a month).
detail: Until that question is answered clearly, nothing else needs to be done. "What stops us doing an extra million a month" is a better question than "why am I not growing", because it points to a single constraint.
numbers: Example $1M/month to $5M/month.
### YT-s59jbVBprn0#03 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:18
claim: The only reasonable external reason for a plateau is that you have saturated your total addressable market or the market has shrunk.
detail: If you have saturated one niche (say 70% of poodle owners), hop to another vertical. Otherwise the reason is that you do not know how.
numbers: Example 70% of a niche.

### YT-s59jbVBprn0#04 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:58
claim: A friend's business selling digital ad products to newspapers could not grow because newspapers shrank about 25% a year, to about 5% of their size ten years earlier.
detail: His growth tracked the inverse of the industry, so he was limited to servicing survivors. The example shows a true market cause.
numbers: 25% annual shrinkage compounding over ~10 years; ~5% of former size.

### YT-s59jbVBprn0#05 | CASE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:46
claim: A weight-loss info-product seller at about $4M a year planned apparel, supplement and cream lines; Hormozi told her to take the $4M business to $10M instead.
detail: Her answer on why she had not was that she did not know how, which is the problem to solve rather than creating a new business.
numbers: $4M a year to $10M a year.

### YT-s59jbVBprn0#06 | CASE | topic: sales-team | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:35
claim: A young owner at about $150,000 a month had many fixable issues (LTGP, pricing, messaging), but the true constraint was not having scaled a sales team, so Hormozi refused to discuss anything else.
detail: Two weeks later, asked how to use the call, Hormozi again asked whether the sales team was built; it took two or three repeats of "that is the only thing that matters now" before the owner accepted it.
numbers: $150,000/month.

### YT-s59jbVBprn0#07 | TACTIC | topic: scaling-constraints | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:37
claim: Phrase the deficiency as a "how do I" question so it becomes solvable: how do I recruit, manage and train a sales team; how do I get more customers through a channel I am allowed to use?
detail: The clinic owner's product could not be advertised on Facebook, so the question became acquisition through other channels. The alternative, "I don't know what to do, so I will start another business", is the common bad answer.

### YT-s59jbVBprn0#08 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:26
claim: A business grows to the level of its owner's competence, so a $4M entrepreneur's next business will also be about $4M unless beliefs, traits or skills change.
detail: Running two companies as CEO is rare (he says Elon Musk is the exception) and starting another triples complexity.
numbers: $4M entrepreneur gives $4M business.

### YT-s59jbVBprn0#09 | EXAMPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:29
claim: Constraints come in sequence: a bigger sales team, then onboarding and training for it, then consistency of process, then lead supply.
detail: Focus on only the current constraint; the company looks fast from outside but feels clear and manageable inside.
