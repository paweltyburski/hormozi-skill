### POD-0530#01 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: The host's real-estate education mastermind lifted retention by halving the second-year price and keeping it there for as long as members stay.
detail: People first stayed about a year, took what they wanted and left. The host learned they stay for community, network, accountability and the quarterly in-person event. He then cut the year-two price in half and promised it would stay at that level indefinitely. Retention rose sharply.
numbers: Year-two price set at 50% of year one (illustrated later as $12,000 then $6,000).
applies_to: Education and mastermind memberships.

### POD-0530#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:04
claim: Gym Launch kept clients by staffing for experience: a large support team marked personal milestones, because people stay loyal to a person more than to a product.
detail: Hormozi says outsiders call it an information business, yet it had a 40-person support team. The team reacted to births, weddings, birthdays, holidays and a client's first trip, and to revenue milestones. In e-learning masterminds the results side (network, information) is usually covered, while the experience side is neglected: setting expectations, onboarding well and proactive contact.
numbers: 40-person support team at Gym Launch.

### POD-0530#03 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: offers | fw: GROWTH Onboarding | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:57
claim: At the start, ask each buyer why they joined and give them a personal roadmap, because people buying the same product are solving different problems.
detail: Most programmes contain far more than a member can consume. A roadmap built from the stated reason (for example network versus education) gives the shortest path to the value this person wants, in the form they want it. Hormozi says this dramatically raises the value they receive.

### POD-0530#04 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:02:30
claim: Use the buying power of a large member network to bundle discounts on services members already buy, so the savings can exceed the membership fee.
detail: With a few hundred entrepreneurs in a network, negotiate group rates on services and products everyone uses (yours or third parties'). Present the savings as part of the membership so that staying looks like a no-brainer and leaving looks foolish.
numbers: Illustration: network of 200-500 members; year-one fee $12,000, year-two fee $6,000.
applies_to: Masterminds, associations, communities.

### POD-0530#05 | CASE | topic: content-brand | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:25
claim: Hormozi priced his first book at about a dollar on Amazon and posted it once; it spread by word of mouth, because the goal was sharing, not book income.
detail: He first wrote it as an internal manual and decided to publish with one social post and no other marketing. Pricing near zero removed the risk of trying it, and treating a book as content (his videos are free too) made sense. His reasoning: if the book does not make people tell friends, there is no reason to promote it. After about a year it had roughly 5,000 reviews, with 5,500-6,000 hoped for in year one.
numbers: Cover price about $0.99 (Amazon took about $0.66); about 5,000 reviews at roughly 1 year.

### POD-0530#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:56
claim: A product whose value far exceeds its price becomes its own marketing, and reaching that ratio takes far more work than merely publishing.
detail: Hormozi estimates that publishing a book is about 20% of the effort of producing one so good that readers' value is far above what they paid and they tell others. With that ratio the product markets itself.

### POD-0530#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:20
claim: Giving all education away for free works as a partner-finding filter: the few who apply it and reach $3-10M a year come back already trusting you.
detail: The stated model is to document what they actually do and share it, accepting that nearly nobody ever pays. The rare executors who use the material without hand-holding and grow past a revenue level become the people with whom alignment and partnership are easiest, since the material has already proven itself to them. He prefers partners for ten years to selling more $10,000 products.
numbers: 99.9% never pay; executors in the example reach $3M, $5M, $8M, $10M per year.

### POD-0530#08 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: other | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:13
claim: Founders who are a couple keep a unified front: no public disagreement and no displays of private affection in front of the team; disputes wait until after the meeting.
detail: Hormozi says staff worry about working for a husband-and-wife pair, so behaviour in front of the team is kept professional, and they correct each other privately or on chat afterwards. Only a close inner circle (video editor, assistants) sees them together. Long-term partnership needs alignment on mission, values and lifestyle.

### POD-0530#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:27
claim: When picking businesses to back through a downturn, prefer low capital expense, high cash flow models in a growing category, since they hold pricing power and flex easily.
detail: Their reasoning: e-learning is growing because formal education is expensive and slow, so demand for income-producing skills persists while the formal supply shrinks. In inflation, firms that need no capital to expand have easier pricing power. During COVID all three of their businesses had to pivot, which was painful but left them better.
numbers: E-learning claimed to grow about 2.5x over five years (as stated on the podcast).
applies_to: Choosing or acquiring businesses.

### POD-0530#10 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:16
claim: Leila Hormozi: they back market leaders or likely leaders in a niche, not distressed firms, because in a downturn the market leader typically survives.
detail: Leila Hormozi: in a bad economy demand for nice-to-haves may fall, but demand for money-making skills stays as high, if not higher, so firms teaching niche income skills (for example fixing a hair salon, becoming a plumber) should stay in demand. They also consider whether the founder could do well in a downturn.
### POD-0530#11 | BENCHMARK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:50
claim: Leila Hormozi: making a business independent of its founder's face takes maybe 18-24 months, or about three years to do it really solidly.
detail: Leila says their portfolio companies plan this over three to five years so the founder keeps the choice to exit or stay. Her rule of thumb: for every Alex she needs about 10 people, some filling skill gaps and some acting as additional faces. More operational complexity, but a sturdier business.
numbers: Leila Hormozi: 18-24 months (basic), about 3 years (rock solid); about 10 people per founder face.
### POD-0530#12 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:36
claim: Spread the personality brand across several faces: promote niche experts and leaders, and produce content for them through a shared post-production team.
detail: The metaphor is a theme park with many characters instead of one mascot. The host's real-estate firm took its best students and coaches, gave them separate channels, and the post-production team applies the same thumbnails and editing as the main channel. A new podcast is hosted by someone else. Edify them by being seen with them first, then praising them when they are alone.

### POD-0530#13 | PRINCIPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: finance-exit | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:21:10
claim: Founder-fronted businesses lean on ads and organic content, so add acquisition methods that do not need the founder, which also reassures an acquirer.
detail: Alternatives named: affiliate channel, strong referral programme, manual outbound team, large blasts to existing lists, cold call, cold email, cold DM. At Gym Launch over half of sales came from the outbound team. Diversifying by channel and by method of acquisition is what makes buyers comfortable.
numbers: Gym Launch outbound: about 5,000 dials a day, said to produce around $100,000 (figure approximate); over 50% of sales from outbound.

### POD-0530#14 | CASE | topic: lead-getters | also: verticals | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:22:43
claim: Prestige Labs and a later business were run entirely through affiliates, one with a two-level super-affiliate structure, so the founder did not have to be the face.
detail: Prestige Labs reached about 3,000 affiliates and was 100% affiliate sales. The second business had super affiliates on two levels for the same reason. Even in paid ads, featuring customers and their results instead of the founder works because prospects care about what they see of themselves in the ad.
numbers: Prestige Labs: about 3,000 affiliates, 100% of sales.

### POD-0530#15 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:23:39
claim: To get the first top-tier hire, use a personal favour from your network or pay a recruiter; one strong hire then endorses you to the next.
detail: Early on you have little besides money to attract talent. Either pull a favour for one senior person, or spend heavily on a recruiter for a couple of key roles. Those people bring networks and make the company look legitimate, so later hiring becomes easier.

### POD-0530#16 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:25:15
claim: Gym Launch landed its CFO by borrowing the brand of a top recruiting firm that normally served only companies above $100M.
detail: A friend vouched for their growth rate, so the firm accepted them. Candidates replied because the firm was the best in the country. Lesson: if your own brand is weak, associate with someone else's.
numbers: Recruiter's usual minimum: companies above $100M (revenue; unclear in audio).

### POD-0530#17 | EXAMPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:25:50
claim: Acquisition.com makes recruiting its most hands-on service: it screens and places leaders in portfolio companies, and the placed people give it extra visibility into each business.
detail: The inbound flow of candidates and its portfolio's scale make candidates feel safe. Placed leaders who know and trust the firm give it more sources of information than the founder alone, so it can better judge how the company is doing. Hormozi's view: the more high-quality people placed where there is a deficit, the faster the growth.
