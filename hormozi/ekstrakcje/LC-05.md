The missing piece was brand; weeks later he started making content.

### LC-05#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: outreach | fw: Core Four | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:18
claim: Posting content has more leverage than warm outreach — the same effort reaches 10 or 10 million people — but takes more skill, because you compete with everyone and good work gets copied.
detail: Warm outreach runs out of time: telling 10 people means 10 conversations. Content scales, but you compete with everyone for attention and, if good, get copied, so you must keep innovating. You improve only by doing a lot over a long time; eventually a brand beats any one person doing outreach (an outreach army is covered later).

### LC-05#05 | FRAMEWORK | topic: content-brand | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Core Four | novelty: canon | ts: 00:04:19
claim: Content's pros: it grows your warm audience, makes all other advertising more effective, and costs only time; its cons: it is hard to personalise, you compete with everyone, and you must keep changing because people copy you.
detail: Pros: (1) followers are people you can contact one-to-one, so content and outreach feed each other as an ecosystem; (2) it lifts all other advertising; (3) it costs time, not money — a viral video's exposure is free, giving high return per dollar. Cons: (1) you cannot ask Cindy about her kids and Larry about his business in one piece; (2) standing out is hard; (3) copying forces constant change. The full ecosystem takes about a decade; early on, one or two methods can be enough. Do one, get good, then add.

### LC-05#06 | FRAMEWORK | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Content Unit (Hook-Retain-Reward) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:36
claim: The basic unit of content has three parts: a hook that gives a reason to consume, retention that keeps people long enough to get value, and a reward that satisfies the reason they came.
detail: Drawn from his own content and top creators; functions matter more than names. Without a hook nothing is consumed; without reward they will not consume your next piece. A unit can be as short as a meme (all three at once); long content contains many units spliced together.
quote: Hook, retain, reward.

### LC-05#08 | FRAMEWORK | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Five Topic Sources | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:11
claim: Hormozi draws topics from five sources: the far past (life lessons), the recent past, the present (real-time capture), trends where he has expertise, and manufactured events.
detail: (1) Far past — lessons that changed your behaviour (e.g., his brand conversion). (2) Recent past — scan your calendar for the last day or week for interesting conversations and situations; if unsure what interests people, cover everything and let the audience show you. (3) Present — capture observations as they happen (he used to email himself; now he tweets). (4) Trending — current events, only within your expertise. (5) Manufactured — invent something interesting, do it and document it (eating the world's hottest chip, living on $100 a month for a year). He uses the first four and rarely the fifth.

### LC-05#12 | FRAMEWORK | topic: content-brand | also: paid-ads | fw: Seven Headline Characteristics | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:09
claim: A meta-analysis of news headlines (shared by Dr. Kashey) found seven traits of high performers: recency, relevance, celebrity, proximity, conflict, unusualness and ongoing stories — combine as many as you can.
detail: Use the components, not a fill-in formula. (1) Recency — "just happened" beats "five years ago"; also a reason to keep remaking content on the same topics. (2) Relevance — nurses care about nurse issues; talk to moms about moms. (3) Celebrity — a boring breakfast becomes interesting if it is Bezos's. (4) Proximity — a fire next door, not across the country. (5) Conflict — pineapple on pizza, hero vs villain, freedom vs security, justice vs mercy (both valid ideals in tension, so division never ends). (6) Unusual — the circus's six-fingered man; a one-in-a-thousand trick shot spares viewers the 999 misses. (7) Ongoing — unfolding stories with possible twists, like minute-by-minute live news. Stack them and you have everyone's attention.

### LC-05#13 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: paid-ads | fw: Content Unit (Hook-Retain-Reward) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:15:13
claim: Format content to resemble what has already rewarded people on that platform, so they generalise past rewards to yours — otherwise better-packaged content hooks them first.
detail: Each platform has unspoken, evolving norms (the meme of one person's LinkedIn vs Facebook vs Instagram vs Tinder photo). People click what resembles past rewards — Hormozi clicks nearly every "dark Kermit" meme. Study top-performing content on the platform and make yours resemble it, not copy it. He shows one video formatted differently for Reels, TikTok and Shorts. The re-cut first five seconds (4,000 → 850,000 views) was this kind of packaging fix.

### LC-05#14 | FRAMEWORK | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Lists-Steps-Stories | novelty: canon | ts: 00:17:43
claim: Retention runs on curiosity — people want to know what comes next — and the three structures that create it are lists, steps and stories, which can be woven together.
detail: The course itself mixes all three (stories in nearly every section, numbered lists, step sequences) to hold attention and deliver what was promised.

### LC-05#15 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Lists-Steps-Stories | novelty: canon | ts: 00:17:43
claim: For list-style content (a run of items on a single theme), put the item count in the headline or opening seconds, because viewers stay longer.
detail: Examples: "top 10 mistakes", "7 ways I invested $1,000 in my 20s" (with a big payoff), "28 ways to stay poor". A visible number sets expectations about what is coming, and in his experience people watch or read longer as a result.

### LC-05#16 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Lists-Steps-Stories | novelty: canon | ts: 00:18:55
claim: Steps must be done in order to reach a result, so they are less flexible than lists but give a more explicit reward for consuming all of them.
detail: If early steps are clear and valuable, people want the rest ("38 steps to selling a house"). Examples: "3 steps to a great hook", "how I write headlines in 7 steps", "the morning routine that boosts my productivity". List items stand alone (four of twelve money tips still help) — flexible but weaker pull to finish; four of twelve steps may give little. Steps are harder to write but pull people to the end.

### LC-05#17 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Lists-Steps-Stories | novelty: canon | ts: 00:20:00
claim: Stories — events that happened, might happen or never will — retain because people want to know how they end; the ones worth telling carry a lesson.
detail: Star Wars shows even impossible stories pull people through. His examples: "My editor made me do 19 drafts — here's what I did to him"; from sleeping on a gym floor to the top floor of a five-star hotel. Tell the first part and people want the rest.

### LC-05#18 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Content Unit (Hook-Retain-Reward) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:21:58
claim: Always close the loops you open: creators who keep teasing "but wait, there's more" without paying off get one full watch, then lose the viewer.
detail: Long-run click-through and engagement come from people having been rewarded last time. Reward them enough and they stop scrolling whenever they see you; all your content performs better because your brand is known for delivering.

### LC-05#19 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:22:46
claim: Content quality is value per second: there is no such thing as too long, only too boring — in his own opinion attention spans have not shrunk, standards have risen.
detail: The person who quits a 10-second video after three seconds may binge a 900-page book or an eight-hour series. Families once gathered round the radio; biology has not changed in 50 years, the environment has — far more options compete for attention, so mediocrity is not tolerated. Be better than the competition, not just shorter.

### LC-05#20 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Content Unit (Hook-Retain-Reward) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:23:34
claim: You cannot guarantee someone feels rewarded, but you raise the odds by hooking the right audience, retaining them with lists, steps and stories, and clearly satisfying exactly what the hook promised.
detail: A cookie is no reward to someone who just ate twelve. Failure modes: "six ways to grow your dental practice" that delivers five, or six obvious ones — time spent, nothing gained, so they will not return. Deliver all six well and "five ways to hire a receptionist" gets watched. Wrong audience also fails: "seven ways to make up with your spouse" shown to single men. Unrewarded viewers neither return nor share, and sharing grows the brand.

### LC-05#21 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:25:05
claim: Is your audience growing? If not, your content is not good enough yet — it is not the algorithm.
detail: (1) The audience decides, not you. (2) Rewarding means matching or exceeding the expectation they came with — make a promise, keep it; do it enough and your promises become trusted. (3) Growth is the test; if absent, practise more, read feedback, improve. (4) Give more value than everyone else and keep your hooks' promises, and the audience will grow.

### LC-05#22 | DEFINITION | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Content Unit (Hook-Retain-Reward) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:26:20
claim: Long-form content is many content units strung together and short-form is few; drop-off happens where the hook-retain-reward arcs stop pulling people forward.
detail: Applies to long videos, podcasts, shorts, tweets and blogs. Like Lord of the Rings, a long piece runs many arcs at once — side quests, character stories, main plot. Where too few arcs keep people curious, they leave for something likelier to reward them. Rewarding for longer takes more skill.

### LC-05#23 | DECISION_RULE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Content Unit (Hook-Retain-Reward) | novelty: canon | ts: 00:27:08
claim: Start with short-form and build up — retain someone for five seconds, then fifteen, and so on.
detail: Comedians start with five-minute bits and over a year or two string one-minute jokes into an hour-long set.

### LC-05#24 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:27:57
claim: Make all content for strangers — avoid inside references and give newcomers a few seconds of context; regulars do not mind and newcomers feel included.
detail: Unexplained references to past content make newcomers feel like the outsider hearing "you had to be there." A quick "if you're new here…" costs regulars nothing and grows the audience faster.
