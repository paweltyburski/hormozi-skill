### POD-0096#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:42
claim: Once better specialists are hired, the entrepreneur's job is to keep the highest standards and break the team's beliefs about what is possible.
detail: Standards he names: speed (how fast something can happen), product excellence, and customer service quality. Growth earlier meant learning a skill and then handing it off; later it becomes pushing limits and making people beside you believe in them.

### POD-0096#02 | CASE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:50
claim: A tech founder merged four databases in 30 days after engineers called a 60-day merge impossible.
detail: Most engineers refused. He fired the two or three loudest naysayers on the spot with two months' severance, then had the rest explain what each part did, step by step. Each "why can't we" produced a small estimate (16 hours, then three days for two people) and the whole job finished in half the time first demanded.
numbers: 60-day target, delivered in 30 days; severance two months.

### POD-0096#03 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:42
claim: When someone says it cannot be done, ask them to break the problem down to physics: what physically prevents it?
detail: Usually the barrier is a limiting belief, not a law. Apply it to goals as well: given a one-year goal, ask why it cannot happen in two months and what would need to be true to do it in eight weeks. Conventional wisdom yields conventional results.
numbers: year goal reframed as 8 weeks.

### POD-0096#04 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:39
claim: Lady Boss quadrupled an eight-figure business in about six weeks and hired 30 support reps in 14 days.
detail: A campaign took off, and the owner (Brandon Poole) held daily group interviews for 14 days rather than the reasonable one or two hires per week. The belief that necessity demanded it let him protect the surge. Standards are often about speed.
numbers: 4x revenue in about 1.5 months; 30 reps in 14 days; reasonable pace would be 1-2 per week.

### POD-0096#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:31
claim: The person with the highest standard in a department should lead it, even if they currently report to someone with lower standards.
detail: Example: a customer service head with standards of 80 out of 100 and an employee at 90 on response time, politeness and knowledge. Look for sticklers who ask how it can be better, because they are the ones who can do the job he can't once the company outgrows his oversight. He needs someone with as high a standard of excellence as his own.
### POD-0096#07 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:04
claim: Run measurable beta tests of a new fulfillment method on struggling customers, and repeat until it delivers the outcome.
detail: Take the best performers' methods, consolidate and modularise them, give them to people not doing well, and measure results using the exact fulfillment you plan to use. If they fail to get the outcome, redo it, as many times as it takes. He sees few in his space doing this for real.
