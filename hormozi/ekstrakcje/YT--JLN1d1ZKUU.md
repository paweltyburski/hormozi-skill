### YT--JLN1d1ZKUU#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Pick the one most compelling point and communicate it in the most compelling way; many messages at once cannot be caught.
detail: Hormozi illustrates by tossing several objects versus one. Quoting Ken Segall (Insanely Simple), a list of points means strategic thinking was not done. The discipline must come from the founder, because simplicity takes more work than complexity, which is the child of lazy prioritising.

### YT--JLN1d1ZKUU#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:55
claim: Ask "what one thing, if true 12 months from now, would change everything for the business?"
detail: Candidate answers: an owned dedicated lead channel, a steady supply of technicians for an HVAC firm, or cash conversion cut from 90 days to 30 days per sale. Once that one thing is achieved, objectives 2 to 5 may have changed anyway because of the new resources. If the CEO cannot name it, the CEO has failed at the job (Frank Slootman).
numbers: Example cash conversion 90 days to 30 days.

### YT--JLN1d1ZKUU#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:47
claim: Let small fires burn and stay with the high-leverage solution rather than trying parallel fixes when it is slow.
detail: A big fix may show nothing after 30 days, but launching a second route drains resources from the one that will work. He admits to doing this himself out of impatience. Prefer sure bets with top leverage to many small bets.

### YT--JLN1d1ZKUU#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Value vs Willingness-to-Pay Matrix | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:32
claim: Build by subtraction, like carving jade, so the product is what remains after removing everything that does not matter.
detail: Contrast with the Western blank canvas to which you keep adding. Ask which distractions you create for the business and the customer. Quote: "perfect every detail and limit the number of details." If you promise help with one thing, help only with that.

### YT--JLN1d1ZKUU#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: verticals | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:07
claim: Raising Cane's runs a menu of about seven items, and one best sandwich would draw lines.
detail: The question "if we did only this one thing, would we hit our goals?" is posed with the chicken chain's tiny menu; the one-sandwich thought experiment lists every detail to perfect: ordering, waiting, sourcing, temperature, order of assembly, price.

### YT--JLN1d1ZKUU#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:52
claim: Outsized returns sit at the end of the curve (90th to 99th percentile), which needs depth that spreading thin prevents.
detail: Being 75th percentile earns little more than average; the jumps in market capture happen between 90th and 95th and between 95th and 99th.

### YT--JLN1d1ZKUU#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:39
claim: Spend spare capacity on one order-of-magnitude bet, not on incremental fixes like close rate or margin.
detail: Incremental changes are routine sausage-making. If staff run at 70-80% utilisation, the remaining 20-30% should fund one real bet that is worth making.
numbers: Utilisation 70-80%, spare 20-30%.

### YT--JLN1d1ZKUU#08 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:10:25
claim: Use inversion: ask how much you can take away and still have a good, probably better, product.
detail: Case from Skool Games: members cancelled mainly from overwhelm. Fixes: cut calls from three a week to one, move the big download from monthly to quarterly, remove native in-app items, and introduce a new step only when needed to get the desired value.
numbers: Calls cut from three to one per week; download monthly to quarterly.

### YT--JLN1d1ZKUU#09 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:46
claim: One priority needs fewer meetings and fewer people, and projects should be time-boxed, such as 12 weeks even in big firms.
detail: A small group of strong people is enough. The easiest way to ruin a project is to give it too much time. Also accelerate with a big public goal, an aggressive timeline, frequent check-ins and removing people who add approval loops.
numbers: Project window 12 weeks.

### YT--JLN1d1ZKUU#10 | EXAMPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: leads-fundamentals, verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:14:47
claim: Customers often want only one result: Gym Launch owners on a feedback call said they needed leads, nothing else.
detail: Agencies keep tacking on services when delivering the one thing that matters would make clients content. Exception: for Fortune 100 clients the one thing may be full integration.
