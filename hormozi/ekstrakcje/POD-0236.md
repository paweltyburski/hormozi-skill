### POD-0236#02 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:42
claim: Read the book while listening to its audio at about 3x speed, with the e-book synced to the audiobook.
detail: Tip from Russell Brunson. The audio sets your pace so reading does not stall, and hearing plus seeing the words stores the content in two memories. Amazon's synced audio and Kindle versions let you switch at the same spot.
numbers: Audio at 3x to 3.5x speed.

### POD-0236#03 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:09
claim: The audio length at your chosen speed gives the book's exact duration, so you can schedule it in set sessions.
detail: Example: a thick book took about 3 hours 15 minutes, which he split into two daily sittings of roughly 1 hour 45 (his figures). Since the event he finished a book and a half.
numbers: About 3h15 total for one thick book at high speed.

### POD-0236#04 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:54
claim: His own rule: if the first section of a book gives him no value, he stops reading it; he also thinks many people should read fewer books, but more thoroughly.
detail: He dropped his old rule of finishing any book started. What counts is how much you retain and apply, not how many books you have read; many people who claim to have read a book cannot say what it contained.
### POD-0236#05 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:40
claim: Reread the few best books repeatedly until you can reproduce their frameworks, maths and scripts on demand and explain them to someone.
detail: The test is that if the book vanished you would still know it; if you can only say "go read it", you do not know it yet. He likens it to rereading scripture, since different lessons surface at different life stages.
numbers: Rereads one edition of Expert Secrets about 8 to 9 times; the first edition of DotCom Secrets 5 times.
