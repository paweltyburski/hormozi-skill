### MZ-kJQtg5Jzr44#01 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: His investment screen favours high cash-flow service businesses because price, product and client experience can be changed without software development.
detail: The model is to buy a minority interest and deploy a proven playbook. He names four targets: niche e-learning businesses; brick-and-mortar chains that already have several successful locations and could go national; tech-enabled services with only a small software component; a fourth he does not recall. He says services reach high margin if the owner knows how to deliver value.
applies_to: Acquisition.com portfolio selection

### MZ-kJQtg5Jzr44#02 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:29
claim: Instead of coaching a photographer who sold a $5,000 hybrid agency and coaching product to about 100 businesses, he funded and built the studios himself.
detail: The founder had one location and roughly 100 B2B customers. Hormozi disliked that model, learned that a studio earns far more than it costs to open, and chose to front the build cost of every new studio. Business is children's storybook photography (customers leave with a book featuring them). Over about a year the chain went from one location to about 13, and he expects it to be worth more than his other businesses combined.
numbers: $5,000 price of the old B2B product; about 100 B2B customers; 1 to about 13 locations in a year; a 20% retained interest (which company unclear in audio).
