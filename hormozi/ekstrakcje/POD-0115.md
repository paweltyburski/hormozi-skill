### POD-0115#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:54
claim: Rate the business on five core functions in flow order: lead generation, lead nurture, sales, fulfillment, retention or ascension.
detail: Lead nurture splits into scheduling, showing, and getting prospects to show up intending to buy (warm-up). HR and finance are two more functions, which most smaller brick-and-mortar businesses need not concern themselves with. Growth speed depends on putting all attention into the single bottleneck, since competitors share the same time, talent pool and information.
numbers: Hormozi cited about a $50M run rate in just under two years.
### POD-0115#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: management-ops | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:10
claim: Every quarter, locate the one bottleneck in the flow and do nothing else.
detail: If the business is not growing, one of the five functions is the bottleneck. Ignore every other guru, manifesto and task until it is fixed. Fixing means maintaining the performance of the repaired step while moving to the next one, so you avoid whack-a-mole.

### POD-0115#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: scaling-constraints | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:43
claim: Before buying more leads, check whether fulfillment or sales is what is actually holding growth.
detail: Poor fulfillment might be why customers do not refer, as they may be half embarrassed to be paying you. If many people walk in but few buy, the problem is sales, not lead volume.
numbers: example: 10 people come in, 2 buy (20% close) means a sales problem.
### POD-0115#04 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:43
claim: Fix a bottleneck with one of three Ps: a person, a process or a project.
detail: A person takes over once you can teach it (you trained a trainer and got your hours back for about $15). A project is the owner acquiring a missing skill, such as training salespeople or automating nurture, then creating a system that works even when absent. Reframe problems as the skill deficit you must close, and watch for your lack of a consistent sales environment rather than blaming the rep.

### POD-0115#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: unit-economics | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:07:37
claim: Do not optimise a stage that is not the bottleneck; with 3% monthly churn and few leads, work on leads.
detail: Cutting churn from 3% to 1.5% is the wrong use of effort when inquiries are the constraint. At two new customers a month you can personally pamper everyone; at 30 new recurring clients a month, attrition spikes and becomes the next bottleneck.
numbers: churn 3% per month (97% kept); 2 versus 30 new clients per month.
### POD-0115#06 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: Ascension Path | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:52
claim: Run the "abundant exchange" exercise: list what you would deliver at five times the price, then do everything without a hard cost.
detail: Ask coaches what they would provide if members paid $1,000 a month instead of $200, list it all, cross off only items with hard costs, and do the rest consistently. Customers then feel they receive far more than they paid, which triggers referrals and retention.
numbers: $200 paid versus $1,000-level service.

### POD-0115#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:54
claim: As the business grows, the owner's work narrows to strategic allocation, and sales and traffic are bought back.
detail: Hormozi no longer does sales or traffic; his skill is looking across the market and deciding where all resources go for the next nine months. This takes little time but is highly valuable, and he funds the rest of his time with money he would otherwise earn on calls.
numbers: 9-month resource allocation horizon.
