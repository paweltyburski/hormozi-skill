### YT-8C_6qojTA78#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: finance-exit | fw: Track Lose Print | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:31
claim: Treat profit you don't need to live on as an experimentation budget; expect to burn some to find scalable keywords.
detail: Hormozi would plan to waste about $200,000 on bad keywords to find roughly six that scale to $100,000 a month, which would 5x the business. ### YT-8C_6qojTA78#03 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:30
claim: To scale search ads to colder markets, use broader keywords and a bridge (advertorial) page, matched to levels of awareness.
detail: Levels: unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware. Existing keywords target the most aware; each step up has about ten times more clicks but needs a different hook. For product-aware, send traffic to an advertorial type page, then the sales page; clicks may cost a tenth. Problem-aware searches ("is your house boring? extra room?") start where the person is. A good media buyer would know this.
numbers: Colder keywords: ~10x the volume, clicks possibly 1/10 the price.

### YT-8C_6qojTA78#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Virtuous Cycle of Price | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:18
claim: Reinvest spare cash where the business's growth comes from: capex for physical models, talent and brand for service models.
detail: Brick and mortar opens another location; manufacturers buy another machine; a service firm reinvests in better talent (its price should keep rising with quality) and in brand moments. Brand stunts cost more than direct response but build memory (the Red Bull space jump). If you cannot charge more than competitors, you aren't better yet.

### YT-8C_6qojTA78#06 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:20

### YT-8C_6qojTA78#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:43
claim: A media-arbitrage e-commerce model tends to cap around $10-12M, with margin and cash flow shrinking as CAC rises.
detail: Reasons: CAC climbs and gross margin falls, supply chain issues grow, and look-alike copies list on Amazon and undercut price. Hormozi hears weekly from owners who want a portfolio of products to sell; he has seen almost none manage it. Optimising might triple the business but you then hit the cap.
numbers: Cap about $10-12M; $12M revenue example ended at thin margins (figure unclear in audio).

### YT-8C_6qojTA78#09 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:28

### YT-8C_6qojTA78#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:45
claim: Without a defensible product, brand is the only differentiator, and buyers purchase brands, not products.
detail: A winning product draws copies that undercut you, return on spend falls and you leak profit to competitors. Private equity buys brands. Either have a patent plus a legal team that can enforce it, or build the brand. Lead with brand: legal protection is easier once big, and small companies get drowned in fees.
numbers: Brand route could turn a $50M business into a $500M one (his illustration).
