### MZ-H4hvAxNVyyc#01 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A solo gas-station broker earning $2M in commission wants $8M but fears hiring after a failed attempt cost him client time.
detail: He now does portfolio deals (eight- and nine-figure). Hormozi diagnoses supply constraint. Two paths: keep solo, where rising ticket size and network bring more income over about ten years, or build a team and accept a 20-30% dip in selling time while learning to recruit, hire and train.
numbers: $2M revenue now; solo path about $10-15M in roughly ten years.

### MZ-H4hvAxNVyyc#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:32
claim: A friend of Hormozi's, an investment banker with a four-person team, does about $10M a year and has grown 20-30% a year just via rising average ticket size, with about the same number of deals.
numbers: $10M per year; 4-person team; 20-30% annual growth.
detail: Hormozi offered this as one path for the caller, saying it probably takes realistically 10 years and that it's a lot of money and all take home.
### MZ-H4hvAxNVyyc#03 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:36
claim: Recruit brokers by offering the deals too small for you on a 50-50 split.
detail: This gives inbound interest and early earnings without pulling them off their current pipeline, and the spiffs offset some lost volume.

### MZ-H4hvAxNVyyc#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:55

detail: Faster growth requires giving up some profit now.
