### LC-09#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Efficiency matters more than creativity: all advertising works, so the only real question is how well.
detail: If nobody knows about your stuff and you start advertising, more people will, and the odds of making money rise. Ads and methods differ only in efficiency — what comes back for what goes in.

### LC-09#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:46
claim: Paid ads show return ad by ad, so the goal is not the best ad ever but ads good enough to scale — then scale them hard.
detail: With content or outreach it's hard to see which piece or message drove the return; with ads you see X in and Y out per ad. What matters most: make more than it costs.

### LC-09#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: paid-ads | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:46
claim: Earn at least 3x in lifetime gross profit what a customer costs to acquire — and the faster you recover CAC, the faster you scale.
detail: $300 lifetime profit → acquire for under $100. Breaking even in one or two days lets you recycle ad money on a one-day cash cycle instead of 30 days or six months.
numbers: LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1; ideal payback 1–2 days.

### LC-09#04 | FRAMEWORK | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: Track Lose Print | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:45
claim: Ad spending moves through three phases: track money, lose money, print money.
detail: Track: tracking in place before spending. Lose: you must spend before money returns and most ads lose. Print: once an ad reliably returns more than it costs, spend as much as you can. Also the "how much" answer in the ads checklist: a learning budget moving through these phases.

### LC-09#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: - | fw: Track Lose Print | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:45
claim: You lose far more often than you win in paid ads, but the wins pay for all the losses.
detail: Of 300 ads perhaps 295 fail, 5 are profitable and 1 is a home run; you push the home run until it repays every failed test. Controlling test budgets tilts the overall return your way.
numbers: ~295 losers, ~5 winners, ~1 home run per 300 ads.

### LC-09#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: management-ops | fw: Track Lose Print | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:57
claim: Running ads without tracking is betting in a casino without knowing the odds.
detail: Even multi-million-dollar owners often know only total spend and return, not the source. Being able to point at the ad that works and make more like it unlocks big scale.

### LC-09#07 | EXAMPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: - | fw: Track Lose Print | novelty: canon | ts: 00:03:57
claim: $100 on each of 10 ads, where nine return nothing and one returns $500, looks like a $500 loss — it is exactly the moment to double down.
detail: Really it is 5:1 on one ad and 0:1 on nine. Most people quit here. Instead put $10K on the winner, get $50K back, and the $500 loss becomes irrelevant — then you are in the print phase.
numbers: $1,000 test → −$500; then $10K → $50K on the winner.

### LC-09#08 | DECISION_RULE | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: Track Lose Print | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:12
claim: If an ad returns more than it costs, spend as much as you possibly can; a fixed "marketing budget" (e.g. $10,000 a month) tells him the person doesn't understand paid ads, or doesn't work them as well as they could.
detail: If a machine paid $10 per $1 inserted, your budget would be every dollar that fits. Find the winner via tracking, then scale it; ROAS targets and budgets stop mattering.

### LC-09#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: paid-ads, unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:58
claim: Done right, each customer funds the next — one, two, four — until acquisition stops being the constraint and the bottleneck moves to sales or delivery.
detail: In every Hormozi business, constraints were post-acquisition (salespeople, delivery staff, touch-up artists). If you're not making money at that point the model is wrong; if you are, you have cash to fix the rest. Paid ads are more advanced because they carry higher risk and you compete with big players, but nothing scales faster.

### LC-09#10 | DEFINITION | topic: unit-economics | also: - | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:44
claim: Lifetime gross profit (LTGP) = gross profit per purchase × purchases over the customer's life.
detail: Gross profit = price − delivery cost. Hormozi prefers LTGP to LTV/CLV because revenue-based terms mislead; compare lifetime margin, not revenue, with CAC.
numbers: $15 price − $5 cost = $10 × 10 purchases = $100 LTGP.

### LC-09#11 | CASE | topic: unit-economics | also: paid-ads | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:45
claim: A meal-delivery company's marketers celebrated $100 CAC for $500 customers — but at 10% gross margin each yielded $50 gross profit, a loss on every sale.
detail: Business of a friend (name as heard: Ready Prep Meals). Revenue ignores the costs behind it. If you don't know your gross margins, work them out before running paid ads.
numbers: CAC $100; revenue $500; 10% margin → $50 GP → −$50/customer.

### LC-09#12 | FRAMEWORK | topic: unit-economics | also: paid-ads | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: canon | ts: 00:10:19
claim: LTGP above CAC makes money, below loses, and above 3x is a home run to scale; improve the ratio by lowering CAC or raising LTGP.
detail: Lower CAC through efficiency: better ads and targeting, fewer funnel steps, better sales, landing-page tests, cheaper platforms, more widely distributed media. Raise LTGP through a better business model.
numbers: Ratio >1 profitable; >3 home run.

### LC-09#13 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: paid-ads, money-models | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: canon | ts: 00:11:04
claim: A common mistake is concluding advertising doesn't work when the business model is what's broken; ads reveal how you measure up to competitors.
detail: If others profitably acquire on the same platform and you can't, the gap is usually money per customer, not the ads.

### LC-09#14 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: paid-ads | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:11
claim: ALAN's data showed CAC within one industry almost always within 1–3x across competitors, while winners earned about 10x more per customer.
detail: ALAN was software for agencies acquiring small-business clients. Without a brand (big asterisk), marketing skill is roughly a 3x lever on CAC; the real separator was revenue per customer.

### LC-09#15 | CASE | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models, product-delivery | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: canon | ts: 00:13:18
claim: A competitor selling to personal trainers matched Gym Launch's monthly sales, yet Gym Launch made 56x more profit because it earned about 10x more per customer.
detail: Similar sales velocity and acquisition cost. He thought he had a sales/marketing problem; really he couldn't deliver enough value to charge enough. Lesson: be good enough at ads to get people in and let the business model do the rest.
numbers: Same sales/month; ~10x revenue per customer; ~56x profit.

### LC-09#16 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models, paid-ads | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: canon | ts: 00:13:32
claim: Work both levers, but the big gains come from the business model: CAC can only fall so far, while LTGP can keep rising — unless a massive brand drives CAC toward zero.
detail: If a competitor can afford $5,000 per customer and you can afford $50,000, you win everywhere.

### LC-09#17 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: strategy-positioning, unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:14:41
claim: With higher LTGP you can outbid everyone in ad auctions, deliberately pushing CPMs and your own CAC beyond what competitors can afford — an "ethical monopoly".
detail: Platforms auction attention to the highest bidder. Bid above everyone, raise CPMs, and price competitors out. Gym Launch did this for years.

### LC-09#18 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: paid-ads | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: canon | ts: 00:14:41
claim: 3:1 is the floor; Hormozi's lifetime return exceeds 36:1, acquisition.com targets at least 10:1 for portfolio companies, and the best run 20:1+.
detail: 3:1 is a minimum for scaling, not the goal.
numbers: Floor 3:1; Hormozi >36:1; portfolio target ≥10:1; best 20+:1.

### LC-09#19 | DEFINITION | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics, paid-ads | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition | novelty: canon | ts: 00:15:46
claim: Client-financed acquisition: a customer pays more in the first 30 days than it cost to acquire and serve them, so customers fund acquiring the next ones.
detail: The usual problem: LTGP beats CAC over the lifetime but not after the first purchase, so cash arrives months later — a cash-flow drag that cripples scaling. Clearing CAC within 30 days funds growth indefinitely without outside capital.

### LC-09#20 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition | novelty: canon | ts: 00:16:35
claim: A $15/month membership ($10 GP, 10-month stay, $30 CAC) is a healthy 3.3:1 but takes three months to break even, so growth needs debt, investors or savings.
detail: Cash position: month 1 −$20, month 2 −$10, month 3 $0, month 4 +$10. Spend is capped by the cash you can front and wait to recoup. Hormozi scaled without debt or outside capital by fixing this.
numbers: LTGP $100; CAC $30; 3.3:1; break-even month 3.

### LC-09#21 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: unit-economics | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition | novelty: canon | ts: 00:17:44
claim: A $100 upsell taken by 20% of customers adds $20 up front; with the first $10 month it covers the $30 CAC, making acquisition self-funding from day one.
detail: Put ad spend on a credit card, pay it off from first transactions, repeat — compounding without outside money; the later monthly $10 is pure profit. To find upsells ask what else these customers need: as the lead magnet leads to the core offer, the core offer should lead to an upsell, endlessly.
numbers: $100 × 20% = $20; $10 + $20 = $30 = CAC.

### LC-09#22 | FRAMEWORK | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models, scaling-constraints | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition | novelty: canon | ts: 00:19:16
claim: Two tiers of advertising mastery: tier one is LTGP:CAC above 3:1; tier two is breaking even or better within 30 days, which lets growth compound monthly.
detail: 2:1 within 30 days, fully reinvested, compounds 2x, 4x, 8x, 16x, 32x by month five and ~4,000x in a year. Another constraint will appear first, but leads stop being the bottleneck — the purpose of the course. More in the money models training.
numbers: 2:1 in 30 days → ~4,000x customers in 12 months if reinvested.

### LC-09#23 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: offers | fw: More Better New | novelty: canon | ts: 00:20:15
claim: Upsells come easiest in three forms: a larger quantity of what was purchased, a superior version, or a new add-on that goes with it; failing that, just ask the customer what else would help.
detail: More: a bigger fries or drink. Better: a premium or organic version. New: a complement, like fries alongside a burger. When out of ideas, ask what else you could do to make life easier for them, then sell whatever they name.

### LC-09#24 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: paid-ads | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:21:36
claim: Don't confuse a sales problem with an advertising problem: if the right people show up on calls and don't buy, the ads work and closing is the issue.
detail: A company fixed issues in sequence — no clicks, wrong people, no-shows — until the right avatar was on calls but not closing. The founder kept blaming ads. Solve the actual bottleneck stage by stage.

### LC-09#29 | FRAMEWORK | topic: paid-ads | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Core Four | novelty: canon | ts: 00:24:46
claim: Paid-ads checklist: you advertise your offer or lead magnet on any platform where you can buy your audience's attention, to targeted or lookalike audiences, daily, on a learning budget, with 300 variations, for as long as it takes.
detail: How: call-out, value (what/who/when), CTA. Why: engaged leads to sell. How much: learning budget, track → lose → print. How many: 30 hooks × 10 value pieces, each tested on tiny budgets, winners scaled (needs tracking). How long: until it works — if someone else in your industry can run paid ads, so can you; it's a skill learned by trial and error.

### LC-09#30 | TACTIC | topic: paid-ads | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: canon | ts: 00:25:33
claim: When capacity is the constraint, set ad budget as customers you can handle × CAC, then fix the constraint and raise spend.
detail: The budget limit comes from delivery, not advertising; focus on removing the delivery constraint.
numbers: 20 customers/month × $100 CAC = $2,000/month ≈ $70/day.

### LC-09#32 | DECISION_RULE | topic: paid-ads | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Core Four | novelty: canon | ts: 00:27:25
claim: Do paid ads last among the Core Four, funded by a deliberate learning budget from the cash and avatar knowledge the other methods produce.
detail: Earlier methods give spare cash flow and knowledge of how customers talk and self-identify, raising early odds. Set the budget in reverse, e.g., "I expect to lose $1,000 a month until it clicks" — the usual pattern is lose, lose, lose, then it skyrockets.
