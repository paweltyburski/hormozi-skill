### YT-Cr0KdqZ954c#01 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: verticals | fw: Gross Profit | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:02
claim: Meal delivery with a 10% gross margin makes only $70 of lifetime gross profit on $700 of revenue per customer, so a $200 CAC loses money.
detail: Food costing $9 sold at $10 leaves a 10% margin. A customer ordering $700 of food looks valuable on revenue but yields $70 of gross profit, so spending $200 to acquire that customer is a loss.
numbers: $9 cost, $10 price, 10% gross margin; $700 revenue = $70 gross profit; $200 CAC.

### YT-Cr0KdqZ954c#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: pricing | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:24
claim: Worked cap on CAC: with an 80% margin and $1,000 lifetime revenue, gross profit is $800, so CAC must stay under about $266 to keep 3:1.
detail: The target is lifetime gross profit above 3x acquisition cost; derive the maximum CAC by dividing lifetime gross profit by three.
numbers: 80% margin, $1,000 lifetime revenue = $800 gross profit; 3:1 floor means CAC below ~$266.

### YT-Cr0KdqZ954c#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: finance-exit | fw: Payback Period | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:44
claim: He prefers "30-day cash" over payback period because most credit cards and credit lines are interest-free for the first 30 days.
detail: If 30-day cash at least equals CAC, other people's money funds acquisition and repays within the interest-free window, leaving the customer and later upsells and continuity as profit.

### YT-Cr0KdqZ954c#04 | TACTIC | topic: unit-economics | also: money-models | fw: Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:26
claim: To lift weak 30-day cash, add an upsell, a liquidation offer, a special offer in week one or two, ancillary products, an affiliate relationship or paid referrals out.
detail: The larger 30-day cash is, the more you can spend per customer and the wider you can open the acquisition net, and it still counts in lifetime value.
