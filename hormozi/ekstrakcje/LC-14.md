### LC-14#01 | CASE | topic: lead-getters | also: money-models, verticals | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Prestige Labs reached $380–450K in weekly sales without acquiring a single supplement customer directly — gym owners sold the supplements to their own members.
detail: In his own gyms, supplement upsells (protein, pre-workout, fat burner, vitamins) to every new member covered all marketing costs, giving him an effectively unlimited ad budget. After three to four years of Gym Launch he spent about 18 months developing formulas with a biochemist and a free "supplement selling system" for brick-and-mortar gyms, launched via a webinar to his gym owners. He spent $3M on inventory and $1M on a custom point of sale. Weeks one to three: almost no sales — break-even on inventory looked about 5.5 years away. Week four: $100K Monday, $110K Tuesday, and similar after. The business later sold for many millions. (His pre-launch fears are on The Game podcast, episode 98.)
numbers: $3M inventory + $1M POS; first five strong weeks: $429K, $383K, $411K, $404K, $452K weekly sales.

### LC-14#02 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: product-delivery | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:18
claim: Design the affiliate system around the problems you had when you were in the affiliate's shoes — build the business you wished had supported you.
detail: As a gym owner he wanted high-ticket bundles (inventory sold out too fast), supplement subscriptions so each member pays two memberships ("two paychecks a month"), a checklist-style point of sale he could turn toward a customer on an iPad as he had on paper, and drop-shipping so gyms never carry stock. Pre-buying $6–10K of supplements before each promotion was a cash drag and a risk; the new system let gyms sell and collect cash with no inventory risk.
numbers: $6,000–10,000 per promotion previously tied up in gym inventory.

### LC-14#03 | DEFINITION | topic: lead-getters | also: - | fw: Lead Getters | novelty: canon | ts: 00:05:54

detail: Affiliates look like referrals from outside but differ under the hood: the relationship is a negotiated partnership, not a happy customer passing on a name. Hormozi used affiliates at Prestige Labs, ALAN (software) and other businesses.
numbers: $50–75M lifetime sales generated through affiliates across his businesses (he had not checked exact figures; probably ~$75M).

### LC-14#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: - | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: canon | ts: 00:06:39
claim: The Prestige Labs deal was all upside for gyms: he took the costs and risks, and they got money, simplicity and a marketing advantage.
detail: He fronted the capital, ran the warehouse, let gyms sell products they otherwise could not with no cash risk, paid commissions more generous than they could earn alone, simplified the point of sale, and trained them to sell. ### LC-14#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: unit-economics, scaling-constraints | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: canon | ts: 00:07:24
claim: Acquiring affiliates instead of customers removes the plateau, because each affiliate is a node that keeps producing customers every month.
detail: Selling a fixed number of customers per month eventually plateaus where new sales equal losses. Selling the same number of affiliates stacks: each month's cohort keeps sending customers, so revenue climbs without more outreach or ad spend, until you have an army pushing traffic your way through the incentive structure.
numbers: Illustrative hypothetical ("let's just say"): 10 affiliates/month at $10K lifetime value each adds $100K/month every month ($100K, $200K, $300K, $400K, $500K…) vs plateauing at $100K/month selling 10 customers worth $10K.

### LC-14#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Core Four | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:35
claim: Affiliates run the Core Four on behalf of both businesses ("us and them"), which gives leverage: the team needed to sell 1,000 affiliates is the same size as one selling 1,000 customers, with far more leverage.
detail: He never marketed to supplement buyers; gyms got gym members and sold his supplements to them. A team that can sell 1,000 weight-loss customers can sell 1,000 gym owners — same team size, far more leverage. The Core Four is also how you recruit lead getters: the relationship runs both ways.

### LC-14#07 | FRAMEWORK | topic: lead-getters | also: - | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: canon | ts: 00:08:35
claim: Building an affiliate army has six steps: find ideal affiliates, make them an offer, qualify them through terms, decide how to pay them, launch, and keep them advertising.
detail: (1) Find the ideal affiliate — a business with a warm audience like your customers. (2) Make an affiliate offer and advertise it like any offer (callout, value, call to action). (3) Qualify them: they become affiliates when they understand and accept terms designed to force a fast first win. (4) Pay them: decide what you pay for and how much, using three tiers and product. (5) Launch with Whisper-Tease-Shout for a big early win. (6) Keep them advertising through ongoing integration: they give away your lead magnet, sell your lead magnet, or sell your core offer.

### LC-14#13 | SCRIPT | topic: lead-getters | also: offers | fw: Value Equation | novelty: canon | ts: 00:12:34

detail: Dream outcome: make more money from current customers and get more leads for less. Perceived likelihood: high, because their customers already want the product. ### LC-14#14 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: retention-ltv | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: canon | ts: 00:13:40
claim: Qualify affiliates with terms that force a fast first win: make them a customer, then make them an expert.
detail: Customer: they must buy and try the product before selling it — ethical (they should believe in it) and it creates skin in the game. This first purchase is an activation threshold, not part of their lifetime value, so do not price it high. Expert: require a short certification before they sell, so they do not dilute the brand or promise what you cannot deliver. It can take 10 minutes or a week depending on product complexity.

### LC-14#15 | PRINCIPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: unit-economics | fw: Maximum Allowable CAC | novelty: canon | ts: 00:14:38
claim: Pay affiliates for the exact behavior you want, and cap total payout at your maximum allowable CAC — one third of gross profit if you target 3:1 LTGP:CAC.
detail: Getting affiliates bought in is the first problem; keeping them bought in is the second. Pay in money and free product — affiliates who consume the product enjoy the goodies on top of cash. First define what they are paid for, then how much, derived from the CAC your economics can afford.

### LC-14#16 | FRAMEWORK | topic: lead-getters | also: unit-economics, sales-team | fw: Three-Tier Affiliate Commissions | novelty: canon | ts: 00:15:14
claim: An affiliate consultant advised exactly three commission tiers (not five, not two): signup, activation, sustained performance; in Hormozi's worked example they pay 25%, 50% and 100% of maximum allowable CAC.
detail: Learned from an expensive affiliate consultant hired by the private equity firm that bought Gym Launch: not five tiers, not two — everyone understands three. Tier 1: everyone who accepts the terms (signs up, buys product or a certification); small. Tier 2: after the key activation point, reverse-engineered from what your best affiliates did (e.g. buy, finish certification, use product a month, make a set number of posts or a launch) — activity-based, so fully in their control, and doubles pay forever. Tier 3: after sustaining a material sales level (yours may be $500 or $50,000/month) for one to three consecutive months; they must keep it there.
numbers: Example: max allowable CAC $40 on a $100 sale → Tier 1 $10, Tier 2 $20, Tier 3 $40 per sale.

### LC-14#17 | TACTIC | topic: lead-getters | also: pricing | fw: Three-Tier Affiliate Commissions | novelty: canon | ts: 00:17:15
claim: Pay affiliates in product where possible: they value it at retail, you pay only your cost.
detail: This arbitrage of value is a cheap, effective way to keep them winning. Lower tiers can be paid entirely in free product or service. As affiliates send more customers they will usually prefer cash — cashing 100 massages a month becomes unrealistic.
numbers: $200 of product valued at $200 may cost you $40–60; a massage with ~$30 hard cost is perceived at $70–100, worth more to a low-volume affiliate than a $30 check.

### LC-14#19 | FRAMEWORK | topic: promotions-launches | also: lead-getters | fw: Whisper Tease Shout | novelty: canon | ts: 00:20:13
claim: Whisper-Tease-Shout launches an affiliate (or any product) in three escalating phases: curiosity, value, call to action.
detail: Whisper (curiosity): keep the product a mystery and hint at how big it is; short, infrequent messages — like a trailer showing King Kong's foot and "Summer 2025", or Hormozi's posts about version 6 (of 19) of his unnamed second book. Tease (value): reveal the product, publish the launch date, show the elements of value using the what-who-when framework. Shout (call to action): give specific actions to take at launch; pound the audience with bonuses, scarcity, urgency and first-buyer guarantees; frequency rises to three days, two days, one day, hours out. For affiliates, the launch creates a big early win before moving them to a fully integrated model.

### LC-14#20 | FRAMEWORK | topic: lead-getters | also: money-models | fw: Affiliate Army | novelty: canon | ts: 00:22:32
claim: After the launch, keep affiliates advertising through one of three integration models.
detail: (1) The affiliate gives your lead magnet away free with their own offer; you upsell recipients into your core offer. (2) The affiliate sells your lead magnet (book, event, service, software, sample) as an upsell to their offer; you upsell from there. (3) The affiliate sells your core offer directly, alone or bundled with theirs at a higher price — the simplest model, not necessarily the most effective; some businesses and influencers run entirely on this. In (1) and (2) your lead magnet (sample, trial, one step of a multi-step process) makes the affiliate's offer more valuable, so they can charge more and get more leads. All three have been scaled massively by different businesses.

### LC-14#27 | BENCHMARK | topic: unit-economics | also: lead-getters | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: canon | ts: 00:30:31
claim: Affiliate returns are calculated by comparing the cost to acquire an affiliate with the gross profit of all customers they send over their affiliate lifetime, after their payout.
detail: Widget example: affiliate CAC $4,000 (expensive). Average affiliate sells $10K/month for 12 months = $120K. At 75% gross margin, COGS is $30K, leaving $90K gross profit. Paying affiliates 40% of gross profit ($36K) leaves $54K. $54K ÷ $4K gives the LTGP:CAC ratio — he states 12.5:1, though the arithmetic gives 13.5:1. The model scales down: move the decimal (e.g. $40 CAC, $100/month) and it still works.
numbers: CAC $4K; $120K lifetime sales; $90K GP; $36K payout; $54K net GP; ≈12.5–13.5:1.

### LC-14#28 | PRINCIPLE | topic: lead-getters | also: outreach, unit-economics | fw: Core Four | novelty: canon | ts: 00:32:03
claim: When a product is too cheap to justify direct outreach, sell it in bulk through affiliates instead.
detail: Cold outreach to sell a single $70 lotion does not pay. Cold outreach to recruit businesses that resell the lotion does, because the affiliate's lifetime gross profit covers the acquisition cost. There is always a way to use the Core Four; you may have to reimagine what you sell and to whom.

### LC-14#29 | DECISION_RULE | topic: unit-economics | also: lead-getters | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: canon | ts: 00:33:04
claim: If affiliate LTGP:CAC is below 3:1, lower CAC, get more affiliates to activate, or raise LTGP through better integration.
detail: (1) Lower CAC by improving affiliate ads, offers and sales process. (2) Raise activation — this improves both sides at once (higher LTGP, lower effective CAC), so in affiliate businesses it is usually where he focuses a lot of attention; tools are a launch or activation process and tiers that reward activating. (3) Raise LTGP alone by improving the long-term integration, i.e. picking and refining one of the three integration models.
numbers: Target ≥3:1 LTGP:CAC.
