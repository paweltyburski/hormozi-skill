### POD-0227#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: BANT | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:01:41
claim: A deal needs seven things: four from the buyer (budget, authority, need, timing) and three trust scores from the seller (the "three tens": product, salesman, company, each 10 out of 10).
detail: BANT comes from software sales, where a lead passes to the account executive only if all four are met. The seller-side trust items he first heard from Jordan Belfort and Grant Cardone. Requirements scale with deal size (a free trial versus a $50,000 or million-dollar software year) but the seven stay the same.
numbers: 4 buyer requirements + 3 seller trust items.

### POD-0227#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: sales-process | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: BANT | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:48
claim: A business-operations owner found 95% of his sales came from applicants who wanted to start immediately or the next day.
detail: He reviewed six months of sales applications; only 5% of sales came from people who wanted a later start, so he stopped calling those.
numbers: 95% of sales from start-now or next-day; 5% from later; 6 months of data.

### POD-0227#03 | CASE | topic: sales-process | also: paid-ads | fw: BANT | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:04
claim: Adding a four-question BANT qualification to the application raised cost per booked call 2.5x yet gross sales went up.
detail: Questions: need, authority to decide alone, budget, and start date (immediately or later). Reps then begin with a clean slate and only the three seller components left. The metric that rose was cost per booked call, not acquisition cost.
numbers: cost per booked call up 2.5x; gross sales rose.
