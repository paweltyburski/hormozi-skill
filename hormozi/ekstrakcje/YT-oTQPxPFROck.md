### YT-oTQPxPFROck#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: offers | also: strategy-positioning, verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Four pillars make an education business legitimate: selective admission, data instead of income promises, free content with paid implementation, and graduates who can fail.
detail: He contrasts a get-rich-quick course that needs only a credit card with Harvard, which turns people away and sells brand endorsement.
applies_to: Courses, coaching, education

### YT-oTQPxPFROck#02 | TACTIC | topic: sales-process | also: objections-closing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Define a qualified lead as someone with the highest likelihood of success and screen out the rest, because unqualified buyers become angry reviewers.
detail: Screening metrics on who is a poor fit cut bad reviews and scam accusations.

### YT-oTQPxPFROck#03 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: content-brand | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:32
claim: Track the share of customers who succeed at 30, 60, 90 days and 12 months, and publish the top 20%, average and median rather than selected income stories.
detail: Data lets you avoid sensational claims and match expectations to elapsed time. Harvard publishes what the top 20%, average and median graduates earn and where they go. Harder to sell, but it builds a brand.
numbers: Measurement points: 30, 60, 90 days, 12 months.

### YT-oTQPxPFROck#04 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: Lead Magnet Steps | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:13
claim: Give the course content away free everywhere and sell implementation, which keeps demand above supply and prices high.
detail: A sales school would publish all lessons and sell review of the student's calls, real-time feedback and performance monitoring. Better free content than rivals' paid content makes people assume your paid offer is better.

### YT-oTQPxPFROck#05 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:04:24
claim: Rank-order graduates and endorse only the best, so the endorsement protects the brand.
detail: To get strong graduates: pick people with aptitude, cut the rest (he would reject about 90% of sales-school applicants), and base expectations on data.
numbers: Reject about 90% of applicants (illustrative).

### YT-oTQPxPFROck#06 | EXAMPLE | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:41
claim: A selective programme with only about 25 strong graduates can justify a high price because outcomes are far above average.
detail: Illustration: graduates earning about $300,000 versus about $60,000 for an average salesperson. Harvard charges about $250,000 for four years.
numbers: $300,000 vs $60,000; 25 graduates.
