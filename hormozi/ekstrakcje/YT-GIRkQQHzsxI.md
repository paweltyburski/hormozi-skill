### YT-GIRkQQHzsxI#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:48
claim: There are two time-investment styles: manager schedules (many small slots) and maker schedules (a few long blocks).
detail: Based on Paul Graham's essay. Managers often split days into 20+ chunks, from 5 or 15 minutes up to 90 minutes; an empty slot is a missed return, and the day ends at the last meeting. Makers (builders, writers, editors, coders, entrepreneurs doing big work) need only two or three 4-6 hour blocks a day, usually a morning and post-lunch one, and work open to goal. Switch hats by day or week.
numbers: managers often 20+ slots/day; makers 2-3 blocks/day, 4-6 hours each.
### YT-GIRkQQHzsxI#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:49
claim: One meeting in a maker's morning costs far more than it appears: it breaks the block and drains focus before and after.
detail: A block cannot resume after an interruption. A pending meeting prevents flow, since one tracks the clock and prepares (a Zeigarnik effect, open loops). Taking one morning meeting uses one of about 10 weekly blocks, or 10% of output, while for a manager it is one of 100+ slots. Managers' asks cost makers roughly ten times more.
numbers: about 10 maker blocks a week; 1 meeting = 10% of weekly maker output.

### YT-GIRkQQHzsxI#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:00
claim: Managers often judge makers by their own calendar standard and read an empty calendar as slack, which creates a vicious cycle.
detail: When a maker falls behind, the manager checks in more, causing further delay. The maker must either refuse and offend, or accept and lose half a day, often for a meeting without an agenda. Networking introductions work the same way.

### YT-GIRkQQHzsxI#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:56
claim: His highest predictor of success was ruthlessness with his time, trusting that relationships survive if he keeps achieving.
detail: At month's end, 100% of what moved the business forward came from maker time. Early on, he lacked accomplishments to justify it. Roles such as CEO or COO have more manager time legitimately, but maker time still gives leverage on it.

### YT-GIRkQQHzsxI#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:53
claim: Inverted schedules: his wife (the operator) has one maker day and four manager days, while he has four maker days and one manager day.
detail: Each keeps the minority type because it gives leverage on the other days. When writing his book he did nothing but writing for days.
numbers: 1 maker + 4 manager days vs 4 maker + 1 manager day.

### YT-GIRkQQHzsxI#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:15:30
claim: Managers should do three things: weigh the double cost of a meeting, treat a maker's "no" as commitment not offence, and ask the team their ideal week.
detail: Double cost: coordinating disturbs once, the meeting again. Ask whether an impromptu meeting can wait for a set cadence. If a manager truly needs a maker, say so and give prep time. His media team asked editors for their ideal week: meetings bunched together, other days empty, coordination at fixed times.
### YT-GIRkQQHzsxI#07 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:28
claim: When a maker's block is breached, fill the whole block with meetings like a manager, and offer standard meeting hours elsewhere.
detail: Slice the ruined block into chunks and clear all ad hoc meetings in it. Set fixed meeting windows (e.g. Monday and Thursday afternoons), push non-urgent requests there and leave some gaps. Tell people when you will be slow to reply, because unmet expectations anger people. Then actually work in the blocks, so empty calendars do not confirm suspicions.

### YT-GIRkQQHzsxI#08 | EVOLUTION | topic: management-ops | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:21:02
claim: His own schedule moved through three versions as a founder: nights, weekends and mornings first, then a 50/50 split, then whole maker days.
detail: V1 (gym owner): maker work early morning before the gym opened at 9, plus weekend blocks, about nine blocks a week, building training, nutrition and advertising systems, i.e. double work. V2: mornings until noon or 1 pm for maker work, afternoons for meetings, planned back to front so the day's end absorbs meetings and no 45-minute gaps appear. V3: two to four maker days and one stacked manager day.
numbers: V1 about 9 maker blocks/week (5 mornings + 4 weekend); V3 2-4 maker days.

### YT-GIRkQQHzsxI#09 | TACTIC | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:26:34
claim: Organisations can mandate a quiet day for makers with no meetings or messages; theirs is Wednesday.

For remote teams, extend trust with project deadlines, measure output and avoid Slack checks that interrupt. Signal the hat you wear ("maker hat today").

### YT-GIRkQQHzsxI#11 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:33:06
claim: After every recurring meeting ask: do I need to do this again, and could a person or system replace it?
detail: His wife deletes meetings first each week and reviews all recurring meetings quarterly, combining them and removing attendees. Founders trade control for freedom by extending trust with guardrails and measured output. Let attendees leave when they add no value.
