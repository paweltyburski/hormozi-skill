claim: Rich people spend money to buy time, ambitious people spend time to buy skills, and lazy people buy distractions.
detail: He pairs the groups: money buys time, time buys skills. Skills are what carry across generations, while assets passed to someone without skills lose their meaning. Lazy people avoid the discomfort of admitting inadequacy by consuming flattering content, and feeling entitled to things means never being grateful for them.

### YT-oDK4g5na4Jw#05 | TACTIC | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:44
claim: The "12 by 30" exercise: work 12 hours a day for 30 days straight with no weekends to recalibrate how much you can output.
detail: By the end you learn you are not fragile, how much more you could work, how fast goals can move, that you do not need to keep it up forever but have the gear for a season, and that others work more than you. ### YT-oDK4g5na4Jw#06 | DEFINITION | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:08:24
claim: Work equals volume times leverage; early on, increase volume, which builds skill, which then raises the output per unit of effort.
detail: Beginners must do far more repetitions. The repetitions create skill, and skill raises the return on each repetition. He adds that you also get better at working itself with practice, so the answer to "I am not productive when I work" is to work more until the skill of working improves.

### YT-oDK4g5na4Jw#23 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:57:47
claim: Four levels of facing a trade-off: "should" (expect it free), "too much" (offer less than the price and complain), "bidding" (raise until you meet the price), and "source" (you set the standard).
detail: Uses the shoe price analogy: say shoes cost 300, a person offering 30 complains; the bidder goes 30, 50, 100 until 300. At the source level you stop using others' approval as the test and set a standard above the market. Requirement: it takes what it takes.
numbers: Analogy: $30 offer against $300 price.
