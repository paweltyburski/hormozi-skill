### MZ-5IC9H1PNRps#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:45
claim: A niche ERP for window-cleaning companies replaces patchwork software (inventory, CRM) but has no customers yet.
detail: Target firms average about $5M revenue, with about 20 in a state. A partner knows the industry. The owner debates pricing on value (slow uptake) versus lower price and back-end retail sales through the inventory module.
numbers: $99 entry plus $34.95 per employee seat per month; about 20 prospects per state; average revenue ~$5M.

### MZ-5IC9H1PNRps#02 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: pricing | also: offers | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:54
claim: Ask how much money the product saves or makes the buyer.
detail: Software subscription savings are minor; the primary value proposition should be the benefit of the system.
### MZ-5IC9H1PNRps#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: objections-closing | fw: Value Equation | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:50
claim: Business owners care about two things: money saved and how hard onboarding is, so lead with speed.
detail: Promise full migration off old systems in 24 hours, then settle on 48. A savings guarantee of twice the price was floated (unclear in audio).
numbers: onboarding in 24 to 48 hours; savings at 2x the price.

### MZ-5IC9H1PNRps#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:50
claim: A low per-seat price needs a very large customer count, and this is enterprise SaaS with painful sales.
detail: Customers are companies with seats within; the number of seats is what drives revenue. Be ready to suffer.
