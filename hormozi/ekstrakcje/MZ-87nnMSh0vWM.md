### MZ-87nnMSh0vWM#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: The time horizon you build for changes the foundation, so the fastest route to $1M is not the fastest route to $10M or $100M.
detail: He uses a block-tower exercise: with five seconds, five minutes or five years the best base differs. A one-client agency can reach $1M quickly, but reaching $100M requires different early choices. People want the speed of a one-storey building with the height of a hundred, skyrocket, plateau, and sometimes must take two steps back to rebuild the foundation.
numbers: Founders think in 5-year-to-exit terms; he suggests 50-year thinking.

### MZ-87nnMSh0vWM#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:35
claim: Deciding to run a business forever made him focus on the "factory" (durable systems) rather than the prototype, as Musk and Bezos did.
detail: He says building a prototype is easy while building the factory is far harder (he cites about 50x for cars). He cites Tesla building its own batteries and charging network, and Amazon choosing logistics as its moat, as long-horizon decisions whose moat shows up years later.
numbers: Factory said to be about 50x harder than a prototype.

### MZ-87nnMSh0vWM#03 | CASE | topic: retention-ltv | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:06
claim: Two companies with equal $3M revenue differ hugely if one loses all its customers each year and the other keeps them.
detail: Company B sells 100, 200, then 300 new units yearly and loses every customer, so revenue equals that year's sales. Company A sells 100 new a year and keeps everyone, reaching 300 customers and $3M by year three. B must sell more every year just to grow, so acquisition cost compresses its bottom line. A can add new customers or distribution on top of a retained base.
numbers: Three years, 100 new customers per year at about $1M per 100; B needs ever more (he states 600 new in year four, unclear in audio).

### MZ-87nnMSh0vWM#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: sales-process | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:43
claim: Strong marketing and sales skill can mask a leaky product; the business craters the moment sales slow.
detail: With a fixed sales velocity (say 100-1,000 sales a month) and customers who do not stay, you must always seek more distribution to refill a hole in the back of the bus. A sticky business is rarer than one that needs constant selling.

### MZ-87nnMSh0vWM#05 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: Price to Value Discrepancy | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:30
claim: When an owner says they are running out of time, a first question he often asks is what their margin is.
detail: If margins are thin they cannot afford help. That is a symptom; the root cause is an offer where price and terms are too low for what is delivered, or no marketing and sales motion that shows the value. At roughly 70% margin the answer is simply to hire.
numbers: About 70% margin is his example of "hire help".
### MZ-87nnMSh0vWM#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: operator-mindset | fw: Grand Slam Offer | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:20
claim: New founders price from their own wallet, assume their easy skill is easy for everyone, and so undercharge and fill their plate.
detail: Price depends on what the customer will pay, not what you feel you deserve. The cycle is undercharging, thin margin, doing all the work, never saying no, then having no time or cash to train or hire.
