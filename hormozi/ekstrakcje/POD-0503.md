### POD-0503#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:48
claim: After an exit, he built a holding company in business services and education, where he has an edge, rather than real estate.
detail: Liquidity leaves a pile of cash to redeploy; he buys assets in worlds he knows, adding value beyond capital and time. Social media serves to give value to the 99% without selling, while companies at $3M-$10M find them.
numbers: Average portfolio company $17M top-line revenue (as of August 2022); 11 companies; goal of gross portfolio revenue, then $1B.

### POD-0503#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:07
claim: Growing existing portfolio companies is faster and easier than adding new ones, so there is no acquisition quota.
detail: He deliberately has no deal-count goal, to avoid forcing acquisitions or incentivising deal-doing. Gross portfolio revenue can grow either way. It is a "pick right and wait" game, not quantity.

### POD-0503#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:48
claim: Typical deal: minority stake of 20-30%, in high cash-flow, low-capex businesses; avoids e-commerce as a capital sink.
detail: Sometimes a cheque, sometimes services for equity. If a business needed capital to expand (such as more corporate locations), there would be an argument about where the capital would go, but most of their businesses are not in that situation. Acquisition.com helps the founder in an exit.
numbers: Stake 20-30%; one portfolio company is in diligence with a private equity buyer, at the founder's pre-stated exit number after fees and taxes.
### POD-0503#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:56
claim: Information businesses are valued as service businesses: a transactional, founder-driven course seller has little value.
detail: A buyer wants predictability and growth. Founder-face businesses selling transactionally fail the first test, and are not worth pursuing for the second. With a large addressable market and recurring nature attached, it is a service business sellable at 10-15x EBITDA.
numbers: 10-15x EBITDA for transferable, recurring service businesses.

### POD-0503#05 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:08
claim: Making a founder-led business sellable takes about two years of removing the founder from ads and fulfilment.
detail: Remove the founder first from the ads, then from the content and delivery (calls, events, workshops, webinars). The easy-to-remove place is ads.
numbers: Transition ~2 years.

### POD-0503#06 | EXAMPLE | topic: management-ops | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:01
claim: Pre-sale involvement level at Gym Launch and Prestige Labs: direct reports only CEO and COO, a weekly one-on-one, quarterly meetings.
detail: They sold the two as a bundle. The founders did not speak at events, run coaching calls or train customers, did not attend weekly or monthly meetings, and only attended the quarterlies.

### POD-0503#07 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:48
claim: Gym Launch took two years to become sellable, following the banker's checklist, and sold with no earn-out or seller financing.
detail: They went to market early and asked bankers what was wrong: too much founder face and key-man risk, dependence on one or two channels, churn too high in licensing, no proof of an adjacent growth space. They fixed each, then sold: they walked away the day of signing, no consulting period.

### POD-0503#08 | EXAMPLE | topic: sales-team | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:13:48
claim: To diversify channels, they built a 26-person cold-call team; over half of sales at sale time came from cold calls and cold email.
detail: Hormozi values knowing sales would continue whatever happened to paid ads. The leadership team was hired out so the founders left the day to day.
numbers: 26-person cold call team; over 50% of sales from cold calls and emails.

### POD-0503#09 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:14:54
claim: Churn was cut in half via onboarding and customer-experience work and remixing the product suite to match avatars to products.
detail: They also proved a premium suite with a representative sample of sales, showing the next owner it had legs.
numbers: Churn halved over about two years; enterprise product suite 3x the price of the existing product.

### POD-0503#10 | TACTIC | topic: money-models | also: retention-ltv | fw: Continuity Offers | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:53
claim: To add recurring revenue to an info product, split the solution into one-time information and consumables such as accountability and community.
detail: Information is valuable the day before you get it, less the day after. Accountability, community and tools keep value tomorrow, so they can be sold as a back end building monthly recurring revenue. Finding recurring product-market fit comes first.

### POD-0503#11 | EXAMPLE | topic: money-models | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:17:38
claim: For a low-income audience such as personal development, the recurring layer is many cheap tools (tax services, website, funnel software).
detail: Their buyers are early-stage and lack revenue, so the back end must be low cost and tied to needs they will have as they start a business.

### POD-0503#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:12
claim: He likes businesses that sell one specific way of making money (such as rank-and-rent websites), not vague "make money online".
detail: A specific vehicle lets you predict needs and plug into future revenue streams. Customer success becomes key: more successes lead to more back-end revenue, and enterprise value rests on the likelihood of moving customers from their vehicle into yours.

### POD-0503#13 | FRAMEWORK | topic: verticals | also: money-models | fw: Offer Stack | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:20:55
claim: Gym Launch had two products: a turnkey acquisition system (front end) and a recurring back end of fresh ads, accountability, team-scaling systems and a white-labelled CRM.
detail: Front end: ads, pages, emails, meal plans, sales recordings (20,000 recordings), pricing, upsell, downsell and supplement scripts. Back end: new tested ads (ads fatigue), accountability, hiring systems for trainers, sales and front desk, and a gym CRM partner. They tried to send the back end to those who were successful at implementing the front end.
numbers: 20,000 recordings of others selling the same thing.
