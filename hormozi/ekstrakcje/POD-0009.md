### POD-0009#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: CAC | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:48
claim: What you can pay for traffic equals earnings per click, set by page conversion and price; the owner with the highest earnings per click can outbid everyone.
detail: Affiliate marketing logic: a flashlight offer paying affiliates $2-3 per click let sources costing 10-50 cents a click earn 6-10x. Apply it to a sales pipeline: conversion percentage times price tells you the most you can spend to acquire.
numbers: Affiliate payout $2-3 per click vs click cost $0.10-$0.50 = 6-10x; that offer did $20M in two months.

### POD-0009#02 | EXAMPLE | topic: paid-ads | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:05:34
claim: A $1,000 programme through a page converting 20% and a close rate of 20% earns about $40 per click.
detail: 1,000 clicks give 200 leads, 40 sales and $40,000. Offline conversion (phone, then visit) supports far higher prices than an online cart, so brick-and-mortar services can outbid online retailers. Hormozi claims this is ten times the earnings per click of a top affiliate offer.
numbers: 1,000 clicks, 200 leads, 40 buyers at $1,000, $40 per click.

### POD-0009#03 | CASE | topic: pricing | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:00
claim: A rep new to a boot camp sold about 80 people in the first 20 days at $600 for six weeks, in a gym that charged $99 a month.
detail: The rep had no price doubts because he was just told the price. The owner had sold an eight-week challenge for about $200. Same market, $100 a month against $100 a week.
numbers: About 80 sales in 20 days at $600 per six-week programme; former price $99/month or about $200 per challenge.

### POD-0009#04 | CASE | topic: paid-ads | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:24
claim: A client paid $40 per lead yet made about $130,000 in 22 days because 52% of leads bought a $2,500 offer.
detail: He ignores his lead cost like Hormozi does, since his conversion process makes it irrelevant. Sales count and spend do not reconcile in audio: stated 61 sales and about $4,000 of advertising, while $130,000 divided by $2,500 is about 52.
numbers: $40/lead; 52% close; $2,500 offer; about $130,000 gross in 22 days; about $4,000 ad spend (unclear in audio).

### POD-0009#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: unit-economics | also: paid-ads | fw: CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:21
claim: Work out your acquisition ceiling from front-end revenue per lead, not back-end projections.
detail: He avoids back-end numbers because they get exaggerated. A lot of times, he says, constantly trying to cut lead cost is not where the focus needs to be; focus on conversion process and pricing, and knowing your numbers lets you spend more confidently.
