### POD-0284#01 | DEFINITION | topic: strategy-positioning | also: money-models | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A new product line uses existing resources, while a new business needs new people, resources and capital.
detail: An upsell is a higher version of the same thing (group to personal training); a cross-sell is a different vehicle for the same need (group training to nutrition coaching). Food prep or apparel would be a new business. Large companies become a collection of smaller businesses once they pass roughly $10M.
numbers: multi-business structure from about $10M+ revenue.

### POD-0284#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:21
claim: When clients need an adjacent service, consider becoming an affiliate for a partner instead of building it, because the opportunity cost of building it might often be very high.
detail: His meals company makes almost nothing and he would now send customers to local providers or a national company in return for a discount or commission. Four reasons: no cost of delivery, stacked partner offers, all-profit commission, and flexibility.
### POD-0284#03 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: lead-getters | fw: Four Levels of Bonus Mastery | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:13
claim: Negotiate group deals with partners and bundle them as bonuses with a real price attached.
detail: Example: a chiropractor selling a $2,000 pain programme (12 to 18 weeks) approaches massage, orthotics, mattress and meal-prep businesses, asks for free or discounted items for all customers and a payment per referral. Bundled as, say, a true $150 massage value, it supports a higher package price while costing nothing.
numbers: $2,000 programme; example bundle worth $150.

### POD-0284#04 | BENCHMARK | topic: lead-getters | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:59
claim: Affiliate commissions can be 30% to 50% of revenue, far above typical business margins of 10% to 20%.
detail: Split discounts: if a partner gives 20% off, customers get 10% and you keep 10%. His group earns about $1M a year nearly passively from introductions to partners whose products it would recommend anyway.
numbers: commission 30-50% of revenue; own margins 10-20%; about $1M per year passive.

### POD-0284#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:46
claim: Affiliate status lets you switch to a better provider; owning the service creates a conflict when someone else is better.
detail: Owning a massage clinic under your chiropractic practice means paying staff, insurance and a bias to defend your own provider, while a referrer simply moves clients.

### POD-0284#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:37
claim: Buying or absorbing the partner's business makes sense as the exception, when the enterprise value you send it surpasses that of your existing vehicle.
detail: Otherwise, he says it would usually benefit you to simply take the small kickbacks and commissions and stay focused on your core.
