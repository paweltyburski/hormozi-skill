### YT-K3cSPJDlcVc#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Being stuck at a level comes from a wrong belief, a missing character trait or a missing skill, and all three need checking.
detail: Specialists tend to talk about only one: skills, character or mindset. Ask of any owner or business at a plateau which of the three is the gap.

### YT-K3cSPJDlcVc#02 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:50
claim: An online-marketing owner at about $1.5M a month said no one in his space made over $2M a month; Hormozi called that a belief problem, citing gyms.
detail: When he entered gyms nobody there did more than about $3M a year, and his company passed $30M a year. Ask the owner why, in physical terms, they cannot pass that: for a mass-market consumer business there is no shortage of customers. The owner ended the talk believing $10M a month was possible.
numbers: $1.5M/month owner; space ceiling $2M/month; gym space ceiling ~$3M/year then $30M+.

### YT-K3cSPJDlcVc#03 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:50
claim: Test a belief by adding up your competitors: with ten rivals at $2M a month each, one better than all could reach $20M a month.
detail: Each competitor has its own way of acquiring and fulfilling customers; if you do each better, nothing stops you from taking their combined revenue. Real caps exist only when you serve a small fixed population, such as 50,000 US chiropractors, and even then you expand to adjacent verticals.
numbers: 10 competitors x $2M/month = $20M/month; 50,000 chiropractors.

### YT-K3cSPJDlcVc#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:48
claim: Many owners stop growing once their personal needs are met because they do not truly believe the product helps enough people.
detail: Past your own spending needs, the only reason to grow is that the product benefits others, which shifts the focus from self to others. The trait check is also patience to see it through.

### YT-K3cSPJDlcVc#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:41
claim: A missing skill can be met by learning it or by finding the right person; as long as you know what needs to happen, you can find the "who".
detail: Example: lacking the skill of recruiting someone to open a new sales channel. He told an owner at only $50-100K a month asking when to hand off running the business that he was just starting.
numbers: $50,000-$100,000 per month (unclear in audio).

### YT-K3cSPJDlcVc#06 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:42
claim: Ask whether you have actually sold a meaningful share of your marketplace; at 1%, 0.5% or 0.1% the cap is skill, belief or trait, not the market.
detail: Then check whether all or half of combined competitor revenue could be yours if you were way better than all of them, and only then look at traits and skills.
