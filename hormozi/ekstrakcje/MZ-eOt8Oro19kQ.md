### MZ-eOt8Oro19kQ#01 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: The owner of a performance-parts business was offered about 3x profit with no plan for what next; Hormozi leaned against selling.
detail: Products are exhaust, intercooler and intake systems for German cars; almost all one-time buyers. Offer: part upfront, part seller-financed. After about 20% tax he would net about $3M on top of existing savings of a few million, which changes little in daily life.
numbers: Revenue about $5M, profit $2.1M, offer $6M (3x), $4.5M upfront plus $1.5M seller financing; repeat buyers 10 to 15 only.

### MZ-eOt8Oro19kQ#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:54
claim: People always want what they lack, and selling removes the good parts of the business along with the bad.
detail: After selling you must replace the good, probably by starting again, and a non-compete stops you taking your team.

### MZ-eOt8Oro19kQ#03 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:47
claim: Ask whether the seller has another business they want to start and how much they have already saved.
detail: With this caller, who answered "nothing serious" and had no plan for what to do next, he pointed out that he would probably work again.
### MZ-eOt8Oro19kQ#04 | TACTIC | topic: finance-exit | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:01
claim: Before selling, test whether you can get what you want (such as travel) inside the business.
detail: Imagine being forced to solve it without selling, for example a week off every six to twelve weeks.

### MZ-eOt8Oro19kQ#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: finance-exit | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:18
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: Hormozi would not sell unless there is a specific next thing the money would do; weigh the sale proceeds against the profit you would earn anyway and the transition work.
detail: Here proceeds equal about 24 months of profit, plus 6 to 12 months of post-sale work at much lower pay. He questioned hoping for a guarantee from the world.
numbers: Proceeds about 24 months of profit; transition 6 to 12 months.
### MZ-eOt8Oro19kQ#06 | CASE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: content-brand, verticals | fw: Track Lose Print | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:19
claim: A retired nurse selling coaching for nurses moving into life insurance wanted to scale from $1.5M to $10M while removing herself as the personal brand.
detail: She sells a high-ticket programme with one-to-many Zooms, 150 customers, other coaches who need her to act, and a funnel based on digital challenges. Hormozi said she is too early to delegate and should spend more.
numbers: Revenue $1.5M, profit about $1.2M; ads for 9 months; one challenge spent $25,000 for about $500,000 (about 20x); goal $10M this year.

### MZ-eOt8Oro19kQ#07 | DIAGNOSTIC_Q | topic: scaling-constraints | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:10:14
claim: He asks what exactly is delivered and which part is not scalable, then how many customers and whether delivery is one-to-many.
detail: Then asks what stops the same delivery serving 1,000 customers.

### MZ-eOt8Oro19kQ#08 | EXAMPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:36
claim: Advice to this caller, not stated as a general rule: he is super early, so don't try to remove yourself yet; spend much more to grow first.
detail: Hormozi suggested spending $500,000 to make $10M for a couple of years, after which he'd have around $20 to $30M to hire strong people, who are not cheap. Doing it now would risk a large share of his income and probably shrink the business.
numbers: Target example: spend $500,000 to make $10M, versus spending $25,000 and making $500,000 so far.
### MZ-eOt8Oro19kQ#09 | CASE | topic: hiring-talent | also: verticals | fw: Buy vs Build | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:14:57
claim: A wealth management firm for federal employees needed three licensed advisors fast and compared training, recruiting and tryout cohorts.
detail: Licences are technical and regulated. Hormozi called it a many-ways-up-the-mountain problem where all three work; buying talent is faster, building adds long-term value.
numbers: Revenue about $7M, EBITDA $3M, goal $10M EBITDA; 7,000 target clients.

### MZ-eOt8Oro19kQ#10 | PRINCIPLE | topic: hiring-talent | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:29
claim: Service margin is the gap between what a person's skill costs and what you can charge after training them.
detail: Pay people at current skill, add skills, charge for the new skills. The gap grows with marketing and sales strength or training strength.

### MZ-eOt8Oro19kQ#11 | TACTIC | topic: hiring-talent | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:04
claim: Recruit already-licensed junior advisors who struggle early at big firms, offering an acquisition system and a revenue split.
detail: Target them in their first three years before the golden handcuffs; the firm feeds clients. He prefers exceptional training over a survive-or-leave model.
numbers: First three years of a junior advisor's career.

### MZ-eOt8Oro19kQ#12 | PRINCIPLE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: hiring-talent | fw: Theory of Constraints | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:14
claim: Plan for 7 to 70, not 7 to 10, and build the recruiting, onboarding, training, performance and compensation system with a clear career path.
detail: Client acquisition was reliable, so manpower was the constraint, and fixing it unlocks enterprise value. Think what you will need at 10x that you lack now.
numbers: Target thinking: $7M to $70M.
