### MM-22#01 | DEFINITION | topic: money-models | also: objections-closing | fw: Downsell Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:00
claim: Downsells, the money model's third prong, follow a "no": fit the customer's budget by changing how they pay or what they get.
detail: How they pay: now vs over time. What they get: quality, quantity, or something different. Usable anywhere — up front, after an upsell yes or no, or on the back end — to lift 30-day profit (00:03:15). Coming next: payment plans, trial with penalty, feature downsells.

### MM-22#02 | CASE | topic: objections-closing | also: pricing, money-models | fw: Downsell Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:00:46
claim: A car salesman cut the same insurance from $5,000 to $400 step by step and lost the buyer's trust in the car price too.
detail: A friend first refused because of the price, finally because she no longer trusted him; she then doubted the car's price and didn't want to buy it from him.

### MM-22#03 | PRINCIPLE | topic: pricing | also: objections-closing, money-models | fw: Downsell Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:32
claim: Never offer the same thing for less — that is discounting, not downselling, and it shows your prices are made up.
detail: The customer will doubt every later price and tell others; you trade trust for a buck. Offer something different for less instead. If they want exactly that but won't pay, tough — though a payment plan (less now, more over time) is fine (00:02:18).

### MM-22#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: money-models | fw: Downsell Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:01:32
claim: A "no" rejects this offer, not all offers — use it to learn what they really want.
detail: Treat the refusal as information and profit from it.

### MM-22#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: objections-closing | also: money-models, sales-process | fw: Downsell Offers | novelty: canon | ts: 00:02:18
claim: Downsells are trades: personalise, don't pressure — more of what they like, less of what they don't, at a matching price.
detail: Give something only to get something. Refused a large soda? Offer a small, a juice or a coffee — not offering help is what's offensive. Think a hundred ways to offer what you have, not a hundred products.
