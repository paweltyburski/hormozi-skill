### POD-0190#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:25
claim: Competitors do not steal customers; you lose them, because they were open to leaving.
detail: Treat a customer relationship like any other: someone who is satisfied is not available to a rival's pitch. This puts the responsibility, and the power to fix it, with you, and removes needless worry about competitors.

### POD-0190#02 | TACTIC | topic: strategy-positioning | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:03:39
claim: Help firms in your own space, even rivals, because the market is huge and a community tests and innovates faster than one company.
detail: Hormozi held 10-15 conversations in about a month with gym agencies and gurus, offering marketing and retention methods. They were shocked, and the goodwill built reciprocal relationships. He says his income was unchanged. Exception: he does not help people who publicly attack him. A neighbourhood with several gyms on the same system still leaves hundreds of gyms not using it.
numbers: Roughly 6 gyms using his system versus about 500 not, in one example local area; 10-15 conversations in about a month.

### POD-0190#03 | SCRIPT | topic: management-ops | also: hiring-talent | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:23
claim: When a trainer leaves to open a competing gym, offer to help them rather than reacting with anger or arguing back.
detail: Three options: respond petty, stay silent, or help. Silence beats arguing because observers remember only pettiness. Helping works best because people act in their own interest: ask how you can help them win, point to the many overweight people both can serve, and share lessons from your early mistakes. It also stops them from badmouthing you.

### POD-0190#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:40
claim: Attract by values (psychographics) not only demographics; helping a competitor costs little because their buyers hold different beliefs.
detail: Best clients stay because their values match yours. Hormozi is polarising by design and attracts people willing to grind. Going vanilla in the middle makes you untrue to yourself.

### POD-0190#05 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: retention-ltv | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:40
claim: The real competitor in every market is doing nothing, apathy, not rivals.
detail: Gym owners are united against the couch, against staying home and skipping appointments. Your capacity is small next to demand, so improving service and retention matters more than rivalry.

### POD-0190#06 | EXAMPLE | topic: management-ops | also: sales-team | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:08:53
claim: Leaders should do frontline work first-hand, such as Zappos executives spending two weeks in customer service.
detail: Hormozi still takes sales calls though it makes no financial sense, to understand each role's problems and decide from reality. He says anyone should sell first, work chat first, before managing.
numbers: Zappos executives start with 2 weeks of frontline customer service.
