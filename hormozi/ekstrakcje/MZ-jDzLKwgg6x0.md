### MZ-jDzLKwgg6x0#01 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:00
claim: At the top end returns concentrate in the winners, but achieving them costs proportionally in the things the achievement was meant to buy.
detail: At some point the hard work is learning you cannot work harder and must change what got you here; you can work to get anything but must sacrifice other wants to keep it.

### MZ-jDzLKwgg6x0#02 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:01:10
claim: Hardship persists because you habituate to one kind of cost but life changes the type and intensity, so each new kind of failure hurts again.
detail: His example: a front-desk problem at a monthly event repeated three times, each time fixed in one version and failing in another. He is comfortable paying long hours and lost social time, which he no longer counts as a sacrifice.

### MZ-jDzLKwgg6x0#03 | CASE | topic: management-ops | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:34
claim: He challenged a team to deliver a planned 12-week project in seven days, tracking hours worked together, with a goal of under 50 hours for eight people.
detail: The team worked roughly 12-14 hour days in one boardroom, and every member afterwards said they wished they could work like that always. His point: once you know how to work, work is self-reinforcing; the pain is the other things you must give up.
numbers: 12 weeks compressed to 7 days; target under 50 team hours; eight people; about 22.5 hours logged after the first part.

### MZ-jDzLKwgg6x0#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: strategy-positioning | also: operator-mindset | fw: Leverage | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:05:36
claim: Working smart (high-leverage choices) only wins against dumb competitors; once everyone works smart, working hard is what is left.
detail: He compares it to a weightlifting system that breaks all but one athlete. He treats someone stopping at a few million a year as quitting too soon, though he is looking for the sweet spot where he stops making some trades.
numbers: Stopping point "a few million a year" described as too early (his view).
