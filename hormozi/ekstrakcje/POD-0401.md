### POD-0401#01 | BENCHMARK | topic: content-brand | also: leads-fundamentals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:27
claim: Six months of 80 posts a week produced roughly a million-person audience across platforms and a large inbound flow, with no ads.
detail: Added about 300,000 YouTube subscribers, about 350,000 TikTok followers, about 100,000 Twitter followers, about 150,000 Amazon buyers of the 99-cent book, and 400,000 podcast downloads a month. ### POD-0401#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: paid-ads | also: content-brand | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:12
claim: Valued at a paid-media rate, the organic reach was worth about $2 million a month in exposure.
detail: Computed at a $25 CPM from his own business experience; he stresses it is exposure that pays him rather than costs him.
numbers: $25 CPM; about $2M a month of equivalent exposure.

### POD-0401#03 | BENCHMARK | topic: content-brand | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:14:59
claim: Running this at his volume cost about $20,000 a month for one person's content team, or $40,000 a month for two principals.
detail: Separate editors for Twitter, YouTube, LinkedIn, podcast, and Instagram/TikTok. He uses money to make up for limited time, says it can be scaled down however you want, and says you could probably do it on your own.
numbers: $20,000/month per person; $40,000/month for two.
### POD-0401#04 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:15:49
claim: His own time commitment is small: a daily mind dump, a weekly Twitter review of about four hours, one day a month recording shorts and a half day recording 4-8 direct-to-camera videos.
detail: Total roughly two days a month plus four hours a week. Twitter drives the rest: he tweets about five times a day, and the winners become the video topics.
numbers: 2 days/month + 4 hours/week; 4-8 YouTube videos per half-day session; about 5 tweets/day.

### POD-0401#05 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:19
claim: Long-video structure: hook or question, a relevant story, the framework applied in that story, and why it works; longer videos repeat the story-and-framework block.
detail: Based on a long interview with MrBeast, who reduced performance to click-through rate times watch time. The video must fulfil the promise of the thumbnail so viewers stay.

### POD-0401#06 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:18:19
claim: Short-video structure: hook or question, then the tweet itself as the "hammer" (already proven to convert), then an example, then an explanation.
detail: Example conversion: a tweet thread of 28 ways to stay poor became a video with 28 rules for reprogramming your mind to be rich, with only the headline changed.

### POD-0401#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Depth-then-Width | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:34
claim: Short clips provide breadth and long videos provide depth, so he makes both.
detail: He first disliked shorts, then noticed he had never consumed a long piece by a creator he follows closely, so reach and depth need separate formats.

### POD-0401#08 | TACTIC | topic: content-brand | also: lead-getters | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:19:27
claim: Record two versions of each type of call to action (other channels, lead magnet, share or tag, review, low-ticket product, submit a question) and have editors inject them at the start, middle and end.
detail: Without CTAs the content produces impressions and followers but no result. Podcast downloads grew from about 20,000 to 400,000 a month after this change. Elsewhere he later uses about five versions per type.
numbers: 2 variants per CTA type here (about 5 in the later livestream).

### POD-0401#09 | DECISION_RULE | topic: content-brand | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:09:06
claim: Focus on what is inside the content, not the wrapper; the production-quality gap matters about 20%, the substance about 80%.
detail: He built two expensive studios and published three videos a week, then saw a poor-quality video recorded on holiday outperform a polished one.
numbers: Subscribers went from 600 to 70,000 in the first 12 months, before the volume increase.

### POD-0401#10 | EXAMPLE | topic: content-brand | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:11
claim: His three goals for starting video content: attract internet owners above $3M who share his values, help smaller owners reach that deal size, and document best practices.
detail: Inbound applications arrived on their own once the content ran. He wanted the records that founders such as Bezos never left, including near-death moments of the company.
numbers: Acquisition.com takes $3M+ internet businesses to $30M+ sellable ones; minimum deal size $3M.
applies_to: Owner of a services or investing firm that needs qualified inbound.

### POD-0401#11 | EXAMPLE | topic: content-brand | also: strategy-positioning | fw: Core Four | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:06:02
claim: What changed his mind about building an audience: celebrity brands that monetised well, plus evidence that organic works for B2B, not just consumer products.
detail: Examples he cites: a celebrity-built beauty brand, a $2B+ tequila brand launched off an organic audience, a fighter's whiskey brand, but all consumer e-commerce, which he disliked. B2B proof came from a podcast guest getting about 3,000 applications a month and a marketer reaching about $100 million a year in B2B agency services off inbound. A friend's willingness to pay the price of harassment for impact was the last push. Previously all his companies grew on paid ads, affiliates, outbound teams or referrals.
numbers: About 3,000 applications/month (guest, unverified); about $100M/year B2B agency revenue off inbound (Neil Patel, as stated).

### POD-0401#12 | BENCHMARK | topic: leads-fundamentals | also: outreach | fw: Rule of 100 | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:20
claim: Effort gap in cold calling: 10 calls every three days when motivated, versus 200 dials every day before lunch, is about a 50x difference, not 2-4x.
detail: Told alongside the flyer lesson (300 flyers tested, when the business owner tested 5,000 and then ran 5,000 a day). Separately, Cardone told him he had about ten times his content on Instagram and said it's about volume.
numbers: 10 calls per 3 days vs 200 per day = about 50x; flyers 300 vs 5,000 test, then 5,000 per day.
### POD-0401#13 | EXAMPLE | topic: content-brand | also: - | fw: Proof Over Promise | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:23:10
claim: Thought experiment for authority: the same keynote delivered by an unknown with a 1,000-person audience would be received as preachy and pointless.
detail: The frame decides how content is consumed (a billionaire, or a famous founder tweeting a toilet joke, gets huge reach). Hormozi says the content model is "boring" and that the real driver of growth is having evidence: he built the companies first. Weak frames include 18-year-old relationship coaches and business coaches at about $5,000 a month.
numbers: Example audience size: 1,000 total; coach income example: $5,000 a month.

### POD-0401#14 | DECISION_RULE | topic: content-brand | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:25:17
