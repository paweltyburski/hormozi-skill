### YT-by3ZEoo-Quc#01 | FRAMEWORK | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:00:00
claim: There are four sources of money for a purchase: past money (savings), income money, debt (future earnings) and new money.
detail: Past money is saved earnings, income is what you earn monthly, debt borrows from the future, and new money is extra earnings created specifically for the purchase. He observes that the friends who are wealthiest with money are okay buying things that are a bit grandiose; new money is the category he personally likes.
### YT-by3ZEoo-Quc#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:03:23
claim: For a want that is not an investment, fund it from new money earned within a defined period, and use debt only if it can be repaid within a year.
detail: Do not touch savings or the monthly income flow. Debt is acceptable only with a clear repayment plan, no prepayment penalty and payoff inside about a year. Example: work one extra day a week for a year to buy a car or boat. It can be a month of overtime or six months.
numbers: Debt allowed only if repaid within about 12 months.

### YT-by3ZEoo-Quc#03 | DEFINITION | topic: operator-mindset | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:36
claim: "Sawdust money" is income generated from resources you already own but underuse, aimed at one specific project.
detail: Underused resources include spare time, a spare room, an extra client project or one-to-one services. The want becomes motivation to find resources instead of draining existing ones. A friend's line: "write yourself a swimming pool", as Paul McCartney would write a song to pay for one.

### YT-by3ZEoo-Quc#04 | CASE | topic: finance-exit | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:01:45
claim: He bought a 36,000 sq ft building he did not need and committed to making it earn more than it cost, which led to launching advisory services.
detail: He wanted a commercial-grade home gym and meetup space. His justification was that portfolio companies spent about $4M a year on event venues that the building could replace. His wife suggested a 5,000 sq ft building; he chose the larger one and promised it would pay for itself. Another owner choosing an office 2.5x pricier was told to go ahead if he could pay it off within a year.
numbers: Building 36,000 sq ft vs 5,000 suggested; about $4M a year spent on venues across portfolio.

### YT-by3ZEoo-Quc#05 | EXAMPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: operator-mindset | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:04:26
claim: A single mother who could not afford the gym membership offered to drive for Uber one day a week to pay for it and became a long-standing member who lost 100 pounds.
detail: He uses it as a case of choosing to earn the extra instead of declining the purchase.
numbers: One day of driving per week; 100 pounds lost.

### YT-by3ZEoo-Quc#06 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: finance-exit | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:06:39
claim: Stay ahead of spending by tying each large purchase to its own extra earnings, not by raising lifestyle with income.
detail: Meant for people who already have some money; if broke, focus only on making new money. It applies to business equipment too: if a nicer machine cannot be justified, earn the difference yourself. He reports never regretting a purchase made this way, and enjoying the work more because the target is known.
