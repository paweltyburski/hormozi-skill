### YT-N5MExtki_VI#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: $100M Scaling Roadmap | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:40
claim: A couples-coaching business earned good margin but was capped by its own time and bled cash on ads.
detail: The owners coach entrepreneur couples (businesses at about $150K a year or more). A $5,000 12-month group mentorship (one weekly call, community) and a $25,000 12-month accelerator (customised plan, quarterly 2-on-2 strategy calls, weekly small-group Q&A, daily access in Slack). ### YT-N5MExtki_VI#05 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: product-delivery | fw: Delivery Cube | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:16:59
claim: When supply is the constraint there are four levers: raise the price, change the delivery ratio, productize, or hire.
detail: Ratio means moving from one-to-one to one-to-four, one-to-six, then one-to-many. Hormozi goes back and forth with the client to choose the lever that fits their appetite, cash and goals, noting each lever has businesses that succeeded with it. Which to pick depends on which problem the owners would rather solve.

### YT-N5MExtki_VI#07 | BENCHMARK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:14:21

### YT-N5MExtki_VI#09 | PRINCIPLE | topic: offers | also: sales-process | fw: Bonus Checklist | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:13:39

### YT-N5MExtki_VI#11 | PRINCIPLE | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: Value vs Willingness-to-Pay Matrix | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:46:58
claim: Extra unused features raise cancellations because the buyer sees how little of the price they consume.
detail: Planet Fitness found 80% of members mostly use cardio and some weights, so it dropped courts and pools and cut price. Gyms charging $59-89 still lost people who felt they used a fraction. A bundle of seven amenities for $29.99 reads as waste. Give the buyer 100% of what they want and charge for it.
numbers: ~80% of members use mostly cardio and weights; clients feel they use "20% of their $59".

### YT-N5MExtki_VI#12 | TACTIC | topic: offers | also: pricing | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:11:32

### YT-N5MExtki_VI#15 | SCRIPT | topic: promotions-launches | also: sales-process | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:36:09
claim: Two-day event rhythm: intro, meeting blocks, lunch re-pitch, more meetings, a short pitch, and dinner for buyers.
detail: Day: intro, two meeting blocks, lunch with a soft re-pitch of 15-30 minutes that answers objections the team heard the day before while still teaching, two more blocks, wrap. ### YT-N5MExtki_VI#17 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: sales-process | fw: Challenge Structure | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:26
claim: Replace the five-day challenge with a single three-to-four-hour "date night" event that ends in a pitch.
detail: The owners' clients loved the date-night challenge. One long session in-state lets you cover the same information with intros and outros removed (about three quality hours out of five) and weave in more. Fewer people drop out because there is no day two, so about twice as many reach the pitch. The date-night frame gives permission for length; weekday does not matter. Open with the frame "date night is both business and fun".
numbers: Expected twice as many people at the pitch; day-one show 9% vs 4-5% on day two.

### YT-N5MExtki_VI#19 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: money-models | fw: Payment Plan Downsell | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:25:14

### YT-N5MExtki_VI#24 | DECISION_RULE | topic: paid-ads | also: unit-economics | fw: CAC | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:31:31

### YT-N5MExtki_VI#25 | PRINCIPLE | topic: verticals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: Sales to Fulfillment Continuum | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:17:51
claim: Coaching businesses rarely scale by hiring coaches because the founder's hard-to-teach skill gets diluted; a smaller ratio keeps the full skill.
detail: If a method can be taught in a day it is probably not that complex. Hormozi has not seen coaching beyond about $30M a year without a distributed model. His "shot glass" picture: pouring one expert into many small groups preserves concentrated expertise, while hiring gives a diluted full glass. Group ratio also improves as the expert practises. Example: a famous speaker fills an auditorium of about 10,000 people paying around $5,000 each.
numbers: ~$30M a year ceiling for coaching he has seen (his experience, not a rule).

### YT-N5MExtki_VI#26 | FRAMEWORK | topic: scaling-constraints | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-framework | ts: 00:20:18
claim: Each capacity lever turns you into a different kind of business.
detail: Hiring makes you a recruiting, training and culture business (and pay must match the founder's skill). Productizing makes you a marketing and sales business because delivery is automated. Raising price with fixed supply makes you a brand business: customers must become stories and referrals that justify rising prices. Changing ratio simply kicks the can: practice lets the same slot hold more value.

### YT-N5MExtki_VI#27 | DECISION_RULE | topic: product-delivery | also: retention-ltv | fw: Delivery Cube | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:43:25
claim: Move one-on-one clients into small groups of about four; above seven it becomes one-to-many, and change cadence before ratio.
detail: Up to about seven people is still small-group. Start with monthly one-on-four plan-setting sessions rather than quarterly, since cadence is easier to change than ratio. The framing: fewest decisions that matter; do not make clients dependent on you for daily questions. Hormozi says unlimited one-to-one weekly would need a $200,000 price.
numbers: Group ceiling ~7; $200,000 price for weekly one-to-one.

### YT-N5MExtki_VI#29 | TACTIC | topic: objections-closing | also: offers | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:41:52
