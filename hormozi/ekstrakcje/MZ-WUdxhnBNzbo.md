### MZ-WUdxhnBNzbo#01 | CASE | topic: verticals | also: scaling-constraints | fw: - | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:00:00
claim: A music-lesson school out of space, filled only by word of mouth and not very profitable, wants a second site.
detail: Hormozi says do not open another: a second unprofitable site only dilutes. Fix the model first, e.g. raise prices if at capacity.

### MZ-WUdxhnBNzbo#02 | DECISION_RULE | topic: scaling-constraints | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:54
claim: Nail it, then scale it: for a chain, location one must print money with a simple model first.
detail: At 100 locations, going from one to two does nothing and perfecting one does everything. Needing many small tasks to keep a site afloat means the model is broken.

### MZ-WUdxhnBNzbo#03 | TACTIC | topic: promotions-launches | also: verticals | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:02:35
claim: Every successful chain has an exceptional grand-opening procedure; test the offer on the existing site.
detail: Gym Launch could fill a location in 30 days. Work out LTV to CAC at the new higher price and capacity, then budget the opening.

### MZ-WUdxhnBNzbo#04 | EXAMPLE | topic: unit-economics | also: promotions-launches | fw: LTGP:CAC | novelty: new-example | ts: 00:02:35
claim: Grand opening budget worked example: 200 kids, $50 CAC each, 3x efficiency loss, 30 days costs triple.
detail: $50 per child gives $10,000 at perfect efficiency. Divide efficiency by three, since not everyone buys at once, and a year-long spend becomes about $30,000 in 30 days. Add it to the location's return-of-capital figure.
numbers: $50 CAC per kid; kid worth ~$2,000; 200 kids; $10,000 over a year versus $30,000 in 30 days.

### MZ-WUdxhnBNzbo#05 | BENCHMARK | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:04:11

detail: About 10% on top of a base, up to 20% when very lucrative; it is worth it when the manager earns six figures.
numbers: 10-20% profit share; manager pay above $100K.
