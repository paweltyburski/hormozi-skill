### POD-0080#01 | TACTIC | topic: product-delivery | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Sell three days a week instead of unlimited, and open a session only once you know it will fill.
detail: A three-day plan roughly doubles capacity. Opening many sessions in hope of signing people up leaves half-empty classes. He calls these start-up choices painful to correct later.
numbers: Three days a week roughly doubles capacity versus unlimited.
applies_to: New group-training gyms.

### POD-0080#02 | BENCHMARK | topic: pricing | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:42
claim: Price the lowest tier at $170-$210 a month and semi-private at $500-$700; nothing between $210 and $500 is worth offering.
detail: He adds that the facility should be able to serve at least 24 people per hour. Semi-private at $500-$700 a month works out to roughly $125-$175 a week.
numbers: Lowest tier $170-$210/month; semi-private $500-$700/month (about $125-$175/week); no tier between $210 and $500; capacity 24+ per hour.
applies_to: Group-training gyms (2018 US prices).

### POD-0080#03 | TACTIC | topic: pricing | also: unit-economics | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:00:45
claim: Bill weekly rather than monthly to collect more money.
detail: Offered as one of the easy tactical changes, listed with the schedule and price rules. No reasoning beyond the extra collection is given.

### POD-0080#04 | PRINCIPLE | topic: product-delivery | also: strategy-positioning | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:03:13
claim: If a gym claims it is amazing but cannot get people in, the experience is not good enough: half of all gyms are below average, and above average is still not good.
detail: A truly great gym produces word of mouth and fills itself. Category economics send most of the gains to the category leader, like the top product on a shopping site with thousands of reviews against a hundred. He would have spent less effort on marketing and sales and more on a memorable experience, and treats lack of friends and family referrals at scale as the sign you have not succeeded yet.
numbers: Example: top listing ~2,000 five-star reviews vs second ~100.

### POD-0080#05 | TACTIC | topic: retention-ltv | also: product-delivery | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:07:32
claim: Test your experience by imagining you may not market or sell, then fix the details that would make people bring friends.
detail: Examples to inspect: what the first five seconds of a class look like, the exact words and energy of the trainer, clothing, where people stand, whether members greet each other, whether everyone is greeted by name. SoulCycle is cited as built on an experience so good that members spread it without a recurring membership. Chipotle is the other example of a business that needs little marketing.

### POD-0080#06 | DECISION_RULE | topic: hiring-talent | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:08:20
claim: Hire an operator as soon as you possibly can and pay them what they are worth.
detail: Visionary founders typically are not good at details, meetings, billing and communication cadences, which still matter for customer experience. He says he underpaid people early on as a mistake; he credits Layla's operations skill for Gym Launch's fast growth. The operator should be someone who truly cares about customers. He would probably suggest starting around the high $30,000s to $40,000 a year, and says every market is different.
numbers: Starting salary idea (probably): high $30,000s to $40,000 per year.
### POD-0080#07 | PRINCIPLE | topic: operator-mindset | also: management-ops | fw: - | novelty: new-detail | ts: 00:12:42
claim: The last thing he learned scaling was to accept "no", which usually means "yes, but not yet" or "not now".
detail: Rather than push, ask what would make the person comfortable doing it. The business is a reflection of the owner, so it grows when the owner does.
